Stock Market Today: Oil Hits Its Highest Since April and the Dow Drops 653 Points

The Dow fell 652.82 points, or 1.23%, on Friday as Brent crude settled at $116.72 after more attacks on Saudi Arabia. The S&P 500 lost 0.52% for a second weekly loss.

Nora Whitfield·Markets editor·23 Oct 2026, 5:15 p.m. ET·3 min read

Oil set the tone on Friday. Brent crude, the global benchmark, settled at $116.72 a barrel, up 3.1% and its highest settlement since 29 April, after more attacks on Saudi Arabia and talk of renewed US strikes on Iran. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 652.82 points, or 1.23%, to 52,215.16, more than double the S&P 500’s 0.52% drop to 7,623.68, and the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.21% to 25,955.34.

Oil rose for a third week

Yemen’s Houthi rebels launched another wave of attacks on Saudi Arabia, The National reported on Friday, a day after Saudi Aramco evacuated its headquarters in Dhahran. The New York Times reported this week that the US and Israel have drawn up plans for renewed strikes on Iran after the 3 November midterms. “We could end it very quickly. Do we want to finish it off or just want to let it wither?” President Trump told reporters on Friday.

US crude settled at $103.27, up 3.8%, and Brent gained about 6% for the week, its third weekly rise. Energy was the best S&P 500 sector, up 2.51%, and Exxon Mobil (XOM) and Chevron (CVX) closed at record highs. Airlines paid the bill: United Airlines (UAL) fell 4.0% and Delta Air Lines (DAL) 3.1%.

Inflation worries spread

S&P Global’s monthly survey of US companies found the prices they charge rising at the fastest pace since August 2022. “Cost pressures remain a key concern,” said Chris Williamson of S&P Global Market Intelligence. The University of Michigan’s final October reading of consumer sentiment slipped to 48.0 from 48.1 in September, a five-month low, Bloomberg reported.

That leaves the Federal Reserve, which raised rates by a quarter point on 16 September, in a bind. Traders expect it to hold at next week’s meeting but put the odds of a December hike at about 80%, according to CME FedWatch.

Nowhere left to hide

Thursday’s hiding places got sold too. Consumer staples, the best sector on Thursday, were the worst on Friday, down 1.83%, and Procter & Gamble (PG) fell 2.8% a day after its best session since 2020. About 7 in 10 S&P 500 stocks fell, 367 against 135 that rose, by our count.

The Dow gives more weight to pricier shares, so drops in Caterpillar (CAT), down 2.4%, and Goldman Sachs (GS), down 1.8%, hit it harder than the S&P 500.

SpaceX rattled the phone companies

AT&T (T) slid 8.0% and T-Mobile US (TMUS) 7.7% after Bloomberg reported that SpaceX is in talks for at least 80,000 US cell sites for a phone network of its own. Tower owner Crown Castle (CCI) soared 9.1%, the best performer in the S&P 500.

Job cuts at AmEx, a rally in security

American Express (AXP) fell 3.7% after beating profit forecasts and then saying on its call that it would cut about 5% of its global workforce, roughly 3,500 jobs by 24/7 Wall St’s count. Finance chief Christophe Le Caillec told CNBC the aim is to make AmEx “leaner, more agile, and also to prepare for AI.”

Cybersecurity was the one corner of tech that rallied hard: CrowdStrike (CRWD) soared 8.3% on an upbeat Evercore ISI survey of security resellers, 24/7 Wall St reported, and Palo Alto Networks (PANW) jumped 7.2%. Apple (AAPL) briefly passed Nvidia (NVDA) as the world’s most valuable company at the open as its first foldable iPhone went on sale, but Nvidia was back ahead within about ten minutes, by our estimate from five-minute prices; Apple closed down 0.14%.
