Alphabet (GOOGL) Shares Plunge After Search Falls Short and Cash Burn Grows

Alphabet plunged 12.6% on Thursday, its worst day since its 2004 listing, after search revenue missed estimates and free cash flow came in at negative $9.07 billion.

What happened

Shares of Google’s parent company Alphabet (GOOGL) plunged 12.6% on Thursday after its third-quarter search revenue, profit, and cash flow all fell short of what Wall Street wanted. Yahoo Finance and IBTimes said it was the stock’s worst day since Alphabet went public in 2004.

The results came out after the close on Wednesday. Revenue rose 25% to $127.58 billion, just below the $128.22 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG. Search revenue grew 12% to $63.35 billion, which Reuters and Yahoo Finance both said missed estimates. Earnings of $2.12 a share were well below the roughly $3 expected, partly because of an $8.66 billion loss on stakes Alphabet holds in other companies, the release showed.

“The bears will argue this is the first quarter that AI is starting to seriously hurt their search business,” Pivotal Research analyst Jeffrey Wlodarczak said, as quoted by Yahoo Finance and IBTimes. The shares closed at $283.82, down 12.6% from the previous close.

Why it matters

Investors have two worries, and this report fed both. The first is that AI chatbots draw people away from Google search, which still brings in about half of Alphabet’s revenue. The second is cost: Alphabet spent $51.36 billion on property and equipment in the quarter, more than double the $23.95 billion a year earlier. Free cash flow, the cash left after that spending, was negative $9.07 billion, the second negative quarter in a row.

More is coming. Alphabet kept its 2026 spending forecast at $195 billion to $205 billion, and “we continue to expect our CapEx to increase significantly in 2027,” Chief Financial Officer Anat Ashkenazi said on the call. That overshadowed Google Cloud, whose revenue more than doubled, up 103% to $30.71 billion. Bank of America cut its price target to $380 from $430, Investing.com and Finbold reported, and Yahoo Finance estimated that the drop erased close to $500 billion of market value during the session.

What the market is telling us

Alphabet’s shares moved 5% or more on 6 of the last 252 sessions. The stock is down 9.3% this year, and at $283.82 it sits 29.5% below its 52-week closing high of $402.62 from 13 May 2026. $1,000 invested in Alphabet five years ago would be worth $1,917 today.

A cloud business growing 103% is a real asset, but until search growth steadies and cash flow turns positive again, we think investors will keep pricing Alphabet on its spending rather than its sales.
