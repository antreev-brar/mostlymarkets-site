Amazon (AMZN) Q3: AWS Speeds Up, but Ads and Profit Miss Estimates

AWS grew 39%, ahead of estimates, but Amazon missed on profit and ads and guided Q4 operating income below forecasts. The stock slid 4.4% after hours.

Marcus Lindqvist·Big tech and earnings·29 Oct 2026, 4:45 p.m. ET·2 min read
AMZNAmazon
▼ 4.43% $233.00 after hours, 29 Oct

Amazon (AMZN) reported third-quarter revenue of $201.94 billion, up 12% year on year, just short of the $202.31 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC. Earnings of $1.89 a share missed the $1.96 estimate. The shares slid 4.4% after hours to $233.00 at 4:44 p.m. ET, on top of a 4.25% fall in the regular session.

Amazon (AMZN) Q3 2026 highlights

  • Revenue: $201.94 billion vs analyst estimates of $202.31 billion (12% year on year, a 0.2% miss)
  • EPS (GAAP): $1.89 vs analyst estimates of $1.96 (a 3.6% miss)
  • AWS revenue: $45.98 billion, up 39% year on year, vs $45.29 billion expected (StreetAccount)
  • Advertising revenue: $19.64 billion, up 11% year on year, vs $20.82 billion expected (StreetAccount)
  • Capital spending: $77.26 billion on property and equipment in the quarter, up from $35.10 billion a year earlier
  • Q4 guidance: revenue of $240.0-247.0 billion vs $244.51 billion expected (LSEG); operating income of $28.5-33.5 billion vs about $33 billion
  • Market value: $2.63 trillion at Thursday’s close

What stood out

AWS, Amazon’s cloud-computing arm, had its best quarter in years. Revenue rose 39% to $45.98 billion, ahead of the $45.29 billion analysts expected, according to StreetAccount data cited by CNBC. The Associated Press said it was the fastest growth in nearly five years, up from 37% in the second quarter.

Chief Executive Andy Jassy went further in the results release. “Our AWS investment in functionality and infrastructure is working, with over 39% year-over-year revenue growth in Q3 ($184 billion revenue run rate) and projected revenue growth rates of over 40% both in 2027 and in 2028,” he said.

Advertising was the soft spot. Ad revenue grew 11% to $19.64 billion, short of the $20.82 billion StreetAccount forecast; Yahoo Finance had the estimate at $20.91 billion. Amazon blamed “timing anomalies primarily related to Prime Day,” which fell in the second quarter this year, and said total revenue growth would have been about 4 percentage points higher without that shift.

Operating income still rose 61% to $28.08 billion, and the operating margin of 13.9% was what Amazon called its “highest operating margin ever.” Earnings per share fell from $1.95 because a year ago included $9.5 billion of pre-tax gains on Amazon’s stake in Anthropic.

The outlook

Amazon expects fourth-quarter revenue of $240.0 billion to $247.0 billion. The $243.5 billion midpoint sits just below the $244.51 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC and Reuters. The bigger gap is profit: operating income of $28.5 billion to $33.5 billion has a $31 billion midpoint, against about $33 billion expected, according to Yahoo Finance and investingLive.

Spending is not slowing. Amazon bought $77.26 billion of property and equipment in the quarter, more than double a year earlier, and still expects 2026 cash capital spending of about $220 billion. Free cash flow over the past 12 months was negative $42.4 billion, against positive $14.8 billion a year earlier, CNBC and the Motley Fool noted. Amazon said it expects ad growth to return to “the high teens” in the fourth quarter.

Our take

AWS is doing what investors hoped, and a forecast of 40%-plus growth for two more years is a bold one to put in writing. But the stock had already fallen 4.25% during the day as Alphabet’s slide stirred worries about AI spending, Investing.com and CNBC reported, and this report gave those worries more to chew on: negative free cash flow and a weaker profit guide. Next quarter, we’ll watch whether ads really bounce back to high-teens growth and what Amazon says about 2027 spending.
