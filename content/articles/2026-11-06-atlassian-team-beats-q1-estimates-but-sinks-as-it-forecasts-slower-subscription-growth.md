Atlassian ($TEAM), the maker of the Jira and Confluence work-tracking software, reported revenue of $1.77 billion for its fiscal first quarter after the close on Thursday, up 24% from a year earlier and ahead of analysts' estimates, Benzinga and Seeking Alpha reported. Adjusted earnings were $1.65 a share against $1.44 expected, according to Benzinga Pro data. But the company expects subscription growth to slow, and the stock was **down 13.5%** at $158.50 before the open on Friday, after closing at $183.29 on Thursday.

## What stood out

The cloud business is doing the work. Cloud revenue grew **31%** to $1.30 billion, while revenue from Data Center, the software customers run on their own servers, rose 5% to $391 million, according to the results release. Atlassian is moving customers off Data Center on purpose, so the cloud figure is the one that tells you whether demand is holding.

Profit improved faster than sales. The adjusted operating margin rose to 32% from 23%, and the company swung to a GAAP profit of $91 million from a $52 million loss. "Enterprise AI has hit an inflection point," Chief Executive Mike Cannon-Brookes said in the release.

## The outlook

The forecast is what the stock is trading on. Atlassian expects subscription ARR, its yearly recurring revenue, to grow about **18.5%** this fiscal year, down from the 23% it just reported, and second-quarter revenue of $1.825 billion to $1.835 billion. Those targets are slightly higher than in August, SiliconANGLE and FourWeekMBA reported, but they still describe slowing growth.

For the second quarter it expects cloud growth to ease to about 27% and Data Center revenue to fall about 14%. Its non-GAAP operating margin target of about 26% is below the quarter's 32%.

## Our take

The quarter was good: a 4% revenue beat and free cash flow of $463 million. The problem is the shape of the year, as revenue has been flat at about $1.77 billion for three quarters and the company itself expects ARR growth to lose about 4.5 points. After more than tripling from its April low close of $57.15, the stock was priced for speed-up, not slowdown. The next test is the second-quarter report in early February: cloud growth of 27% or better would say the slowdown is the Data Center wind-down, not demand.
