## How it got here

Walmart was built on one idea: sell everyday goods for less than anyone else, and make up the thin margin on volume. That model brought in **$713.2 billion** of revenue in the fiscal year to January 2026, its 10-K shows.

The turning point of the past five years has been online. Walmart's global e-commerce sales reached $150.4 billion in fiscal 2026, and its advertising business, which sells space on its website and app to the brands on its shelves, grew 46% that year, according to Yahoo Finance and an analysis of its 10-K by sec-api. Those businesses earn far more per dollar than groceries, which is why investors have paid up for the stock.

This year the stock fell after both of its reports, on 21 May and 20 August, and then rallied 23.4% from its August low. It reports again before the market opens on Thursday. **Is Walmart now a higher-margin business that deserves a premium, or a grocer priced like a tech company?**

## The business in one line

Shoppers pay Walmart for groceries and household goods several times a week, members pay a yearly fee for Walmart+ and Sam's Club, and brands pay it to advertise to those shoppers.

## The numbers that matter

### Growth
Revenue grew **5.0% a year** over five fiscal years, above the 4.6% median of the 35 consumer-staples companies we compare it with, and 5.9% in the latest quarter from a year earlier. For a company this size that is steady, not fast: Costco's revenue grew 10.1% in its latest 12 months.

### Profitability
Its operating margin was **4.4%** in the 12 months to July 2026, in the bottom quarter of its group, against a median of 13.6%. That is the discount model working as designed, but it has barely moved: up 0.1 points over five years.

### Earnings per share
Earnings per share grew **11.5% a year** over five years, in the top quarter of the group, where the median was 1.1%. Buybacks helped a little, with the diluted share count down 1.3% in two years.

### Cash
This is the weak spot. Free cash flow was **1.8% of sales** in the 12 months to July, $13.5 billion, in the bottom tenth of the group, because capital spending ran at $29.4 billion against $42.9 billion of cash from operations. Free cash flow in fiscal 2026 was $14.9 billion, below the $25.8 billion of fiscal 2021.

### Returns and balance sheet
Return on capital was about **18.6%**, above the group median of 14.2%, so the money it spends earns a decent return on paper. Net debt was $38.9 billion at the end of July, 1.2 years of operating profit, which is comfortable.

## Why the stock is where it is

1. **Fuel costs hit its shoppers.** On 21 May the shares fell 7.3% after Walmart guided second-quarter adjusted EPS to $0.72 to $0.74, below the $0.75 analysts expected, and said it had absorbed about $175 million of higher fuel costs, CNBC and Proactive Investors reported.
2. **The third-quarter guide missed and US sales slowed.** On 20 August the shares fell 9.2%, their worst day since May 2022 in our price data, after Walmart guided third-quarter adjusted EPS to $0.62 to $0.64 against a 68-cent estimate and reported US comparable sales growth of 2.6%, Yahoo Finance and Stocktwits reported.
3. **The profit growth was partly one-off.** About $2.9 billion of tariff refunds added a net 7.5 points to second-quarter adjusted operating income growth of 17.4%, and Walmart said it would spend the refunds on price cuts in the third quarter, Benzinga and Investing.com reported.

## Testing the worry

**The squeeze on shoppers is partly temporary.** US comparable sales slowed from 4.1% in the first quarter to 2.6% in the second, and heating oil prices are up 59% from last winter, CNBC reported on Saturday. Walmart wins share when budgets are tight, but a household spending more on fuel spends less on the higher-margin general goods that lift its margin, so this worry lasts as long as energy prices stay high.

**The tariff boost is temporary, and contained.** Strip out the 7.5 points from refunds and second-quarter adjusted operating income still grew about 10%, at the top of the 7% to 10% range Walmart had guided, Investing.com reported. Walmart raised its full-year sales growth guide to 4% to 5% from 3.5% to 4.5% in August, its release said.

**The cash worry is the one that survives.** Capital spending of $29.4 billion a year, on automated warehouses, stores and delivery, is what holds free cash flow at 1.8% of sales. Management is betting that automation lowers the cost of every online order; until free cash flow climbs back above its fiscal 2021 level, that bet is unproven.

## The moat

- **Scale.** Net sales of $706.4 billion in fiscal 2026 let Walmart buy more cheaply than any rival; Costco, the closest in model, had $303.2 billion of revenue in its latest 12 months, and Target $107.7 billion.
- **Stores as warehouses.** Walmart ships online orders from stores close to the customer, and its e-commerce sales reached $150.4 billion in fiscal 2026, $99.6 billion of it in the US.
- **An ad business on top of the shopping.** Walmart Connect, its US ad arm, grew 43% in the second quarter and global advertising 38%, Zacks and Investing.com reported, sales that cost little to add.

Against its rivals: Costco grows faster at a slimmer 3.9% operating margin; Target's revenue grew 0.2% a year over two years at a 5.6% margin; Amazon earns a 13.1% operating margin but had negative free cash flow in its latest 12 months.

## The risks we'd watch

1. **A margin that doesn't move.** Each 0.5 point of operating margin is worth about $3.7 billion of operating profit on $736 billion of sales, about 11% of the $32.3 billion it earned in the last 12 months; the price needs that point to come.
2. **Price cuts eating the refunds.** Walmart is spending the $2.9 billion tariff refund on lower prices in the third quarter, so profit growth will slow from the second quarter's 17.4%.
3. **Spending that stays high.** If capital spending stays near $29 billion while operating cash flow grows 5% a year, free cash flow stays near 2% of sales for years.

At 42.8 times expected earnings, Walmart earns 2.3% on its share price, against 5.14% from a 10-year Treasury on Friday. That gap only closes if earnings grow faster than the 11.5% a year of the past five years, which takes a higher margin, not just more sales.

## Our verdict

**Business: Solid. Price: Above its usual price.** Walmart has turned modest sales growth into 11.5% a year of earnings growth, earns 18.6% on its capital, and is building advertising and membership income that cost little to add. But its margin has barely moved in five years, its free cash flow is lower than in fiscal 2021, and at 46.2 times trailing earnings, against a median of 40.6, the stock prices in the margin gains before they show up. Thursday's report is the next test: analysts expect adjusted EPS of about $0.64 against $0.62 a year earlier, according to Fiscal.ai estimates cited by Yahoo Finance, on revenue of about $187.3 billion, TradingView and BigGo reported. The tension is plain: investors are paying a tech multiple for a grocer whose tech-like profits haven't reached its cash flow yet.
