Jensen Huang told Nvidia ($NVDA) employees at an all-hands meeting on Tuesday that he had broken the company's code of conduct, called it unacceptable and said he is in therapy, according to The New York Times and The Wall Street Journal, as reported by The Hill and others. It came a day after the board reinstated him as chief executive, and he said his direct reports will now send the board reviews of his behavior.

Nvidia **fell 5.9% to $155.38** on Tuesday, giving back part of Monday's 31.5% gain ([our story](/news/2026-11-24/nvidia-and-micron-shares-slide-what-happened)). The admission matters because the board's quick review cleared Huang's return while Washington says its questions are open: the chairs of three House committees said the reinstatement does not "erase the national security threats" and kept transcribed interviews with Huang and General Counsel Tim Teter on **1 and 2 December**. Senate Banking Chairman Tim Scott called the return "reckless" and said Huang will testify to his committee on 3 December. The SEC inquiry into Nvidia's accounting is also still open ([what the evidence says](/news/2026-11-23/nvidias-crisis-has-moved-from-its-boss-to-its-books-what-the-evidence-says)).

What's next: the House interviews on Tuesday, 1 December, the first time Huang answers lawmakers on export controls and his ties to a former Megaspeed executive.
