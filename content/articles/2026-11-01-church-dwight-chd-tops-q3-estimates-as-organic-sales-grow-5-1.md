Church & Dwight ($CHD), the maker of Arm & Hammer baking soda and OxiClean, reported third-quarter organic sales growth of **5.1%** on Friday, 30 October, well above the roughly 3% it had forecast. Adjusted earnings rose 13.6% to 92 cents a share, above the 90 cents analysts expected, according to Investing.com, and net sales of $1.60 billion topped the roughly $1.58 billion forecast. The stock rose 1.2% to $107.23 on Friday, a day the S&P 500 fell 1.8%.

## What stood out

Shoppers bought more, rather than paying more. Volume added **4.6 points** of the 5.1% organic growth and price and mix only 0.5 points, the release showed. Chief Executive Rick Dierker called it a "third consecutive quarter of industry-leading growth" on the call, according to BigGo Finance and the transcript.

Organic growth excludes acquisitions, exits and currency moves, which is why reported net sales grew only 1%. That gap shows in our filings data: sales grew 1.6% a year over the last two years, below the 2.9% median for the 35 consumer staples companies we cover.

The business turns sales into cash well. Free cash flow was 16.9% of sales over the last 12 months, against a peer median of 10.3%, and its operating margin of 18.0% ranks in the top third of the group, by our calculation from its filings.

## The outlook

Church & Dwight raised its 2026 organic growth forecast to about 5% and tightened adjusted EPS growth to about 7%, from 6% to 8%. For the fourth quarter it expects organic growth of about 4% and adjusted EPS up 17% to 19% from a year earlier, the release said.

## Our take

A strong quarter where it counts: volume-led growth well above its own forecast, and a raised outlook. The catch is the price. At 27.4 times expected earnings against a consumer staples median of 16.6, the stock assumes growth stays near 5%, so the fourth-quarter organic number, guided at about 4%, is the one to watch.
