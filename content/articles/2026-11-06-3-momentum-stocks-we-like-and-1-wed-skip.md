Momentum is a stock's recent run, and this week tested it. On Thursday 5 November the Nasdaq fell 5.5%, its worst day since April 2025, as the fastest risers of 2026 were sold hardest. A run is worth keeping only when the business is improving underneath it, so we looked for the stocks whose numbers justify the climb and the one whose climb has left its numbers far behind.

The screen: share-price return over the six months to Thursday 5 November, from our daily price files, leaving out the stocks in our lists of the last 14 days. We then checked each leader's growth, margins, cash and valuation against its peers from SEC filings, long run first.

```picks
like: VEEV, MPC, WDAY
skip: MRNA
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Veeva Systems ($VEEV)

**Six-month return: 84.3% to 5 November.** Veeva sells cloud software built for drugmakers, from the systems that run clinical trials to the tools sales reps use.

1. **The growth has compounded for years.** Revenue grew 16.9% a year over five fiscal years, in the top tenth of the 19 health-care companies we compare it with, whose median is 10.3%, and it is still growing 17.6% a year in the quarter to July 2026.
2. **It is the most profitable company in its peer group.** Its operating margin was 29.9% in the 12 months to July 2026, against a peer median of 3.6%, and it has widened 2.9 points over five years.
3. **Earnings follow the sales.** EPS grew 18.2% a year over five years, against 3.0% for the median peer.

**The price:** 49.7 times trailing earnings, below its median of 53.6 times since September 2024, though its forward P/E of 31.5 is nearly twice its peers'.

**What could go wrong:** its share count rose 0.3% in two years while peers bought back stock, so the per-share story rests entirely on growth. Its next quarterly results need to keep revenue growth near 17%.

### 2. Marathon Petroleum ($MPC)

**Six-month return: 55.8% to 5 November.** Marathon is the largest US oil refiner, turning crude into gasoline, diesel and jet fuel.

1. **Its last quarter was a big beat.** Marathon earned an adjusted $26.93 a share in the third quarter, against the $24.47 analysts expected, as its refining margin rose to $45.70 a barrel, its release said ([our recap](/news/2026-11-03/marathon-petroleum-mpc-beats-q3-estimates-and-sells-two-refineries-for-4-2-billion/)).
2. **It hands the cash back.** Its diluted share count fell **15.7%** in two years, the most of the 25 energy stocks in our coverage, where the median rose 0.3%.
3. **It earns high returns on its capital.** Return on equity was 63.0% in the 12 months to September 2026, in the top tenth of energy peers, whose median is 17.5%.

**The price:** 7.3 times trailing earnings, the bottom of its 7.3 to 29.4 range since November 2024, and 4.9 times free cash flow.

**What could go wrong:** refining margins swing with fuel demand and oil prices, and Marathon's gross margin of 13.0% over two years is in the bottom quarter of energy stocks. The stock is already 23.1% below its 14 October high; a fall in the refining margin below $30 a barrel in the fourth quarter would undo the case.

### 3. Workday ($WDAY)

**Six-month return: 52.9% to 5 November.** Workday sells cloud software that large companies use to run payroll, hiring and their finances.

1. **The run is a recovery, not a mania.** Even after six strong months, the stock was down 6.8% this year and 14.3% over 12 months on 5 November.
2. **It is cutting its share count.** Diluted shares fell **8.1%** over two years, in the top quarter of our 32 software companies, whose median rose 0.3%.
3. **It turns sales into cash.** Its free-cash-flow margin was 28.0% in the 12 months to July 2026, in line with the software median of 28.8%, and its operating margin has widened 13.3 points over five years.

**The price:** 16.0 times forward earnings, against a software median of 26.7, and 39.1 times trailing earnings, below its median of 51.7 since August 2024.

**What could go wrong:** revenue grew 12.8% in the latest quarter, in the bottom quarter of software peers. Workday reports on 24 November, its announcement said; growth slipping toward 10% would mean the low multiple is deserved.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Moderna ($MRNA)

**Six-month return: 397.4% to 5 November.** Moderna makes mRNA vaccines for COVID and RSV and is testing a cancer vaccine, intismeran.

1. **The quarter was good, but it is one quarter.** On Friday Moderna reported third-quarter revenue of $1.31 billion, up 29%, against the $885 million analysts expected in an LSEG survey, and swung to a profit of $0.38 a share, CNBC and MarketScreener reported ([our recap](/news/2026-11-06/moderna-mrna-swings-to-a-q3-profit-and-raises-its-sales-outlook/)). Its new 2026 forecast implies a fourth quarter below a year earlier, by our arithmetic from its filings.
2. **The long-run numbers are the worst in its group.** Revenue fell 33.6% a year over the two years to June 2026, last of the 15 drugmakers we compare it with, and the operating margin was **-149.0%** over 12 months, against a peer median of 19.6%.
3. **It still burns cash.** Its free-cash-flow margin was -55.8% in the 12 months to June 2026, and its share count rose 3.6% in two years.

**The price:** 15.0 times book value, with no earnings to put a P/E on, after a 761.9% rise this year to Friday's close.

**What would change our mind:** the run rests on the cancer vaccine, as Barron's and Investor's Business Daily noted on Friday. Positive late-stage intismeran data with a filing date, or two more quarters of profit outside the vaccine season, would give the price numbers to stand on.
