Wall Street comes back from Thanksgiving this morning to a rally it has been waiting all month for. Nasdaq 100 futures **rose about 2.5%** overnight, Reuters reported, as investors returned to the chip and AI-server stocks that had dragged the index toward its worst month since December 2022. Memory makers, optical-network suppliers and Nvidia's server partners led the premarket gains in our data.

The bounce comes with two warnings attached. Bond yields rose with stocks, and traders now put the odds of a Fed rate hike on 9 December at about 99%, according to CME FedWatch data cited by Reuters. And stocks trade only until 1 p.m. ET, so a light holiday session will have to carry the move.

It closed on Wednesday 37.6% below its 6 October high, after S&P Global [put its credit rating on watch](/news/2026-11-25/sp-puts-nvidias-nvda-aa-rating-on-watch-for-a-downgrade-over-governance/).

Dell ($DELL) and Supermicro ($SMCI) were named in the FT report as partners told to halt the Southeast Asia shipments, yet both are outpacing Nvidia this morning. Our read: buyers are weighing the size of the November fall more heavily than the loss of four smaller markets. The first full test is the regular session at 9:30 a.m.

JPMorgan's quant team led by Khuram Chaudhry calls this a three-stage bear market: the sharp fall is largely done and a short rebound should follow, but slowing growth could push stocks to new lows in 2027, MarketWatch and Longbridge reported. XTB's Kathleen Brooks told Reuters the recovery looks technical, and that the questions that caused the panic, AI profits and financing costs, are still unanswered.

Spot gold rose nearly 3% overnight as the dollar's rally stalled and stocks steadied, Reuters reported, and the Wall Street Journal tied the precious-metals rally to returning risk appetite. FXStreet and Financefeeds put spot gold back above $3,600 an ounce. It is still far below its record from earlier this year, because a Fed hike raises the cost of holding a metal that pays no interest.

Treasury yields rose in the shortened post-Thanksgiving session, the Wall Street Journal reported, and Reuters said Treasuries dipped as stocks rallied. A stronger economy and a near-certain hike on 9 December leave bonds little reason to rally. Oil rose on fresh attacks on Middle East shipping and energy sites, the Journal and EnergyNow reported, and Reuters sources say OPEC+ is set to keep its first-quarter output targets steady when it meets on Sunday.

## What's next

All times ET.

- **Friday, 27 November:** stocks trade until 1 p.m. Nvidia, Dell and Supermicro trade for the first time on the shipping halt; Vertex on its FDA delay.
- **Sunday, 29 November:** OPEC+ meets.
- **Monday, 30 November:** Rubrik ($RBRK) reports after the close.
- **Tuesday, 1 December:** the ISM manufacturing index and October job openings. Marvell, NetApp ($NTAP), MongoDB ($MDB) and Credo ($CRDO) report after the close.
- **Wednesday, 2 December:** Salesforce ($CRM) and Snowflake ($SNOW) report after the close.
- **Friday, 4 December:** the November jobs report at 8:30 a.m., the last big number before the Fed decides on 9 December.

We think the rebound is a test of positioning, not of the AI story: Marvell and Micron fell by a third in a month, so a 7% bounce barely dents that, and Marvell's own results on Tuesday are the first fresh evidence on demand.
