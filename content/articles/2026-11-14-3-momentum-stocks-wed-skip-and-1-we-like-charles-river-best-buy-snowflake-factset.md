On Friday, 13 November, the 10-year Treasury yield closed above 5% and the market's fastest runners of the past six months met a harder test: whether the business has kept pace with the stock. Momentum lists usually reward the winners; ours asks which winners have earned it.

Our screen ranked the stocks in our coverage by their price return over the six months to 13 November 2026, from our own daily closes, then checked each against its revenue growth, margins, cash flow and valuation in SEC filings, judged against the peers our fundamentals data groups it with. We left out stocks we listed in the last 14 days.

```picks
skip: CRL, BBY, SNOW
like: FDS
```

## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. Charles River Laboratories ($CRL)
**Six-month price return: 124%, to 13 November 2026.** Charles River runs the animal studies and lab tests that drugmakers need before human trials.

1. The recovery is real but small: third-quarter revenue fell 2.2% to $982.7 million after it sold businesses, though it grew **4.7% organically**, and it raised its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast to $11.35 to $11.55, BioSpectrum Asia and Webull reported.
2. Over two years, revenue has shrunk 1.0% a year, in the bottom 3% of the 61 health care companies we compare it with, and its operating margin has fallen 14.2 points over five years to 4.0% over the last 12 months.
3. It is buying growth: on 9 November it agreed to buy Frontage's bioanalysis and drug-metabolism businesses, Reuters and Dow Jones reported, adding a deal to a balance sheet with net debt of $2.2 billion.

**The price:** 27.5 times expected earnings against a peer median of 19.7 times, and 40.5 times free cash flow.

**What would change our mind:** organic growth that holds above 5% for a full year with the operating margin back above 10%.

### 2. Best Buy ($BBY)
**Six-month price return: 84.9%, to 13 November 2026.** Best Buy is the largest US electronics retailer.

1. The stock has outrun the sales: revenue fell 2.5% a year over five years, in the bottom 1% of the 62 consumer companies we compare it with, and grew **3.6%** in its latest quarter.
2. It keeps little of each sale: an operating margin of 4.1% over the last 12 months and a two-year free cash flow margin of 3.7%, both in the bottom quarter of its group.
3. The run came without news: the stock rose 6.7% to a 52-week closing high on 9 November with no announcement or filing behind it, Trefis reported, and it reports its third quarter later this month.

**The price:** 14.8 times expected earnings, cheap against a peer median of 19.9 times, but its trailing P/E of 17.3 is above its two-year median of 16.1.

**What would change our mind:** comparable sales growth at the top of its 1% to 3% guidance range for the quarter, with a margin that rises with it.

### 3. Snowflake ($SNOW)
**Six-month price return: 84.8%, to 13 November 2026.** Snowflake sells cloud data storage and analysis, billed by how much customers use.

1. Growth is excellent: revenue rose 35.1% in its latest quarter, in the top quarter of the 31 software companies we compare it with, and in September it raised its fiscal 2027 product revenue forecast to **$6.07 billion**, about 36% growth, The Standard and TIKR reported.
2. It still loses money: its operating margin was minus 22.8% over the last 12 months against a software median of 9.7%, and its diluted share count rose 4.5% in two years.
3. The run has already cracked: the stock closed 21.1% below its 9 October high on Friday.

**The price:** 114.8 times expected earnings against a peer median of 26.6 times, and 85.9 times free cash flow.

**What would change our mind:** its 2 December report; product revenue growth above its own 37% to 38% guidance with a smaller operating loss would justify more of the price.

## 1 stock we like

### FactSet ($FDS)
**Six-month price return: 56.5%, to 13 November 2026.** FactSet sells financial data and analysis software to banks and fund managers on subscriptions.

1. It turns sales into cash: its free cash flow margin was **27.6%** over two years, above the 22.3% median of the 46 financial-data, exchange and payments companies we compare it with.
2. It shrinks its share count: diluted shares fell 7.7% in two years, in the top quarter of its group, and its return on equity of 26.6% is in the top quarter too.
3. The plan is modest and specific: at its investor day on 10 November it reaffirmed fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS of $19.25 to $19.65 and set targets of about 6% organic revenue growth and about 10% adjusted EPS growth a year to fiscal 2029, its release said.

**The price:** 17.0 times expected earnings against a peer median of 17.7 times, and 16.6 times free cash flow; its trailing P/E of 22.9 is above its two-year median of 20.5. Even after the run, the stock is up only 14.7% this year.

**What could go wrong:** AI tools that let clients build their own research. Revenue growth of 6.0% a year over two years is in the bottom quarter of its group, so any slowdown in subscriptions would leave little growth for the multiple to rest on.
