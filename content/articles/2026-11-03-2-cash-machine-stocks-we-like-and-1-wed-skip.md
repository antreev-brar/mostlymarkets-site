Free cash flow, the cash a company has left after running and investing in the business, is what pays for buybacks and dividends. On Monday 2 November Apple ($AAPL) guided its margin lower on Monday 2 November as memory costs rise, a cost that flows straight into the memory makers' cash. We screened our coverage for the companies that turn the most of their sales into free cash, so the question is which of those margins will last.

The screen: free-cash-flow margin (operating cash flow minus capital spending, as a share of revenue) over the 12 months to the latest quarter, from SEC filings, leaving out the stocks in our lists of the last 14 days. We then checked each against its industry peers and its own record, long run first.

```picks
like: CME, MSCI
skip: SNDK
```

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. CME Group ($CME)

**Free-cash-flow margin: 62.1% in the 12 months to September 2026.** CME runs the world's biggest futures exchange, earning a fee on every contract traded and cleared.

1. **It has done this for years, not one quarter.** Its free-cash-flow margin averaged 63.0% over two years, the highest of the 36 exchanges, payment firms and asset managers in our coverage, where the median is 23.8%.
2. **Trading is still growing.** Average daily volume hit a third-quarter record of 29.4 million contracts, up 16% on a year earlier, and revenue rose 13% to $1.74 billion, CME said on 21 October, as Mondo Visione and Capital.com reported. Market-data revenue hit a record $243 million.
3. **Its margins keep widening.** The operating margin rose 10.9 points over five years to 65.7%, top of its peer group.

**The price:** 25.1 times trailing earnings, a shade under its median of 26.0 times since November 2024.

**What could go wrong:** CME earns on activity, so a calm market costs it volume. Sales grew 6.0% a year over five years, in the bottom quarter of its peers, and a fall in average daily volume below a year earlier in its monthly reports would be the first sign.

### 2. MSCI ($MSCI)

**Free-cash-flow margin: 45.9% in the 12 months to September 2026.** MSCI builds the stock indexes that funds track and benchmark against, and sells the data and analytics around them.

1. **Its per-share growth compounds.** EPS grew 17.1% a year over five years, ahead of its peers' 15.7%, helped by an 8.2% cut in its share count over two years.
2. **Clients stay.** Organic subscription run rate, the yearly value of its recurring contracts, grew 8.7% in the third quarter, and its retention rate rose to 95.0% from 94.7%, MSCI's results release shows, as Financial Modeling Prep reported.
3. **The cash is steady.** Its free-cash-flow margin averaged 47.0% over two years, in the top quarter of its peers.

**The price:** 33.7 times trailing earnings, below its median of 36.7 times since October 2024, though above its peers' 17.6 times forward.

**What could go wrong:** Part of its revenue is a fee on the assets in funds that track its indexes, which rose with record ETF assets this year, the release says. A sharp fall in stock prices would cut that fee directly.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Sandisk ($SNDK)

**Free-cash-flow margin: 59.9% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Sandisk makes NAND flash, the storage chips in phones, laptops and data-center drives.

1. **The margin is a price spike, not a record.** Revenue rose 378% year on year to $11.03 billion in the quarter to 2 October, its release shows, because flash prices soared. Over two years its free-cash-flow margin averaged 48.7%, and a year earlier the business was a fraction of today's size.
2. **The cash has already peaked once.** Free cash flow fell to $6.31 billion in the latest quarter from $7.08 billion in the one before, even as revenue rose 23%, by our reading of its filings; that is 57% of sales, down from 79%.
3. **Supply is coming.** TrendForce, the Taiwan-based tracker, said in July that NAND supply growth should outpace demand in 2027 and that conditions would ease in the second half. Our narrative today sets out how the [memory boom is turning into the device makers' bill](/news/2026-11-03/memorys-boom-has-become-the-device-makers-bill-what-the-evidence-says/), and those buyers are already shipping fewer phones.

**The price:** 4.9 times forward earnings against a peer median of 18.7 times. The stock is 54.0% below its 25 June high: the market already prices falling profits, and the cheap multiple is the cycle, not a bargain.

**What would change our mind:** a TrendForce forecast of rising NAND contract prices into the second half of 2027, which would mean the supply wave is smaller than feared.
