Anthropic's long-awaited debut went well for Anthropic and badly for almost everything else on Thursday. The AI lab's shares closed at **$97.17**, 17% above their $83 offer price, but the S&P 500 fell 2.0% to 7,175.83 for its sixth straight loss, its longest losing streak since 2024, AP and Bloomberg reported. The Dow dropped 1,101.11 points, or 2.2%, to 49,097.06 and the Nasdaq Composite lost 2.3%, as the two big companies that own more than a quarter of Anthropic gave up a pre-market rally.

## Anthropic rose; its owners didn't

Anthropic opened at $111.50 shortly before noon, 34% above the offer price, and touched $114.99 before sliding back to $97.17 by the close, CBS News and Stocktitan reported. That still made it the largest stock-market debut on record, Benzinga said, after the [$81.3 billion offering](/news/2026-11-18/anthropic-prices-its-ipo-at-83-a-share-above-its-70-to-78-range/) priced on Wednesday.

The pop did not carry over. Amazon ($AMZN), which will own 14.7% of Anthropic, **fell 3.9%** and Alphabet ($GOOGL), which will own 13.7%, lost 3.5%, according to the stakes in the IPO filing cited by the Motley Fool. Both had been up 2.5% to 4.1% before the open, as [this morning's Daily](/daily/2026-11-19/) noted. A week ago Wall Street was [betting the IPO could rescue the AI trade](/news/2026-11-15/wall-street-is-betting-anthropics-ipo-can-rescue-the-ai-trade-3-numbers-will-decide-it/); on day one, money went into Anthropic and came out of the rest.

## The AI sell-off spread

AI stocks kept falling on worries that the boom may be over, AP reported. Nvidia ($NVDA) **fell 3.3% to $145.98**, a new 52-week low and 39.0% below its 6 October closing high, by our data. AMD ($AMD) lost 7.7% after Hunterbrook disclosed a short position alongside an investigation into its China links ([our story](/news/2026-11-19/amd-amd-shares-plunge-after-hunterbrook-shorts-it-over-china-links/)), and chip-renting neoclouds Nebius ($NBIS) and CoreWeave ($CRWV) fell 9.4% and 6.1%.

The selling was not limited to tech. More than nine in ten S&P 500 stocks fell, AP reported, which undercuts the story that money leaving AI would hold up the rest of the market, the case we laid out [in this morning's narrative](/news/2026-11-19/the-average-stock-is-up-while-the-s-p-500-is-down-wall-street-is-betting-techs-lead-is-over/). UnitedHealth ($UNH) lost another 5.6%, extending its slide on a proposed Medicare audit rule.

## Earnings: one big loser, two winners

Williams-Sonoma ($WSM) **plunged 15.2%**, its worst day since April 2025, after a quarter that only [matched estimates with help from tariff refunds](/news/2026-11-19/williams-sonoma-wsm-q3-matches-estimates-with-tariff-refund-help-and-the-stock-slides/). PDD Holdings ($PDD) rose 11.0% as [marketing cuts lifted its profit](/news/2026-11-19/pdd-holdings-pdd-misses-q3-revenue-estimates-but-beats-on-profit-as-it-cuts-marketing/), and Palo Alto Networks ($PANW) added 6.4% after its forecast raise. Walmart ($WMT) slipped 1.0% despite [raising its outlook](/news/2026-11-19/walmart-wmt-beats-q3-estimates-and-raises-its-outlook-for-the-third-time/).

After the close, Intuit ($INTU) sank 14.6% at 4:15 p.m. ET: it beat first-quarter estimates but forecast second-quarter revenue of $4.93 billion to $4.95 billion, below the roughly $5.11 billion analysts expected, and agreed to sell Mailchimp to Bending Spoons for about $3.68 billion, its release and Reuters reported. Ross Stores ($ROST) fell 9.0% after hours on sales that matched estimates at $6.26 billion.

## Rates stay the backdrop

The 10-year Treasury yield edged down 0.02 percentage point to 5.05%. That relief was small next to the day's rate news: [JPMorgan now expects a Fed hike](/news/2026-11-19/jpmorgan-now-expects-fed-rate-hikes-at-each-of-the-next-three-meetings/) at each of the next three meetings, joining BofA and Deutsche Bank. Gold fell 2.2% to $3,660 and the VIX, Wall Street's fear gauge, jumped 15.8% to 28.44.

Nvidia's slide and the Anthropic pop now leave the AI trade with two prices: a new listing worth well over a trillion dollars, and the chipmaker supplying it at its lowest close in a year. The next test is whether buyers return to the old AI names once the new one has settled.
