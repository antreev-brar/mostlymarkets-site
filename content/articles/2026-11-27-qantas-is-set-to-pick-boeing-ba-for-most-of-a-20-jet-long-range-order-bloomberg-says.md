Qantas is set to give Boeing ($BA) the bulk of an order for about **20 long-range jets**, Bloomberg reported on Friday, citing people familiar with the matter. Most would be 787 Dreamliners, with some Airbus A350s making up the rest, according to the report, carried by Simple Flying and Investing.com.

Qantas told Bloomberg it is still talking with plane makers and has made no decision; Boeing referred questions to Qantas, and Airbus declined to comment. The jets would help replace Qantas's A380 superjumbos, and higher fuel costs have made more efficient planes more urgent, the reports said. It would follow Xiamen Airlines' [agreement to buy 50 Boeing 737s](/news/2026-11-24/boeing-ba-wins-a-7-4-billion-order-for-50-737-jets-from-china-southerns-xiamen-airlines/) on Tuesday. Boeing's shares were up **1.6%** at $167.21 premarket as of 6:19 a.m. ET, and are down 24.2% this year to Wednesday's close.

What isn't known yet is the final split between the two plane makers and when Qantas will announce it.
