On Wednesday morning, Fed Governor Christopher Waller told CNBC's "Squawk Box" that a **half-point** rate hike is probably necessary at the 8-9 December meeting, unless his colleagues clearly signal another increase in January. The day before, New York Fed President John Williams said at an Axios event in New York, "I don't think the situation calls for dramatic action."

Neither disputes that the Fed raises rates in December. The argument is how hard, after core consumer prices rose 0.3% for a third straight month in October. Futures put about **95% odds** on a quarter point on Wednesday morning, TipRanks reported, and gold fell to a 14-month low on the debate ([our story](/news/2026-11-18/agnico-eagle-freeport-mcmoran-and-newmont-shares-slide-what-happened/)).

## The case for going faster

### Christopher Waller, governor, Federal Reserve Board

**A half point now, unless the Fed promises January.** Waller said three straight months of 0.3% core inflation met the threshold he set in a London speech on 10 November, and that inflation is moving the wrong way and spreading across more prices, CNBC and Investing.com reported. A quarter point would do, he said, only if the committee signals a January hike clearly; otherwise "you have to go 50 in December to put it in place and get it done." The 2-year Treasury yield rose after his comments, CNBC and Bitcoin Magazine reported.

### Alberto Musalem, president, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis

**The rate is about a full point too low.** Musalem said on Tuesday that the Fed's 3.75% to 4.00% range needs several more increases before spring 2027, Reuters and CNBC reported, which at a quarter point a time is up to four hikes ([our brief](/news/2026-11-17/feds-musalem-says-rates-may-need-to-rise-a-full-point-by-spring/)). He doesn't argue for a bigger single step, but his path ends where Waller's does, faster. He does not vote on rates this year.

## The case for a quarter point

### John Williams, president, Federal Reserve Bank of New York

**More hikes, but no jump.** Williams backed further tightening but rejected a 50 basis point move at the Axios event on Tuesday, Yahoo Finance, TheStreet and the Seoul Economic Daily reported. Investors still put about 98% odds on a quarter point after he spoke, Yahoo Finance reported. As the New York Fed's president he is the committee's vice chair and a permanent voter, so his view carries more weight than most.

### Beth Hammack, president, Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland

**Steady steps at consecutive meetings.** Hammack said a gradual path of hikes is the best way forward, in a Bloomberg TV interview reported by Investing.com and FXStreet in early November. She warned that if an "inflationary mindset" sets in, the Fed could fall behind and have to move in larger steps to a higher peak, so her case for small steps is conditional on inflation expectations holding.

## Where they agree

All four want higher rates, and none expects December to be the last hike. That matches the Reuters poll of 10 November, in which all of the economists surveyed expected a quarter-point increase in December, FXStreet and Yahoo Finance reported. The split is over speed: Waller and Musalem think the Fed is behind, and Williams and Hammack think steady steps will catch up.

## The number that decides it

Waller drew the line himself: core inflation at 0.3% a month, about 3.6% a year, is his trigger. October's consumer prices rose 0.4% overall and 0.3% at the core, the Globe and Mail reported. Before the vote, the market will read two things: the minutes of the October meeting at 2 p.m. ET today, which show how many officials shared Waller's worry, and whether the Fed's leaders start signaling January, the condition under which Waller says a quarter point is enough.

## Our read

The evidence favors **a quarter point on 9 December**. Williams votes and leads the committee's center, futures give a half point about one chance in twenty, and even Waller says a quarter point works if January is signaled. But on the bigger question, how many hikes follow, the hawks are winning: Musalem's full point and Waller's January condition both point to a rate well above 4% by spring, and the 2-year yield at 4.80% already sits inside the 4.75% to 5.00% range a full point of hikes would reach. The date that tells us is 2 p.m. today, when the minutes show how many officials were already where Waller is now.
