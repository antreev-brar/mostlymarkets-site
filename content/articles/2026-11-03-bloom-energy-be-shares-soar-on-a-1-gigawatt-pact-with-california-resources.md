## What happened

Shares of fuel-cell maker Bloom Energy ($BE) **soared 11.4%** to $218.61 by 10:15 a.m. ET on Tuesday, after it signed a preliminary agreement with oil producer California Resources to study at least 1 gigawatt of fuel-cell power paired with carbon capture.

The two companies announced the non-binding memorandum of understanding after Monday's close, alongside California Resources' third-quarter results, Investing.com and Benzinga reported. The projects would sit in Central and Northern California, run Bloom's fuel cells on natural gas, and capture the carbon dioxide they emit for storage underground through California Resources' Carbon TerraVault unit, which said up to **3 million metric tons** of CO2 a year could be captured. The companies plan to look for buyers of the power among utilities, cities and data centers.

## Why it matters

The pact points Bloom at a second set of buyers. Its sales growth this year has come mostly from data centers, and its revenue rose **137%** from a year earlier in the latest quarter in our data, the fastest among its peers; pairing its gas-fired fuel cells with carbon storage gives it a pitch to California utilities and cities that need low-emission power.

Oppenheimer, which rates the stock Perform, said the agreement helps reduce Bloom's dependence on a handful of data-center customers, according to Investing.com. The caveat is in the word non-binding: no customer, price or start date has been agreed, and a deal in California still needs permits.

## What the market is telling us

Bloom Energy's shares moved 5% or more on 107 of the last 252 sessions, so double-digit days are routine for this stock. If today's gain holds, it will be the biggest one-day rise since 12 August, when the stock rose 12.3%. It is up 125.9% this year, but at Monday's close of $196.25 it sat 43.3% below its 52-week closing high of $345.85 from 22 June, and $1,000 invested five years ago would be worth $6,106.

At about 55 times forward earnings, more than twice the median of its peers in our data, the price already assumes Bloom keeps winning deals of this size. Today's move says investors will pay up even for a study; the test is whether it turns into signed contracts.
