## What happened

Shares of flash-memory maker Sandisk ($SNDK) **plunged 22.3%** on Thursday, with Micron ($MU) down 17.5% and AMD ($AMD) down 17.3%, as investors bet that Democrats' sweep of Congress and key governor races will slow the building of AI data centers, Reuters and CNBC reported. Western Digital ($WDC) fell 17.4%, Seagate ($STX) 16.3% and Dell ($DELL) 14.9%.

Reuters reported that the Nasdaq had its biggest one-day drop in 19 months on fears that tougher rules on AI would slow the data-center buildout, and that the Philadelphia Semiconductor index lost more than 10%. The fear has a concrete source: in Texas, Democrat Gina Hinojosa, who campaigned for a moratorium on new data centers, won the governor's race, and Wells Fargo estimates that about 27% of planned US data-center capacity is in the state, CNBC and The Daily Reporter reported. Qualcomm's ($QCOM) weak profit forecast on Wednesday evening, which CNBC cited as a second weight, added to the selling.

The shares closed at $806.43 (Sandisk), $656.39 (Micron) and $404.40 (AMD).

## Why it matters

These are the stocks that rose most on the idea that AI servers would keep soaking up every memory chip and drive made. A pause on new data centers in the state with a quarter of the planned capacity goes straight at that demand, so the stocks priced for the longest run of shortages fell furthest. On Wednesday the market had shrugged off the same election result; on Thursday it did not.

## What the market is telling us

Micron's shares have moved 5% or more on 75 of the last 252 sessions, and Thursday was its third-biggest move of the year. It is still up 130.0% this year, but at $656.39 it sits 45.9% below its 52-week closing high of $1,213.56 from 25 June. Sandisk, up 239.7% this year, is now 65.5% below its June high, and $1,000 in Micron five years ago would be worth $9,002 today.

For AMD, the last fall this big was on 4 February 2026 (-17.3%); it has lost 37.4% in a month even after a strong quarter. Our read: the demand data from the companies themselves has not changed this week, so the next test is evidence of a real delay, a stalled permit or a cut capex plan, rather than an election map.
