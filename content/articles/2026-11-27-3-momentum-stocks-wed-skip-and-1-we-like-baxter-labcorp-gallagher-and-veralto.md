The S&P 500 is in a correction, yet a handful of steady, unglamorous companies have done the opposite: at Wednesday's close, four of them sat within 4% of their 52-week highs after gaining more than 40% in six months. Momentum like that usually means investors have run to safety, and safety is only worth paying for if the business underneath is good.

Our screen ranked the stocks in our coverage by price return over the six months to 25 November, from our price files, leaving out anything on our lists in the last 14 days and companies under a pending takeover. We then judged the top names on five years of SEC filings against their peers.

```picks
skip: BAX, LH, AJG
like: VLTO
```

## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. Baxter International ($BAX)
**Six-month return: 41.6% to 25 November.** Baxter makes hospital products: drip pumps, IV solutions and surgical supplies.

1. **It hasn't grown in five years.** Revenue shrank 0.7% a year over five fiscal years, against 10.0% growth for the median of the 19 device makers we track.
2. **It loses money on its operations.** Its operating margin was -1.9% in the 12 months to September 2026, down 16.6 points over five years, and its gross margin of 32.1% is half the 63.5% peer median.
3. **The recovery is early.** Third-quarter sales rose 5% to about $2.98 billion and Baxter raised its 2026 outlook on 29 October, its release showed, yet the stock fell 7.7% that day.

**The price:** 13.2 times expected earnings, against 18.6 times for peers. That discount is the reason it's tempting, and it reflects the margins.

**What would change our mind:** a positive operating margin for a full year, with sales growth holding at 5% or better.

### 2. Labcorp ($LH)
**Six-month return: 40.4% to 25 November.** Labcorp runs medical testing labs and does lab work for drug trials.

1. **Five years of no growth.** Revenue was flat over five fiscal years, the bottom of the 17 health care services, insurers and distributors we have five years of data for, against a 9.5% median, as pandemic testing faded.
2. **The recent quarter was good.** Revenue rose 6.9% to $3.81 billion in the third quarter and adjusted EPS 14.5%, and Labcorp raised its 2026 guidance on 28 October, its release showed.
3. **It carries debt.** Net debt of $5.6 billion equals 3.7 years of operating profit, high for a business growing about 6% a year.

**The price:** 30.3 times trailing earnings, above its own median of 27.4 times since late 2024. The run has already priced in the better quarter.

**What would change our mind:** a fall back toward its usual multiple, or growth above 7% that lasts more than a quarter.

### 3. Arthur J. Gallagher ($AJG)
**Six-month return: 44.3% to 25 November.** Gallagher is an insurance broker: it arranges cover for companies and earns commissions and fees.

1. **Growth is mostly bought.** Revenue rose 20.5% a year over two years, but organic growth, from existing businesses, was 5% in the third quarter, as [our recap](/news/2026-10-30/arthur-j-gallagher-ajg-q3-profit-matches-estimates-as-organic-growth-holds-at-5/) showed.
2. **Shareholders paid for it.** Diluted shares rose 17.1% in two years, while the median for its group fell 5.6%.
3. **Returns are thin.** Return on equity of 7.1% is the lowest of the 23 insurers and brokers we track, against a 17.9% median.

**The price:** 20.3 times expected earnings, against 13.5 times for peers, and 44.7 times trailing earnings, above its own median of 43.3 times.

**What would change our mind:** organic growth back above 7%, or return on equity climbing toward its peers.

## 1 stock we like

### Veralto ($VLTO)
**Six-month return: 44.0% to 25 November.** Veralto sells water-quality testing and treatment equipment and the product-labelling and packaging systems that factories use.

1. **It earns a lot on what it sells.** Gross margin over the last two years was 60.1%, against a 39.0% median for the industrial companies we track, and its free cash flow was 18.5% of revenue, against 12.7%.
2. **Returns are high.** Return on capital of about 22.9% is in the top quarter of its group, against a 13.4% median.
3. **The latest quarter beat and raised.** Third-quarter sales rose 7.5% to $1.509 billion, with core sales up 5.1% and adjusted EPS up 19.2%, and Veralto raised its 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $4.49-4.52 from $4.35-4.43 on 29 October, its release showed.

**The price:** 25.4 times expected earnings, against 20.2 times for peers, and 29.8 times trailing earnings, above its own median of 27.8 times since late 2024. We'd be paying up for quality, not getting a bargain.

**What could go wrong:** management guided its fourth-quarter adjusted operating margin to about 24.5%, flat on a year ago, as acquisitions dilute it, Finviz and Quartr reported. Two flat quarters would leave little to justify the premium.
