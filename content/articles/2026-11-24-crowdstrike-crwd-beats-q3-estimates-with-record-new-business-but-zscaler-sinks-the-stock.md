CrowdStrike ($CRWD) reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of **$1.58 billion** before the open on Monday, up 28% from a year earlier and above the $1.529 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters. Adjusted earnings of $0.34 a share beat the roughly $0.31 consensus, Benzinga and Yahoo Finance reported. The stock still fell 8.9% to $208.97 on Monday, because the company announced in the same release an all-stock deal to buy Zscaler ($ZS) for $43.9 billion.

## What stood out

The number CrowdStrike investors watch is net new annual recurring revenue, the yearly subscription value added in the quarter, because it leads revenue by several quarters. It came in at a record **$419.2 million**, up 58% from $265 million a year earlier, the release showed, as Reuters and Grafa reported. Total ARR growth sped up to 27%, from 23% a year ago, which says customers are buying more modules, not just renewing. *Verdict: the best quarter for new business the company has reported.*

The quarter was also more profitable in cash. Free cash flow was a record $474.4 million, up from $295.9 million a year earlier, or 30 cents of every sales dollar, just above the 27.7% two-year median for the 32 software companies we track. Under standard accounting (GAAP) the company still barely breaks even, because it pays heavily in stock, which its adjusted numbers leave out ([our breakdown](/news/2026-11-23/crowdstrike-crwd-bets-its-pricey-stock-on-zscaler-is-the-business-worth-it/)).

## The outlook

CrowdStrike raised its fiscal 2027 revenue forecast to **$6.078 billion to $6.084 billion**, from $5.991 billion to $6.011 billion, against a $6.006 billion consensus, and its adjusted EPS forecast to $1.28 from $1.25 to $1.26, Benzinga and Longbridge reported. For the fourth quarter it guided to revenue of $1.638 billion to $1.644 billion and adjusted EPS of about $0.36. It also forecast fiscal 2028 net new ARR growth of at least 20%, excluding Zscaler.

All of those numbers leave out the deal. Each Zscaler share becomes 1.135 CrowdStrike shares, so Zscaler holders will own a large slice of the combined company, and CrowdStrike targets $1 billion of extra yearly revenue within 30 months ([the deal](/news/2026-11-23/crowdstrike-crwd-agrees-to-buy-zscaler-for-43-9-billion-in-stock/)).

## Our take

The quarter was strong on the measure that matters most, and the guidance went up, so the 8.9% fall is a verdict on the deal, not the business: it was the stock's biggest one-day drop since 5 November, in our data. JPMorgan's Brian Essex wrote that Zscaler would dilute CrowdStrike's growth and cash margins, TheFly reported, and at 133 times free cash flow the shares price in years of fast growth either way. Next quarter's net new ARR, reported in early March, is the test of whether the Falcon platform keeps its pace while management works on a merger.
