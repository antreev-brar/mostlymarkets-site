Eaton ($ETN), the maker of power-distribution gear for data centers, utilities and buildings, reports third-quarter results before the open on Tuesday, 3 November. Its shares sit **20.4% below** their 12 August closing high, after a 16.1% drop in the past month that included a 6.0% fall on 30 October.

Last quarter, reported on 31 July, sales rose 21% to about $8.5 billion, with 14% organic growth, and adjusted EPS of $3.15 beat the $3.08 consensus, Eaton's release and MarketBeat show. Organic revenue from data centers grew about 65%, and Eaton raised its 2026 organic growth forecast to 11% to 13% from 9% to 11%. The stock closed up 7.3% that day.

## What analysts expect

The Zacks consensus is adjusted EPS of **$3.54**, up 15% from $3.07 a year earlier, on revenue of $8.48 billion, up 21%; MarketBeat has the same EPS and $8.53 billion of sales. Eaton's own guide was $3.46 to $3.56, so the consensus sits near the top of it.

## What to watch

- **Orders.** Book-to-bill, new orders against sales shipped, was 1.2 last quarter; Kavout noted electrical-sector data-center order growth slowed from 240% in the first quarter to 85% in the second, so the third-quarter figure tests whether that slowdown continued.
- **The full-year range.** Adjusted EPS guidance is $13.40 to $13.60; a raise to the top would show the data-center demand is still running ahead of plan.
- **Margins.** Segment margin was 23.1% in the second quarter; the question is whether new capacity costs eat into it as volumes climb.

## How the stock has done

The stock is up 14.9% this year and closed Monday at $366.07. It moved 5% or more on 17 of the last 252 sessions, and $1,000 put into Eaton five years ago would be worth $2,133 today. Its 30 October drop came on the day of the AI-stock sell-off in our [market wrap](/news/2026-10-30/stock-market-today-intels-worst-day-since-1974-sinks-the-nasdaq-2-9/), so Tuesday's order numbers are also a read on that trade.
