Exxon Mobil ($XOM) said on Monday that it had started production at Uaru, its fifth oil development off Guyana, lifting the country's installed capacity to **1.1 million barrels a day**, Reuters and GuruFocus reported. The new floating production ship, MODEC's Errea Wittu, is expected to reach its initial capacity of 250,000 barrels a day in the first quarter of 2027, the company said.

Uaru is a **$12.7 billion** project aimed at more than 800 million barrels of oil, Exxon said when it approved it in 2023, as reported by Energy Now. Uaru joins four ships already pumping off Guyana, and its 250,000 barrels a day would add nearly 30% to what the first four can produce, by our arithmetic. It arrives with oil prices falling: US crude traded near $87 on Monday, down 1.7%, as Middle East exports recovered, CNBC reported.

Exxon's shares were down 1.1% at $167.63 at 12:15 p.m. ET, up about 39% this year after a 41.8% gain to Friday's close in our data. The next markers are the ramp-up to 250,000 barrels a day in early 2027 and Exxon's fourth-quarter results; see [our recap of its third quarter](/news/2026-11-01/exxon-mobil-xom-tops-q3-estimates-as-refining-profit-more-than-doubles/).
