The AI hardware trade is having its worst stretch of the autumn. On Thursday 531 of the 580 stocks we track fell and chip stocks led the S&P 500 to a sixth straight loss ([our Daily](/daily/2026-11-20/)), and the suppliers of test gear, fiber and power parts have been sold with them. That is when the difference between growth that turns into cash and growth that has to be paid for starts to matter.

We screened chip, networking, power and data-center companies in our coverage on revenue growth over the latest 12 months, from SEC filings, leaving out stocks from our lists of the past 14 days and Lumentum, which is being bought. Then we judged each against its peers: long-run growth first, then margins, cash, returns and price.

```picks
like: KEYS, TXN, GLW
skip: MRVL
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Keysight Technologies ($KEYS)
**Free cash flow margin: 24.4% over the two years to July 2026.** Keysight sells the test and measurement equipment that chip, network and phone makers use to design new products.

1. It turns about a quarter of its sales into free cash, against a **13.4% median** for the 31 hardware makers in our coverage, which puts it in the top quarter. Its 63.7% gross margin is also 20 points above the median.
2. The growth has arrived. Revenue rose 41% to $1.99 billion in the quarter to October and orders jumped 53%, it reported on Thursday ([our recap](/news/2026-11-19/keysight-keys-beats-q4-estimates-as-orders-jump-53-and-guides-above-forecasts/)), so the order book is still growing faster than sales.
3. The weak spot is the long run: revenue grew 5.0% a year over five fiscal years, just below the peer median, which is why we want orders to keep leading.

**The price:** 38.6 times trailing earnings, below its own median of 46.6 since 2024, though 21.2 times forward earnings is above the 17.2 peer median.
**What could go wrong:** orders falling back below $2 billion in the January quarter would say the network build-out has peaked.

### 2. Texas Instruments ($TXN)
**Operating margin: 40.8% in the 12 months to September 2026.** TI makes analog and embedded chips, including the power-management parts that every data-center rack needs.

1. That margin is 11 points above the **29.6% median** for the 25 chipmakers in our coverage, in the top quarter, and free cash flow reached 35.2% of sales over the same 12 months, against a 22.2% median.
2. Data center has become a growth engine. Third-quarter revenue rose 28% to $6.06 billion and data-center sales roughly doubled, the company and Futurum Group reported on 21 October; its fourth-quarter guide of $5.8 billion to $6.3 billion sat above the $5.73 billion analysts expected, Benzinga reported.
3. The long-run record is the catch: revenue grew 4.1% a year over five fiscal years and earnings per share fell 1.8% a year, both bottom quarter, so the current boom is recent.

**The price:** 37.1 times trailing earnings, in line with its median of 36.7 since 2024, and 27.2 times forward earnings against a 21.2 peer median.
**What could go wrong:** an industrial slowdown, still its biggest market, would hit just as investors are paying for the data-center story; its guide for the first quarter in January is the test.

### 3. Corning ($GLW)
**Earnings per share: up 27.6% a year over five fiscal years.** Corning makes glass and the optical fiber that links AI servers inside and between data centers.

1. That is twice the **14.0% median** for its hardware peers and top quarter, and its operating margin has widened 10.1 points over five years, also top quarter.
2. AI is now the driver. Optical Communications sales rose 44% to $2.38 billion in the third quarter, enterprise network sales 84%, and it guided fourth-quarter core sales up about 19% to $5.2 billion to $5.3 billion, the company said on 27 October, as Crypto Briefing and 24/7 Wall St reported.
3. The weak spots are returns and debt: return on capital of about 11.9% is below the 16.5% median, and net debt is 1.9 years of operating profit.

**The price:** 43.1 times trailing earnings, about half its median of 82.2 since 2024, after a 58.6% fall from its June high; 27.5 times forward earnings is still above the 17.2 peer median.
**What could go wrong:** optical growth slowing in the fourth quarter, when the AI build-out is being questioned, would leave the stock looking expensive against peers.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Marvell Technology ($MRVL)
**Return on capital: about 6.3%, against a 17.8% peer median.** Marvell designs custom AI chips and networking chips for the cloud giants.

1. The growth is real: revenue rose 22.5% a year over five fiscal years, top quarter among chipmakers. But it earns little on the money tied up in the business, bottom quarter, and its 16.5% operating margin over the past 12 months is well below the **29.6% median**.
2. It keeps issuing shares. Diluted shares rose 6.4% in two years, against a flat median, and on 2 November it agreed to buy Lumentum in an all-stock deal valued at about $115 billion, after which Marvell holders would own about 67% of the combined company, the companies said ([our story](/news/2026-11-02/marvell-mrvl-agrees-to-buy-lumentum-lite-for-115-billion-in-stock/)).
3. The deal isn't due to close until the first half of 2028, so holders carry the integration risk for more than a year, and the stock has fallen 43.4% in a month and sits 56.2% below its June high.

**The price:** 24.6 times forward earnings against a 21.2 peer median, and 70.3 times free cash flow.
**What would change our mind:** better terms for Marvell holders, or a quarter in which custom-chip sales lift the operating margin toward the peer median.
