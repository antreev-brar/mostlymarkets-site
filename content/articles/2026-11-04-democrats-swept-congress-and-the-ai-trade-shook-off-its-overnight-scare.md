Democrats won control of the Senate overnight, according to AP projections cited by PBS and Forbes, and were projected to take the House too. Traders read it first as a threat to the AI building boom: Nasdaq-100 futures fell as much as **2.4%** in Asian hours, Reuters reported, according to Finimize, as investors priced in investigations of the tech industry and pushback on data centers.

Then the selling stopped. By about 4:20 a.m. ET, S&P 500 futures had turned up, Reuters reported, helped by strong results from the AI hardware makers that reported after Tuesday's close: AMD ($AMD) was up 6.3% before the open and Arista Networks ($ANET) 12.4% by 6:15 a.m. ET in our data. The election's losers this morning are narrower: Elon Musk's companies, which face a new Democratic governor in Texas who campaigned on halting new data centers.

Rabobank analysts tied the Nasdaq's early drop to expectations of tighter AI rules under a Democratic Congress, FXStreet reported, and City AM said analysts saw slim odds of laws that would stop the data-center buildout, but expected investigations. The recovery fits the case in [our story on the midterms](/news/2026-11-02/wall-street-is-betting-stocks-rally-after-the-midterms-whoever-wins/): a divided Washington passes little, and a president friendly to AI still holds the veto.

Tesla ($TSLA) was **down 4.3%** at $381.32 before the open, and SpaceX shares fell about 4%, CNBC and Investing.com reported, after Democrat Gina Hinojosa was reported to have unseated Texas Governor Greg Abbott. Hinojosa has called for a moratorium on new data-center permits, a direct threat to SpaceX's AI computing plans in the state, Investing.com reported.

AMD's revenue rose 45% to $13.4 billion and it guided fourth-quarter sales to about $17 billion, above the $16.2 billion expected ([our recap](/news/2026-11-03/amd-amd-beats-q3-estimates-and-guides-q4-sales-to-17-billion/)). At $514.25 before the open, it would be above Friday's $504.80 close, erasing all of Monday's 7.5% fall. Its new chip deals with OpenAI and Meta will also bring a charge of about $1 billion a quarter, Reuters reported.

Supermicro's revenue nearly quadrupled to $19.9 billion, Seeking Alpha and Startup Fortune reported. The split shows where the money is going: to the data center, not the phone ([our Skyworks recap](/news/2026-11-03/skyworks-swks-sinks-17-after-its-first-forecast-with-qorvo-misses/)).
