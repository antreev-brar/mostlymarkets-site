Stocks fell for a fourth straight session on Tuesday as software companies led the losses two days before Anthropic starts trading, and Salesforce ($CRM) **sank 9.5%**, the Dow's worst performer, Kiplinger and Seeking Alpha reported. The Dow dropped 848.75 points, or 1.6%, to 50,759.42, the S&P 500 lost 1.0% to 7,340.53 and the Nasdaq Composite 0.7% to 24,328.38. After the close, Nvidia ($NVDA) reported revenue and a forecast above what Wall Street expected.

## Software takes the hit before Anthropic lists

Salesforce closed at $194.52, its **biggest one-day drop since May 2024** by our price data, and is now down 26.6% this year. The rest of the group fell with it: Intuit ($INTU) lost 7.2%, ServiceNow ($NOW) 6.5% and Atlassian ($TEAM) 6.0%. Microsoft ($MSFT) fell only 2.3%, but its size made it the biggest drag on the S&P 500 by our data. Seeking Alpha and MLQ.ai tied the selling to Anthropic's offering, which prices on Wednesday: investors fear that AI agents will do the work that per-user software licenses charge for, and the $64 billion sale needs cash that fund managers must raise somewhere.

None of them had [news of their own](/news/2026-11-17/salesforce-mongodb-and-sap-shares-slide-what-happened/). RBC Capital Markets analyst Rishi Jaluria put the fear bluntly on a podcast, saying "the AI companies are going to try to destroy Salesforce entirely," Benzinga reported. Anthropic itself is [set to price above its $70 to $78 range](/news/2026-11-17/anthropic-is-set-to-price-its-ipo-above-its-70-to-78-range/), the WSJ and CNBC reported.

Kiplinger and Invezz also pointed to a run of technology outages: CNN reported on Tuesday that the National Security Agency's classified internal network was down, and CBS News said there was no evidence of a cyberattack. Nearly nine in ten S&P 500 stocks fell, the AP reported, and the VIX, the options market's gauge of expected swings, **rose 11.1%** to 25.46.

## Insurers, banks and retailers joined in

UnitedHealth ($UNH) fell 5.4% with no company news; the only fresh item was a call from Senator Elizabeth Warren to break up the big insurers ([our story](/news/2026-11-17/why-unitedhealth-unh-stock-is-down-today/)). JPMorgan ($JPM) lost 2.4% on the day it [named a new finance chief](/news/2026-11-17/jpmorgan-jpm-names-bregje-de-best-cfo-as-jeremy-barnum-steps-aside/).

Results did not protect the reporters. Home Depot ($HD) fell 2.5% even though its [sales beat forecasts](/news/2026-11-17/home-depot-hd-tops-q3-estimates-and-names-cfo-richard-mcphail-ceo/), Disney ($DIS) 1.5% after [topping estimates and lifting its dividend](/news/2026-11-17/disney-dis-tops-q4-estimates-and-raises-its-dividend-33/), and JD.com ($JD) 7.7% after [missing on revenue](/news/2026-11-17/jd-com-jd-misses-q3-revenue-estimates-at-309-6-billion-yuan-as-shoppers-hold-back/). Nike ($NKE) jumped at the open on its [2031 targets](/news/2026-11-17/nike-nke-targets-5-billion-of-added-sales-and-a-12-to-14-margin-by-2031/), then reversed to close down 6.3%.

The few gainers were in AI networking chips: Arm ($ARM) rose 4.8%, Astera Labs ($ALAB) 4.6% and Broadcom ($AVGO) 2.5%. We found no single news cause for those moves. TSMC fell 3.7% for a second day of losses ([our story](/news/2026-11-17/micron-and-tsmc-shares-fall-what-happened/)).

## Nvidia beats, and guides above forecasts

Nvidia closed 0.4% lower at $155.46 before its first report since Jensen Huang quit. After the bell it said third-quarter revenue rose 103% from a year earlier to **$115.8 billion**, above its own guidance of $108 billion and the roughly $109.2 billion analysts expected, according to its release and LSEG data. It forecast fourth-quarter revenue of $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, against an LSEG consensus of $125.25 billion, and assumed no data-center chip sales to China, Reuters and the company said.

The call with interim Chief Executive Colette Kress starts at 5 p.m. ET. The 10-year Treasury yield **eased 0.02 percentage point** to 5.10%, even after St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said rates may need to [rise a full point by spring](/news/2026-11-17/feds-musalem-says-rates-may-need-to-rise-a-full-point-by-spring/), and [October retail sales](/news/2026-11-17/retail-sales-slowed-to-0-2-in-october-but-the-core-gauge-beat-forecasts/) came in as forecast.
