AstraZeneca ($AZN) announced three regulatory wins before Monday's open, Reuters and LSE.co.uk reported. The FDA approved its immunotherapy Imfinzi with Pfizer's ($PFE) Padcev for muscle-invasive bladder cancer in patients who can't take cisplatin chemotherapy, based on a trial in which the combination **cut the risk of death by 42%**, Pharmaceutical Technology reported; it approved Klygefa, a once-weekly self-injected drug for the nerve-muscle disease generalised myasthenia gravis, which also won EU approval; and EU advisers recommended Baxfendy, its pill for hard-to-control high blood pressure.

Baxfendy matters most for the stock. It is already approved in the US, and the European Medicines Agency's advisers backed it for adults whose blood pressure stays high on two or more medicines, making it the first drug of its kind in line for the EU, AstraZeneca and Fierce Pharma said. Imfinzi is already on the market, so the bladder-cancer approval widens an existing seller rather than adding a new one.

The shares rose 0.2% to $184.87 in premarket trading, in our data, and are up 5.1% this year, 11.9% below their February high. The European Commission makes the final call on Baxfendy, and no date is set; AstraZeneca reported a third-quarter beat on 1 November ([our recap](/news/2026-11-01/astrazeneca-azn-tops-q3-estimates-and-shares-jump-7-on-a-savings-plan/)).
