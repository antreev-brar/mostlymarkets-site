J.P. Morgan's economists, led by Chief US Economist Michael Feroli, said on Thursday they now expect the Federal Reserve to raise rates by a quarter point at each of its next **three meetings**, in December, January and March, Reuters and Newsquawk reported. December was already in their forecast; January and March are new. "These hikes aren't to deliver a bad labor market, but to preserve credibility when they have no compelling narrative for a pause," they wrote, according to Reuters.

The call puts the biggest US bank alongside BofA Global Research and Deutsche Bank, which also expect three hikes, Reuters reported. It follows [Fed minutes showing "almost all" officials expect more hikes](/news/2026-11-18/fed-minutes-show-almost-all-officials-expect-more-rate-hikes-and-flag-ai-as-an-inflation-risk/) and St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem's [case for a full point by spring](/news/2026-11-17/feds-musalem-says-rates-may-need-to-rise-a-full-point-by-spring/). For stocks priced on falling rates, three hikes in four months raises the discount on every future dollar of earnings.

Markets already price a December hike as near-certain, with an 8% chance of a half-point move, according to CME's FedWatch tool cited by Reuters. The Fed announces its next decision on 9 December, at the end of its two-day meeting.
