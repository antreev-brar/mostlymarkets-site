Agilent Technologies ($A) reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of **$2.04 billion** after the close on Monday, up 9.6% from a year earlier and above the $1.99 billion analysts expected, according to the Zacks consensus. Adjusted earnings of $1.86 a share beat the $1.72 consensus and the top of its own $1.71-to-$1.74 forecast. The stock jumped 8.9% to $191.75 after hours, as of 4:14 p.m. ET, after closing up 3.3% at $176.04.

## What stood out

The beat holds up without the refund. Strip out the 7-cent benefit from tariff refunds and adjusted earnings were $1.79 a share, still above both the consensus and Agilent's own range, the release showed. *Verdict: a clean beat, not an accounting one.*

Life sciences and diagnostics, the unit that sells instruments to drugmakers and clinical labs, **grew 12% to $843 million**, or 9% on a core basis that strips out currency and deals. That is faster than the 11% it grew in the third quarter, and instruments are the cyclical part of the business, the one that moves with drugmakers' spending budgets.

CrossLab, the services and consumables arm, grew 6% to $799 million, in line with the roughly $800 million analysts expected. These are recurring sales, so they steady the business rather than drive it. Chief Executive Padraig McDonnell said in the release that Agilent "closed FY26 well ahead of our initial outlook, with momentum building quarter by quarter."

## The outlook

For fiscal 2027, Agilent forecast revenue of $8.00 billion to $8.15 billion, 6% to 8% growth on the $7.55 billion it booked in 2026, and adjusted EPS of **$6.73 to $6.86**, up 7.3% at the midpoint. For the first quarter it expects revenue of $1.96 billion to $2.00 billion and adjusted EPS of $1.54 to $1.57, which CNBC said topped the FactSet consensus. The full-year margin forecast includes no further tariff refunds.

## Our take

We were impressed that the beat came from the instrument business, the cyclical part that had to recover for the stock's run this year to make sense. On the other hand, guidance of 5% to 7% core growth is close to Agilent's five-year pace of 5.4% a year, a bottom-quarter record among the 62 peers in our data. The stock already trades at 26.8 times expected earnings against a 17.8 times median for those peers, so a 9% after-hours jump asks the 2027 plan to land at the top of its range.
