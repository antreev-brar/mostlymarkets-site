GSK has settled its patent lawsuits against Moderna ($MRNA), Reuters and Law360 reported on Friday, and the two asked a Delaware federal court to dismiss the US claims with prejudice, so they can't be refiled. GSK had sued in 2024, saying Moderna's COVID shots Spikevax and mNEXSPIKE and its RSV shot mResvia used its patented lipid nanoparticles, the fatty bubbles that carry mRNA into cells.

Moderna said the deal was reached on 28 October, covers the disputes in the US and Europe, and gives both companies patent cross-licenses in infectious disease, with **no payments** owed either way, Reuters reported. That matters because GSK had been seeking royalties on the shots, and Moderna paid $950 million earlier this year to end a similar fight with Arbutus and Genevant, Fierce Pharma reported at the time. This one ends without a payment.

Moderna shares closed **up 5.3%** at $254.19 on Friday, a move driven by its third-quarter results ([our recap](/news/2026-11-06/moderna-mrna-swings-to-a-q3-profit-and-raises-its-sales-outlook/)); the settlement surfaced in the afternoon. The stock is up 762% this year. GSK's similar suit against Pfizer and BioNTech is still running, Reuters reported.
