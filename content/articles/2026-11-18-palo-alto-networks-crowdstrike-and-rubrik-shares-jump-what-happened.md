## What happened

Shares of cybersecurity companies Palo Alto Networks ($PANW), CrowdStrike ($CRWD) and Rubrik ($RBRK) **jumped 5% to 7%** in the morning session on Wednesday, bouncing back from two days of selling after Nvidia's ($NVDA) forecast eased fears about AI spending. As of 10:16 a.m. ET, Palo Alto was up 7.2% at $331.60, Rubrik 6.5%, CrowdStrike 5.9% and Cloudflare ($NET) 5.0%.

Investing.com tied CrowdStrike's gain, which ended a four-session slide, to Nvidia's third-quarter results and $130 billion fourth-quarter guide, which Reuters and Bloomberg said lifted beaten-up AI stocks before the open ([our Nvidia recap](/news/2026-11-17/nvidia-nvda-tops-q3-estimates-and-guides-to-130-billion-in-its-first-report-without-huang/)). Zscaler ($ZS) rose 4.9% and Fortinet ($FTNT) 4.5% by the same time in our data. The buying was narrow: Nvidia itself was flat and Salesforce ($CRM) was **down 2.6%**, so the money went to security rather than to software as a whole.

## Why it matters

Tuesday's sell-off was about AI agents doing the work that per-seat software charges for ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-18/wall-street-is-betting-ai-will-empty-softwares-seats-3-numbers-will-decide-it/)). Security sells protection that grows with the number of AI systems a company runs, not with its headcount, and Wednesday's split between Palo Alto and Salesforce is the market drawing that line.

The bounce also runs into a test. Palo Alto reports its fiscal first quarter after the close today, with analysts expecting adjusted EPS of $0.97 on revenue of $3.31 billion, according to Zacks consensus data ([our preview](/news/2026-11-17/what-to-expect-from-palo-alto-networks-panw-q1-earnings/)). After its last report, on 2 September, the stock fell about 9% in a day despite beating estimates, as margin worries outweighed 34% revenue growth, SiliconANGLE and 24/7 Wall St. reported.

## What the market is telling us

Palo Alto's shares moved 5% or more on 30 of the last 252 sessions, so today's move is large but not rare. Before today the stock was up 67.9% this year, and at Tuesday's $309.29 close it sat 26.3% below its 52-week closing high of $419.91 from 6 October. CrowdStrike sat 21.3% below its own 6 October high, and Rubrik 26.0% below its 28 October high.

We read this as a relief bounce, not a verdict: the group had fallen hardest in the AI scare and is recovering fastest on the first good news, and Palo Alto's guidance tonight decides whether it holds.
