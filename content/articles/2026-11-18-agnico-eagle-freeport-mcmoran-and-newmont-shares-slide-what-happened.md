## What happened

Shares of gold miners Agnico Eagle Mines ($AEM) and Newmont ($NEM) and copper producers Freeport-McMoRan ($FCX) and BHP Group ($BHP) **slid 5% to 7%** in the morning session on Wednesday after gold fell to a 14-month low and copper kept sliding, as Federal Reserve officials pressed for faster interest-rate hikes. As of 10:16 a.m. ET, Agnico Eagle was down 7.0% at $160.85, Freeport 7.0%, Newmont 5.8% and BHP 5.0%.

Spot gold fell 2.2% to about $3,765 an ounce by 9:55 a.m. ET after touching its lowest level since 26 September 2025, Reuters and TipRanks reported. Fed Governor Christopher Waller told CNBC on Wednesday that "we have to be a little more aggressive in our rate hikes to try to get inflation down," and MarketWatch reported he said rates may need to rise 100 basis points or more. St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said a series of hikes will probably be needed, Quartz reported. Higher rates lift the dollar and the return on cash, both of which make gold, which pays nothing, less attractive.

Copper fell to a near five-month low on the London Metal Exchange as the firmer dollar and rate-hike worries weighed, Reuters and the Globe and Mail reported.

## Why it matters

Miners' costs are mostly fixed, so a fall in the metal price comes almost straight out of their profit, which is why the shares fell more than twice as far as gold. TipRanks reported that futures markets put a **95% chance** on a quarter-point hike at the Fed's December meeting, and Waller's comments raise the odds of more after that.

Copper adds a second problem: it was also hit by doubts about AI data-center spending, Reuters reported, and BHP and Rio Tinto ($RIO) are each down more than 4% today. The next test is the minutes of the Fed's last meeting, due this afternoon.

## What the market is telling us

Agnico Eagle's shares moved 5% or more on 27 of the last 252 sessions. Before today it was up 2.0% this year, and at Tuesday's $173.01 close it sat 31.4% below its 52-week closing high of $252.19 from 2 March. Freeport sat 30.8% below its 25 August high, and down 20.7% over the past month.

We think the miners are trading as a bet on the Fed, not on their mines: until rate-hike expectations stop rising, a cheap-looking gold stock can get cheaper.
