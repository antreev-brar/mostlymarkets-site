JD.com ($JD) said before dawn on Tuesday that third-quarter revenue rose 3.5% to **309.6 billion yuan**, short of the 311.5 billion yuan analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters. Adjusted net profit rose 44% to 8.3 billion yuan but missed the 8.6 billion yuan estimate, Reuters and the South China Morning Post reported. Its US shares were **down 2.8%** at $26.77 at 6:30 a.m. ET.

The miss matters because JD is a read on what Chinese households will spend: Reuters and the SCMP both blamed cautious shoppers in a slowing economy. The better news was cost control: losses in its New Businesses segment, led by the food-delivery push it started in 2025 against Meituan and Alibaba ($BABA), fell by nearly 40%, the company said, as reported by MLQ. In our data the stock had fallen 4.0% this year to Monday, and sat 18.4% below its May high.

Alibaba reports before the open on Thursday, the next test of whether China's online retailers can grow while shoppers hold back.
