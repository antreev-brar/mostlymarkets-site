On Tuesday, Bank of America's November survey showed fund managers cut their tech holdings by the most since 2006, Bloomberg and CNBC reported, and software stocks fell 4% to 7% by mid-morning. Investors leaving expensive growth stocks tend to look for cheap ones next, so we went looking at what "cheap" buys today.

Our screen took the large US stocks with the **lowest forward P/E** (price divided by next year's expected earnings) among those whose revenue grew in the 12 months to September 2026, from SEC filings in our fundamentals data, leaving out stocks we listed in the last 14 days. The median of the 16 was 8.1 times. We then checked each one's growth, cash and returns against its own industry: a low multiple is only a bargain if the earnings behind it will last.

```picks
skip: VLO, DVN, PINS
like: OMC
```

## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. Valero Energy ($VLO)
**Forward P/E: 5.4 times on 17 November 2026.** Valero is one of the largest US oil refiners, turning crude into gasoline and diesel.

1. **The earnings are a margin spike.** Its refining margin was **$39.24 a barrel** in the third quarter against $13.14 a year earlier, and earnings per share rose to $23.89 from $3.53, its third-quarter release said, as Yahoo Finance reported.
2. **The business underneath is not growing fast.** Revenue grew 6.8% a year over two years, behind the 10.1% median of the 25 energy stocks we cover, and its 11.5% operating margin is in their bottom quarter.
3. **The stock has already priced in a lot.** Valero was up 127.1% this year at Monday's close, though 19.6% below its 14 October high.

**The price:** 5.4 times expected earnings against an energy median of 11.6 times; the low multiple says the market already expects margins to fall back.

**What would change our mind:** a refining margin above $30 a barrel in its fourth-quarter report, due in late January.

### 2. Devon Energy ($DVN)
**Forward P/E: 8.2 times on 17 November 2026.** Devon is a US shale oil and gas producer, now centred on the Permian Basin.

1. **Its growth was bought with shares.** Revenue grew 23.5% a year over two years, but the diluted share count rose **83.9%**, the worst of the energy stocks we cover, after the all-stock Coterra merger closed in May, Rigzone and World Oil reported.
2. **Returns are weak.** Return on equity was 8.6% in the 12 months to September, in the bottom quarter of its group against a median of 17.5%.
3. **Output is set to shrink.** It guided 2027 production of 1.6 million barrels of oil equivalent a day against 1.668 million in the third quarter, after agreeing to sell its Eagle Ford assets for $4.2 billion, its release and Yahoo Finance said.

**The price:** cheap against peers, but its trailing P/E of 11.1 is above its own two-year median of 8.3. Its strong cash (a 20.2% two-year free cash flow margin, top tenth) is why it isn't worse.

**What would change our mind:** the $1 billion of yearly merger savings showing up in per-share cash flow by the end of 2027.

### 3. Pinterest ($PINS)
**Forward P/E: 8.9 times on 17 November 2026.** Pinterest runs the image-search and shopping-ideas site, paid for by advertisers.

1. **Growth keeps slowing.** Revenue grew 20.1% a year over five years, 16.1% over two, **11.9%** in the latest quarter, and it guided 8% to 12% for the fourth quarter, its 29 October release said.
2. **It missed its own range.** Third-quarter revenue of $1.174 billion fell short of the $1.190 billion to $1.210 billion it had forecast, as cross-border sellers cut spending in Europe after a tariff exemption changed, TIKR and PPC Land reported; the stock closed 2.8% lower the next day.
3. **Little of the revenue reaches profit.** Its 4.2% operating margin over 12 months is behind most of the internet companies we cover, whose median is 13.1%.

**The price:** 8.9 times expected earnings against an internet median of 19.0 times, and 8.4 times free cash flow; its 27% free cash flow margin is the best argument for it.

**What would change our mind:** fourth-quarter revenue at the top of its range, with Europe growing again, in its February report.

## 1 stock we like

### Omnicom Group ($OMC)
**Forward P/E: 8.2 times on 17 November 2026.** Omnicom is an advertising group, now including its former rival Interpublic, selling creative, media-buying and marketing services.

1. **Its underlying growth is speeding up.** Organic revenue growth, which strips out the deal, rose to **7.2%** in the third quarter from 3.9% in the first, and adjusted EPS rose 22% to $2.73, its third-quarter release and Exchange4media said.
2. **The deal savings are arriving.** Management said it is on track for $900 million of cost savings in 2026, according to the call transcript and Earnings Whispers.
3. **The cash is real.** Free cash flow was 11.1% of revenue over two years, at the median of our communication services stocks, and the stock trades at 8.3 times that cash.

**The price:** 8.2 times expected earnings against a sector median of 16.2 times. Its trailing GAAP P/E of 49 is inflated by merger charges, so the forward and cash multiples are the fairer read.

**What could go wrong:** net debt of $6.4 billion is 4.1 years of operating profit, and management credited part of the third quarter to the World Cup and the midterm elections, which won't repeat in 2027. If organic growth fell back to 4%, the savings would be doing all the work.
