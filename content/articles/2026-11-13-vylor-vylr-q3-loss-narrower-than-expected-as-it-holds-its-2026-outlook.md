Vylor ($VYLR), the seed company Corteva spun off on 1 October, reported an adjusted loss of **$0.42 a share** for its third quarter after the close on Wednesday, 11 November, narrower than the $0.52 loss analysts polled by FactSet expected, according to MarketScreener. Pro forma net sales fell 1% year on year to $938 million, ahead of the $922.9 million FactSet consensus. The stock fell as much as 3.3% on Thursday, to an intraday low of $69.69, then closed at $72.06, down 0.1%; it was up 0.4% at $72.31 as of 12:15 p.m. ET on Friday.

## What stood out

The third quarter is the dead season for a seed seller: farmers in the Americas buy for spring planting, so July to September is mostly costs. That is why a loss is normal here, and why the beat against FactSet matters less than the full-year number.

The quarter's drag was volume. Vylor said weather hurt volumes outside the Americas, and its shift to licensing its soybean traits in Brazil, rather than selling the seed, cut reported revenue by about $50 million in the quarter, according to the call transcript reported by Investing.com. Licensing earns less revenue but a fatter margin, which is the trade management is making on purpose. On a GAAP basis, which counts one-off costs the adjusted figure leaves out, the pro forma loss was $0.72 a share, or $480 million, the release showed.

## The outlook

Vylor kept its 2026 targets: about $10.4 billion of net sales, about $2.8 billion of operating EBITDA, and operating EPS of $2.25 to $2.35. It also announced a **$3 billion share buyback** and a quarterly dividend of $0.13 a share, the release said. Management said it expects more licensing income and better margins in the fourth quarter, and on the call it pointed to record grain, oilseed and biofuel demand as support for 2027, Reuters reported.

## Our take

A narrower loss than expected and an unchanged outlook in its first report as a public company is a clean start, and a $3 billion buyback is about 6% of its market value. The bar is the fourth quarter: Vylor has to deliver the licensing income and margin gains it promised to reach $2.8 billion of EBITDA. At 88 times trailing earnings, which still include spin-off costs, the price assumes it does.
