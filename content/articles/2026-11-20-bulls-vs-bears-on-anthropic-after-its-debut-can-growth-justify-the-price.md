Anthropic's second day of trading on Friday turned into a referendum on its price. The AI lab priced its record IPO at $83 a share on Wednesday, opened at $111.50 on Thursday and closed at $97.17, CBS News and Investing.com reported; on Friday morning it was sliding back toward the offer price, MarketWatch and CNBC reported, as the wider AI sell-off resumed. The first wave of analyst coverage arrived in the middle of it, and it splits on one question: is Anthropic's growth fast enough, for long enough, to carry a valuation of $1.5 trillion or more?

The number at the center is how fast revenue is growing. Anthropic's revenue rose to $16.96 billion in the third quarter from $11.56 billion in the second, its IPO filing showed, growth of **47% in a quarter**, down from 144% the quarter before ([our story on the filing](/news/2026-11-02/anthropics-ipo-filing-shows-17-billion-of-q3-sales-and-a-swing-to-an-operating-loss/)).

```highlights
The short version
- **What's happening:** Six of seven analysts covering Anthropic rate it a buy, with targets of $105 to $155, while the stock slipped back toward its $83 IPO price on Friday.
- **Why it matters:** Anthropic's spending is revenue for its big owners Amazon ($AMZN) and Alphabet ($GOOGL) and for the chipmakers; if its price can't hold, the AI trade loses its newest support.
- **Our read:** The bulls' targets need growth to stay near triple digits a year; the latest quarter's slowdown to 47% means Q4 revenue is the number that decides it.
```

## The bull case

### Arjun Bhatia, analyst, William Blair
**Anthropic is a generational company priced at a reasonable multiple of future sales.** Bhatia started coverage at Outperform, putting fair value at $130 to $150 a share, and argued the stock trades at about 9 times estimated 2027 revenue and 6 times 2028, Investing.com and GuruFocus reported. He sees revenue reaching $414 billion by 2030, about 8% of what he estimates is a $5 trillion AI market, with gross margins in the mid-80s percent after the cost of running its models.

### Scott Berg, analyst, Needham
**Growth will stay above 150% next year.** Berg started at Buy with a $150 target, Investing.com and TipRanks reported, on the view that Anthropic is a leading vendor in a market that could take a slice of a $56 trillion global labor pool. He expects growth of more than 150% in fiscal 2027; on his numbers, this year's quarterly slowdown is a pause, not a peak.

### Ben Reitzes, head of technology research, Melius Research
**Margins, not just sales, will justify the price.** Reitzes started at Buy with a $155 target over 24 months, Investing.com and Stocktwits reported, projecting long-term earnings power of more than $5 a share with operating margins above 30%. That is the bull case's answer to the third quarter's $567 million operating loss: the computing bill is a cost of scale that falls as a share of sales.

## The bear case

### Jay Goldberg, analyst, Seaport Research Partners
**The company is great, but the price asks for "an incredible leap of faith."** Goldberg started at Neutral with no price target, CNBC and TipRanks reported, saying he is "not negative" but cautious. His worry is the slowdown from 144% to the 40s in a quarter, which he expects may not improve much in the fourth; to support today's value, he argues, Anthropic needs much faster growth over the next two years than that pace implies.

### Karen Kwok, columnist, Reuters Breakingviews
**A $2 trillion debut may mark the peak, not the start.** In a column on 19 November, Kwok argued the debut could give the AI industry another day of confidence and keep the data-centre build going for a while, under the headline that the IPO "may be last hurrah." In October she warned that falling prices for AI models were a "deflation trap" that usage growth had to outrun, citing the same slowdown in quarterly sales.

## Where they agree
Nobody disputes the business. Even Goldberg calls Anthropic "an incredible company," and every view rests on the same filing figures. Deepwater's Gene Munster, who is bullish on AI, said in a post on X on Thursday that the Nasdaq's fall on debut day was "not an indictment of AI" but what happens "when many were on the same side of the trade," Benzinga and Stocktwits reported: investors sold other AI stocks to make room for the new one. That cuts both ways, since it explains why the IPO did not lift the rest of the sector.

## The number that decides it
The fourth-quarter revenue figure, in Anthropic's first report as a public company, early next year. The bulls' targets need growth to re-accelerate toward Berg's 150%-plus a year; Goldberg's caution is right if quarterly growth stays in the 40s or slows further.

## Our read
The evidence favors the bulls on the business and Goldberg on the price, as it did [before the IPO](/news/2026-11-07/bulls-vs-bears-on-anthropics-ipo-is-1-5-trillion-a-fair-price/). Bhatia's 9 times 2027 sales only looks reasonable if 2027 revenue is roughly two and a half to three times the third quarter's annual pace of $68 billion, by our arithmetic, and the latest hard data point, a quarter at 47% growth, shows growth slowing, not speeding up. Too early to tell, but the fourth-quarter report will tell: growth back above 60% in a quarter would make the targets look cautious, and growth below 40% would, we think, leave little to hold the stock above its $83 IPO price, the stock [Wall Street was betting](/news/2026-11-15/wall-street-is-betting-anthropics-ipo-can-rescue-the-ai-trade-3-numbers-will-decide-it/) would rescue the AI trade.
