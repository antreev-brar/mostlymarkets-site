## What happened

Shares of AI hardware companies Bloom Energy ($BE), CoreWeave ($CRWV) and Dell Technologies ($DELL) **jumped 5% to 7%** in the morning session on Thursday, as Anthropic's record IPO, priced on Wednesday night, pulled buyers back into the stocks that sold off hardest this month. As of 10:15 a.m. ET, Bloom was up 7.2% at $183.24, CoreWeave 5.1% at $50.26 and Dell 4.7% at $327.67.

Anthropic sold 980 million shares at $83, above its $70 to $78 range, raising $81.3 billion in the largest IPO on record, CNBC reported ([our brief](/news/2026-11-18/anthropic-prices-its-ipo-at-83-a-share-above-its-70-to-78-range/)). Nearly every corner of the AI trade rose at the open, Yahoo Finance and Invezz reported. Benzinga and GuruFocus tied CoreWeave's gain to the debut and noted that Anthropic is already a CoreWeave customer, while Benzinga said Bloom's rise came as the IPO lifted AI power stocks.

Dell had support of its own. Citi analyst Asiya Merchant named it a top IT hardware pick on 16 November, keeping a Buy rating while trimming her price target to $550 from $600, TipRanks and GuruFocus reported. Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE) rose 3.7% and Super Micro Computer ($SMCI) 3.4% by the same time in our data.

## Why it matters

The demand for these stocks rests on one chain: AI labs raise money, rent or buy computing power, and that power needs servers, data centres and electricity. Anthropic raising more than any company ever has in an IPO is the strongest evidence in weeks that the money at the top of the chain is still there ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-15/wall-street-is-betting-anthropics-ipo-can-rescue-the-ai-trade-3-numbers-will-decide-it/)).

The rally is fading, though. The S&P 500 opened at 7,389 but was up only 0.4% at 7,355 by 10:15 a.m. ET, near its low of the morning. Dell reports its third quarter on 24 November, which will show whether AI server orders held up through the sell-off.

## What the market is telling us

Dell's shares moved 5% or more on 54 of the last 252 sessions, so a move this size is routine for it. The stock is up 148.7% this year, and at Wednesday's $313.11 close it sat 46.8% below its 52-week closing high of $588.40 from 17 September. CoreWeave sat 65.3% below its May high and Bloom 50.6% below its June high.

We read this as a relief rally on someone else's news: none of the three gave investors new numbers today, and Dell's report on Tuesday is the first that can.
