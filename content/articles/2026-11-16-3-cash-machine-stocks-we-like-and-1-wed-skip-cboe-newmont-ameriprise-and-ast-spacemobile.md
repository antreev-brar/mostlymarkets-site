On Monday, Cboe Global Markets ($CBOE) closed at a 52-week high of $377.50, up 50.4% this year, while AST SpaceMobile ($ASTS) **sank 12.3%** to a 52-week low after a rocket failure on Sunday rattled space stocks. One business throws off cash every quarter; the other needs outside money to keep building.

Our screen ranked 16 large US stocks by free cash flow margin (the cash left after running the business and paying for equipment, as a share of revenue) over the 12 months to September 2026, from SEC filings in our fundamentals data, leaving out stocks we listed in the last 14 days. We then checked each name's two-year cash, growth and price against its own industry.

```picks
like: CBOE, NEM, AMP
skip: ASTS
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Cboe Global Markets ($CBOE)
**Free cash flow margin: 47.9% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Cboe runs options, stock and futures exchanges, including S&P 500 index options and the VIX.

1. **The cash is real, if lumpier than the headline.** Its quarterly cash flow swings with trading money it holds, so the two-year margin of **31.8%** is the fairer number, still above the 22.3% median of the 46 exchanges, brokers and asset managers we cover.
2. **It is getting more profitable.** The operating margin rose 11.8 points over five years to 36.4%, in the top quarter of its group, and earnings per share grew 19.5% a year.
3. **The engine is still speeding up.** Third-quarter net revenue rose 18% to $712.2 million on record trading in same-day S&P 500 options, and Cboe raised its 2026 organic growth target to "high teens", its 30 October release said, as reported by Zacks. On 9 November it added Mini-Russell 2000 binary options to the prediction-market contracts it began listing in June, Reuters reported.

**The price:** 25.7 times expected earnings against a peer median of 17.5 times, but its trailing P/E of 26.9 sits just under its own two-year median of 28.0, and it trades at 15.6 times free cash flow.

**What would change our mind:** a quarter in which index options volume falls year on year.

### 2. Newmont ($NEM)
**Free cash flow margin: 37.9% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Newmont is the world's largest gold miner.

1. **It turns gold into cash better than any miner we cover.** Its two-year free cash flow margin of **33.7%** is the highest of the 28 materials stocks in our data, against a median of 6.2%.
2. **The cash goes back to owners.** The diluted share count fell 8.5% over two years, in the top tenth of its group; Newmont said on 22 October it has retired more than 10% of its shares since February 2024, according to Kitco and its release.
3. **The growth is not only the gold price.** Revenue grew 14.5% a year over five years, twice the materials median, with $3.7 billion of net cash at the end of September.

**The price:** 12.7 times expected earnings against a materials median of 16.4 times, and 11.4 times free cash flow. The stock is 18.4% below its 25 August high.

**What could go wrong:** Newmont warned that fourth-quarter cash flow will be hit by water-treatment costs at Yanacocha in Peru and severance payments, and guided all-in costs near $1,900 an ounce, its highest of the year, Kitco and Mining.com reported. A lower gold price would squeeze it further.

### 3. Ameriprise Financial ($AMP)
**Free cash flow margin: 36.4% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Ameriprise runs financial advisers, the Columbia Threadneedle asset manager and an annuity business.

1. **It compounds per share.** Earnings per share grew **24.4% a year** over five years against a peer median of 15.7%, while revenue grew 9.6% a year: buybacks and higher margins did the rest.
2. **It shrinks its share count faster than almost anyone.** Diluted shares fell 10.6% over two years, in the top tenth of its group. In the third quarter it returned about $1.0 billion, roughly 93% of operating earnings, and approved a new $5.5 billion two-year buyback, its 29 October release said.
3. **The quarter was solid.** Adjusted operating EPS rose 14% to $11.26, or 22% excluding a one-off actuarial charge, the release said.

**The price:** 10.5 times expected earnings against a peer median of 17.5 times, below its own two-year trailing median, and 6.1 times free cash flow, the cheapest of our three.

**What could go wrong:** its fees follow the level of client assets, so a falling stock market cuts revenue directly; Earnings Whispers flagged slower asset growth in the quarter.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### AST SpaceMobile ($ASTS)
**Free cash flow margin: minus 1,259% in the 12 months to September 2026.** AST SpaceMobile is building a network of large satellites that connect directly to ordinary smartphones.

1. **It spends about $12.60 for every dollar it takes in.** Free cash flow was **minus $1.8 billion** on revenue of $143 million over 12 months, and it guided fourth-quarter capital spending of $400 million to $500 million, according to its third-quarter release and Finviz.
2. **Owners pay for it in shares and debt.** The diluted share count rose 92.8% over two years, the worst in its group, and long-term debt reached $4.08 billion at the end of September, from $2.21 billion at the end of 2025.
3. **The schedule keeps slipping.** It now targets about 45 satellites in orbit by mid-2027: Monday's fall followed the loss of a United Launch Alliance Vulcan rocket carrying Amazon satellites, which had none of AST's aboard, Yahoo Finance and Benzinga reported.

**The price:** no profits, so no P/E; the stock trades at 5.2 times book value, and it is down 60.6% this year and 78.5% below its 28 May high.

**What would change our mind:** quarterly revenue running above its capital spending.
