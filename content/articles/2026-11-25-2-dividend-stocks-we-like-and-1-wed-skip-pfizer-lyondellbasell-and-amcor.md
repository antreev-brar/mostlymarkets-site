With Nasdaq futures down again this morning on tariff reports, money is looking for income it can count on. A yield of 6% is only shelter if the cash behind it is real.

Our screen took the highest-yielding stocks we cover that we haven't listed in the past 14 days, then checked whether free cash flow in the 12 months to September 2026 covers the dividend at its current rate, using SEC filings. Cover is free cash flow divided by the yearly dividend bill (the dividend per share times shares outstanding), by our arithmetic.

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. Pfizer ($PFE)
**Dividend yield: 6.0% at Tuesday's close of $28.46.** The drugmaker behind Eliquis, Prevnar and Ibrance, paying 43 cents a quarter, its 352nd straight quarterly dividend.

1. **The cash covers it.** Free cash flow was $11.8 billion in the 12 months to September, against a dividend bill of about **$9.8 billion**, 1.2 times cover. Its 18.5% free cash margin is in the bottom quarter of the 13 drugmakers we can measure, so the cover is adequate rather than generous.
2. **The core is growing again.** Third-quarter revenue of $16.6 billion was flat, but excluding its Covid products it grew 7% operationally, and launched and acquired drugs brought in $3.8 billion, up 32%, the company and Indian Pharma Post reported. Pfizer raised its 2026 revenue forecast to $62 billion to $64 billion from $60.5 billion to $62.5 billion.
3. **The long run is the weak part.** Earnings per share fell 3.6% a year over five years, bottom quarter among its peers, because the Covid windfall of 2021 and 2022 is gone. It is still buying growth: last week it agreed to pay $330 million up front for three early autoimmune programs from Starna Therapeutics, with up to $2.3 billion more in milestones.

**The price:** 10.1 times expected earnings, against a 16.8 median for drugmakers, a discount for the patents ahead.
**What could go wrong:** Pfizer expects a significant hit to revenue from patent expiries between 2026 and 2030 on drugs including Eliquis, Ibrance and Xtandi, Zacks reported on Nasdaq, citing its filings. Net debt of $60.8 billion leaves little room if new drugs don't fill the gap.

### 2. LyondellBasell ($LYB)
**Dividend yield: 5.0% at Tuesday's close of $55.62.** The Houston-based maker of plastics and chemicals, mostly polyethylene and polypropylene.

1. **The reset left cash to spare.** It halved its quarterly dividend to 69 cents from $1.37 in February, citing a prolonged industry slump, Bloomberg and TIKR reported, and declared another 69 cents on 20 November. At that rate the dividend costs about $890 million a year, against **$2.4 billion** of free cash flow in the 12 months to September: 2.7 times cover, the best on this list.
2. **Profits came back.** Third-quarter net income was $758 million, against a loss of $738 million for all of 2025, and operations brought in $1.7 billion, its release showed. Cash rose about $600 million in the quarter even after a €500 million debt repayment, and Plastics Today tied the profit jump to recovering polyethylene prices.
3. **It is a cyclical, priced like one.** Revenue grew 1.7% a year over five years, bottom quarter among the 26 materials stocks we cover, and its 12.1% gross margin is less than half their 28.6% median.

**The price:** 8.4 times expected earnings against a 15.3 median for materials stocks, and 7.4 times free cash flow.
**What could go wrong:** net debt of $9.1 billion is 3.3 years of operating profit, so another slide in plastic prices would test the new dividend; its fourth-quarter results in late January are the next check.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Amcor ($AMCR)
**Dividend yield: 5.9% at Tuesday's close of $44.70.** The packaging maker that took over Berry Global in 2025. It raised its dividend this month, Simply Wall St reported on Webull and Yahoo Finance.

1. **The dividend costs more than the cash.** At about $2.64 a share a year, by our data, the payout costs about $1.2 billion, against **$772 million** of free cash flow in the 12 months to September: 0.6 times cover. In the quarter to September, $828 million of free cash flowed out, its 10-Q showed.
2. **The deal diluted owners.** Diluted shares rose 60.9% over two years, the most among the materials stocks we cover, and earnings per share fell 4.6% a year over five years, bottom quarter.
3. **Debt is heavy.** Net debt of $14.0 billion is 7.4 years of operating profit, by our data, and leverage ended September at 3.76 times, Nasdaq's call summary showed.

**The price:** 11.3 times expected earnings against a 15.3 median, but 26.4 times free cash flow, which is the multiple that pays a dividend.
**What would change our mind:** a full year of free cash flow above the dividend bill, starting with its December-quarter results.
