Temu, the overseas shopping app of PDD Holdings ($PDD), has committed to change how it sells in the European Union, the European Commission said on Thursday, Reuters and RTE reported. Temu will stop showing fake discounts and pressure-selling messages, drop forced games such as the spin-the-wheel screen shoppers met on entering the app, and make refunds and pricing information clearer.

The commitments settle a consumer-protection action led by Ireland's consumer regulator with Belgian and German authorities, which national agencies will now monitor across the EU. That lowers the risk of fines, but it also takes away tools Temu used to win orders cheaply, just after PDD's revenue [missed estimates in the third quarter](/news/2026-11-19/pdd-holdings-pdd-misses-q3-revenue-estimates-but-beats-on-profit-as-it-cuts-marketing/) and it cut marketing by 17%. The stock closed at $87.42 on Wednesday, down 22.9% this year and 28.7% below its January high.

The open question is cost: neither the Commission nor Temu has said what the changes will do to European sales. US markets reopen for a half session on Friday.
