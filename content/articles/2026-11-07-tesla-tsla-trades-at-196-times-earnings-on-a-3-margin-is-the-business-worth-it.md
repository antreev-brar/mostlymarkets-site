## How it got here

Tesla went from a niche maker of expensive electric cars to the world's most valuable carmaker on one turn: the Model 3 and Model Y made electric cars a mass product. Revenue rose from $31.5 billion in 2020 to $81.5 billion in 2022, and the operating margin peaked at **16.8%** that year, its filings show.

Then growth stopped. Revenue has gone sideways since: $96.8 billion in 2023, $97.7 billion in 2024 and $94.8 billion in 2025. The operating margin fell every year, to 4.6% in 2025.

The latest quarter, reported on 21 October, showed revenue flat at $28.05 billion and operating income down 56% to $718 million, Electrek and Stock Titan reported, as spending on AI and Optimus rose. On Thursday the stock **plunged 10.8%** on fears the midterm results will slow Elon Musk's data-center plans ([our story](/news/2026-11-05/why-tesla-tsla-stock-is-sinking-today/)), and on Friday Musk said Tesla will stop building the Cybertruck in Texas to make room for the Cybercab ([our story](/news/2026-11-06/tesla-tsla-will-wind-down-cybertruck-production-by-mid-2027/)). **Is there a business here worth $1.42 trillion, or only a promise?**

## The business in one line

Drivers pay Tesla once for a car and then keep paying for charging, software, insurance and servicing; utilities and businesses pay it for giant batteries; and a small but growing number of riders in Austin now pay it for driverless trips.

## The numbers that matter

### Growth

Revenue grew **24.6% a year** over five fiscal years to 2025, above the 12.8% median of the 66 consumer companies we track. But nearly all of that came early: over the last two years it grew 3.3% a year, in the bottom quarter of the group. Deliveries fell 2.1% from a year earlier in the third quarter to 486,532, though they were up 8.8% for the first nine months, Electrek and Drive Tesla Canada reported.

### Profitability

The operating margin was **3.3%** in the 12 months to September 2026, against a group median of 13.8%, and it has shrunk 1.7 points over five years while the median company's widened 5.5 points. The gross margin did recover to 19.6% in the third quarter from 16.8% in the second, our data from its filings shows; the squeeze is coming from operating spending, which rose largely on AI and Optimus, Electrek and Grafa reported.

### Cash

Free cash flow was **$3.3 billion** in the 12 months to September, 3.2% of revenue, in the bottom fifth of its group. It swung from a loss of $1.09 billion in the second quarter to a gain of $1.53 billion in the third. Capital spending reached $13.0 billion in the first nine months of 2026, up from $8.5 billion for all of 2025, and Tesla now guides to about $20 billion for the year, CleanTechnica and Money365 reported.

### Returns

Tesla earns about **3.4% on its capital**, by our approximation, against a group median of 23.3%, in the bottom tenth. Return on equity was 3.6%. On today's numbers, the $89.3 billion of shareholders' equity in the business earns less than the 4.86% a 10-year Treasury paid on Friday.

### Balance sheet

This is the strength. Tesla had $17.9 billion of cash and $8.0 billion of debt at the end of September, net cash of **$9.9 billion**, and diluted shares rose only 1.3% in two years. It can fund a lot of experiments before it needs outside money.

## Why the stock is where it is

The stock is down 20.4% this year and 26.9% from its December high, though still above its 29 July low of $298.32. From a worried investor's side, three things put it there:

1. **Profit is shrinking.** Third-quarter operating income fell 56%, and GAAP earnings per share fell to $0.22 from $0.39.
2. **The cash went negative.** On 23 July the stock fell 14.5%, its worst day of the year by our data, after second-quarter profit missed estimates, KuCoin and Invezz reported; free cash flow was negative that quarter.
3. **Politics.** It lost 13.8% in the two sessions after Election Day as investors priced in a harder road for Musk's data centers in Texas and his federal contracts.

## Testing the worry

**Shrinking profit: partly lasting.** Some of it is a choice: spending on AI, the Cybercab and Optimus is pushing up costs, and that is an investment, not a decay. But the regulatory-credit income that once padded margins fell 72.9% in a year and is not coming back, and Tesla's China-made sales rose 10.3% in October while China's whole electric-car market grew about 12% ([our story](/news/2026-11-03/teslas-tsla-china-made-sales-rose-10-3-in-october-but-fell-29-from-september/)). The car business is holding volume, not gaining share.

**Cash: lasting, and the worry that survives.** Management said on the October call that capital spending will rise in 2027 and become significant in 2028, for Optimus lines, AI computing, the Cybercab and a chip factory, according to the transcript published by The Motley Fool and Not a Tesla App's summary. A $20 billion year would already use more than the $18.7 billion of operating cash Tesla generated in the 12 months to September. The net cash cushion is real, but it is $9.9 billion, not $100 billion.

**Politics: contained for now.** Nothing in Tesla's car or energy sales changed on Election Day. The risk is concentrated in the Texas data-center plans and the merger talks between special committees at Tesla and SpaceX, and no rule has changed yet.

## The moat

1. **It is the only large electric-car maker that makes money.** Tesla's 3.3% operating margin is thin, but General Motors ($GM) earned 2.3% and Ford ($F) lost 3.8% over the last 12 months, and Rivian ($RIVN) ran at minus 52.9%.
2. **Batteries for the grid.** Energy storage deployments hit a record 13.7 GWh in the third quarter, up about 10%, even though energy revenue fell 16% on tariff-related refunds, Just Auto and Drive Tesla Canada reported.
3. **A live driverless service.** Robotaxi rides in Cybercabs began in Austin on 3 September, its quarterly update said, and Not a Tesla App reported Dallas is next.

## The risks we'd watch

1. **Dilution from Musk's pay.** Shareholders approved an award in November 2025 of up to 423.7 million shares over ten years, tied to goals including an $8.5 trillion market value, a million robotaxis and a million robots, CBS News and Fox Business reported. That is about 10.7% of today's 3.95 billion shares.
2. **Product bets that fail.** Musk said the Cybertruck lost a net $1.9 billion after selling 70,000 against tooling for 125,000 a year, and it is now giving its factory space to the Cybercab. A slow Cybercab ramp would repeat that on a bigger scale.
3. **A capital-spending wave without returns.** If spending doubles toward 2028 while operating cash stays near $18.7 billion, free cash flow turns negative for years, and today's 3.4% return on capital falls further before any payoff.

At $1.42 trillion, Tesla is valued at about 440 times the roughly $3.2 billion it earned over the last 12 months. To be worth that at 25 times earnings, a rich multiple for a mature company, it would need to earn about $57 billion a year, some 18 times today's profit and more than half of today's total revenue. Cars alone at a 3% margin cannot get there; the price is a bet on robotaxis, robots and energy.

## Our verdict

**Business: Mixed. Price: Above its usual price.** Tesla has a strong balance sheet, the best margins among the electric-car makers we cover and a working robotaxi, but its sales have stalled for two years, its returns on capital sit in the bottom tenth of its group, and the spending wave is still ahead. At 393.6 times trailing earnings it trades above its own two-year median of 280.8 times, so the stock is pricing the robotaxi future more fully than usual, not less. What would change our mind is Cybercab scale: paid driverless rides in several cities with a cost per mile Tesla discloses. The next checks are the fourth-quarter delivery report in early January and the Dallas launch Tesla has promised. Tesla has to spend more than it earns to become the company its price already assumes it is.
