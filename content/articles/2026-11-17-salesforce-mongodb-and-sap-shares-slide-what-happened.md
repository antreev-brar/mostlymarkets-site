## What happened
Shares of business-software maker Salesforce ($CRM) **sank 7.2%** to $199.43 by 10:15 a.m. ET on Tuesday, database company MongoDB ($MDB) slid 5.7% and German business-software group SAP ($SAP) fell 4.5%. Most of the industry went with them: Atlassian ($TEAM) was down 5.2%, Workday ($WDAY) 4.9%, Intuit ($INTU) 4.9% and ServiceNow ($NOW) 4.7%.

There is no clear news behind it. None of the three put out results or guidance, and TradingKey's report on the open found no company-specific trigger for Salesforce. The one piece of new evidence is about positioning: Bank of America's November survey of fund managers, published before the open, showed the share overweight tech fell to a net 12% from 37% in October, the biggest one-month cut since 2006, Bloomberg and CNBC reported. Only SAP had news of its own, and it was small: board member Thomas Saueressig will leave in March 2027 to run Ionos, Dow Jones and Bloomberg reported.

## Why it matters
The tech sector in our universe was down about 1.4% on average, so software fell roughly three times as hard. That gap fits the worry that has hung over these stocks since ServiceNow's [worst day on record](/news/2026-10-29/servicenow-now-stock-has-its-worst-day-on-record-after-soft-forecast/) on 29 October: that AI agents will do work companies now pay for by the seat. We set out both sides in our [ServiceNow debate](/news/2026-11-01/bulls-vs-bears-on-servicenow-now-is-ai-breaking-the-software-model/).

That worry has a date this week: Anthropic, whose Claude tools hit software stocks when they launched on 10 November, Benzinga reported, starts trading on Thursday. Our [story on its IPO](/news/2026-11-15/wall-street-is-betting-anthropics-ipo-can-rescue-the-ai-trade-3-numbers-will-decide-it/) sets out what that listing has to prove.

## What the market is telling us
Salesforce's shares moved 5% or more on 13 of the last 252 sessions; if Tuesday's fall holds, it is the stock's worst day since 23 April. It was already down 18.9% this year at Monday's close, 19.3% below its 52-week high, and MongoDB was down 24.5%. SAP had held up better, down 3.7% this year. A sell-off with no company news and a survey showing funds leaving tech says this is about positioning more than business; Salesforce's third-quarter results after the close on Wednesday 2 December are the first hard numbers that can test it.
