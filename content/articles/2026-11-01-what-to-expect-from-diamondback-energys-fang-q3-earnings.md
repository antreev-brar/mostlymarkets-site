Diamondback Energy ($FANG), the Texas oil producer focused on the Permian Basin, reports third-quarter results after the market closes on Monday, 2 November, with a call at 9 a.m. ET on Tuesday. The stock closed Friday **0.8% below** its 52-week high after US crude rose 15.2% in October, its fourth straight monthly gain, on supply risks from the war with Iran.

Last quarter, adjusted EPS was $6.48, up from $2.67 a year earlier and above the $5.96 Zacks expected. Oil output averaged 525,000 barrels a day, the top of its range, and free cash flow was $2.3 billion. But the stock **fell 3.5%** on 4 August, the day after the report. On the call, management dropped its fixed buyback formula in favor of buying shares when it sees value and paying down more debt when oil is high.

## What analysts expect

- **Adjusted EPS:** $4.92 according to Zacks, up 60% from $3.08 a year ago. Compilers disagree more than usual: Barchart's consensus is $4.47 and MarketBeat's $5.72, so the size of any beat depends on the yardstick.
- **Oil output:** Diamondback guided to 517,000 to 527,000 barrels a day for the quarter.

The quarter's oil price was lower than the second quarter's: Exxon Mobil ($XOM) said on 30 October its upstream profit fell from the second quarter on lower crude prices. That helps explain why the consensus is below last quarter's $6.48.

## What to watch

- **Hedges.** Diamondback said in an 8 October filing that it expects a $97 million cash loss on derivative settlements for the quarter. Hedges lose money when oil rises, so a higher fourth-quarter price cuts both ways.
- **The 2027 plan.** Chief Executive Kaes Van't Hof said on the August call there is a path to "low single-digit organic growth" in oil output next year. With crude above $100, any firmer growth plan or bigger capital budget will set the tone.
- **Cash returns.** Chevron ($CVX) beat estimates on 30 October and closed up 1.8%, with refining doing the heavy lifting. Diamondback has no refineries, so its case rests on buybacks and debt cuts from its free cash flow.

## How the stock has done

Diamondback closed Friday at $212.08, **up 41.1%** this year and 14.3% over the past month, just under its 52-week closing high of $213.69 from 4 May. It has moved 5% or more on five sessions in the past year, and $1,000 invested five years ago is worth $1,979 today.
