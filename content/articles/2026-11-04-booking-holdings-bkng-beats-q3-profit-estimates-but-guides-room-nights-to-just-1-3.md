Booking Holdings ($BKNG) reported third-quarter adjusted earnings of **$4.66 a share** after the close on Tuesday, up 17% from a year earlier and ahead of the $4.48 analysts expected, according to Zacks data cited by the AP. Reported revenue fell 1% to $8.89 billion, short of the $9.56 billion Zacks consensus, because of a $900 million charge tied to a Federal Trade Commission case. The shares, which closed up 1.3% at $155.67, were down 0.4% at $155.00 before the open on Wednesday, by 6 a.m. ET in our data.

## What stood out

Demand was better than the headline. Room nights, the number of hotel and rental nights booked, grew **7%**, faster than the 5% of the second quarter, Investing.com reported from the company's slides. The adjusted profit beat also leaned on buybacks: the average share count fell 7.5%, according to 247 Wall St. and GuruFocus.

The revenue miss is an accounting entry for a fight that isn't settled. Booking said it recorded the $900 million revenue reduction and a $100 million accrued loss over Priceline's dealings with Guest Reservations while it negotiates with the FTC, Reuters and Skift reported. "Although a settlement has not been reached," the company said, it booked the charge for accounting purposes; Skift noted the agency could still seek more.

## The outlook

The forecast is the problem. Booking expects fourth-quarter room-night growth of **1% to 3%**, and blamed October's slowdown on disruption to Middle East travel, higher airfares and unrest in some European cities, Reuters and Yahoo Finance reported. Gross bookings are forecast to grow 2% to 4% as reported, a fifth to two-fifths of the third quarter's 10% pace, so the slowdown reaches the dollars spent, not just the nights booked.

## Our take

The quarter was good and the charge is a one-off, but a fall from 7% to as little as 1% growth in one quarter says the conflict in the Middle East and costlier flights are now reaching travel demand. At 14 times forward earnings, against a peer median of 19.5 in our data, the stock already prices in a slowdown; it is down 27.3% this year. The test is the February report: whether room nights recover once October's disruption passes, and what the FTC settlement finally costs.
