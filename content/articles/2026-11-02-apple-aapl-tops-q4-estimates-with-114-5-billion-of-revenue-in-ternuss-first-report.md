Apple ($AAPL) said at 4:30 p.m. ET on Monday that its fiscal fourth-quarter revenue **grew 12% to $114.5 billion**, ahead of the $113.4 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC. Earnings per share rose 14% to $2.10, against the $1.99 forecast, including a 5-cent benefit from tariff refunds, the company said. It was the first report under Chief Executive John Ternus, who succeeded Tim Cook on 1 September.

iPhone revenue rose 17% to $57.6 billion, beating the $56.5 billion LSEG estimate, with only about eight days of iPhone 18 Pro sales in the quarter, CNBC reported. Services grew 10% to $31.6 billion, and iPad sales fell short of estimates. Gross margin was 47.9%, about a point of it from tariff refunds, the release said, inside the 47% to 48% Apple had forecast.

The stock closed down 1.8% at $338.32 before the report. The next number is the December-quarter outlook on the call at 5 p.m. ET, where costlier memory chips are the risk to margins, as our [breakdown of Apple](/news/2026-11-01/apple-aapl-became-wall-streets-ai-haven-is-the-business-worth-the-price/) set out.
