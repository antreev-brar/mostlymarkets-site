Moderna ($MRNA) reported third-quarter revenue of $1.31 billion on Friday morning, **up 29%** from a year earlier and far above the $885 million analysts expected in an LSEG survey, CNBC reported. It earned $0.38 a share against the loss of $1.25 the survey expected, and raised its 2026 revenue growth forecast to 15% to 25% from up to 10%. The stock was up 4.0% at $251.09 as of 10:15 a.m. ET.

## What stood out

The beat came from COVID vaccines sold abroad. Revenue rose mainly on "higher COVID vaccine sales in international markets," delivered under long-term deals with governments such as the UK, Canada and Australia, the release said. Costs fell at the same time: research spending dropped 27% as late-stage infectious-disease trials wound down, which is what turned a $200 million loss a year ago into a $162 million profit.

Chief Executive Stéphane Bancel pointed to the pipeline: the US approval of mFLUSIVA, its flu vaccine, and positive late-stage results for intismeran, its personalized cancer vaccine, in melanoma. The flu shot will get only a limited US launch this season because it was approved after the annual contracting cycle, the release said, so it adds little to 2026 sales.

## The outlook

Moderna now expects 2026 revenue growth of **15% to 25%** and cut its research budget to about $2.7 billion from $2.9 billion. The jump in its year-end cash forecast is mostly borrowed money: cash rose to $8.0 billion in September largely on $2.6 billion raised from convertible notes, net of a $950 million litigation settlement payment, the release said.

## Our take

A good quarter, but not the reason the stock was up 718.8% this year before Friday; Barron's tied that rally to the cancer-vaccine program, and noted Friday's results show COVID still pays the bills. The new forecast is also less generous than it looks: with $1.85 billion booked in the first nine months against $1.94 billion for all of 2025, it implies fourth-quarter revenue of about **$0.39 billion to $0.58 billion**, below the $678 million of a year earlier, by our arithmetic from its filings. The next test is the further intismeran readouts the company says are coming, which matter more to a $100 billion valuation than any vaccine season.
