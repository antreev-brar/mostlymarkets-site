On Friday, the four stocks in this list closed between **46% and 50% below** their 52-week highs, after a year in which two separate fears hit them: that AI tools will take customers from software companies, and that new state rules after the 3 November midterms will slow the building of AI data centers ([our narrative on that](/news/2026-11-05/wall-street-is-betting-the-midterms-will-slow-the-ai-buildout-3-numbers-will-decide-it/)). A falling price makes a good business cheaper, but it can also be the first sign that the business is getting worse.

Our screen ranked profitable companies in our coverage by how far they closed below their 52-week high on Friday 13 November, from our price files, and left out stocks we listed in the last 14 days. We then checked the leaders on SEC-filing numbers: growth over five years and two, margins, free cash flow, returns on capital and debt, each against peers.

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Intuit ($INTU)
**49.5% below its 52-week high of $676.55 from 26 December 2025.** Intuit sells TurboTax tax software and QuickBooks accounting software for small businesses.

1. It throws off cash: free cash flow was **40.4% of revenue** in the 12 months to July 2026, in the top quarter of the 32 software companies we cover, against a median of 28.8%.
2. It has compounded: revenue grew 17.4% a year over five fiscal years, above the 15.9% peer median, and earnings per share 16.8% a year.
3. The bad news is known: with its fourth-quarter results in late August it guided to 9% to 10% revenue growth in fiscal 2027, down from 14%, and said pricing was pushing do-it-yourself TurboTax customers away, Stocktwits and CFO Dive reported. The stock fell about 12% on the report.

**The price:** 14.4 times expected earnings against a software median of 26.6 times, and a trailing P/E of 20.7 against its two-year median of 48.4.

**What would change our mind:** Intuit reports its fiscal first quarter on Thursday 19 November. A cut to the 9% to 10% growth forecast would say fiscal 2027 isn't the bottom, which is the evidence JPMorgan and BofA said they were waiting for.

### 2. Vertiv ($VRT)
**46.3% below its 52-week high of $376.23 from 14 May 2026.** Vertiv sells the power and cooling equipment that keeps data-center servers running.

1. Demand is still rising: third-quarter sales jumped **61%** from a year earlier to $4.31 billion and organic orders more than doubled, the company said on 28 October, raising its 2026 sales forecast by $650 million.
2. It earns a lot on what it invests: return on capital of about 42% is in the top tenth of the 89 industrial stocks we cover, against a median of 13.4%.
3. It is getting better, not just bigger: its operating margin is up 13 points over five years, to 20.1% in the 12 months to September 2026.

**The price:** 25.0 times expected earnings, a little above the 21.7 times industrial median, but a trailing P/E of 37.8 against its own two-year median of 70.5.

**What would change our mind:** orders. The stock fell 14.0% on 5 November after Texas's governor-elect promised to keep new data-center approvals paused; a fall in organic orders in its next report would show the pause reaching its order book.

### 3. Comfort Systems USA ($FIX)
**46.2% below its 52-week high of $2,066.51 from 22 June 2026.** Comfort Systems installs the heating, cooling, plumbing and electrical systems inside factories, hospitals and data centers.

1. Its backlog of signed work hit a record **$17.8 billion** at the end of September, up from $9.4 billion a year earlier, its release said.
2. The growth is long-running: revenue rose 26.1% a year over five years and earnings per share 47.8% a year, both in the top tenth of industrials.
3. It beat by a distance: third-quarter earnings of $13.91 a share topped the $12.71 Zacks consensus, and operating cash flow of $1.01 billion was almost double the $553 million of a year earlier.

**The price:** 19.4 times expected earnings, below the 21.7 times industrial median, and a trailing P/E of 24.0 against its two-year median of 39.0.

**What could go wrong:** technology customers were 61% of revenue in the third quarter, up from 46% a year earlier, Zacks reported. A pause in data-center building would hit its fastest-growing work first.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Oracle ($ORCL)
**49.5% below its 52-week high of $248.15 from 1 June 2026.** Oracle sells databases and business software and rents out AI computing power from its own data centers.

1. It spends more than it makes: free cash flow was **negative $28.7 billion** in the 12 months to August 2026, or minus 40% of revenue, the worst among the 32 software companies we cover.
2. Owners are paying for the build: Oracle sold $20 billion of new shares in its fiscal first quarter and carries about $125 billion of debt, ERP Today and TIKR reported, and its diluted share count is up 5.2% in two years.
3. Profitability is going the wrong way: operating margin fell 7 points over five years, the steepest drop among its peers, even as its backlog of signed contracts reached $664 billion.

**The price:** 14.7 times expected earnings against a software median of 26.6, which looks cheap only if the backlog turns into cash on time.

**What would change our mind:** a date for positive free cash flow, which Oracle hasn't given, and a lower spending plan when it reports its fiscal second quarter in December.
