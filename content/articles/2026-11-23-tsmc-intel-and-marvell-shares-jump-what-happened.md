## What happened
Shares of contract chipmaker Taiwan Semiconductor ($TSM), known as TSMC, **rose 15.9%** by 10:15 a.m. ET on Monday, with Intel ($INTC) up 14.4% and networking-chip maker Marvell Technology ($MRVL) up 20.5%. The trigger was Nvidia's ($NVDA) decision before the open to reinstate Jensen Huang as chief executive, 11 days after he resigned, which Bloomberg and CNBC reported set off a rebound across AI-linked stocks ([our story](/news/2026-11-23/nvidia-nvda-shares-soar-29-before-the-open-after-it-reinstates-jensen-huang/)).

The smaller AI-networking names moved even more: Credo Technology ($CRDO) was up 24.0% and Astera Labs ($ALAB) 17.4% at 10:15 a.m. ET. No chipmaker in the group put out news of its own; Bloomberg, CNN and Reuters tied the move to Huang's return, which came after a week in which the S&P 500 and the Dow closed in a correction, more than 10% below their records.

## Why it matters
Huang's resignation on 12 November had become the face of last week's AI sell-off, and every chip supplier on Nvidia's bill of materials fell with it. TSMC makes Nvidia's processors, Marvell and Credo sell the links that wire them together, and Intel had rallied this year on hopes of outside customers for its factories. Taking the leadership question off the table lifts them all at once.

It doesn't settle the rest. TSMC still has to work through the US rule that halted some of its shipments to Chinese customers last week ([our brief](/news/2026-11-19/tsmc-tsm-and-samsung-halt-some-chip-shipments-to-chinese-customers-under-new-us-curbs/)), and the inquiries into Nvidia's China sales are open ([our analysis](/news/2026-11-23/nvidias-crisis-has-moved-from-its-boss-to-its-books-what-the-evidence-says/)).

## What the market is telling us
TSMC's shares moved 5% or more on only 21 of the last 252 sessions, and its biggest daily move in that year was a 10.8% fall on 5 November, so a gain near 16% would be its largest in at least a year. Even at $369.35 it would sit about 24% below its 5 October closing high of $485.80. Intel moves far more often, 5% or more on 72 of 252 sessions, and closed Friday up 39.0% this year but 63.6% below its June high. We read Monday as a relief rally priced off one headline: the next real test of chip demand is Dell's ($DELL) report on Tuesday, the first big AI-server customer to speak since the sell-off.
