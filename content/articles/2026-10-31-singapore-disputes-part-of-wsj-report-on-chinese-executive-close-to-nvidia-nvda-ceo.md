Singapore's Home Affairs Ministry said on Saturday there had been no investigation of Alice Huang's ties to China, contradicting a Wall Street Journal report on Friday that Singapore officials had conducted one. The Journal called her a Chinese executive close to Nvidia ($NVDA) Chief Executive Jensen Huang and suspected of giving China information.

On Thursday, The New York Times reported that US intelligence agencies had concluded she was gathering information for Beijing, that President Trump was briefed about a year ago but didn't act, and that officials initially didn't believe Jensen Huang knew. She became a senior executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore cloud customer of Nvidia.

Nvidia called Jensen Huang's dealings with her routine and said "the diversion allegations against this regional cloud were thoroughly investigated and debunked last year." A US inquiry into whether Megaspeed helped China evade chip curbs is now with the Justice Department, the Times reported; Singapore's police say theirs continues.

Senator Elizabeth Warren wrote on X on Thursday that Jensen Huang and Nvidia's board "owe Congress answers."

Nvidia slid 4.2% on Friday, less than the chip index's 7.2% drop ([our story](/news/2026-10-31/the-chip-index-fell-7-2-in-a-day-where-did-the-damage-land/)).

We see a policy risk more than a sales risk. Nvidia's **$108.0 billion** forecast for the quarter that ended 25 October left out China's data centers but counted clouds elsewhere. Its August 10-Q says the Remote Access Security Act, a bill to bar cloud services to companies with a parent in China, could have a material impact.

Nvidia reports third-quarter results on Tuesday 17 November; its call starts at 5 p.m. ET.
