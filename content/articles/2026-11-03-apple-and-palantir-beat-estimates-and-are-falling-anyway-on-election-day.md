Apple and Palantir both beat Wall Street's forecasts after the close on Monday, and both are falling this morning. By 6:15 a.m. ET, Apple ($AAPL) was **down 4.1%** before the open, after guiding its holiday-quarter gross margin below estimates as memory chips get dearer, and Palantir ($PLTR) was down 7.6%, as investors looked past 96% sales growth to a quarter of slow new deals.

It caps a session in which the market's leaders kept leaking. Oil sank on Iran diplomacy and lifted the Dow, but a reversal in chip and AI shares took the Nasdaq Composite down 1.07%, and Oracle ($ORCL) sold its new shares overnight below Monday's close. Americans vote in the midterms today, and AMD ($AMD) reports after the close.

## Yesterday in seven takes

### Apple's record quarter can't outrun its memory bill

Revenue rose 12% to $114.5 billion, ahead of the $113.4 billion expected, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC, and iPhone sales grew 17%. But Apple expects memory prices to keep rising after December, the Seoul Economic Daily and Investing.com reported, and the stock had closed at a record on Thursday ([our recap](/news/2026-11-02/apple-aapl-beats-q4-estimates-but-memory-costs-squeeze-its-margin-forecast/)).

### Palantir's sales grew 96%; its new contracts grew 6%

```stat
[{"value": "+96%", "label": "Palantir's Q3 revenue growth", "note": "To $2.31 billion, year on year", "tone": "up"},
 {"value": "+6%", "label": "Total contract value signed in Q3", "note": "$2.92 billion, year on year"},
```

Palantir raised its 2026 revenue forecast to about $8.53 billion, but the total value of contracts it signed in the quarter grew just 6% from a year earlier, and US commercial bookings 11%, The Motley Fool and FourWeekMBA reported. Bookings are the pipeline for next year's sales, which is why a stock priced for growth sold off on the slowdown ([our recap](/news/2026-11-02/palantir-pltr-beats-q3-estimates-as-us-commercial-sales-overtake-government/)).
