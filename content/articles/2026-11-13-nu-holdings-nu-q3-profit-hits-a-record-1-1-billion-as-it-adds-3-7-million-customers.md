Nu Holdings ($NU), the Brazilian digital bank, reported record third-quarter net income of **$1.1 billion** after the close on Thursday, up 31% year on year on a currency-neutral basis, its release shows. Earnings of 22 cents a share matched the 22-cent Zacks consensus cited by FXStreet, and Benzinga also called them in line, though Investing.com's consensus was a cent higher. The shares closed down 4.0% at $11.83 before the report and were up 3.8% at $12.28 before Friday's open.

## What stood out
Nu kept adding customers at scale and getting more efficient doing it. It gained **3.7 million** in the quarter to reach 143 million, nearly 120 million of them in Brazil, 16.6 million in Mexico and 6.1 million in Colombia, the release and MarketBeat's call summary show. Its efficiency ratio, costs as a share of revenue where lower is better, improved to 19.3% from 20.3%.

The balance sheet grew alongside. The credit portfolio reached $41.6 billion and deposits **rose 19%** to $47.5 billion, while funding costs fell, the release shows. Nu also launched Nu US and Nu Global in the quarter; the company said Nu US reached customers in all 50 states within weeks.

## The outlook
Nu doesn't give quarterly profit guidance. Outlets disagree on whether it raised or trimmed its full-year efficiency target, so we leave that out until the filing settles it. The next question is how fast the US and global launches spend money before they earn it.

## Our take
Profit grew 31% while costs fell as a share of revenue, so the Brazilian engine is still compounding at 143 million customers. The stock is down 29.3% this year and trades at 12.2 times forward earnings against an 11.0x median for 15 banks in our data, so it no longer carries much of a premium. The risk to watch is the cost of the new markets, which shows up first in the efficiency ratio next quarter.
