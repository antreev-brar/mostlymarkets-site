On Monday 2 November, Apple ($AAPL) guided its holiday-quarter gross margin to **45% to 46%**, down from 47.9% in the quarter just ended, and blamed mostly one thing: memory chips. The story Wall Street is now telling is that AI data centers' hunger for memory has turned the memory makers' record profits into a bill for everyone who builds phones and PCs, and that the bill has now reached even the strongest buyer. Apple's then-chief executive Tim Cook named it in July; Intel's ($INTC) chief executive, Lip-Bu Tan, repeated it on 29 October.

The stakes: Apple was **down 3.8%** before the open at 7:14 a.m. ET, Dell ($DELL) and HP ($HPQ) fell 10.8% and 9.6% on Friday 30 October after Intel's warning, and Micron ($MU), which sells the memory, is up 192.1% this year.

- **What's happening:** Memory now costs phone makers about 350% more than a year ago, by IDC's estimate; Apple guided its margin to 45%-46%, Samsung's phone arm lost money for a second quarter, and phone shipments fell a record 22%.
- **Why it matters:** Every dollar of memory price is nearly pure profit for Micron, SK hynix and Samsung's chip arm, and a cost for Apple, HP, Dell and the Android brands, who must eat it or raise prices and sell fewer devices.
- **Our read:** The evidence supports it, but the squeeze is sorting winners from losers: big brands are passing it on and gaining share, while the budget end shrinks. The weak link is how long prices keep rising.

## The story in one sentence

> "We're in what I would characterize as a hundred year flood on the memory pricing." Tim Cook, then Apple's chief executive, on its earnings call on 30 July 2026

The chain runs like this: memory makers steer their wafers to AI servers, so less is left for phones and PCs, so contract prices jump, and because a memory chip costs about the same to make whatever it sells for, nearly every extra dollar lands as the makers' profit. Device makers then choose between thinner margins and higher prices, and higher prices mean fewer phones and laptops sold. That is why the same quarter brought Samsung Electronics a record operating profit of 107.4 trillion won and a loss in its own phone business.

## The evidence for it

### 1. Apple, the strongest buyer, is guiding its margin lower

Chief Financial Officer Kevan Parekh's 45% to 46% guide sits below the 46.3% average estimate, CNBC reported, and he tied it mainly to memory. Apple also said it expects memory prices to keep rising after December, with less help from parts it bought earlier at lower prices, the Seoul Economic Daily and Investing.com's call transcript reported.

### 2. Samsung makes memory, and its phone arm still loses money

Samsung's mobile and network division lost about **600 billion won** (about $450 million) at the operating level in the third quarter, after its first-ever loss of about 700 billion won in the second, Money Today and Notebookcheck reported. A year earlier the unit made a profit, the Korea JoongAng Daily reported. The biggest phone maker in IDC's third-quarter count is losing money on phones while its chip arm sets records on the memory it sells to rivals.

### 3. Phones just had their worst quarter in IDC's records

Shipments fell **22%** year on year to 255.3 million in the third quarter, from 327.3 million, the biggest drop in IDC's data, the tracker said on 15 October, reported by The Register and I-Connect007. Omdia counted a 20% fall and the lowest third-quarter total since 2013, Light Reading reported. IDC put memory costs for phone makers about 350% above a year earlier.

### 4. In PCs, memory is about a third of the cost

HP Chief Financial Officer Karen Parkhill said on 25 February that memory would make up roughly **35%** of the parts cost of its PCs this year, up from 15% to 18% the quarter before, The Register and PC Gamer reported. HP then cut its full-year adjusted EPS forecast in May to $2.90 to $3.10, from $2.90 to $3.20, its release shows.

Intel's Tan pointed on 29 October to "the unprecedented memory crunch and the resulting softness in PC demand," NBC News reported, and Benzinga reported that he said the shortage was pushing PC makers to cut inventories. TrendForce expects notebook shipments to fall again in 2027 as component costs rise, it said on 8 October, a forecast Evertiq also reported.

### 5. The price rises haven't stopped

TrendForce forecast on 30 September that contract prices for conventional DRAM would rise another **10% to 15%** this quarter, after an estimated 93% to 98% in the first. Each rise now comes on a far higher base, so it still adds real dollars to every phone and laptop ([our story on the slowdown](/news/2026-10-31/memory-chip-prices-are-still-rising-just-more-slowly/)).

## The case against it

The loudest doubter on duration is Kye-hyun Kyung, a senior adviser and former head of Samsung's chip division, who said at a National Academy of Engineering of Korea forum in May that new Chinese capacity could start pushing memory prices down in the second half of 2027 or early 2028, PC Gamer and Notebookcheck reported. Three counter-points hold up to the same standard:

1. **The big brands are pricing it through.** Apple's iPhone revenue rose 17% last quarter and it guided December-quarter sales up 7% to 10%. In IDC's count, Samsung and Apple gained share even as Xiaomi, OPPO and vivo shipped 29% to 35% fewer phones, Fonearena and I-Connect007 reported. The squeeze lands hardest on cheap Android phones, not on the premium end.
2. **The increases are shrinking.** TrendForce's quarterly DRAM rise has narrowed three quarters running, and IDC said phone makers' memory costs rose by a low double-digit percentage on the quarter, against roughly 50% to 100% in earlier quarters.
3. **The market isn't pricing a clean transfer.** From 28 October to Monday's close, Micron fell 15.2% and Sandisk ($SNDK) 23.5%, more than Apple's 1.6% and HP's 12.4%, by our prices. Investors are selling the memory makers on fears that profits have peaked, which is the opposite of what a long squeeze implies.

The first is the strongest: a cost that the biggest brands can pass on is a mass-market problem, not an industry-wide margin collapse.

## What would change minds

- **Qualcomm ($QCOM) reports after the close on Wednesday 4 November.** A cut to its phone-chip outlook tied to customers building fewer handsets would show the squeeze spreading from phone makers to their suppliers.
- **HP's fiscal fourth-quarter report, expected in late November**, brings its first outlook for fiscal 2027. A forecast for wider PC margins would be the first sign the cost peak has passed.
- **TrendForce's first-quarter 2027 DRAM forecast, due around the turn of the year.** A flat or falling call would break the story; another double-digit rise would extend it into Apple's March quarter.

## Our read

**The evidence supports it.** Apple's guide, Samsung's phone losses, HP's margin and a record fall in phone shipments all point the same way, and memory prices are still rising this quarter. The weakest link is duration: the increases are shrinking and the biggest brands are passing costs on, so the damage is concentrated on budget phones and PCs. The one number we'll watch is TrendForce's first-quarter 2027 DRAM forecast; our earlier pieces track the same cycle from the sellers' side ([the 2027 sell-out](/news/2026-10-31/ai-memory-for-2027-is-sold-out-before-its-made-what-the-evidence-says/)) and [Apple's quarter](/news/2026-11-02/apple-aapl-beats-q4-estimates-but-memory-costs-squeeze-its-margin-forecast/).
