California Attorney General Rob Bonta said on Saturday that his investigation of Nvidia ($NVDA) covers possible criminal violations of state law, not only civil ones, if the company or former Chief Executive Jensen Huang gave investors and the public false information about chip sales to China. He told Bloomberg News, as Reuters and Seeking Alpha reported, that the probe is at an early stage and has reached no conclusions, and that his office could seek a court order and claw back profits from any illegal sales.

It raises the stakes on the [subpoena Bonta served on Thursday](/news/2026-11-19/california-subpoenas-nvidia-nvda-over-china-chips-and-what-investors-were-told/), which sits alongside the SEC's [examination of Nvidia's accounting](/news/2026-11-20/sec-probe-of-nvidia-nvda-is-examining-its-accounting-reuters-reports/). A criminal case would add penalties on top of any civil settlement, and it keeps a legal overhang on a stock that **fell 14.0%** on Friday, its worst day since January 2025, by our price data.

No charge has been filed. What comes next is Nvidia's response to the subpoena, which Bonta has said may be followed by further subpoenas.
