## What happened

Shares of laser maker Lumentum ($LITE) **sank 11.0%** to $748.39 as of 10 a.m. ET on Monday, and its buyer, Marvell Technology ($MRVL), fell 6.4% to $183.75, hours after the two signed an all-stock merger the companies valued at about $115 billion including debt. A takeover target usually rises on the day its deal is signed. Lumentum was up 3.0% before the open at 7:30 a.m. ET, down 4.7% by 9:30 a.m., and fell further once regular trading began; CBS News and 24/7 Wall St. also reported both stocks lower on the news.

The cause is in the terms. Lumentum holders get 4.5 Marvell shares each, not cash, so the offer is only worth what Marvell stock is worth, the companies said in their release. At Marvell's 28 October close, the day Bloomberg first reported the talks, that came to $1,132 a share. At 10 a.m. on Monday it came to **$826.88**, and Lumentum traded 9.5% below even that.

## Why it matters

Investors have now marked down the deal twice. Marvell fell 22% in the two sessions after the talks leaked, as holders balked at issuing so many new shares, MarketScreener and Yahoo Finance reported; Monday's drop takes it to 27% below its 28 October close. Every dollar Marvell loses now takes $4.50 off what Lumentum holders will receive.

The gap between Lumentum's price and the offer is the market's price for waiting. The deal needs shareholder and regulatory approval and isn't expected to close until the first half of 2028, the release said, which is a long time to carry the risk that it falls apart. Merger-arbitrage funds usually lock in such a gap by buying the target and selling the buyer short, which adds selling pressure on Marvell for as long as the gap stays wide. Rival Coherent ($COHR), which would face a bigger combined competitor in AI-networking optics, was up 0.8% at 10 a.m.

## What the market is telling us

Lumentum's shares moved 5% or more on 114 of the last 252 sessions, so big swings are routine for it. The last drop this big came on 22 October 2026, when the stock fell 11.5%. Through Friday the stock was up 128.1% this year, and $1,000 invested in it five years ago would have been worth $10,180.

We read the 9.5% gap as doubt about the 2028 finish line more than about the strategy: Lumentum holders are now, in effect, long Marvell at a discount. The next test is the merger filing, which will show how much each company's holders are told the deal adds to earnings.
