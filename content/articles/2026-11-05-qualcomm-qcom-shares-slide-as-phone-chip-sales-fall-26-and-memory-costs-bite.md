Qualcomm ($QCOM) reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $10.32 billion on Wednesday, down 8% from a year earlier, as sales of its smartphone chips fell 26% to $5.14 billion, short of the $5.25 billion analysts expected, according to Visible Alpha data cited by Reuters. Adjusted earnings fell 28% to $2.17 a share. The stock **slid 9.5%** on Thursday to $156.75, in a market already selling chips on midterm fears.

## What stood out

The phone business is shrinking while the rest grows. Handset chips fell 26% in the quarter and 13% for the fiscal year, while automotive rose 89% and internet-of-things chips 4%, according to the results release. Qualcomm also said it faces "a broad-based increase in input costs across wafer fabrication, assembly, test, advanced packaging, memory and other materials," and that price rises to cover them will only show fully in its chip margins in the second half of fiscal 2027.

The squeeze shows in profit: the chip unit's pre-tax margin fell to 24% from 29% a year earlier. That is the bill we described in our [narrative on memory costs](/news/2026-11-03/memorys-boom-has-become-the-device-makers-bill-what-the-evidence-says/): phone makers pay more for memory and buy fewer chips around it.

## The outlook

The revenue forecast was better than feared: its $10.8 billion midpoint sits above the $10.48 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters. Profit was the problem. The $2.25 adjusted EPS midpoint is just below the $2.27 analysts expected, Quartz and CNBC reported, and Qualcomm expects handset chip sales to fall by a mid-single-digit percentage from this quarter, with Apple's orders still shrinking.

Chief Executive Cristiano Amon pointed to the rest of the business: "Fiscal 2027 marks the start of our non-handset revenue inflection," he said in the release, reiterating a $5 billion target for data-center revenue in fiscal 2027.

## Our take

The cars and data-center story is real, but it is still smaller than the phone business that is losing ground to memory costs and shrinking Apple orders. At 17.1 times forward earnings, below the chipmaker median of 19.3 times in our data, the stock prices in some of that. The next test is whether March-quarter handset sales stabilize as the price rises come through.
