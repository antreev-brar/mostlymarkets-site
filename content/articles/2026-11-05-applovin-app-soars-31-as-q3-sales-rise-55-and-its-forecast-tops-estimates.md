AppLovin ($APP), whose software places ads inside mobile games and apps, reported third-quarter revenue of $2.19 billion on Wednesday, up 55% from a year earlier and ahead of the $2.07 billion analysts expected, according to Bloomberg estimates cited by Yahoo Finance. Diluted earnings rose to $4.30 a share from $2.45, also ahead of estimates, Yahoo Finance and Benzinga reported. The stock **soared 31.0%** on Thursday to $356.10, its best day since 7 November 2024, on a day the Nasdaq fell 5.5% ([our market wrap](/news/2026-11-05/stock-market-today-nasdaq-sinks-5-5-into-a-correction-on-midterm-ai-fears/)). It was one of the few big stocks to rise in a sell-off aimed at AI hardware, since AppLovin sells software and ads, not data-center parts.

## What stood out

The margins are what set AppLovin apart. Net income was **66%** of revenue and adjusted EBITDA, a measure of operating profit before non-cash costs, 84%, the release showed. In our data its operating margin over the past 12 months, 77.8%, is the highest of the 19 communication-services companies we can rank, against a median of 18.4%.

Almost all of that cash goes back to shareholders, which is why the share count keeps shrinking: diluted shares fell 3.9% over two years in our data. AppLovin spent $1.4 billion buying back and withholding 4.1 million shares in the quarter, about all of its $1.4 billion of free cash flow, and its board added $3.2 billion to the buyback.

## The outlook

AppLovin expects fourth-quarter revenue of **$2.365 billion to $2.395 billion**, up about 43% to 44% from a year earlier, with an adjusted EBITDA margin of 84% to 85%. That was above the roughly $2.25 billion analysts expected, Yahoo Finance and Benzinga reported. That means growth slows from 55% to the low 40s, but the dollar gain is larger: the midpoint, $2.38 billion, would be about $720 million more than the $1.66 billion it booked a year earlier, in our filings data.

## Our take

This was a clean beat with a forecast to match, and the stock had room to react: even after rising 45.0% in two days, it is down 47.1% this year and 51.5% below its December 2025 high. At 20.1 times forward earnings in our data, it trades above the sector median of 15.6 times but far below its own two-year median trailing multiple of 62.8 times. The question for the next quarter is whether growth holds in the 40s once the comparison gets tougher.
