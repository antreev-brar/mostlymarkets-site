J.M. Smucker ($SJM) raised its full-year adjusted earnings forecast to **$10.80 to $11.20 a share** on Tuesday morning, from $10.50 to $11.00, after second-quarter results that Reuters said beat estimates on both revenue and profit. Adjusted EPS rose 52% to $3.20 in the three months to October, including a 22-cent benefit from tariff refunds, while net sales fell 2% to $2.3 billion, the company's release said.

The raise is mostly refunds. The 30-cent lift at the midpoint is close to the 22 cents of refunds booked this quarter, and Smucker reaffirmed its sales and free cash flow guidance, so the underlying outlook barely moved. That helps explain the reaction: the shares **slipped 1.1%** to $131.30 before the open, as of 7:21 a.m. ET, after a 35.8% gain this year left them 2.0% below their 52-week closing high.

What isn't known yet is how much of the business grows without the refunds: the sales guidance it reaffirmed, from August, still calls for net sales to fall 1% to 2% this fiscal year.
