Ross Stores ($ROST) reported third-quarter earnings of **$1.91 a share** after the close on Thursday, above the $1.82 Zacks consensus we cited in [our preview](/news/2026-11-18/what-to-expect-from-thursdays-earnings-ross-stores-keysight-and-williams-sonoma/) and above its own $1.75 to $1.83 guide. Sales rose 12% to $6.26 billion, in line with estimates, Reuters reported. But Ross guided fourth-quarter earnings to $2.16 to $2.25, below the $2.26 analysts expected, and the shares **sank 9.0% to $200.01** after hours as of 4:15 p.m. ET, after closing at $219.88.

## What stood out

Shoppers came through the door. Comparable-store sales, which strip out new stores, **rose 8%** on top of a 7% gain a year earlier, "primarily driven by customer traffic," Ross said in its release. Chief Executive Jim Conroy credited marketing that is "attract[ing] more customers" and said results "exceeded our expectations."

Unlike last quarter, this beat was clean. The second quarter's $2.66 a share included about 60 cents of refunded tariffs, but the third quarter had none; the operating margin still rose 0.7 point to 12.3%. Ross opened 51 stores in the quarter, completing its plan of 115 for the year, and bought back $319 million of stock.

## The outlook

Ross kept its fourth-quarter comparable-sales forecast at 4% to 5%, against a 9% gain a year earlier, and put earnings at $2.16 to $2.25, growth of 8% to 13%. The $2.21 midpoint is short of the $2.26 consensus, Investing.com and MarketBeat reported. It raised its full-year earnings forecast to $8.75 to $8.84 a share, including the 60-cent tariff refund.

Keeping the holiday comparable-sales range unchanged after an 8% quarter tells investors management expects growth to roughly halve over Thanksgiving and Christmas, against a tougher comparison.

## Our take

The quarter was strong: traffic-led sales growth, a wider margin, and no refund help. The problem is the price. Ross was up 22.1% this year at Thursday's close and traded at 25.6 times forward earnings against a median of 19.7 times for consumer peers in our data, and above its own trailing median since 2024, so a cautious holiday guide was enough to knock 9% off. If the comparable sales beat the 4% to 5% guide the way the third quarter beat its own, the guide will look conservative; the fourth-quarter report will tell.
