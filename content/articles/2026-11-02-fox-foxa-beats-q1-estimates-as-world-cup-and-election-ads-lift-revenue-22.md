Fox Corporation ($FOXA) reported on Monday morning that revenue for its fiscal first quarter, the three months to September, **rose 22%** from a year earlier to $4.57 billion, above the $4.23 billion four analysts surveyed by Zacks expected, the Associated Press reported. Adjusted earnings of $2.54 a share beat the Zacks consensus of $1.99, up from $1.51 a year ago. The stock was up 3.0% at $65.15 as of 10:15 a.m. ET.

## What stood out

Advertising did the work. Ad revenue **jumped 47%**, as the end of the FIFA Men's World Cup, which Fox broadcast in the US, and early spending on the midterm elections both landed in the quarter, the company said and Benzinga reported. Distribution revenue, the fees cable and streaming bundles pay to carry Fox News and the Fox network, grew a steadier 9% to $2.08 billion.

Because ad dollars from big events cost little extra to sell, most of the gain dropped to profit: adjusted EBITDA, a measure of operating profit before depreciation, rose 54% to $1.64 billion, GuruFocus and Investing.com reported from the release.

## The outlook

Fox doesn't give quarterly revenue guidance. Its finance chief said on the call that the company expects to resume share buybacks in the current quarter, Investing.com reported, which matters for a stock trading at 10.7 times expected earnings, according to our quote data.

## The long view

This quarter flatters a business that has grown slowly. Over five fiscal years to June, Fox's revenue grew **5.8% a year**, against 13.6% for the median communication-services company in our coverage, and its earnings per share grew just 1.2% a year, according to its SEC filings. Event years such as this one, with a World Cup and an election, lift the numbers, and the years in between give some of it back.

The price reflects that. At 11.0 times expected earnings, Fox trades below the 15.1 times median of its peers in our data, with net debt of only $2.4 billion at the end of June.

## Our take

A good quarter, but a borrowed one: the World Cup is over, and political ads stop after the 3 November midterms, so the 47% ad growth won't repeat next year. The steadier signal is distribution's 9% rise, which says Fox News still commands higher carriage fees as the cable bundle shrinks. Through Friday the stock was down 13.4% this year, which suggests investors had already priced in this year's events as one-offs.
