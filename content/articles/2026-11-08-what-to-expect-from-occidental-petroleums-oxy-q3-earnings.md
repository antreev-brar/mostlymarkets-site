Occidental Petroleum ($OXY), the Permian Basin oil producer, reports third-quarter results after the close on Monday, 9 November, with its call at 1 p.m. ET on Tuesday, Zacks and MarketBeat reported. It reports a week after US crude, about $103 a barrel on 30 October, began sliding as the US and Iran pursued diplomacy, CNBC reported ([our oil narrative](/news/2026-10-31/the-story-wall-street-is-telling-about-100-oil-and-the-fed/)).

Last quarter, on 5 August, Occidental earned $2.40 a share on an adjusted basis from continuing operations, against the $1.92 Zacks consensus, as output of 1.433 million barrels of oil equivalent a day beat the top of its guidance, its release showed. It raised its quarterly dividend to $0.28 and cut debt by $1.9 billion, and the stock rose 4.1% the next day, in our data.

## What analysts expect

The Zacks consensus is **$1.43 a share** on an adjusted basis, up from $0.64 a year earlier, on revenue of $6.73 billion, Zacks reported via TradingView and Yahoo Finance; MarketBeat's consensus is $1.45 on $6.80 billion. Zacks expects strong US output and lower interest costs to help, and its midstream and marketing arm to earn less as the gap between natural gas prices at the Waha hub in West Texas and the Gulf Coast narrowed.

## What to watch

- **Production.** Occidental guided to 1.40 million to 1.44 million barrels of oil equivalent a day for the quarter; a beat would be its second in a row.
- **The fourth-quarter outlook.** With US crude at $90.34 on Friday, in our data, investors will want to know how much of the third quarter's higher prices carries into the spending and buyback plan.
- **Debt.** Principal debt stood at $11.8 billion after the second quarter's $1.9 billion cut, its release said; further cuts free cash for shareholders.

## How the stock has done

Occidental **fell 5.1%** on 2 November as oil slid, and closed Friday at $56.38, down 11.9% from the previous Friday and 6.5% over the past month in our data. It is still up 37.1% this year, and 14.9% below its closing high of $66.24 from 30 March. $1,000 invested five years ago would be worth $1,687 today.
