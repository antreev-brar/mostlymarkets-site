On Monday, chip stocks were falling again before the open, a day before Nvidia ($NVDA) reports its first quarter since Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive: Micron ($MU) was **down 5.3%** in premarket trading as of 7:15 a.m. ET, Western Digital ($WDC) 5.2% and AMD ($AMD) 5.2%, in our data, as Asian chipmakers slid overnight ([our daily](/daily/2026-11-16/)). Selling that broad stops telling good suppliers from expensive ones.

Our screen ranked 16 chip, networking, power and data-center companies by revenue growth over their latest 12 months, from SEC filings, leaving out stocks we listed in the last 14 days; the median grew 32.8%. We judged the fastest on what that growth leaves the owner: free cash flow, returns on capital and five-year earnings growth, then the price.

```picks
like: MU, WDC, ADI

```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Micron Technology ($MU)
**Revenue growth: 256.3% in the 12 months to 3 September 2026.** Micron is one of three companies that make most of the world's DRAM, the working memory beside every AI processor.

1. It has compounded through a cycle, not just this year: revenue grew **36.9% a year** over five fiscal years, in the top tenth of the 25 chipmakers we cover, against a median of 14.2%.
2. The growth is cash: free cash flow was 44.3% of revenue over the last 12 months, the highest of its peers, and it holds $35.6 billion more cash than debt.

**The price:** 4.4 times expected earnings against a chipmaker median of 22.3 times, and a trailing P/E of 10.5 against its own median of 26.2 since late 2024. At 35.9% below its June high, the price already assumes profits fall a long way.

**What would change our mind:** a falling forecast. Sales halved in fiscal 2023, and Micron has guided to about 13% growth this quarter after 74% in the spring ([our breakdown](/news/2026-10-31/micron-mu-earns-87-gross-margins-is-this-the-top/)). A next-quarter guide below this quarter's $61.5 billion would mean the cycle has turned.

### 2. Western Digital ($WDC)
**Revenue growth: 43.0% in the 12 months to 2 October 2026.** Western Digital makes hard drives, and 88% of its sales now go to cloud companies that store the data AI models are trained on and generate.

1. Its latest quarter sped up: revenue rose **55% to $4.38 billion** in the three months to 2 October, and it guided to $5.0 billion to $5.2 billion this quarter, up roughly 66% to 72%, its release on 22 October showed.
2. It is getting more profitable as it grows: operating margin was 39.4% over the last 12 months, up 27.3 points over five years, in the top tenth of the 31 hardware makers we cover.
3. The profit is cash: free cash flow was 29.6% of revenue over the last 12 months, against a peer median of 13.5%.

**The price:** 12.1 times expected earnings against a hardware median of 18.0 times, and a trailing P/E of 11.5 against its own median of 23.5 since late 2024. The shares are 60.4% below their June high, after a 17.4% fall on 5 November on fears of a Texas data-center pause ([our story](/news/2026-11-05/sandisk-micron-and-amd-shares-plunge-what-happened/)).

**What would change our mind:** volumes. Chief Executive Irving Tan called last quarter's 248 exabytes shipped the floor for growth; a guide below that would undercut the case. Its five-year revenue decline of 5.3% a year reflects the flash business spun off as Sandisk ($SNDK) in February 2025.

### 3. Analog Devices ($ADI)
**Revenue growth: 33.6% in the 12 months to 1 August 2026.** Analog Devices makes chips that turn real-world signals into data and manage power in data centers and factories.

1. It turns more of its sales into cash than most chipmakers: free cash flow was **35.6% of revenue** over the last 12 months, in the top quarter of the 25 we cover, against a median of 22.2%.
2. It is growing faster than usual: revenue rose 39.6% in its latest quarter from a year earlier, against 14.5% a year over five fiscal years.
3. Wall Street sees AI in it: Roth Capital started coverage with a buy rating and a $505 price target on 13 November, citing its growing role in AI infrastructure, TradingView and Benzinga reported.

**The price:** 24.6 times expected earnings, a little above the chipmaker median of 22.3 times, but its trailing P/E of 47.2 is below its own median of 63.0 since August 2024.

**What would change our mind:** returns. Return on capital of about 9.6% is below the median of its peers, and earnings per share grew only 6.8% a year over five years, against a median of 17.2%. Another five years like that would make the higher multiple hard to defend.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### AMD ($AMD)
**Revenue growth: 41.9% in the 12 months to 26 September 2026.** AMD sells AI accelerators, server processors and PC chips.

1. The quarter was strong: revenue rose 45% to **$13.4 billion**, and it guided to about $17 billion this quarter ([our recap](/news/2026-11-03/amd-amd-beats-q3-estimates-and-guides-q4-sales-to-17-billion/)).
2. Too little reaches cash: free cash flow was 15.5% of revenue over the last 12 months, in the bottom quarter of its peers, and fell to $554 million in the third quarter from $1.53 billion a year earlier.
3. Growth has not reached the owner: earnings per share rose 5.2% a year over five years against a median of 17.2%, and its operating margin is 3.4 points lower than five years ago.

**The price:** 44.0 times expected earnings, about twice the chipmaker median and more than three times Nvidia's 13.5 times. Earnings would have to roughly double for the multiple to fall to the median. The stock is up 149.4% this year, helped by bets that Nvidia's turmoil sends customers its way ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-13/wall-street-is-betting-nvidias-loss-is-amd-and-broadcoms-gain-tuesday-will-test-it/)).

**What would change our mind:** cash. If AMD delivers the $17 billion quarter with free cash flow back above 20% of revenue, the business would be catching up with its price.
