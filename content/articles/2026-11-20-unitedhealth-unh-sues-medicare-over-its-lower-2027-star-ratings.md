UnitedHealth ($UNH) sued the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services late on Thursday over the 2027 quality ratings of its Medicare Advantage plans, Reuters and STAT reported. The complaint says the agency used measures it had no authority to use, and that the corrected ratings would be **0.5 to 1.5 stars higher**, according to Reuters.

The stars set real money. Plans rated four stars or more earn bonus payments from the government, and insurers have turned to the courts as Medicare payments get tighter, Bloomberg Law reported. UnitedHealth has won this fight before: in November 2024 a federal judge in Texas ordered the agency to recalculate its 2025 ratings, and the agency later dropped its appeal, STAT and Healthcare Dive reported.

The suit lands in a hard month for the company. It lost 14% in two days on a new Medicare audit rule ([our breakdown](/news/2026-11-18/unitedhealth-unh-lost-14-in-two-days-on-an-audit-rule-is-the-business-broken/)), and on Friday it was **up 4.6%** at $345.55 as of 12:15 p.m. ET, after [TD Cowen upgraded it](/news/2026-11-20/unitedhealth-unh-shares-jump-after-td-cowen-upgrade/). The agency declined to comment to Reuters; no hearing date has been reported.
