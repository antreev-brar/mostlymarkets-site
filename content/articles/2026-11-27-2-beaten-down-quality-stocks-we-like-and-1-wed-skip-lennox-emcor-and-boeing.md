November's sell-off has dragged down good businesses along with the speculative ones, and the gap between price and quality is now wide in parts of industry. Lennox ($LII) sits 38.9% below its June high and Emcor ($EME) 33.4% below its May high in our data, after the AI build-out stocks fell through the month and weak demand for home air-conditioners hit Lennox's results.

Our screen took profitable companies in our coverage, ranked by how far each stock closed on Wednesday, 25 November, below its 52-week closing high, leaving out anything we listed in the past 14 days. We then judged each on SEC-filing numbers from `analyse.py`: growth, margins, cash and returns over five fiscal years first, against its sector, then the price against its own history.


## 2 stocks we like

### 1. Lennox International ($LII)

**Below its 52-week high: 38.9%, at Wednesday's close.** Lennox makes heating, air-conditioning and refrigeration equipment for homes and commercial buildings.

1. **It earns more than its peers, and more each year.** The operating margin was 18.8% in the 12 months to September 2026, above the 17.2% median of the 89 industrial companies in our data, and it has widened 6.9 points over five fiscal years. Earnings per share grew 19.8% a year over those five years, in the top quarter of the group.
2. **Its returns are among the best in industry.** Return on capital of about 28% ranks in the top quarter, roughly double the 13.4% median, and free cash flow came to 15.3% of sales over the past 12 months.
3. **The problem is cyclical and named.** On 28 October Lennox cut its full-year adjusted EPS forecast to $21.00 to $21.50, from $23.00 to $24.00, as dealers ran down stock and home-unit volumes fell, the release and Barchart reported. Chief Executive Alok Maskara blamed "heightened competitive pressure" and cost inflation, but commercial revenue still rose 24%, and management expects the destocking to be largely over by the second quarter of 2027.

**The price:** 15.7 times expected earnings, against 20.1 times for its peers; on a trailing basis it trades at 16.2 times against its own two-year median of 23.3 times.

**What would change our mind:** another cut to the profit outlook with the fourth-quarter results early next year, or residential destocking that runs past mid-2027.

### 2. Emcor ($EME)

**Below its 52-week high: 33.4%, at Wednesday's close.** Emcor installs and maintains the electrical, mechanical and plumbing systems inside data centers, hospitals, factories and offices.

1. **Growth has compounded through the cycle.** Revenue grew 14.1% a year over five fiscal years, against an 8.0% median for industrial peers, and 28.8% in the quarter to September 2026, to a record $5.54 billion. Earnings per share grew faster over five fiscal years than at any of the 67 industrial companies with comparable data.
2. **The order book is bigger than ever.** Remaining performance obligations, work signed but not yet done, reached a record **$18.57 billion** at 30 September, up 47% from a year earlier, the 10-Q and Zacks show. Emcor raised its revenue forecast for a third straight quarter, to $20.6 billion to $21.0 billion.
3. **It is buying, not retreating.** It agreed on 13 November to buy P1 Construction, a deal whose terms it did not disclose. The weakness is cash: free cash flow was 7.5% of sales over the past 12 months, in the bottom quarter of its peers, because contractors fund work before they are paid.

**The price:** 15.8 times expected earnings, against 20.1 times for peers; on a trailing basis 18.2 times against its own two-year median of 25.1 times.

**What would change our mind:** data centers make up about $8.74 billion, nearly half, of the order book, the third-quarter call showed, so a cut in big tech's data-center spending plans would hit Emcor's backlog directly.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Boeing ($BA)

**Below its 52-week high: 34.8%, at Wednesday's close.** Boeing builds commercial jets, defense systems and satellites.

1. **It still makes almost no cash.** Free cash flow was 0.0% of sales in the 12 months to September 2026 and minus 3.6% over two years, against a 12.7% median for industrial peers. With its third-quarter results in late October Boeing guided free cash flow to about $3 billion in 2027, well short of the roughly $5.6 billion analysts expected, AJOT and Finimize reported, and the shares fell about 9% that day.
2. **Shareholders have paid for the repair.** The diluted share count rose 28.1% over two years, in the bottom tenth of the group. Net debt was still $36.1 billion at 30 September, 3.7 years of operating profit.
3. **The orders are good news for later, not now.** Boeing [won 50 jets from Xiamen Airlines](/news/2026-11-24/boeing-ba-wins-a-7-4-billion-order-for-50-737-jets-from-china-southerns-xiamen-airlines/) at a $7.4 billion list price on Tuesday, and Qantas [is leaning toward the 787](/news/2026-11-27/qantas-is-set-to-pick-boeing-ba-for-most-of-a-20-jet-long-range-order-bloomberg-says/), but its backlog was never the issue. The cash depends on reaching 47 737s a month, now expected in early 2027, and on certifying the 777X.

**The price:** 17.5 times trailing earnings looks cheap, but price to free cash flow, the measure that matters here, is meaningless while cash flow is near zero.

**What would change our mind:** 737 output at the target rate of 47 a month and 777X certification on schedule, which would put the 2027 cash target within reach.
