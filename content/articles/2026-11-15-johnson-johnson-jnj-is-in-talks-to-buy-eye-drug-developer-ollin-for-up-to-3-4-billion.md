Johnson & Johnson ($JNJ) is in advanced talks to buy Ollin Biosciences, a private developer of eye-disease drugs, for up to **$3.4 billion**, the Financial Times reported on Sunday, citing people familiar with the matter. Reuters and Investing.com carried the report: $2.2 billion would be paid upfront and $1.2 billion in later milestone payments, and a deal could be announced as soon as Monday, though the talks could still fall apart.

The prize is OLN324, an injected drug for wet age-related macular degeneration and diabetic macular edema. In a 164-patient early study this spring it gave vision gains comparable to Roche's Vabysmo, with a hint of longer gaps between shots, Ophthalmology Times reported; two Phase 3 trials are now registered. Ollin launched only in September 2025, with $100 million from ARCH Venture Partners, Mubadala Capital and Monograph Capital.

For J&J, worth **$688 billion**, the price is small; the point is a foothold in retinal drugs, where it sells contact lenses but no treatments. The stock is up 38.1% this year and closed Friday at a 52-week high of $285.75. The next step is a formal announcement, which the FT said could come Monday.
