The story on Wall Street this week is that one listing can restart a stalled trade: if public investors pay up for Anthropic, the $895 billion it has promised to spend on computing becomes believable, and so does the revenue it means for Nvidia ($NVDA), Alphabet ($GOOGL) and Amazon ($AMZN). "The Jenga block holding up the AI trade is the Anthropic IPO," one portfolio manager told Bloomberg on 11 November, and CNBC on Sunday called the deal a "much-needed bright spot" for an embattled trade.

The peg is the order book. By the end of Thursday, 12 November, Anthropic's offering of 825 million shares at $70 to $78 had drawn orders for **about six times** the stock on sale, Bloomberg and Reuters reported. It plans to price on Wednesday and trade on Nasdaq as ANT on Thursday, CNBC reported, two days after Nvidia reports its first quarter without Jensen Huang as chief executive.

## The story in one sentence

> "A generational moment." Denny Fish, portfolio manager at Janus Henderson Investors, on the Anthropic IPO, to CNBC on 27 October.

The chain runs like this: if Anthropic raises about $90 billion at roughly $1.3 trillion, it can keep paying for the computing it has committed to; that spending is the backlog behind its suppliers' forecasts; and a strong debut tells the market the AI trade still has a buyer of last resort in public investors. If the debut falters, the same chain runs in reverse, which is why the deal matters to people who will never own a share.

## The evidence for it

### 1. Orders ran to six times the shares on sale

Demand reached about twice the shares on offer on Tuesday, the roadshow's first day, and **six times** by Thursday, Reuters and Bloomberg reported, citing people familiar with the deal. Bankers called that impressive for an offering this size, and institutional interest ran ahead of the early stage of SpaceX's roadshow in June, Reuters reported.

### 2. Its suppliers are writing $26 billion of checks

Alongside the IPO, Nvidia, Google, AMD, Broadcom, Micron ($MU), Microsoft ($MSFT) and TSMC ($TSM) agreed to buy **$26 billion** of non-voting stock at the IPO price, the amended filing showed and CNBC and Yahoo Finance reported. Nvidia and Google take $10 billion each. With the public offering, that puts the total raise at about $90.4 billion, Bloomberg and Yahoo Finance reported.

### 3. The revenue is growing fast enough to argue about

Anthropic's revenue rose to **$16.96 billion** in the third quarter from $11.56 billion in the second, up 47% in a single quarter, its filing showed ([our story](/news/2026-11-02/anthropics-ipo-filing-shows-17-billion-of-q3-sales-and-a-swing-to-an-operating-loss/)). At the range midpoint, that is about 19 times its third-quarter sales at an annual rate, by our arithmetic.

## The case against it

The doubters are in the order book itself. Investors who put in orders told Reuters on 13 November they question how long Anthropic's lead lasts as Alphabet's Google catches up, and whether it can keep charging premium prices once rivals' models match Claude's. One investor who did the due diligence and passed has a 2028 forecast about 30% below the sell side's, Reuters reported.

1. **The price already fell a third.** Valuation talk ran up to $2 trillion before the AI sell-off; the range sets about **$1.3 trillion** at its midpoint, CNN and NBC News reported. Buyers won that argument before trading starts, and the six-times book was built on the lower price.
2. **Some of the demand is circular.** Of the $90.4 billion raise, $26 billion, or 29%, comes from companies that sell Anthropic chips and cloud capacity or partner with it, by our arithmetic from the filing. Nvidia's $10 billion buys stock in a customer that buys its chips, so it is weaker evidence of outside demand than the order book.
3. **Big AI debuts have faded before.** SpaceX priced at $135 in June and slid below that price for the first time in July as worries about AI spending grew, BNN Bloomberg and the Khaleej Times reported. A six-times book says little about who still holds the stock a month later.

The strongest is the first. A trade that needs rescuing at $1.3 trillion, not $2 trillion, has already been marked down, so a good debut proves demand at a lower bar than the bulls set in October.

## What would change minds

- **Wednesday, 18 November: the price.** A price above $78 would mean demand rose with the valuation; one below $70 would mean the book thinned out after Nvidia's report on Tuesday.
- **Thursday, 19 November: the first close.** A close above the offer price is the minimum; a slide below it in the first weeks, as SpaceX had by July, would undercut the whole thesis.
- **Tuesday, 17 November: Nvidia's fourth-quarter forecast.** Nvidia guided to $108 billion for the third quarter; a forecast that keeps growing tells IPO buyers the computing chain Anthropic pays into is intact ([our daily](/daily/2026-11-15/)).

Since 23 October, the last close before Anthropic's public filing, Nvidia has **fallen 22.8%** and Alphabet 7.6%, while Amazon and Broadcom have risen about 8%, in our price data. Nvidia's drop owes as much to Huang's exit as to AI demand, which is why Thursday's debut is a cleaner read on the trade than Nvidia's own report.

## Our read

**Partly supported.** The demand is documented by two outlets and the revenue growth is in the filing, so the IPO will very likely get done. The weakest link is the claim that it rescues the trade: more than a quarter of the money comes from Anthropic's own suppliers and partners, and a first-day pop proves little, as SpaceX showed. The number we'll watch is where ANT closes against its offer price a month after Thursday.
