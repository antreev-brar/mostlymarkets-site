The US Commerce Department on Monday added Apex Enterprise Solutions, Alibaba's ($BABA) Singapore subsidiary, to its Entity List alongside Megaspeed, the firm at the center of the Nvidia ($NVDA) chip scandal, Reuters and CNBC reported. The listing names **seven companies** in Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand, which now need a US license to buy AI chips and other American technology.

Commerce cited a significant risk that advanced computing products would be diverted to unauthorized users. An Alibaba spokesperson told Reuters the listing was "erroneous," said Apex had never diverted chips subject to US export controls, and said the company would pursue all legal avenues to protect its rights and reputation.

It turns a week of allegations into a formal restriction. Reports that Alibaba was Megaspeed's dominant customer had already drawn a [shareholder lawsuit](/news/2026-11-20/alibaba-baba-is-sued-by-shareholders-over-its-ties-to-megaspeed/), and the [scrutiny of Megaspeed](/news/2026-11-22/nyt-says-us-intelligence-tracked-megaspeed-executives-contacts-with-chinas-spy-agency-nvda/) is part of why [Nvidia's crisis has moved to its books](/news/2026-11-23/nvidias-crisis-has-moved-from-its-boss-to-its-books-what-the-evidence-says/). Alibaba closed **up 6.1% at $86.04** in Monday's AI rally, still 41.3% lower this year; its last after-hours trade, at 7:59 p.m. ET, came before the listing was reported.

What to watch: Tuesday's US open for the first New York reaction, and whether Alibaba challenges the listing formally. The listing is not a criminal charge against any of the companies.
