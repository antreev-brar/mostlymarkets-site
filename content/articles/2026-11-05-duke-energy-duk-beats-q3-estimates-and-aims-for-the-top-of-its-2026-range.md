Duke Energy ($DUK) reported third-quarter earnings of **$1.99 a share** before the open on Thursday, on both an adjusted and a reported basis, up from $1.81 a year earlier and 6% above the $1.87 Zacks consensus. Operating revenue rose 6.6% to $9.10 billion, and the utility said it now expects 2026 earnings near the top of a narrowed range. The shares closed **up 0.4% at $130.08**, a gain on a day the S&P 500 fell 4.4%.

## What stood out

**Rates and weather carried the quarter.** Most of the gain came from recovering the cost of grid investments through customer rates, plus favorable weather, partly offset by higher depreciation, interest and taxes, the company said in the release. Earnings in the main electric segment rose to **$1.82 billion** from $1.66 billion.

*Verdict: a beat built on the regulated formula working as designed, with weather as a bonus that won't repeat on demand.*

**Demand is finally rising.** Electricity load grew about 2% in the quarter, Duke's strongest since 2022, and management reaffirmed 3% to 4% growth for the rest of its plan, according to coverage of the call by BigGo and TradingKey. On the call, executives said 5.2 gigawatts of data-centre load under construction is on schedule for late 2027 into 2028, Motley Fool's transcript and T&D World showed, with $5 billion to $10 billion of possible extra capital spending from large-load customers on top of its $103 billion five-year plan.

## The outlook

Duke narrowed its 2026 adjusted EPS range to **$6.70 to $6.80**, from $6.55 to $6.80, and said it expects to land near the top, Investing.com and BigGo reported. It reaffirmed 5% to 7% annual EPS growth through 2030 and left its $103 billion 2026-2030 spending plan unchanged. It plans to file its first multiyear rate plan in Indiana later this year.

## Our take

We were impressed that load growth has turned into something visible in the numbers, and that the extra data-centre spending is still upside to a plan the market already knows. On the other hand, weather helped, and net debt of $88.1 billion, 9.8 years of operating profit by our data, means every rate case and every move in interest rates matters. Overall, a solid quarter that confirms the thesis without changing it.

Duke rose while the Nasdaq fell 5.5%, and it is up 11.0% this year, 2.5% below its March high. At 19.2 times forward earnings, about the utility median of 19.1, the price assumes the plan gets done, not more. The next test is fourth-quarter results early next year, and whether the large-load upside makes it into the spending plan. Southern Co. ($SO) also reported on Thursday.
