Nvidia ($NVDA) **soared 31.5%** on Monday after its board rehired Jensen Huang, adding about $952 billion of market value, the biggest one-day gain any company has had, and dragging the whole market up with it. The Nasdaq Composite had its best day since April 2025, and overnight Taiwan's market rose 9%, its own best day since then.

This morning the bill arrives. Nvidia is down 5.9% before the open and Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE) 11.5%, after HPE beat and raised but warned that AI servers will squeeze its margins, as of 6:15 a.m. ET in our data. Dell ($DELL) reports tonight, and October PCE inflation tomorrow, before markets close on Thursday for Thanksgiving.

The board voted unanimously before the open to [reinstate Huang](/news/2026-11-23/nvidia-nvda-shares-soar-29-before-the-open-after-it-reinstates-jensen-huang/), 11 days after he resigned. The **$952 billion** gain beat any single day by any company, Seeking Alpha and 24/7 Wall St. reported, and it was the stock's biggest move of the past year in our data ([the market wrap](/news/2026-11-23/stock-market-today-nvidia-adds-952-billion-as-jensen-huang-returns-and-the-nasdaq-jumps-6-5/)).

The board's own investigation, an SEC probe that Reuters said covers its accounting, and Huang's House interview on 1 December are all still open, so the rebound repriced the boss, not the books ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-23/nvidias-crisis-has-moved-from-its-boss-to-its-books-what-the-evidence-says/)).

### HPE beat and raised, and is down 11.5% on one margin number

HPE forecast fiscal 2027 adjusted earnings of $5.05 to $5.35 a share, above estimates, but Chief Financial Officer Marie Myers said a bigger share of lower-margin AI systems would pull the Cloud & AI segment's operating margin to about **14%**, from 18.4% in the fourth quarter, Seeking Alpha and GuruFocus reported. That is the question Dell has to answer tonight: whether AI server growth comes with profit ([our HPE recap](/news/2026-11-23/hewlett-packard-enterprise-hpe-beats-q4-estimates-as-server-sales-nearly-double/)).

### Sanofi will pay $41 billion for Insmed in cash


French drugmaker Sanofi agreed to buy Insmed, the maker of the lung-disease drug Brinsupri, for **$175 a share** in cash, the companies said, as CNBC, Fierce Pharma and RTE reported. Insmed rose about 14% before the open, CNBC said. Sanofi expects the deal to close in the first half of 2027 and to add to earnings per share by the end of 2028, as it looks for growth beyond its eczema drug Dupixent.
