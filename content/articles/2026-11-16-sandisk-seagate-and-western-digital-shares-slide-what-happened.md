## What happened
Shares of flash-memory maker Sandisk ($SNDK) **sank 8.3%** by 10:15 a.m. ET on Monday, with hard-drive makers Seagate ($STX) down 6.2% and Western Digital ($WDC) down 5.7%. The AI hardware sell-off that began on Friday resumed a day before Nvidia ($NVDA) reports, Reuters and CNBC reported, and research firm SemiAnalysis cut its forecast for AI chip spending, Investing.com and Futu News reported.

SemiAnalysis lowered its forecast for global spending on AI accelerators in 2028 by **28%**, and for 2030 to $923 billion from $1.531 trillion, citing tighter capital markets and slower growth at the frontier AI labs, Futu News, PANews and Investing.com reported. It also lowered its forecasts for high-bandwidth memory, the stacked chips that sit beside AI processors, PANews reported. Micron ($MU) fell 7.1% by the same time.

## Why it matters
Storage was the AI trade's second act this year: data centers that run AI models need far more flash and disk to hold the data, and each of these three stocks rose at least threefold this year before turning in June. A lower spending forecast hits that story at its source, because the memory and storage order book follows the chip order book. Sandisk splits its stock eight-for-one after the close on Thursday, according to its 29 October filing with the SEC, and trades on a split basis from Friday 20 November; a split changes the share count, not the value.

## What the market is telling us
Sandisk's shares moved 5% or more on 117 of the last 252 sessions, and the last drop this big came on 5 November, when the stock fell 22.3%. Even at Friday's close of $885.01 it was up 272.8% this year, yet 62.1% below its 25 June record of $2,335. Seagate closed Friday 51.7% below its June high and Western Digital 60.4% below. Nvidia's results after the close on Tuesday 17 November are the next test: a firm outlook would answer the spending worry for a day; a soft one would make SemiAnalysis's cut look early rather than wrong.
