Micron Technology ($MU) **jumped 19.4%** to $709.88 by 12:15 p.m. ET on Monday, after Nvidia ($NVDA) reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive and Citi estimated that the memory market could absorb the end of Nvidia's customer financing. Citi analysts wrote that even if Nvidia-related financing stopped completely, total DRAM demand would fall about 12% and NAND flash demand about 7%, Yahoo Finance and TipRanks reported. Sandisk ($SNDK) rose 15.5%, Western Digital ($WDC) 15.2% and Seagate ($STX) 14.7%.

The note answers the fear behind the memory sell-off: that the money Nvidia lends or guarantees to its customers was propping up chip orders, as [our story on Nvidia's books](/news/2026-11-23/nvidias-crisis-has-moved-from-its-boss-to-its-books-what-the-evidence-says/) set out. Even after Monday, Micron sat 51% below its June closing high at Friday's close, and Micron has said most of its 2027 supply is already sold.

Volume matters less than price: a 12% drop in demand can push memory prices down much further, because buyers can wait. Micron's results on 17 December are the next test ([the memory-boom story](/news/2026-11-21/wall-street-is-betting-the-memory-boom-has-peaked-microns-17-december-report-will-test-it/)).
