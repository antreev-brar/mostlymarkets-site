Nvidia ($NVDA) spent Monday doing what it has done for a month: falling. It closed down 4.1% at $156.14, its lowest close in a year, and pulled the S&P 500 down **1.9%** and the Nasdaq 2.5% for a third straight loss ([our wrap](/news/2026-11-16/stock-market-today-nvidia-drags-the-nasdaq-to-a-third-straight-loss-before-its-results/)). The companies that sell it memory, storage and chip tools fell harder still after SemiAnalysis cut its forecast for AI chip spending.

Tonight, after the close, interim Chief Executive Colette Kress presents Nvidia's first results since Jensen Huang resigned. Overnight the board set up a committee to police export rules, Seoul's market closed below 5,000 for the first time in nine months, and by 5:49 a.m. ET S&P 500 futures were about 0.3% lower, Reuters and MarketScreener reported.

Nvidia has now lost **34.7% from its 6 October high close** and 27.1% in a month, by our price data. Monday's pressure came from a Commerce Department rule to stop Chinese firms renting its chips abroad ([our brief](/news/2026-11-16/us-moves-to-cut-off-chinas-remote-access-to-nvidia-nvda-chips-abroad/)) and the unsettled search for a new chief.

### The suppliers fell almost twice as far as Nvidia

Sandisk ($SNDK) **fell 8.6%**, Micron ($MU) 7.6%, Dell ($DELL) 7.0% and Taiwan Semiconductor ($TSM) 6.3%, after research firm SemiAnalysis cut its estimate of 2028 spending on AI accelerators by 28% ([the storage makers](/news/2026-11-16/sandisk-seagate-and-western-digital-shares-slide-what-happened/), [the debate](/news/2026-11-16/bulls-vs-bears-on-ai-spending-semianalysis-cut-28-morgan-stanley-says-the-cycle-is-intact/)). The pattern is the market's view of the risk: a cut in AI budgets would hit the suppliers' orders before it hits Nvidia's.

Taiwan's market fell 2.3%. TSMC's US shares were down 1.0% at 6:15 a.m. ET in our data, after Monday's 6.3% drop.

Options prices imply a move of about **8.5% in either direction** after the report, a swing of roughly $335 billion, Reuters reported via Investing.com and Global Banking and Finance; Benzinga put the implied move nearer 9%. That is more than twice the stock's average post-earnings move over the past eight quarters, which says traders are pricing the leadership question, not just the numbers ([our preview](/news/2026-11-16/what-to-expect-from-nvidias-nvda-q3-earnings/)).

On Tuesday Nvidia's board created a **National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee**, chaired by director Suzanne Nora Johnson, with its national security and trade compliance teams reporting to it, the company said in a release reported by Bloomberg and Free Malaysia Today. Bloomberg tied it to the US move to cut off China's remote access to its chips. The board repeated that it has no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised.

Home Depot ($HD) said before the open that Chief Financial Officer Richard McPhail will **succeed Ted Decker as chief executive on 23 November**, alongside third-quarter results that topped estimates, its release and CNBC reported. It now expects fiscal 2026 sales to grow about 4.5%, the top of its old range, with comparable sales up about 1.4%. The stock had risen 18.6% in the month into the report ([our preview](/news/2026-11-16/what-to-expect-from-home-depots-hd-q3-earnings/)), so a modest beat left little to buy.

Tyson Foods ($TSN) **jumped 8.7%**, its biggest gain since February 2022, as investors looked past a missed quarter to its fiscal 2027 outlook, Investing.com and Finwire reported ([our recap](/news/2026-11-16/tyson-foods-tsn-misses-q4-profit-estimates-as-beef-losses-deepen/)). Centene ($CNC) rose 4.9% and PG&E ($PCG) 3.2% with no single news cause we could find. In a market where the 10-year yield is above 5%, money leaving AI went to businesses whose profits do not depend on data-center budgets.

## What's next

All times ET.

Fed officials Christopher Waller, Beth Hammack and John Williams speak, Reuters reported. Nvidia reports after the close.
- **Wednesday, 18 November:** Lowe's ($LOW), Target ($TGT) and TJX ($TJX) before the open, Fed minutes at 2 p.m., and Anthropic's IPO pricing at $70 to $78 a share.
- **Thursday, 19 November:** Walmart ($WMT) and Alibaba ($BABA) before the open, and Anthropic's first day of trading.

JD.com ($JD) was **down 3.2%** before the open after third-quarter revenue of 309.6 billion yuan, up 3.5%, narrowly missed estimates, its release and GuruFocus reported. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said nothing on rates in his speech on Monday, CNBC and the AP reported, which leaves today's three speakers to talk about December. We think tonight is less about Nvidia's third quarter than about its fourth-quarter forecast: the suppliers that fell 7% to 9% on Monday are pricing a slowdown that only a weak guide would confirm.
