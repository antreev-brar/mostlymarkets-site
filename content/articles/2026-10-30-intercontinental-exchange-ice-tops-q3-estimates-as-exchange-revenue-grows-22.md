Intercontinental Exchange ($ICE), the owner of the New York Stock Exchange and a string of futures markets, reported third-quarter net revenue of $2.77 billion, up 15% year on year, beating the $2.73 billion Zacks consensus. Adjusted earnings of $2.02 a share, up 18%, topped the $1.97 Zacks consensus. The results came out before Thursday's open, and the shares jumped 4.5% on Thursday to close at $163.04.

The gain was ICE's biggest since 2 July, when it rose 4.9%.

```highlights
Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) Q3 2026 highlights
- **Net revenue:** $2.77 billion vs the $2.73 billion Zacks consensus (15% year on year, a 1.3% beat)
- **Adjusted EPS:** $2.02 vs the $1.97 Zacks consensus (a 2.5% beat); GAAP EPS $1.67, up 18%
- **Exchanges net revenue:** $1.54 billion, up 22% year on year
- **Transaction revenue:** $1.40 billion, up 23%; recurring revenue $1.37 billion, up 7%
- **Adjusted operating margin:** 61%
- **2026 outlook:** recurring revenue growth raised to about 9% for Exchanges and about 8% for Fixed Income and Data Services
- **Market value:** about $91.5 billion at Thursday's close
```

## What stood out

Trading did the heavy lifting. Exchanges net revenue rose to $1.54 billion from $1.27 billion a year earlier, and transaction revenue, the fees ICE earns each time a contract changes hands, rose 23% to $1.40 billion, the release and Zacks showed.

That followed a busy quarter in its futures markets. ICE's monthly statistics showed total average daily volume up 31% year on year in the third quarter, with agriculture and metals volume up 57% and energy up 18%.

The other two units grew more slowly. Fixed Income and Data Services brought in $674 million, up from $618 million, while Mortgage Technology, the home-loan software business, rose 4% to $549 million. Chair and Chief Executive Jeffrey Sprecher said in the release that it was ICE's best third quarter on record.

## The outlook

ICE raised its 2026 forecast for recurring revenue growth, the steadier subscription-style income from data and listings, to about 9% in Exchanges and about 8% in Fixed Income and Data Services. On the call, management said it expects Mortgage Technology transaction revenue to fall in the fourth quarter as mortgage rates stay high.

Sprecher also announced a deal under which ICE will become the exclusive operator of Trade[XYZ]'s regulated markets, a move to bring perpetual futures, contracts with no expiry date popular in crypto trading, onto regulated US venues.

## Our take

This was a clean beat driven by trading volume, and the raised recurring revenue forecast shows the steadier businesses are growing too. Even after Thursday's gain, the stock sits 6.9% below its 52-week closing high of $175.10 from 26 January. Next quarter we'd watch whether futures volume holds up and how deep the expected dip in mortgage transaction revenue turns out to be.
