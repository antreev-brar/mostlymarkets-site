Micron ($MU) **fell 23.6%** this week, one of the steepest weekly falls among the big stocks we track, even though the company is earning the highest margins in its history. The story Wall Street is telling with that price is simple: memory profits have peaked, and the shares should be valued on what comes after.

The news peg was a string of cuts in four days. Research firm SemiAnalysis lowered its AI chip spending forecast on Monday 16 November, GF Securities cut its flash-memory price forecast on Tuesday, and on Friday an analyst survey said Google had trimmed its 2027 orders for its own AI chips. Sandisk ($SNDK) lost 28.4% on the week, Applied Materials ($AMAT) 18.3% and Seagate ($STX) 13.5%, by our data.

## The story in one sentence

> Today's margins are "likely to mark the peak of the current cycle." Pzena Investment Management, a value fund that owns Samsung, in its review of memory makers' margins in May 2026

The market has caught up with that view this month. Benzinga and Bloomberg have both argued that memory stocks look cheapest when profits are closest to their peak, which is how a 3.4 times multiple can be a warning rather than a bargain.

The chain runs like this. If AI spending growth slows in 2027 and 2028, fewer high-end accelerators ship than planned, and with them less high-bandwidth memory (HBM, the stacked chips beside AI processors). That frees wafers for ordinary memory just as new factories open, so contract prices fall, and the makers' record margins have the furthest to drop.

## The evidence for it

### 1. The forecasts for AI spending are being cut

SemiAnalysis lowered its forecast for global spending on AI accelerators in 2028 by **28%**, and cut its 2030 figure to $923 billion from $1.531 trillion, citing tighter capital markets and slower growth at the frontier AI labs, Futu News and PANews reported on 16 November. PANews said it also lowered its forecasts for HBM.

### 2. Flash prices are forecast to stall, then fall

GF Securities said on 17 November that it now expects NAND flash prices to stay flat in the first quarter of 2027 and fall in each of the three quarters after, against an earlier call for a 6% rise, 24/7 Wall St. and Seeking Alpha reported. It sees NAND supply growing **28%** in 2027 and named Sandisk the most exposed.

### 3. The biggest custom-chip buyer may be ordering fewer top-end parts

TF International Securities analyst Ming-Chi Kuo said on 20 November that Google cut planned 2027 shipments of its Broadcom-designed TPU 8i by about 1 million units, roughly 20%, mostly in the pricier Max version, Seeking Alpha and Yahoo Finance reported. Google has announced no change. Fewer top-end accelerators means less HBM bought, which is why a report about Broadcom matters to memory makers ([our story](/news/2026-11-20/broadcom-avgo-shares-sink-after-a-report-that-google-cut-its-2027-tpu-orders/)).

### 4. New supply is dated, and much of it is the makers' own

Micron nearly doubled its spending on plants and equipment to **$30.7 billion** in the year to 3 September, and its annual report dates first output from two new Idaho factories to mid-2027 and late 2028. Kyung Kye-hyun, a former head of Samsung's chip division, said in May that Chinese capacity could push prices down from late 2027 or early 2028, Notebookcheck and PC Gamer reported.

### 5. Memory has done this before

Micron's own filings show how fast a peak fades: in fiscal 2023, the year after record sales, its revenue halved and its operating margin went from 31.5% to minus 37%. The makers' current operating margins, above 70% at all three, leave even further to fall.

## The case against it

The makers themselves say the peak is not here. Kim Jae-june, executive vice president for memory sales at Samsung Electronics, said on its 29 October call that "our 2027 HBM supply volume is already sold out at an agreed target price." SK hynix said on 28 October that long-term contracts with **14 customers** cover more than half of its 2027 and 2028 capacity.

1. **AI memory for 2027 is priced and booked.** Micron said on 30 September it had completed agreements for "the vast majority" of its 2027 HBM supply "with significant price increases year over year," and TrendForce forecast on 29 September that HBM prices would rise 121% in 2027. A forecast cut for 2028 doesn't touch next year's contracts.
2. **The biggest customer just raised its outlook.** Nvidia ($NVDA) guided to fourth-quarter revenue of $130.0 billion on 17 November, above the LSEG consensus, as its Rubin platform, which runs on HBM4, ramps ([our recap](/news/2026-11-17/nvidia-nvda-tops-q3-estimates-and-guides-to-130-billion-in-its-first-report-without-huang/)). Then-Chief Executive Jensen Huang told CNBC in October he didn't expect a memory glut through 2029, The Motley Fool and Digital Today reported.
3. **The price already assumes a fall.** At 3.4 times forward earnings, against its own trailing median of 26.1 times since October 2024, Micron is valued as if this year's profits are a one-off. If earnings merely hold through 2027, the stock is priced for a drop that doesn't come.

We think the first point is the strongest, because it rests on signed contracts, not forecasts. But it covers HBM, which Pzena estimated at about 12% of DRAM bit demand this year; the rest of memory sells on prices that reset every quarter.

## What would change minds

- **Wednesday 17 December:** Micron reports its fiscal first quarter. It guided revenue of about $61.5 billion and an adjusted gross margin of about 86.25%; a margin below that, or softer comments on 2028 pricing, would be the first crack from the makers' side.
- **Early January:** TrendForce's first-quarter 2027 contract-price forecast (this year's came on 5 January). Falling conventional DRAM prices would confirm the bears for everything outside HBM.
- **Broadcom's next results:** its AI revenue outlook for 2027 will confirm or rule out Kuo's TPU cut, the one piece of evidence that touches HBM volumes directly.

## Our read

**Partly supported.** The flash part of the story is well supported: a named broker sees prices falling through 2027 and supply growing 28%, and Sandisk's slide shows the market believes it. The weakest link is HBM, where three makers say 2027 is sold out at higher prices, so a peak in AI memory profits is at least a year away on the evidence we have. At 3.4 times forward earnings, Micron is priced for the bear case already; the number we'll watch on 17 December is its gross margin against the 86.25% it guided.
