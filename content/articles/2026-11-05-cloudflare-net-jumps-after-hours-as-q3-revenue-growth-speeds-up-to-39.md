Cloudflare ($NET), which runs a global network that speeds up and protects websites and AI apps, reported third-quarter revenue of $782.5 million after the close on Thursday, up 39% from a year earlier and ahead of the $736.8 million in Investing.com's consensus. Adjusted earnings were $0.38 a share against the $0.34 expected, Investing.com and Benzinga reported. The shares, which had **fallen 8.3%** in the regular session to $244.51 in the AI sell-off, were up 14.9% at $281.00 in after-hours trading at 5:15 p.m. ET.

## What stood out

Growth sped up. Revenue rose **39%**, up from about 36% in the second quarter in our filings data, the fastest pace "in nearly four years," Chief Executive Matthew Prince said in the release. "We have built the development platform for the AI-powered Internet," he said, and the company crossed $3 billion in annualized revenue.

The cost is in the gross margin, which fell to 72.4% on an adjusted basis from 75.3% a year earlier. Adjusted operating profit still rose to $137.1 million from $85.9 million, and current remaining performance obligations, the contracted revenue due over the next year, grew 37%, close to revenue growth, which suggests the pace can hold.

Cash is the weak spot. Free cash flow was $82.1 million, or 10% of revenue, down from 13% a year earlier, as capital spending rose about 45%, faster than sales, by our arithmetic from the release. Over the past 12 months its free cash flow margin of 13.9% ranks in the bottom quarter of the peers we track, against a median of 18.2%, though it holds $5.13 billion of cash and securities.

## The outlook

Cloudflare expects fourth-quarter revenue of **$845 million to $846 million**, about 38% above the $614.5 million of a year earlier in our data, and adjusted EPS of $0.43. For the full year it now expects $2.963 billion to $2.964 billion, which Investing.com and 24/7 Wall St. described as a raise. Its call started at 5 p.m. ET.

## Our take

This was the best kind of software quarter: faster growth, a wider operating margin and a forecast that keeps the pace. The price is the problem: at 166 times forward earnings in our data before the results, against a peer median of 18 times, it already assumed years of this. After falling 32.3% from its October high, the stock had room to bounce; the gross margin is the number to watch next quarter.
