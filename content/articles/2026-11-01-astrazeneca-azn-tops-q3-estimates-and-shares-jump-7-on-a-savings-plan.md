AstraZeneca ($AZN) reported third-quarter revenue of $16.21 billion on Friday, up 7% year on year and a little above the $16.04 billion analysts expected, Investing.com and Yahoo Finance reported. Core earnings, its adjusted measure, rose 17% to **$2.79 a share**, beating the $2.59 consensus. Its US-listed shares jumped 7.0% to $186.65, the biggest gain since October 2025, after the company also unveiled a plan to save $1.5 billion a year.

## What stood out

**Cancer drugs are now half the company.** Oncology revenue rose 17% to $7.77 billion, the results statement showed, faster than the group's 7%. That growth is what offsets Farxiga, whose sales fell 23% after cheaper US copies arrived.

*Verdict: the engine is healthy; the drag from expiring patents is real but contained.*

**The savings plan drove the stock.** AstraZeneca said it will cut about **$1.5 billion** a year of costs over three years and put the money mainly into its pipeline, Reuters and City AM reported, at a cost of up to $2 billion in one-off charges. The company said the restructuring will not lead to a net reduction in jobs, according to Reuters.

**Investors are looking to 2027.** Chief Executive Pascal Soriot said a series of late-stage trial results due next year could produce more than $20 billion of peak annual sales, Reuters and Investor's Business Daily reported.

## The outlook

AstraZeneca reaffirmed its 2026 forecast, including core EPS growth by a low double-digit percentage. In the first nine months, revenue rose 7% at constant rates to $46.9 billion and core EPS rose 14% to $8.00. The company still targets $80 billion of revenue in 2030.

## Our take

We were impressed by the earnings beat, 8% against a typical 2% to 4%, and by oncology's 17% growth at this size. On the other hand, the revenue beat was only 1%, guidance didn't move, and the $20 billion figure is a peak-sales estimate for drugs whose trials haven't read out. Overall, a solid quarter, and a 7% jump that is mostly a vote for the cost plan and the 2027 pipeline.

The long run supports the case: by our data, revenue grew 17.2% a year over five years, in the top quarter of our drugmaker group, and the shares trade at 17.5 times forward earnings, a little below the 18.1 times peer median, and still 10.9% below February's high. The test comes with the trial results in 2027, and full-year results in February. [AbbVie](/news/2026-11-01/abbvie-abbv-tops-q3-estimates-as-skyrizi-and-rinvoq-grow-23/) also reported on Friday.
