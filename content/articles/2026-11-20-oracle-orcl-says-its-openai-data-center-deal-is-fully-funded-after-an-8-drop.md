Oracle ($ORCL) said after the close on Friday that its data-center contract with OpenAI is fully funded following its final capital raise, and that delivering it does not depend on further talks with vendors, Reuters and Investing.com reported. The shares **rose 3.0%** to $104.22 after hours, as of 5:15 p.m. ET, having fallen 8.5% in the regular session, as the Nasdaq dropped 5.3%, to a 52-week low of $101.19.

The statement answers the question that has driven the stock down **48.1% this year**: how Oracle pays for data centers that cost more than its operations bring in. Its free cash flow was negative $28.7 billion over the 12 months to August, as we noted when we [skipped it on 15 November](/news/2026-11-15/3-beaten-down-quality-stocks-we-like-and-1-wed-skip-intuit-vertiv-comfort-systems-oracle/). Funded is not the same as cheap: the money came partly from new stock and debt, which leaves fewer earnings per share and more interest to pay, and it is part of the [AI money loop](/news/2026-11-16/wall-street-is-betting-ais-1-trillion-money-loop-breaks-at-the-neoclouds-nvidias-report-will-test-it/) Wall Street is now testing.

Oracle's next quarterly results will show whether that cash burn is narrowing.
