Vertex Pharmaceuticals ($VRTX) said after the close on Monday that third-quarter revenue **grew 12% to $3.44 billion**, just above the $3.42 billion analysts expected, according to FactSet data cited by MarketScreener; TradingView put the estimate at the same figure. Adjusted earnings per share of $4.76 were slightly below the $4.79 to $4.82 analysts had forecast, and GAAP EPS fell to $2.56 from $4.20 on costs from its purchase of Crinetics Pharmaceuticals. The shares, which closed down 1.0% at $553.15, were up 2.5% at $567.00 in after-hours trading as of 4:15 p.m. ET.

## What stood out

The cystic fibrosis franchise is moving patients to a newer pill without losing sales. Alyftrek, Vertex's once-daily cystic fibrosis drug, brought in **$706 million**, nearly three times a year earlier, while the older Trikafta fell 8% to $2.44 billion, the release showed; total cystic fibrosis revenue still grew 8% to $3.27 billion.

The new drugs are still small but growing fast. Casgevy, its gene-edited treatment for sickle cell disease, grew 30% on the quarter to $99 million, and the non-opioid painkiller Journavx grew 18% to $58 million. Costs rose faster than sales: combined non-GAAP research, development and selling costs grew to $1.5 billion from $1.3 billion as Vertex builds a kidney-drug sales force, which is why adjusted EPS slipped.

## The outlook

The raised revenue forecast adds $50 million at each end of the range. Vertex also raised its non-GAAP operating-cost forecast to about $6.0 billion from $5.65 billion to $5.75 billion, mostly for Crinetics. Cash and investments fell to $9.8 billion from $12.3 billion at the end of 2025 after $9.0 billion went on the deal and buybacks, partly offset by a $4.5 billion term loan.

## Our take

A steady quarter for a business that earned a 38.0% operating margin over the 12 months to June, against a median of 18.3% for the drugmakers in our coverage, according to its SEC filings. The bigger test is 30 November, when US regulators are due to decide on povetacicept for the kidney disease IgAN; Chief Executive Reshma Kewalramani said in the release that Vertex is "well positioned to establish nephrology as our fifth commercial franchise." At 28.9 times forward earnings, against a drugmaker median of 18.2, the stock already prices in a good share of that.
