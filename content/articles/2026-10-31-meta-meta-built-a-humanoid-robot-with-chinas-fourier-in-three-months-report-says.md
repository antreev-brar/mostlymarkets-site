On Saturday, The Information reported that Meta ($META) built a prototype humanoid robot, code-named Max, with Chinese startup Fourier in about **three months**, and began a second version in September. Meta told the publication the work is internal research, with no plans to sell the robots. Reuters said it could not immediately verify the report.

Meta formed a humanoid-robot team in February 2025, Bloomberg reported then. Li-Chen Miller, Meta's vice president of product for robotics, spent almost two months at Fourier's Shanghai factory, The Information said. That matters: since 28 July, the Federal Communications Commission has denied new foreign-built robots, humanoids included, the approval needed for US sale unless the Pentagon exempts them.

On Friday, S&P Global Ratings cut its outlook on Meta's AA- credit rating to negative, forecasting that capital spending will exceed Meta's [operating cash flow](/news/2026-10-22/meta-meta-shares-slide-after-free-cash-flow-turns-negative/) by **$71 billion** in 2027. S&P said it is unclear when Muse, Meta's new AI agent app, and the models behind it will bring in more than they cost, CNBC reported; Meta disagreed. The Information said Meta sees humanoids as Muse's physical extension.

Our read: S&P's worry is data centers, not robots. But Max rides on the Muse payoff S&P can't date, and since the FCC goes by where a robot is built, not whose it is, a China-built Meta robot would need that exemption for US sale. Next comes January, when Meta plans to start selling $60 billion of new nonvoting shares on the market, most of the stock sale it announced on 26 October.
