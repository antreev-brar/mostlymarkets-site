Entergy ($ETR), the electric utility for Louisiana, Arkansas, Mississippi and southeast Texas, reports third-quarter results before the market opens on Monday, 2 November, with a webcast at 11 a.m. ET, the company said on 26 October. The question is the price of growth: Entergy plans to spend **$67 billion** from 2026 to 2030, mostly to serve data centers and industrial plants.

Last quarter, adjusted EPS was $1.03, down from $1.05 a year earlier, as higher interest costs and extra shares from its stock sales ate into profit, the company said. That still beat the 94 cents Zacks's consensus expected, and revenue rose 5.9% to $3.52 billion. Entergy kept its 2026 forecast of $4.25 to $4.45 a share, but the stock **fell 4.0%** that day, 29 July.

## What analysts expect

- **Adjusted EPS:** $1.71, up 12% from $1.53 a year ago, according to Zacks via Yahoo Finance and Barchart. Other compilers range from $1.65 to $1.73, so whether it is a beat depends on whose figure is used.
- **Full year:** Barchart's consensus of $4.40 a share sits in the upper half of Entergy's range.

A year ago Entergy beat the $1.44 consensus by 9 cents, TipRanks reported.

## What to watch

- **Data-center demand.** On the July call management described a pipeline of 7 to 12 gigawatts of possible hyperscaler load, plus 3 to 5 gigawatts of industrial demand. New signed contracts would show the pipeline turning into sales.
- **The cost of funding it.** Entergy expects to raise about $7 billion of equity through 2030, its quarterly filing says, and interest costs already cut second-quarter profit. Barclays cut its price target to $115 from $128 and Truist to $119, both keeping buy-equivalent ratings, GuruFocus reported.
- **The peer bar.** NextEra Energy ($NEE) reported adjusted EPS of $1.24 on 21 October, up from $1.13, and a renewables and storage backlog of 36.7 gigawatts. In our read, contracted growth without heavier dilution is what Entergy needs to show.

## How the stock has done

Entergy closed Friday at $110.11, **up 19.1%** this year and 9.7% over the past month, but 6.6% below its 52-week closing high of $117.91 from 30 April. It has moved 5% or more on just one session in the past year, and $1,000 invested five years ago is worth $2,138 today.
