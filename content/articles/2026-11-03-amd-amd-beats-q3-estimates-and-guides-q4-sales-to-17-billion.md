AMD ($AMD) reported third-quarter revenue of **$13.4 billion** after the close on Tuesday, up 45% from a year earlier and ahead of the $13.09 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC. Adjusted earnings of $2.09 a share beat the $1.95 consensus, and the shares were up 5.0% at $508.20 by 7:59 p.m. ET, after closing up 3.7% at $483.90.

## What stood out

The Data Center segment, which sells Instinct AI accelerators and EPYC server processors, **nearly doubled** to $8.5 billion and now makes up almost two-thirds of sales. Its operating profit rose to $2.88 billion from $1.07 billion a year earlier, the release showed. "Third quarter was the first quarter that we shipped MI455 and Helios racks for revenue. We will continue to ramp throughout 2027," Chief Executive Lisa Su said on the call, according to transcripts published by Investing.com and Benzinga.

The rest of the business was mixed. Client revenue from PC processors rose 7% to $2.9 billion, Embedded rose 29% to $1.1 billion, and Gaming fell 40% to $773 million on lower sales of console chips and graphics cards.

## The outlook

AMD guided fourth-quarter revenue to **about $17 billion**, which it said would be 66% growth from a year earlier and 27% from the third quarter. Analysts had expected $16.2 billion, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC and SBS. AMD expects an adjusted gross margin of about 55%, a point below the third quarter's 56%.

## The one debate: is the ramp paying cash yet?

Not yet. Free cash flow fell to $554 million, or 4% of sales, from $1.53 billion a year earlier, because AMD spent about $3.0 billion building inventory in the quarter, the cash-flow statement shows; inventories stood at $11.5 billion, up from $7.9 billion in December. That is consistent with a company stocking up for a 27% jump in quarterly sales, but it means the fourth quarter has to turn those racks into cash.

## Our take

A clean quarter: revenue and EPS beat, and the guide cleared consensus by about 5%, the kind of raise a stock down 23.4% over the past month needed. The stock still trades at 42.8 times forward earnings in our data, against a chipmaker median of 24.0 times, so the Helios ramp has to show up in cash flow, not only revenue, when AMD reports again in early 2027.
