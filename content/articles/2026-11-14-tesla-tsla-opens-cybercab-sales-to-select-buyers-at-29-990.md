Tesla ($TSLA) began inviting select customers on Friday to buy its Cybercab, the two-seat car with no steering wheel or pedals, for personal use at an estimated **$29,990** before taxes, InsideEVs and TechXplore reported. Buyers also pay a $299 monthly autonomy subscription and a $1,000 nonrefundable deposit, and Tesla estimates first deliveries in December in Austin, Texas, according to the order page those outlets described.

It is the first time Tesla has taken orders for the robotaxi that much of its value rests on; in September it opened only an interest form for fleet buyers. Tesla trades at **196 times forward earnings** on a 3.3% operating margin ([our breakdown](/news/2026-11-07/tesla-tsla-trades-at-196-times-earnings-on-a-3-margin-is-the-business-worth-it/)), a multiple that only holds up if driverless cars earn far more than the cars it sells today. The catch is in the fine print: a privately owned Cybercab runs only in Tesla's supported service areas, Yahoo Finance and InsideEVs reported, and executives said in October that most production will go to Tesla's own ride-hailing fleet.

Tesla has not said when sales will open to the public, and the next test is whether the December deliveries arrive on time. Tesla shares fell 1.8% on Friday to $390.22 and slipped another 0.8% after hours.
