Novartis ($NVS) is abandoning pelacarsen, a heart drug once seen as a potential blockbuster, after full results presented at the American Heart Association meeting on Saturday showed it lowered lipoprotein(a), an inherited form of cholesterol, by **78%** without cutting heart attacks, strokes or heart deaths, HCPLive and CNN reported. About 12% of patients had a major heart event over roughly five years in both the drug and placebo groups. Investing.com and Seeking Alpha reported that Novartis is shelving the drug, which it licensed from Ionis.

The miss itself was announced on 4 September without numbers; Saturday's data show the drug did no better than placebo at all, BioPharma Dive and Endpoints reported. That matters beyond Novartis: Amgen ($AMGN) and Eli Lilly ($LLY) are testing their own Lp(a) drugs, olpasiran and lepodisiran, which lower the marker further, and Fierce Biotech said the result casts a shadow over both. On the same day, Novo Nordisk's ($NVO) ziltivekimab also failed to reduce heart events in its ZEUS trial, Endpoints reported.

Novartis closed Friday at $162.55, up 17.9% this year, so Monday's open is the first trade on the full data. The next test for the drug class is Amgen's olpasiran outcomes trial.
