This was the week Nvidia ($NVDA) lost the man who built it. Its board opened an inquiry into Jensen Huang on Monday, and on Thursday he resigned as chief executive, with Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress taking over for now, Nvidia said in an SEC filing. By Friday's close the stock had **fallen 12.7%** in five days to $162.85, its lowest close in a year, in our data.

The market barely flinched. The money stayed in AI chips and moved to Nvidia's rivals, AMD ($AMD) and Broadcom ($AVGO), and a 2.3% rally on Monday, when oil fell, carried the S&P 500 to a 2.5% weekly gain. The worry that grew instead was rates: hotter consumer and wholesale prices pushed the 10-year Treasury yield above 5%, and futures now price a Fed rate hike on 9 December as certain.

## The week in eight charts

### Monday's rally was the S&P 500's whole week

The S&P 500 **rose 2.3%** on Monday to 7,549.94, and all 11 sectors gained, AP and Bloomberg reported, as oil fell after President Trump said he was unlikely to order fresh attacks on Iran. The index then added only 0.2% over the next four days to finish at 7,561.67. Monday's gain made back most of the prior week's midterm sell-off ([last weekend's edition](/daily/2026-11-07/)).

## What's next

All times ET.

- **Monday, 16 November:** Baidu ($BIDU) and Tyson Foods ($TSN) report before the open.
- **Tuesday, 17 November:** Nvidia reports third-quarter results after the close, with a call at 5 p.m., its first report without Huang; it guided to **$108 billion** of revenue, plus or minus 2% ([our bulls and bears](/news/2026-11-13/bulls-vs-bears-on-nvidia-nvda-is-13-times-earnings-cheap-without-jensen-huang/)). Home Depot ($HD) and Disney ($DIS) report before the open.
- **Wednesday, 18 November:** Lowe's ($LOW), Target ($TGT) and TJX ($TJX) before the open; Palo Alto Networks ($PANW) after the close.
- **Thursday, 19 November:** Walmart ($WMT) and Alibaba before the open; Intuit ($INTU) after the close.
- **Still unset:** dates for Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick before Congress, CNN and Euronews reported.

We think Tuesday decides the week's argument: a beat with an unchanged outlook would show Nvidia's customers have not walked away, and the 18% gains in AMD and Broadcom rest on the opposite.
