Korea's KOSPI **fell 3.5%** on Friday as Thursday's losses on Wall Street reached Seoul, Yonhap reported, hours before New York's own chip selloff.

Seoul's week showed that the chip trade now pays for news, not records: SK hynix's forecast of shortages through 2030 lifted memory stocks in both cities on Wednesday, while Samsung's shares sank on Thursday despite a record profit it had flagged three weeks earlier.

## Wednesday: SK hynix's record lifted Seoul and New York

SK hynix said on Wednesday that its quarterly revenue had topped 100 trillion won for the first time, that it expects demand for memory to outrun supply through 2030, and that long-term contracts already cover more than half of its 2027 and 2028 capacity ([our story](/news/2026-10-28/sandisk-sndk-and-micron-mu-shares-jump-after-sk-hynixs-record-quarter/)).

Chip heavyweights led the KOSPI, the Korea Times reported: SK hynix **soared 10.89%**, and Samsung, due to report the next day, 9.01%.

In New York the same day, Sandisk ($SNDK) jumped 7.7% and Micron ($MU) 5.9%, and the hard-drive makers Western Digital ($WDC) and Seagate ($STX) rose too.

## Thursday: Samsung's record wasn't enough

On Thursday, Samsung reported a record third-quarter operating profit of **107.44 trillion won**, about $80 billion, nearly all of it from its chip division.

Little of that was news. Samsung had flagged about the same profit in preliminary results on 8 October, and its shares had already soared on Wednesday. The full report then came in a shade under LSEG SmartEstimates' forecasts for both profit and revenue, CNBC reported.

The shares **fell 4.13%**, and the KOSPI gave up its morning gains to close lower. The Korea JoongAng Daily blamed a slight miss in the chip division and the lack of detail on how Samsung will split another 55 trillion to 75 trillion won between buybacks and dividends. Samsung's board will settle that in late January 2027, CNBC reported.

"Investors have been so spoilt by the AI trade that a record result is now treated as the bare minimum rather than a reason to celebrate," Josh Gilbert, APAC lead analyst at eToro, told CNBC.

The memory boom also has a cost inside Samsung: higher costs for memory and other parts pushed its phone and network division to an operating loss, the JoongAng reported. That followed the unit's first quarterly loss, from April to June, which Reuters also blamed on chip prices.

## Friday: Seoul sold first

By the time Seoul opened on Friday, at 8 p.m. Thursday in New York, Intel ($INTC) had warned that a memory shortage was choking PC demand, and its shares had plunged 19.6% after hours ([our Intel recap](/news/2026-10-29/intel-intc-q3-profit-tops-estimates-shares-plunge-on-a-weak-forecast/)).

The KOSPI was down 5.25% by 11:20 a.m. in Seoul, and falling futures set off a "sidecar," a five-minute pause in program trading, Yonhap reported. Its closing report blamed worries about lofty AI valuations, citing Alphabet ($GOOGL), which plunged 12.6% on Thursday after results that raised doubts about returns on AI spending ([our story](/news/2026-10-29/alphabet-googl-shares-plunge-after-search-falls-short-and-cash-burn-grows/)).

By the close, SK hynix had lost more than 7% and Samsung 3%, the Korea Herald reported.

In New York's session, which opened seven hours after Seoul's close, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index sank 7.2%, NBC News reported, and Intel **plunged 28.6%**, its worst day since 1974 ([our market wrap](/news/2026-10-30/stock-market-today-intels-worst-day-since-1974-sinks-the-nasdaq-2-9/)).

## What Seoul's week told US investors

By our count, Seoul's Friday close, which came before New York's selloff, still left the KOSPI about 1.6% above where it stood on Tuesday, before SK hynix reported. In New York, Wednesday's gains were gone by Friday's close.

The two markets peaked in the same week: the KOSPI's record close came on 22 June, and Micron's and Sandisk's closing highs on 25 June. On Friday the KOSPI stood about 37% below its record and Sandisk 51% below its high, so this week's rally was a rebound inside a four-month slide.

Sandisk got Samsung's reception a day later: it beat estimates on Thursday evening and still **plunged 13.5%** on Friday, with a forecast of about 12% sales growth from the previous quarter, roughly half the pace it had just reported ([our story](/news/2026-10-30/sandisk-sndk-tops-fiscal-q1-estimates-as-data-center-sales-jump-61/)).

## Our read

**We think this week's selling was more about the buyers than about the memory shortage.** Neither Korean maker gave ground: SK hynix sees demand outrunning supply through 2030, and Samsung expects supply to stay tight through at least 2028, with its 2027 capacity of HBM, the stacked memory in AI chips, already booked, the JoongAng reported.

The worries Reuters cited on Friday were on the customers' side. Four of the five biggest AI spenders burned cash last quarter, by our count ([our story](/news/2026-10-31/free-cash-flow-is-now-the-scorecard-for-ai-spenders-what-the-evidence-says/)), and memory costs are squeezing PC and phone makers, Samsung's own phone unit among them.

Qualcomm's report on Wednesday is a direct test. Another drop in handset-chip sales blamed on memory would confirm the squeeze; steady sales would suggest Friday's prices assumed too much.
