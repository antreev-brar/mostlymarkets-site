## What happened
Shares of electric-car maker Tesla ($TSLA) **jumped 12.9%** to $390.26 by 10:15 a.m. ET on Monday, as tech stocks rebounded after Nvidia ($NVDA) reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive before the open ([our story](/news/2026-11-23/nvidia-nvda-shares-soar-29-before-the-open-after-it-reinstates-jensen-huang/)). Barron's put the gain down to the tech bounce and to Tesla's next self-driving software; Kalkine said there was no major company announcement behind it.

Chief Executive Elon Musk said on Saturday that version 15 of Tesla's Full Self-Driving software is rolling out and will go wide next month, Investor's Business Daily and Seeking Alpha reported. Over the weekend Barron's and Puck also wrote about the prospect of a merger between Tesla and SpaceX, Musk's rocket company; no deal has been announced.

## Why it matters
Tesla trades less on car sales than on autonomy and AI, which is why it fell with the chip stocks last week and rose with them on Monday. Its shares lost **7.6%** on Friday, the day the S&P 500 closed in a correction. The software news gives the bounce a Tesla-specific story, but the size of the move tracks the market's mood about AI more than one software release.

What's next is the rollout itself: Musk set no date beyond next month, and how widely the new version reaches drivers is the checkable part ([our breakdown of the valuation](/news/2026-11-07/tesla-tsla-trades-at-196-times-earnings-on-a-3-margin-is-the-business-worth-it/)).

## What the market is telling us
Tesla's shares moved 5% or more on only 17 of the last 252 sessions, so a day like Monday is rare for it. The stock closed Friday down 23.1% this year at $345.82, 29.4% below its 16 December 2025 closing high of $489.88. $1,000 invested five years ago would be worth $912 today. We read the jump as the market re-pricing AI risk, not new evidence on Tesla's business: the same stock that rose 12.9% today fell 10.8% on 5 November on the same theme.
