## What happened

Shares of sports-betting operator DraftKings ($DKNG) **plunged 26.9%** to $17.20 as of 10:15 a.m. ET on Friday, after the company said late on Thursday that spending on its new Predictions product would wipe out most of this year's profit and hold down next year's.

DraftKings now expects 2026 adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, adjusted for one-off items) of **$175 million to $275 million**, down from $700 million to $900 million, according to its results release and Benzinga. It also cut its 2026 revenue forecast to $6.35 billion to $6.55 billion, from $6.5 billion to $6.9 billion. Third-quarter revenue rose 16% from a year earlier to $1.33 billion, short of the $1.40 billion analysts expected in LSEG's I/B/E/S survey, Reuters reported.

The bigger blow was the first look at 2027. The company guided to adjusted EBITDA of $800 million to $1 billion, below the roughly $1.12 billion analysts had expected, according to Bloomberg. Revenue is forecast at $7.35 billion to $7.75 billion, about 17% growth at the midpoint, the release said.

## Why it matters

Predictions is DraftKings' answer to prediction markets such as Kalshi, which let customers trade contracts on the outcome of games and events. The company is buying customers with promotions: it plans $175 million to $225 million of Predictions investment in the fourth quarter alone, and expects the product to barely register in 2026 net revenue because promotions offset most of what it takes in, the release said.

Analysts kept their ratings but cut their targets. BMO lowered its price target to $26 from $50 and Bank of America to $23 from $27, Investing.com reported, with BofA pointing to the size of the spending while the legal status of sports event contracts is still unsettled. The selling spread: Flutter, owner of FanDuel, fell about 8% and Penn Entertainment about 4%, 24/7 Wall St. reported.

## What the market is telling us

DraftKings moved 5% or more on 25 of the last 252 sessions, but Friday's drop is far larger than its usual swings. The stock was already down 31.8% this year before Friday, and it traded as low as $16.60 in the morning, below its 52-week closing low of $18.59 from 2 October. $1,000 invested in DraftKings five years ago would have been worth $539 at Thursday's close.

Our read: revenue still grew 16% in the quarter, so the market is pricing the Predictions bill and the doubt about whether it pays back, not a business in decline. The test is whether Predictions reaches the $650 million to $750 million of 2027 revenue the company is guiding to as its spending falls, and the first check on that comes with fourth-quarter results early next year.
