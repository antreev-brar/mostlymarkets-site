## What happened

Shares of life-science tools and diagnostics company Revvity ($RVTY) **soared 11.4%** to $168.86 by 10:30 a.m. ET on Tuesday, after its third-quarter sales grew faster than it had forecast and it raised its growth outlook for the year. The stock traded as high as $171.74, a new 52-week high.

Adjusted earnings came to **$1.37 a share** on revenue of $729 million, against the $1.30 and $694.7 million analysts expected, Investing.com and GuruFocus reported. Organic growth, which strips out currency moves and deals, was 7% on a pro forma basis, above the mid-single-digit rate the company had guided to: 9% in life sciences and 5% in diagnostics. The adjusted operating margin widened to 29.4% from 26.0% a year earlier.

Revvity now expects pro forma organic growth of about **5%** for both the fourth quarter and the full year, up from a range of 4% to 5%, and adjusted EPS of $5.40 to $5.45, according to the results and the call transcripts published by Investing.com and Benzinga.

## Why it matters

Revvity sells research instruments and reagents to drug companies and labs, and diagnostic tests to hospitals, two markets that stalled after the pandemic. Our data shows how long the slump lasted: revenue fell 5.5% a year over five fiscal years, the weakest among its peers. A 9% organic rise in life sciences is the clearest sign yet that research spending is recovering.

The higher margin matters as much as the growth. Revvity's operating margin over the last 12 months, at 12.3% on a reported basis, sits in the bottom quarter of its peers, so every point of improvement lifts earnings faster than sales.

## What the market is telling us

Revvity's shares moved 5% or more on just 15 of the last 252 sessions, and if today's gain holds it will be the stock's biggest one-day rise in at least five years of our price data. The stock was up 56.7% this year at Monday's close, though $1,000 invested five years ago would still be worth only $860.

At about 30 times forward earnings, against a peer median of 19 times, the price already assumes the recovery continues. The fourth quarter, which management now expects to grow about 5% organically, is the next check on whether 7% was a peak or a new pace.
