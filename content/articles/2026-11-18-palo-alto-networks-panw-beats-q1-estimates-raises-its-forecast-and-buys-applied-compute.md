Palo Alto Networks ($PANW) reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of **$3.44 billion** on Wednesday, up 39% year on year and ahead of the $3.31 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC. Adjusted earnings of $1.05 a share beat the 97-cent consensus. The stock rose **8.6% after hours** to $369.19 as of 5:15 p.m. ET, on top of a 9.9% gain in the regular session.

## What stood out

The number investors watch is next-generation security ARR, the annual run-rate of Palo Alto's cloud, AI-driven operations and network-security subscriptions. It reached **$9.73 billion**, about $170 million above the top of the range the company gave in September. Chief Executive Nikesh Arora called it "one of our highest NGS ARR beats ever" in the results release, tying the demand to "an unprecedented threat environment."

The growth is partly bought. The year-ago quarter came before Palo Alto absorbed identity-security company CyberArk, and the release doesn't split acquired revenue from the rest. The cost shows in two lines: diluted shares rose to 838 million from 709 million, and amortization of acquired intangibles jumped to **$282 million** from $39 million.

That is why GAAP operating income fell to $96 million from $309 million while the adjusted figure rose to $1.06 billion. GAAP net income of $482 million was lifted by a $457 million tax benefit, which the release says included a one-time change in its tax position, so the adjusted numbers are the cleaner read of the quarter.

## The outlook

Chief Financial Officer Dipak Golechha said the company is "materially raising" its full-year guidance, and it did across the board. Revenue is now expected at $14.52 billion to $14.57 billion, growth of 26% to 27%, up from 23% to 24% in September. The ARR target rose by $375 million at the midpoint, to $11.475 billion to $11.525 billion.

For the second quarter, Palo Alto guided to revenue of $3.53 billion to $3.55 billion and adjusted EPS of $1.01 to $1.03. Alongside the results, it agreed to buy Applied Compute, a startup founded in 2025 by three former OpenAI researchers that helps companies tune and run open-weight AI models, in an all-stock deal worth $4.5 billion, CNBC and Investing.com reported. It expects the deal to close in its fiscal third quarter.

## Our take

The stock fell 9.3% the day after its September report; this time demand accelerated and the revenue forecast went up by about $400 million. The open question is how much of that is organic, and the all-stock Applied Compute deal adds another layer of dilution to a share count that is up 18% in a year.

At 81 times forward earnings against a software median of 25 in our data, the price already assumes the growth keeps coming. The stock is up 84.5% this year but closed 19.1% below its 6 October high of $419.91.
