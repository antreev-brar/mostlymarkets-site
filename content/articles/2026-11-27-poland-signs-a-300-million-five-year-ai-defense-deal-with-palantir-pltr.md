On Friday, Poland's defense ministry signed a five-year contract with Palantir ($PLTR) worth about **$300 million**, Reuters and Bloomberg reported, for the Maven Smart System, the AI battlefield decision-support software that NATO and the US military already use. Defense Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz signed it at the Polish Army Museum in Warsaw. Palantir shares were **up 3.1%** at $168.63 in pre-market trading as of 9:13 a.m. ET.

It is Poland's first binding contract with the company, after a letter of intent signed with Chief Executive Alex Karp in October 2025, Quartz and TechTimes reported. Spread evenly, the deal is worth about $60 million a year, less than 1% of the $7.29 billion of revenue Palantir booked in the 12 months to September 2026. Its weight is as a European reference: the purchase cuts against efforts by several allies to depend less on US defense technology, Quartz noted, and the ministry said Palantir will have no access to Polish military data.

The stock is 21.8% below its 9 October closing high, and the next read on its government business is its fourth-quarter report, which the company has not yet dated.
