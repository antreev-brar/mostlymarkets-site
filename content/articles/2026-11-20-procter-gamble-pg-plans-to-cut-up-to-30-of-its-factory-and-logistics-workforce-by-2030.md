Procter & Gamble ($PG) plans to cut its manufacturing and logistics workforce, employees and contractors, by **20% to 30% by 2030**, Chief Product Supply Officer Luc Reynaert told investors at a company event, Bloomberg and HR Katha reported on Thursday and Friday. Chief Financial Officer Andre Schulten said P&G is targeting up to $10 billion in productivity savings over fiscal years 2027 to 2030.

The plan leans on automation: P&G already runs 15 fully automated sites with no staff on site and plans more of them, the reports said. Manufacturing employed about 52,000 people, roughly half its workforce, in a May 2025 filing cited by HR Katha, so a 20% to 30% cut on that base would be roughly 10,000 to 16,000 roles. It follows a plan to eliminate 7,000 office jobs over two years, and comes as higher oil prices and weaker demand squeeze the margins of consumer-goods makers.

The shares **rose 0.9% to $169.37** on Friday, as investors moved into defensive stocks during the [day's AI sell-off](/news/2026-11-20/stock-market-today-the-s-p-500-enters-a-correction-as-nvidia-sinks-14/). The next check on whether the savings reach profit is P&G's next quarterly report.
