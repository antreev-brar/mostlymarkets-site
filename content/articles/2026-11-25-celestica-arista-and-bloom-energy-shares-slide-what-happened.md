## What happened
Shares of contract server builder Celestica ($CLS) **fell 13.1%** by 10:30 a.m. ET on Wednesday, fuel-cell maker Bloom Energy ($BE) lost 11.6% and data-center network maker Arista Networks ($ANET) 8.0%, as the AI sell-off spread from chips to the hardware around them. Connector maker Amphenol ($APH) fell 6.2% and cooling and power supplier Vertiv ($VRT) 6.3% in our data.

None of the four had news of its own; Seeking Alpha noted Celestica's drop came with no company news and put the stock at a new 52-week low. CNN and Yahoo Finance tied the day's sell-off to doubts that AI spending will pay off, the US tariff proposals on 14 trading partners and October inflation data that points to a December rate hike. The credit market added to it: the cost of insuring the debt of Oracle, Alphabet, SpaceX and CoreWeave against default hit records, Reuters and StockAnalysis reported, the same worry that [sent Oracle down 8% today](/news/2026-11-25/why-oracle-orcl-stock-is-down-today/).

## Why it matters
These companies are paid when data centers get built: Celestica assembles the servers and network switches, Arista sells the switches, Amphenol the connectors and Bloom the on-site power. If the borrowers behind the build pay more for money or slow down, their orders are the ones that move. So the stocks trade on the market's guess about next year's capital spending, not on this quarter, which is why they fell together with [Micron and the AI chipmakers](/news/2026-11-25/micron-astera-labs-and-credo-shares-slide-what-happened/).

## What the market is telling us
Celestica's shares have moved 5% or more on 75 of the last 252 sessions, and its last drop this big was on 20 November, when it fell 16.0%. At Tuesday's close of $196.64, its lowest of the past year, the stock was down 33.5% this year and 58.4% below its 2 June high; Wednesday's price is lower still. Arista, by contrast, closed Tuesday only 22.2% below its high and up 28.6% this year, and Amphenol 17.1% below. We think the gap says the market is selling hardest where AI is most of the business, as it is for Celestica and Bloom, and less where it is one customer among many, as for Amphenol.
