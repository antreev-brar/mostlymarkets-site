Williams-Sonoma ($WSM) reported fiscal third-quarter adjusted earnings of **$2.19 a share** on Thursday morning, a penny above the $2.18 Zacks consensus, on revenue of $1.99 billion, just short of the roughly $2.00 billion analysts expected. Refunds of tariffs collected under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act lifted the gross margin, and the company only reiterated its full-year outlook. The shares **fell 6.9% to $242.21** in premarket trading as of 9:13 a.m. ET, after closing at $260.09, up 4.0%, on Wednesday.

## What stood out

The margin is the story. GAAP gross margin **rose 1.5 points** to 47.6%, but about 1.4 points of that came from the tariff refunds, the release shows and Seeking Alpha reported, so the underlying gain was close to nothing. The refunds cut the cost of goods by $29.3 million, the company said, and its adjusted EPS of $2.19 included that help, Seeking Alpha reported.

Demand held up better than the margin. Comparable brand revenue **grew 5.1%**, after 6.2% in the second quarter. Chief Executive Laura Alber credited the brands and the company's operating platform, according to the release.

## The outlook

Williams-Sonoma kept its full-year forecast of net revenue growth of 4.7% to 7.2%, comparable brand revenue growth of 4.0% to 6.5%, and an adjusted operating margin of **17.8% to 18.2%**. It had raised that outlook in August. StockStory called the quarter in line with expectations on both revenue and EPS, which is what the selling punished: a stock priced for beats got a match.

## Our take

The business is fine: 5.1% comparable growth and a 17.4% operating margin are good numbers for a home-furnishings seller, and its 19.2% operating margin over the past 12 months ranks above the median of its retail peers in our data. The problem is the price. The stock was up 45.6% this year into the report and trading at 26.6 times trailing earnings, against a two-year median of 21.7 times, so a quarter whose margin gain came from a one-off refund wasn't enough. The number to watch is the holiday quarter's gross margin excluding refunds, due with the next report in March.
