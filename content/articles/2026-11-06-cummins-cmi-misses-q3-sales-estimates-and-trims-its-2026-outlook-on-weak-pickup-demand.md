Cummins ($CMI) reported third-quarter sales of **$9.44 billion** before the open on Thursday, up 13% from a year earlier but short of the $9.89 billion Zacks consensus cited by AP; StockStory, using S&P Global's estimate, put the miss at about 5%. Earnings of $8.25 a share included a $0.54 tax benefit, and without it came to $7.71, below the $8.38 analysts expected. Cummins also cut its 2026 revenue growth forecast to about 10%, from 10% to 13%, and the shares **closed down 4.9% at $448.58**, on a day the S&P 500 fell 4.4%.

## What stood out

**Data centres carried the quarter.** Power generation sales, mostly backup generators, rose **22% to $1.56 billion**, and the Power Systems segment earned a quarter of its sales as EBITDA. "Global demand for power generation equipment remains exceptionally strong, driven by data center buildout," Chief Executive Jennifer Rumsey said in the release.

*Verdict: the best part of the business is getting bigger and more profitable at the same time.*

**Pickups were the hole.** Rumsey said orders for pickup truck engines have weakened, while heavy-duty truck demand in North America strengthened; the Engine segment's sales still rose 20%. On the call, management said about half the sales shortfall came from supply-chain limits that pushed roughly $200 million of shipments into the fourth quarter, and half from the weaker market, according to the transcripts published by Motley Fool and Benzinga.

*Verdict: a timing problem and a demand problem, and only the first one fixes itself.*

## The outlook

Cummins now expects 2026 revenue to grow **about 10%**, the bottom of its old 10% to 13% range, with an EBITDA margin of about 18.2%. "We have updated our full-year revenue and profitability guidance primarily due to continued weaker than expected pickup truck demand in North America," Rumsey said, adding that fourth-quarter revenue should step up from the third quarter and set a record.

## Our take

We were impressed by the margins: a record 18.9% EBITDA margin in a quarter when sales fell short says the mix is shifting toward power generation. On the other hand, a revenue miss of this size and a lower forecast are what moved the stock, and the reported EPS beat on some tallies rests on a one-off tax benefit. Overall, a mixed quarter that doesn't change the data-centre story but shows the truck side can still drag.

The drop came on top of a slide: Cummins is down 13.3% over the past month and 38.4% below its June high, so a lot of disappointment was already in the price. At 13.9 times forward earnings against an industrial median of 21.2 by our data, the market is pricing it more like a truck-cycle company than a data-centre supplier. Fourth-quarter results early next year will show whether the promised record quarter arrives and the $200 million of delayed shipments lands.
