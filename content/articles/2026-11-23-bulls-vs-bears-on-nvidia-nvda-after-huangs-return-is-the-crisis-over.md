On Monday morning Nvidia's board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as chief executive, 11 days after he resigned, and the stock **jumped 30.0%** to $163.16 by 9:51 a.m. ET ([our story](/news/2026-11-23/nvidia-nvda-shares-soar-29-before-the-open-after-it-reinstates-jensen-huang/)). The question now splitting Wall Street is whether that ends Nvidia's crisis or only its most visible part.

The number both sides are arguing about is the discount. At Friday's close of $125.53, Nvidia ($NVDA) traded at about 10 times forward earnings, half the chipmaker median, after the SEC widened its probe to the company's accounting ([our analysis](/news/2026-11-23/nvidias-crisis-has-moved-from-its-boss-to-its-books-what-the-evidence-says/)). Bulls say Huang's return should close much of that gap; bears say the gap was about the books, not the boss.

## The bull case
### Dan Ives, analyst, Yorkville & Co.
**The board had no choice, and a founder-less Nvidia was the bigger risk.** Ives told CBS News on Monday that Huang's resignation was becoming "an economic and global markets issue," and wrote in a client note, reported by CBS and Yahoo Finance, that a founder-less Nvidia was the bigger risk to shareholders. His argument rests on Huang as the person who holds Nvidia's customer and government relationships together.

### Gil Luria, head of technology research, D.A. Davidson
**The decision removes both of last week's big risks.** "This step removes the two big risks that NVIDIA was dealing with last week - concerns about violations of export controls and a messy CEO transition," Luria said, Reuters and the Globe and Mail reported. His reasoning: if the board and the administration are willing to clear Huang, they are signaling that the export-control concern around him is resolved.

### Adam Crisafulli, founder, Vital Knowledge
**Huang is close to irreplaceable.** Crisafulli said Huang built Nvidia into an AI juggernaut while running an unusually flat company, which made him hard to replace, CNBC and CBS News reported. That is the case for the stock's size of move: a flat structure leans on one person, so getting him back restores most of what the exit took away.

## The bear case
### Ipek Ozkardeskaya, senior analyst, Swissquote Bank
**A sharp bounce isn't a recovery while fear stays this high.** "As long as volatility remains elevated, even a sharp bounce is hard to read as the start of a sustainable recovery," she said on Monday, Reuters reported via Investing.com and Yahoo Finance. She argued the AI frenzy built a bubble that has burst; the Cboe Volatility Index closed Friday at 35.89, Reuters reported.

### Muddy Waters Research, short seller
**The problem is in the sales, not the corner office.** Muddy Waters said on 17 November that it is betting against Nvidia, questioning ties between Nvidia's former head of global sales and a former Super Micro Computer executive, TheFly and TheStreet reported ([our brief](/news/2026-11-17/sec-probes-nvidia-nvda-disclosures-and-muddy-waters-says-it-is-short/)). It accused no one of a crime; its bet is that what investigators find about where the chips went matters more than who signs off on the next deal.

### Scott Bessent, Treasury Secretary
**Washington still wants answers.** On Sunday, the day before the reinstatement, Bessent said on CBS's "Face the Nation" that "we do need answers" on Nvidia's chip sales to China, Reuters reported ([our brief](/news/2026-11-22/bessent-says-the-us-still-needs-answers-from-nvidia-nvda-on-its-china-chip-sales/)). He had earlier given the board about 10 days to answer export questions, a window that closes around Wednesday. Nvidia's release on Monday says it will keep cooperating with government investigations and that the board's own inquiry continues.

## Where they agree
Nobody on either side argues the business is broken: Nvidia guided to $130 billion of quarterly sales on 17 November. And even the bulls question the process. Luria also called the reversal a textbook example of poor governance, Reuters reported, saying the board could not have finished its due diligence in so short a time. Fortune noted that the chair had told investors days earlier that the board did not intend to bring Huang back.

## The number that decides it
The forward P/E is the scoreboard. At $163.16, Nvidia trades at 11.3 times expected earnings on our quote data, still far below its peers, which tells us the market has priced the leadership fix but not the inquiries. The next facts arrive fast: the Treasury's deadline around Wednesday, 25 November, and Huang's closed-door House interview on 1 December.

## Our read
The bulls win today's argument and lose the next one. Huang's return removed a real risk, and a 30% rebound that, by CNBC's count, recovers nearly all the losses since he disclosed his affair is a fair price for that. But the discount the bears point to was there before Huang resigned, and it is still there at 11.3 times earnings: it is a discount for what the SEC, California and the Treasury might find. **Too early to tell** on the crisis as a whole; the Treasury's answer this week is the first date that will tell.
