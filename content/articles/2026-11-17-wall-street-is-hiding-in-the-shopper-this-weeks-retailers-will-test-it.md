The story investors are telling this month is that the American shopper is the safe place to wait out the AI sell-off: people keep spending, so the stores they spend in are worth paying up for. The National Retail Federation set the tone on 15 October, forecasting holiday sales up 4.2% to a record $1.013 trillion, and the money has followed since Nvidia's slide began.

The test comes this week. October retail sales from the Census Bureau are due at 8:30 a.m. ET today, Tuesday 17 November, Target ($TGT), Lowe's ($LOW) and TJX ($TJX) report before Wednesday's open, and Walmart ($WMT) reports before Thursday's, Investing.com and Opening Bell Daily reported. In our data, Costco ($COST) **rose 12.6%** in the month to Monday's close, to a 52-week closing high, while Nvidia ($NVDA) fell 27.1%.

## The story in one sentence

> "NRF forecasts 2026 holiday sales growth as consumers remain resilient." (National Retail Federation, 15 October 2026)

The chain runs like this: if jobs and wages hold, households keep spending despite 5% Treasury yields and high gas prices; if they keep spending, retailers' sales and profits grow steadily while AI earnings look uncertain, so investors pay more for them. It is a defensive trade, and it only works if the shopper does not crack.

## The evidence for it

### 1. Retail sales have grown for 13 straight months

The CNBC/NRF Retail Monitor showed core retail sales, which leave out cars, gas stations and restaurants, of **$454.2 billion** in October, up 3.36% from a year earlier and up 0.26% from September, the NRF said on 11 November. It was the 13th monthly gain in a row, Retail Insight Network reported.

### 2. Shoppers feel better than they did a month ago

The University of Michigan's preliminary November sentiment index rose to **51.0** from 48.0, the highest since August and above the 48.0 economists expected, StockAnalysis and InvestingLive reported. Year-ahead inflation expectations eased to 4.3% from 4.7%, the ABA Banking Journal reported. A final reading comes on Friday, 20 November.

### 3. The first two reports this week beat

Home Depot ($HD) said on Tuesday that comparable sales rose **2.2%**, ahead of the 1.8% StreetAccount expected, and lifted its sales forecast to the top of its range ([our recap](/news/2026-11-17/home-depot-hd-tops-q3-estimates-and-names-cfo-richard-mcphail-ceo/)). Disney ($DIS) said guest spending per head rose 4% at its US parks in its fiscal fourth quarter, and US attendance rose 3% excluding an extra week in the quarter ([our recap](/news/2026-11-17/disney-dis-tops-q4-estimates-and-raises-its-dividend-33/)).

### 4. The holiday forecast is above the long-run pace

The NRF's 4.2% forecast for November and December compares with 3.1% growth last year and a 3.1% average over the past 20 years excluding 2020 and 2021, the NRF and Retail Dive reported. Chief Economist Mark Mathews said about half of the growth should come from real spending and half from higher prices.

## The case against it

The doubters are on two fronts: those who say the spending is mostly inflation, and those who say the stocks have already priced in a perfect shopper. Three points, strongest first.

1. **The stocks are expensive.** Walmart trades at about 40 times expected earnings for the next 12 months, close to its 10-year high, by Zacks's count, and Benzinga put it at 44 times. Nvidia, after its fall, trades at about 13 times, Crypto Briefing and The Kobeissi Letter reported. A shopper who merely holds up may not be enough at those prices. This is the strongest objection.
2. **Growth is price, not volume.** Home Depot's customers came in 0.5% less often and spent 3.0% more per visit. The NRF expects about half of the holiday growth to come from higher prices, not more goods.
3. **The pace is slowing, and the bills are rising.** October's 3.36% retail growth was slower than September's 3.73%, by the NRF's own figures. "If gas prices remain higher, we do expect that this will have a larger impact on households at the lower end of the income spectrum," Mathews said on 15 October, as reported by the NRF and the Associated Press.

## What would change minds

- **October retail sales, 8:30 a.m. ET today.** A fall in the control group, the measure that feeds into GDP, would be the first official crack in the story.
- **Target and TJX on Wednesday, 18 November.** Target sells more of what households cut first, such as clothes and homewares; a falling comparable-sales number there would say the lower-income shopper is giving way.
- **Walmart on Thursday, 19 November.** At about 40 times earnings, anything short of a raised forecast would test whether investors keep paying for safety. If Nvidia's results tonight restore faith in AI earnings, the money that came in could leave just as fast.

The rotation is not across the board: TJX and Ross Stores ($ROST) have fallen over the past month, so investors are choosing the biggest chains, not retail as a whole.

## Our read

**Partly supported.** Spending is still growing, sentiment has turned up, and Home Depot and Disney both beat this morning. The weakest link is the price: retail volumes are flat to down, and a Walmart at about 40 times earnings needs more than a shopper who keeps up with inflation. The number we will watch is Walmart's US comparable sales on Thursday, the clearest read on whether the trade has a business under it.
