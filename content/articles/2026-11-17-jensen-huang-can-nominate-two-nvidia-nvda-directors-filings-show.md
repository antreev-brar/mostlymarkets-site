Former Chief Executive Jensen Huang can nominate two directors to the board of Nvidia ($NVDA), Reuters reported on Tuesday, citing the company's proxy and corporate filings. Nvidia's rules let a shareholder who has held more than 3% of the voting power for at least three years put nominees on the company's own ballot, and Huang owns about 3.5%.

It gives the co-founder a route back to influence, hours after Chair Harvey Jones said the board does not intend to reinstate him as chief executive, CNBC and Reuters reported ([our results story](/news/2026-11-17/nvidia-nvda-tops-q3-estimates-and-guides-to-130-billion-in-its-first-report-without-huang/)). On Tuesday Huang said he would be honored to return ([our story](/news/2026-11-17/jensen-huang-says-he-would-be-honored-to-return-as-nvidia-nvda-ceo/)). Nominees would still need shareholder votes, and Nvidia can exclude anyone who has broken its bylaws, Reuters reported.

The test comes at the annual meeting, usually held in June; the board's CEO search, run by Heidrick & Struggles ([our story](/news/2026-11-17/nvidia-nvda-hires-heidrick-for-its-ceo-search-and-talks-to-arms-rene-haas/)), may name a successor before then.
