New York Fed President John Williams said on Monday that the Fed must act "over time" to get inflation back to its 2% goal, and that he expects inflation to end 2026 at about 3.5%, slow to just above 2% next year and reach the target only in 2028, Reuters and Bloomberg reported. In remarks at the University of Mount Saint Vincent in New York, he said risks to inflation are still tilted higher, with energy prices a key driver, and stopped short of committing to a move at the Fed's 8-9 December meeting.

Williams is the Fed's vice chair for its rate-setting committee, so his view carries weight. Markets broadly expect a quarter-point hike in December from the current 3.75% to 4.00% range, with more next year, Reuters reported, and the 2-year yield, which follows rate expectations, rose to **4.68%**. A week ago he said the situation didn't call for "dramatic action," against Governor Christopher Waller's case for a half-point move ([the debate](/news/2026-11-18/the-debate-over-decembers-hike-waller-says-50-williams-says-no-dramatic-action/)); we set out why the Fed isn't expected to rescue this sell-off in [our analysis](/news/2026-11-22/why-wall-street-thinks-the-fed-wont-rescue-this-sell-off/).

The next test is October's PCE inflation data, the Fed's preferred gauge, due on Wednesday ([our week ahead](/news/2026-11-22/the-week-ahead-dell-hpe-pce-inflation-and-a-thanksgiving-short-week/)).
