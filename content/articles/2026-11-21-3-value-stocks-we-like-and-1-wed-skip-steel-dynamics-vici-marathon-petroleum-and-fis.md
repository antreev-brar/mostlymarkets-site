The S&P 500 closed Friday 20 November in a correction, and the selling has pushed a group of profitable companies to prices that assume very little. A low price is only a bargain if the earnings behind it hold up, so this week's value list asks which cheap stocks are cheap for a passing reason and which are cheap because the profits are shrinking.

Our screen took the companies in our coverage whose revenue grew over the 12 months to September 2026, ranked them by forward P/E (price over the next year's expected earnings), and left out stocks we listed in the last 14 days. We then judged each on five years of growth, returns and cash against its own industry, using our analysis of SEC filings.

```picks
like: STLD, VICI, MPC
skip: FIS
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Steel Dynamics ($STLD)
**Forward P/E: 8.6 times, against a 15.3 median for the materials companies we cover**. Steel Dynamics makes steel in electric-arc mills that melt scrap, and recycles the scrap itself.

1. **It compounds faster than its industry.** Revenue grew **13.6% a year** over five fiscal years, more than twice the 6.3% materials median, and earnings per share grew 25.3% a year.
2. **It earns well on its capital.** Return on capital was about 15.4%, in the top quarter of its peers, and diluted shares fell 7.2% in two years as it bought back stock.
3. **More buybacks are coming.** On 9 November the board added a $3.0 billion repurchase authorization, about a tenth of its market value, the company's release said, as Nasdaq and Scrap Monster reported.

**The price:** 15.2 times trailing earnings, against a median of 20.5 times since November 2024, with the stock 27.2% below its June high after a 5.6% drop on Friday.

**What could go wrong:** free cash flow was only 3.5% of sales over two years, behind most peers, because of heavy spending on new mills. If steel prices fall before that spending pays off, the buyback leans on debt.

### 2. VICI Properties ($VICI)
**Forward P/E: 8.4 times, against a 34.4 median for the real estate companies we cover**. VICI owns casino properties, including Caesars Palace, and leases them to operators on long contracts.

1. **It has grown far faster than most landlords.** Revenue grew **26.7% a year** over five fiscal years, in the top tenth of its peers, though the last two years slowed to 4.5% a year.
2. **Cash earnings keep rising.** VICI raised its 2026 forecast for adjusted funds from operations, the cash measure REITs are judged on, to $2.47 to $2.48 a share in its third-quarter release, and set up a $2 billion buyback.
3. **The price already discounts trouble.** At $24.20, the stock trades at about 9.8 times the midpoint of that forecast, and at 0.9 times book value.

**The price:** 11.1 times trailing earnings, about its median of 11.2 times since October 2024, with the stock down 13.9% this year.

**What could go wrong:** it took a $458 million credit-loss charge in the third quarter, most of it tied to Caesars after a Moody's downgrade, the earnings-call transcript and CRE Daily reported. A cut to Caesars' rent would hit its largest income stream.

### 3. Marathon Petroleum ($MPC)
**Forward P/E: 4.8 times, against an 11.1 median for the energy companies we cover**. Marathon is one of the biggest US refiners, turning crude oil into gasoline, diesel and jet fuel.

1. **It returns cash at a pace few peers match.** Diluted shares fell **15.7% in two years**, the most among the energy companies we cover, and it returned $3.3 billion in the third quarter alone, its release said.
2. **Its returns are the best in the group.** Return on capital was about 39.8% over the 12 months to September, against an 11.8% energy median.
3. **This year is a boom.** Its refining margin was $45.70 a barrel in the third quarter, up from $17.60 a year earlier, and it raised its dividend 10%, the release said. Our [recap](/news/2026-11-03/marathon-petroleum-mpc-beats-q3-estimates-and-sells-two-refineries-for-4-2-billion/) has the quarter.

**The price:** 7.2 times trailing earnings, the bottom of its 7.2 to 29.4 range since November 2024, after a run that leaves the stock up 125.8% this year.

**What could go wrong:** the low multiple sits on peak margins. If the refining margin fell back to last year's $17.60, roughly 60% of this quarter's margin would go, and earnings with it.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Fidelity National Information Services ($FIS)
**Forward P/E: 4.6 times, against a 16.1 median for the payments and exchange companies we cover**. FIS runs the core computer systems of banks and processes their card payments.

1. **It has shrunk over five years.** Revenue fell **3.2% a year** over five fiscal years, the weakest of 44 peers, after it sold its Worldpay merchant business.
2. **Its returns are thin.** Return on capital was about 4.6%, behind most peers' 10.6%, after years of large deals.
3. **The outlook just got worse.** On 4 November FIS cut its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast to $5.82 to $5.89 from $6.15 to $6.24, sketched a 2027 in which that figure falls, paused buybacks to pay down debt and announced its finance chief's exit, Benzinga and Earnings Whispers reported. The stock fell about 23% that day.

**The price:** 3.9 times trailing earnings, flattered by gains outside its operations (net profit was 26.8% of sales against a 16.4% operating margin); the stock is down 61.1% this year.

**What would change our mind:** bank conversions delayed into 2027 going live on schedule, and a 2027 forecast that stops the earnings decline. Its fourth-quarter report early next year is the next check.
