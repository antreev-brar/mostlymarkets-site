Applied Materials and Cisco reported after the close on Thursday 12 November, the latest in a week of results from big companies. A beat on one quarter says little about the business, so we took the companies worth $10 billion or more that reported in that window and that we recapped, and judged each on its long-run record first.

The numbers come from SEC filings through `analyse.py`: growth over five fiscal years to 2025, margins, cash and returns over the 12 months to September 2026 (June for Becton Dickinson), each ranked against the company's own industry peers. Prices are as of 10:30 a.m. ET on Friday 13 November.

```picks
skip: HUM, CMI, BDX
like: VST
```

## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. Humana ($HUM)
**Operating margin, 12 months to September 2026: 1.5%.** Humana sells Medicare Advantage health plans to older Americans. In its third quarter it lost 70 cents a share on an adjusted basis, less than expected, and raised its 2026 floor to $9.50 a share, as we [reported](/news/2026-11-06/humana-hum-beats-q3-estimates-with-a-smaller-loss-and-raises-its-2026-outlook/).

1. **Earnings per share shrank 17.2% a year** over five fiscal years, the bottom tenth of 13 health insurers and services peers, whose median grew 3%.
2. Its 1.5% operating margin is less than half the peer median of 3.6%, and it is 4.4 points lower than five years ago: members grew to 6.46 million, but each one earns less.
3. Free cash flow was 1.7% of sales over two years, in the bottom fifth of its group, against a 2.6% median.

**The price:** 29.9 times forward earnings against a peer median of 17.6, after a 73.2% rise this year that leaves the stock 0.6% from its high. That price assumes the margin recovery has already happened.

**What would change our mind:** an operating margin back above the 3.6% peer median for a full year, which the 2027 Medicare rates and its fourth-quarter report in early 2027 will show.

### 2. Cummins ($CMI)
**Revenue growth, two years to September 2026: 2.4% a year.** Cummins makes diesel and natural gas engines, power generators and parts. Its third-quarter sales of $9.44 billion missed the Zacks consensus by 4.6%, and it cut its 2026 growth forecast to about 10%, as we [reported](/news/2026-11-06/cummins-cmi-misses-q3-sales-estimates-and-trims-its-2026-outlook-on-weak-pickup-demand/).

1. Revenue grew 11.2% a year over five fiscal years, ahead of the industrial median of 8%, but the last two years slowed to **2.4% a year**, the bottom fifth of 78 industrials.
2. Its 12.5% operating margin trails the 17.2% median and has gained only 0.5 points in five years, against 3.4 points for the median peer.
3. Free cash flow was 8.3% of sales over two years, below the 12.7% median.

**The price:** 14.3 times forward earnings, against 21.6 for industrials and its own trailing median of 24.6. It is cheap because growth stalled; UBS cut its target to $675 from $741 and Evercore ISI to $617 from $782 this week, TradingView reported.

**What would change our mind:** its Power Systems segment, whose sales grew 15% in the quarter, growing fast enough to lift total revenue growth back above the 6.5% peer median.

### 3. Becton Dickinson ($BDX)
**Return on capital, 12 months to June 2026: 4.8%.** BD makes syringes, catheters and lab equipment for hospitals. It beat fourth-quarter estimates with adjusted EPS of $4.15, but guided for low single-digit revenue growth in fiscal 2027, as we [reported](/news/2026-11-06/becton-dickinson-bdx-beats-q4-estimates-and-guides-for-slower-growth-in-fiscal-2027/).

1. Revenue grew 6.3% a year over five fiscal years, below the 10% median for 19 medical device makers, and the new guidance points slower.
2. Its 10.7% operating margin is in the bottom twentieth of its group, whose median is 18.4%.
3. **Net debt is 6.5 years of operating profit**, and each dollar of capital earns 4.8 cents, half the peer median.

**The price:** 16.8 times forward earnings against a peer median of 19.9, with the stock at a 52-week high after a 38.9% rise this year.

**What would change our mind:** operating margin closing on the 18.4% median while net debt falls, which its next annual report, for fiscal 2027, would show.

## 1 stock we like

### Vistra ($VST)
**Return on capital, 12 months to September 2026: 13.5%.** Vistra is a Texas-based power producer that sells electricity wholesale and to homes. Third-quarter adjusted EBITDA from ongoing operations rose 50% to $2.38 billion, and it raised its 2026 forecast and set a first range for 2027 of $7.7 billion to $8.5 billion, as we [reported](/news/2026-11-06/vistra-vst-jumps-as-q3-profit-rises-50-and-it-raises-its-2026-outlook/).

1. **Revenue grew 13.2% a year** over the last two years, the top fifth of 29 utilities, against an 8.7% median.
2. Its 13.5% return on capital is the best among 28 utilities in our data, whose median is 5.1%.
3. Free cash flow was 11.0% of sales over two years, while the median utility burned cash at -11.3%: it pays for its buybacks from cash, including the $1.5 billion more it authorized in the quarter.

**The price:** 15.0 times forward earnings, against 19.1 for utilities, and 18.2 times trailing earnings against its own median of 29.5 since late 2024. The stock is down 16.6% this year and 25.4% below its high; a Seeking Alpha author argued on 12 November that the price assumes an AI bust in power demand.

**What could go wrong:** net debt is 4.3 years of operating profit, so a fall in Texas power prices would squeeze it faster than a less indebted utility; its operating margin has also slipped 2.5 points in five years.
