## What happened

Shares of AI chip and hardware makers jumped on Tuesday morning. Power-chip maker Monolithic Power Systems ($MPWR) **jumped 8.3%** by 10:30 a.m. ET, data-center connectivity chipmakers Astera Labs ($ALAB) and Credo ($CRDO) both rose 6.9%, and server and storage makers SanDisk ($SNDK), Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE) and Dell ($DELL) gained 4% to 6%.

No company in the group had news of its own. The move was a rebound across the chip sector after steep losses, led by Micron ($MU), Nvidia ($NVDA) and AMD ($AMD), Yahoo Finance and TipRanks reported. AMD was up 5% ahead of its third-quarter results, due after the close today.

## Why it matters

The stocks bouncing hardest are the ones that fell hardest. From 6 October to Monday's close, Astera Labs **lost 41.1%**, Credo 36.6% and SanDisk 35.3%, while Nvidia fell 15.5%, by our price data; the selling came on worries about how long the AI spending boom can last, IBTimes and Yahoo Finance reported. Monday alone took 11.2% off Astera and 7.3% off Credo.

A bounce of this kind is not evidence that the worry has gone. It shows how much of the selling was positioning: these stocks swing on the mood around AI spending far more than on their own news. We set out both sides of that argument in [our bulls-and-bears piece on the chip sell-off](/news/2026-10-31/bulls-vs-bears-on-the-ai-chip-selloff-a-normal-pullback-or-the-peak/), and AMD's business in [our breakdown](/news/2026-11-02/amd-amd-has-signed-up-openai-and-meta-is-the-business-worth-the-price/).

## What the market is telling us

Astera Labs moved 5% or more on 94 of the last 252 sessions, so a 7% day is ordinary for it. Even after today's gain it is about 37% below its 6 October close, and Credo about 32%.

Our read: the rebound is a vote that the sell-off went too far, not a verdict. AMD's results and outlook after 4 p.m. ET tonight are the first hard evidence on whether the AI hardware trade has found a floor.
