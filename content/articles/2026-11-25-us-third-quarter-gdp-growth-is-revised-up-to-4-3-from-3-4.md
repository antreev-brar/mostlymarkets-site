The US economy grew at a **4.3%** annual rate in the third quarter, the Commerce Department said in its second estimate on Wednesday at 8:30 a.m. ET, up from the 3.4% it first reported, Seeking Alpha and FXStreet reported. The upgrade came mainly from higher investment and consumer spending, and it topped the roughly 3.5% economists expected, Benzinga reported.

Faster growth and firmer inflation point the same way for the Federal Reserve. The same morning, [October's core PCE inflation rose to 3.3%](/news/2026-11-25/core-pce-inflation-rose-to-3-3-in-october-a-tenth-above-forecasts-before-a-fed-hike/), a tenth above forecasts, and markets price a quarter-point hike on 9 December at about 98%. A 4.3% pace leaves officials little reason to worry that higher rates are choking demand.

The third quarter ended in September, so the figure says nothing about the AI and tariff shocks of November. The Fed's decision on 9 December is the next test.
