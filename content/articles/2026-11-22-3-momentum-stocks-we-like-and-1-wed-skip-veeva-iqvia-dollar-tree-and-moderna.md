The S&P 500 closed in a correction on Friday, 20 November, and the stocks still carrying six months of gains are not the AI names that led for two years. The top of our momentum screen is made of drug-industry suppliers, a vaccine maker and a discount store, so this week's list asks which of those runs rest on profits and which on hope.

Our screen ranked the companies we cover by their **share price change over the six months to 20 November**, from our price files, and left out stocks we listed in the last 14 days. We then judged the leaders on five years of growth, margins, cash and returns against their industries, from SEC filings.

```picks
like: VEEV, IQV, DLTR
skip: MRNA
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Veeva Systems ($VEEV)
**Six-month return: 66.2%, to 20 November**. Veeva sells cloud software that drugmakers use to run sales teams and clinical trials.

1. **It has compounded faster than its peers for five years.** Revenue grew **16.9% a year** over five fiscal years, in the top tenth of the health care companies it's ranked against, and earnings per share grew 18.2% a year.
2. **It is the most profitable in its group.** Its operating margin was 29.9% over the 12 months to July 2026, the highest of its peers, and that margin has widened 2.9 points in five years.
3. **The latest quarter raised the bar.** Revenue rose 18% to $928.0 million in the quarter to July, and Veeva lifted its full-year forecast to $3.682 billion to $3.687 billion, its release said, as Barchart and Zacks reported, with 12 of the 20 biggest drugmakers committed to its new Vault CRM.

**The price:** 43.6 times trailing earnings, below its median of 52.8 times since September 2024, with the stock 17.6% off its October high.

**What could go wrong:** at 27.6 times expected earnings it costs more than its peers' 15.9 median, so a slowdown in Vault CRM wins would cost the stock its premium. Third-quarter results come on 3 December.

### 2. IQVIA ($IQV)
**Six-month return: 46.4%, to 20 November**. IQVIA runs clinical trials for drugmakers and sells them health data and analytics.

1. **Per-share earnings compound far faster than sales.** Earnings per share grew **40.5% a year** over five fiscal years, in the top tenth of the 65 health care stocks we cover, while revenue grew 7.5% a year.
2. **The reason is margins and buybacks.** Its operating margin widened 6.9 points in five years, in the top quarter, and diluted shares fell 9.3% in two years.
3. **Orders are running ahead of sales.** Net new bookings in its research unit rose 15% to $3.2 billion in the third quarter, a book-to-bill ratio of 1.2, and it raised its full-year earnings forecast, its release said, as Finviz and Capital.com reported.

**The price:** 18.3 times expected earnings, about the 18.8 median of its peers, and 29.9 times trailing earnings against a median of 26.2 times since October 2024.

**What could go wrong:** net debt is **6.3 years** of operating profit, so a drop in drug-trial spending would hit a leveraged balance sheet. Its investor day on 1 December is the next test.

### 3. Dollar Tree ($DLTR)
**Six-month return: 45.3%, to 20 November**. Dollar Tree runs discount stores that now sell items at several price points, not only $1.25.

1. **It earns more on its capital than almost any store chain.** Return on capital was about **32.8%**, in the top tenth of the 35 consumer staples stocks we cover.
2. **It is buying back its shares fast.** Diluted shares fell 11.3% in two years, in the top tenth of its group for buybacks.
3. **The shrinking sales are a sale, not a slump.** Revenue fell 5.3% a year over five years because it sold Family Dollar to Brigade Capital and Macellum Capital in July 2025, the company said, as Retail Dive reported. Sales rose 7.0% in the latest quarter, above most peers.

**The price:** 20.1 times expected earnings, against 17.6 for its peers, and 13.1 times free cash flow.

**What could go wrong:** it reports third-quarter results before the open on Tuesday, 24 November, with the stock only 6.2% below its high, so a holiday forecast that disappoints shoppers' appetite for trading down would hit a stock priced for it.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Moderna ($MRNA)
**Six-month return: 316.0%, to 20 November**. Moderna makes mRNA vaccines and is spending heavily to turn its personalized cancer vaccine into a business.

1. **The profits aren't there yet.** Its operating margin was **-116%** over the 12 months to September 2026, the weakest of the 18 drugmakers we cover, even after a third-quarter profit.
2. **It burns cash.** Free cash flow was -78% of revenue over the same 12 months, and revenue still shrank 29.5% a year over the last two years.
3. **The spending is going up.** At its analyst day on 12 November it guided to cash costs of about $4 billion in 2027 and $4 billion to $4.5 billion a year through 2030, above its earlier plan, and the stock [fell 7.0%](/news/2026-11-13/moderna-mrna-sinks-7-as-it-plans-4-billion-a-year-of-cash-costs-for-a-cancer-push/).

**The price:** no P/E, because there are no full-year profits; 11.5 times book value after a gain of 561.2% this year.

**What would change our mind:** cancer-vaccine sales that move the company to cash breakeven before the 2028 target it gave, or a melanoma approval that brings revenue forward ([our story on the trial data](/news/2026-10-22/why-moderna-mrna-stock-is-sinking-today/)).
