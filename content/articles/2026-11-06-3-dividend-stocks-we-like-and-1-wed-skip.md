On Thursday 5 November, as the Nasdaq fell 5.5%, investors were reminded what a dividend is for: a cash return that doesn't depend on the next AI order. A high yield often means the market expects a cut, so we looked for payers whose cash comfortably covers the cheque, and the one where it barely does.

The screen: dividend yield at Friday's close, from our quotes, leaving out stocks in our lists of the last 14 days. For each, we compared the yearly dividend bill (yield times price times shares outstanding, our estimate) with free cash flow over the last 12 months from SEC filings, then checked growth and debt against peers.

```picks
like: VICI, FIS, PFE
skip: GIS
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Vici Properties ($VICI)

**Dividend yield: 7.3% at Friday's close.** Vici is a real-estate investment trust that owns casinos and leases them to operators.

1. **Its cash covers the dividend.** Operating cash flow was **$2.71 billion** in the 12 months to September 2026, about 1.4 times the roughly $2.0 billion a year the dividend costs, by our arithmetic.
2. **The guidance just went up.** On 29 October it raised its 2026 forecast for adjusted funds from operations, a REIT's main profit measure, to $2.47 to $2.48 a share, from $2.45 to $2.47, its release said, and approved its first buyback, of up to $2 billion, Investing.com and TradingView reported.
3. **Rents have compounded.** Revenue grew 26.7% a year over five years through acquisitions, in the top tenth of 30 real-estate peers.

**The price:** 11.4 times trailing earnings, about its median of 11.2 since October 2024, after a 10.4% fall this year to Thursday.

**What could go wrong:** it took $313 million of credit-loss charges tied to Caesars and is reviewing its Caesars regional lease, TradingView and Yahoo Finance reported. Its share count also rose 5.0% in two years, so a cut in Caesars rent would show up straight in the payout.

### 2. Fidelity National Information Services ($FIS)

**Dividend yield: 6.1% at Friday's close.** FIS runs the core software and payment systems that banks use to process accounts and transactions.

1. **It earns about three times its dividend.** Free cash flow was **$2.78 billion** in the 12 months to September 2026, about 3.1 times the roughly $0.9 billion dividend bill, by our arithmetic, and FIS kept its 2026 free-cash-flow forecast of $2.15 billion to $2.25 billion, its release said.
2. **The stock has priced in a lot of bad news.** On 4 November it cut its 2026 profit outlook and said 2027 adjusted EPS could fall by a high-single to low-double-digit percentage, Investing.com and StockTitan reported, and RBC Capital downgraded it. The stock is down 55.8% this year to Thursday.
3. **Per-share profit has grown.** EPS rose 23.9% a year over five years, above the median of 35 payment, exchange and asset-management peers, helped by a 5.7% cut in its share count over two years.

**The price:** 4.9 times forward earnings against a payments median of 17.9, and 5.3 times free cash flow.

**What could go wrong:** banking revenue is growing slowly and FIS said it will resume big buybacks only once debt falls to about 2.8 times earnings, Investing.com reported. If 2027 free cash flow falls below about $1.5 billion, the cover would get thin.

### 3. Pfizer ($PFE)

**Dividend yield: 5.9% at Friday's close.** Pfizer sells medicines from the blood thinner Eliquis to cancer drugs.

1. **It just raised its forecast.** On 3 November Pfizer raised its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast to $2.95 to $3.05, from $2.80 to $3.00, after a third-quarter beat, its release and BNN Bloomberg reported ([our recap](/news/2026-11-03/pfizer-pfe-beats-q3-estimates-and-raises-its-2026-forecast/)).
2. **The new drugs are growing.** Sales of products other than Covid treatments grew 7% at constant currency, and recently launched and acquired drugs brought in $3.8 billion, up 32%, the release said.
3. **Cash covers the payout.** Free cash flow was **$11.8 billion** in the 12 months to September 2026, about 1.2 times the roughly $9.8 billion dividend, by our arithmetic.

**The price:** 10.5 times forward earnings against a drugmaker median of 18.2.

**What could go wrong:** the cover is the thinnest of our three, and Eliquis, Ibrance and Xtandi lose patent protection over the next few years. EPS fell 3.6% a year over five years, so the new drugs have to keep growing above 30% to keep the cover from shrinking.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### General Mills ($GIS)

**Dividend yield: 6.9% at Friday's close.** General Mills makes Cheerios, Pillsbury dough and Häagen-Dazs ice cream.

1. **Its sales are shrinking.** Revenue fell 3.9% a year over the two years to August 2026, against 3.1% growth for the median of 33 consumer-staples peers, and fell 2.8% in the latest quarter, the weakest of the group.
2. **The cash only just covers the payout.** Free cash flow was $1.55 billion in the 12 months to August 2026, about 1.2 times the roughly $1.3 billion dividend, by our arithmetic, and its free-cash-flow margin fell to 8.5% from 9.7% over two years.
3. **Profits have gone negative.** Its operating margin was **-1.1%** over 12 months against a peer median of 13.6%, and net debt was $13.2 billion in August.

**The price:** 11.4 times forward earnings, among the cheapest of its peers, after a 21.6% fall this year to Thursday.

**What would change our mind:** General Mills is weighing a separation of Häagen-Dazs outside North America, Reuters and Dow Jones reported on Thursday, which could pay down debt. A sale that cuts net debt meaningfully, plus a return to sales growth, would make the yield safer.

Pfizer's cover is about as thin as General Mills'; the difference is direction. Pfizer's new drugs are growing at 32% and its forecast is rising, while General Mills' sales and margins are both going the wrong way.
