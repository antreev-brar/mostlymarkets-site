Fast growth under $50 a share is easy to find this earnings season; growth a company earned is harder. This week's results made the difference plain: Amcor ($AMCR) cut its profit outlook on Wednesday, Keurig Dr Pepper ($KDP) fell on 3 November when its new coffee business squeezed margins, and Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE) sank with AI hardware stocks after the midterms. Monster Beverage ($MNST) sold **18% more cases** of energy drinks in its quarter, with no acquisition behind it.

Our screen took the stocks under $50 a share with positive free cash flow and ranked them by revenue growth over the last 12 months, using SEC filings through the latest quarter and Friday's closes. Then we asked one question of the leaders: is the growth bought or built? We judged each on long-run growth, margins, returns on capital and debt against its peers.

```picks
skip: AMCR, KDP, HPE
like: MNST
```

## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. Amcor ($AMCR)

**Revenue growth, two years: 32.8% a year to September 2026.** Amcor makes flexible plastic and paper packaging and, since buying Berry Global in an all-stock deal, rigid plastic containers too.

1. **The growth came with shares.** Amcor's diluted share count rose **60.9%** in two years, the most among 23 materials peers, and earnings per share fell 4.6% a year over five fiscal years while the peer median rose 12.4%.
2. **The latest quarter was mostly price pass-through.** Net sales rose 7% to $6.13 billion in the September quarter, but about $435 million of the increase was higher raw material costs passed on to customers, the release said. Adjusted EPS slipped to $0.95 from $0.97, and Amcor cut its outlook for the six months to December to $1.70 to $1.80 a share from $1.80 to $1.90, citing energy and distribution costs, according to the call reported by MarketBeat and Zacks.
3. **The debt is heavy for a thin business.** Net debt is $14.0 billion, 7.4 years of operating profit, against a 7.9% operating margin and a 3.2% free cash flow margin over the last 12 months, both below the materials median.

**The price:** 11.1 times forward earnings against a peer median of 17.2, and 19.2 times trailing earnings against its own two-year median of 20.3. Cheap, for a reason.

**What would change our mind:** pricing catching up with costs by the December quarter, as management promised, and leverage heading to its target of about 3 times by the end of 2027.

### 2. Keurig Dr Pepper ($KDP)

**Revenue growth, latest quarter: 75.6% year on year to June 2026.** Keurig Dr Pepper sells Dr Pepper and Canada Dry sodas, Keurig coffee pods and brewers, and now JDE Peet's coffee brands.

1. **The jump is the JDE Peet's deal.** Third-quarter sales **rose 70.6% to $7.34 billion**, mostly from JDE Peet's, and gross margin fell 5.9 points to 48.7% on its lower-margin mix, Investing.com and GuruFocus reported. Management also trimmed its expectations for JDE Peet's on the call.
2. **Returns are low and the debt is high.** Return on capital was about 4.8% in the 12 months to June, against a 14.2% median for 27 staples peers, and net debt was $28.5 billion on 30 June, 8.7 years of operating profit.
3. **A split is still ahead.** The company plans to separate into a beverage company and a global coffee company once its milestones, including leverage at each, are met; it gave no date in its third-quarter release.

**The price:** 37.0 times trailing earnings against its own two-year median of 23.8, and 0.7% below its 52-week closing high after a 30.8% gain this year.

**What would change our mind:** coffee margins recovering and a separation date with leverage near the 3.5 to 4.25 times the company targets for the two companies.

### 3. Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE)

**Revenue growth, latest quarter: 33.7% year on year to July 2026.** HPE sells servers, storage and networking gear to companies and AI builders.

1. **Juniper did much of the work.** Networking revenue rose 74.9% to $2.89 billion in the July quarter, "primarily due to revenue attributable to Juniper Networks," which HPE bought in July 2025, according to its 10-Q as summarized by Quartr and Beancount. Over five fiscal years, revenue grew 4.9% a year, below the 5.6% median for 27 hardware peers.
2. **The margins are thin.** The operating margin was **6.2%** over the last 12 months against a peer median of 18.1%, and return on capital was 5.1%, in the bottom tenth.
3. **The share count is rising.** Diluted shares rose 8.8% in two years, while the median hardware peer's were flat.

**The price:** 9.7 times forward earnings against a peer median of 17.5. After a 39.1% fall from its 9 October high, the stock is still up 86.4% this year, so the market already prices a lot of AI server growth.

**What would change our mind:** networking growth at the top of HPE's 14% to 17% framework for fiscal 2027 and progress on the $600 million of Juniper cost savings it targets by fiscal 2028.

## 1 stock we like

### Monster Beverage ($MNST)

**Revenue growth, last 12 months: 20.2% to September 2026.** Monster makes Monster Energy and other energy drinks.

1. **The growth is its own.** Sales grew **12.5% a year over five fiscal years**, against a 4.6% median for 32 staples peers, and rose 16.7% to a record $2.56 billion in the third quarter as cases sold rose 18.0%, the release showed.
2. **It turns sales into cash.** Its free cash flow margin over two years was 22.2%, more than double the 10.0% peer median, and its operating margin of 28.5% over the last 12 months is in the top quarter of staples.
3. **The runway is abroad.** Sales outside the US rose 28.1% in the quarter to 47% of the total, led by Latin America, up 48.8%.

**The price:** 37.4 times forward earnings, more than twice the 17.7 peer median, and 41.2 times trailing earnings against its own two-year median of 40.2. The quality is fully priced; the stock isn't stretched against its own history.

**What could go wrong:** margins. Gross margin fell 1.0 point to 54.7% on aluminum and freight costs, and management expects can costs to keep rising through at least the first quarter of 2027, Yahoo Finance's call summary reported. Operating margin is already 6.3 points lower than five years ago.
