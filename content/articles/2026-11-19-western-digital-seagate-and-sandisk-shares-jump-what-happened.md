## What happened

Shares of data-storage makers Western Digital ($WDC), Seagate Technology ($STX) and Sandisk ($SNDK) **jumped 4% to 8%** in the morning session on Thursday, as Anthropic's record IPO revived buying in the AI hardware stocks that had led this month's sell-off. Western Digital was up 8.5% at $312.65 as of 9:45 a.m. ET, and by 10:15 a.m. Seagate was up 7.5%, Sandisk 4.4% and memory-chip maker Micron Technology ($MU) 4.7%.

Anthropic priced 980 million shares at $83 on Wednesday night, above its $70 to $78 range, and raised $81.3 billion, the largest IPO on record, CNBC reported ([our brief](/news/2026-11-18/anthropic-prices-its-ipo-at-83-a-share-above-its-70-to-78-range/)). 24/7 Wall St. and Parameter both tied the storage rebound to the debut, and Yahoo Finance reported that memory and chip stocks led a global rally on it.

Micron has a direct stake. It committed about $1 billion to a private placement that sits alongside the IPO, together with Nvidia ($NVDA), Microsoft ($MSFT) and others, Blockonomi and The Motley Fool reported from Anthropic's filings.

## Why it matters

Storage makers are paid by the gigabyte, and AI models are trained on, and answer from, enormous stores of data. When an AI lab raises $81.3 billion, much of it is headed for data centres, and the drives and flash chips inside them. That chain is why these stocks fell hardest when research firm SemiAnalysis cut its AI spending forecast on 16 November ([our story](/news/2026-11-16/sandisk-seagate-and-western-digital-shares-slide-what-happened/)), and why they bounce hardest now.

Sandisk has one more thing going on. Its 8-for-1 stock split takes effect at Friday's open, its 8-K filing says, so its share price will read about an eighth of today's from tomorrow, with no change in the company's value.

## What the market is telling us

Western Digital's shares moved 5% or more on 81 of the last 252 sessions, and the last rise at least this big came on 11 November, when it gained 8.3%. The stock is up 67.3% this year, but at Wednesday's $288.19 close it sat 61.4% below its 52-week closing high of $746.23 from 18 June. Seagate sat 52.8% below its June high and Sandisk 66.8% below its own.

We'd call this a rebound inside a bigger drawdown: the 11 November rally faded within days, and these stocks need a real order number, not someone else's IPO, to recover their June highs.
