Realty Income ($O), the landlord to 1,842 retail and industrial tenants that pays its dividend monthly, said after the close on Monday that adjusted funds from operations, the cash-earnings measure real estate trusts are judged on, **rose 3.7% to $1.12 a share** in the third quarter. Revenue grew 10% from a year earlier to $1.62 billion, and the company raised its 2026 AFFO forecast to $4.45 to $4.46 a share, from $4.44 to $4.45 in August. The shares closed up 1.1% at $57.58 and were little changed after hours, at $57.63 as of 4:13 p.m. ET.

We couldn't confirm a quarterly consensus from a named data provider in two outlets by the time we published. Going in, a Yahoo Finance preview said the Street expected 2026 AFFO of $4.44, the bottom of the old range, so the new forecast sits above it.

## What stood out

The portfolio is close to full and re-lets at higher rents. Occupancy held at **98.8%** across 15,536 properties, and on the properties it re-leased, new rent came to 103.5% of the old, the release said. Realty Income invested $2.6 billion in the quarter, $2.2 billion of it its own share, at a 6.8% initial cash yield, and opened a European joint venture with KKR ($KKR) backed by an initial €528 million.

The dividend is the product. Realty Income announced its 116th straight quarterly increase in September, and monthly dividends of $0.813 in the quarter took 72.6% of AFFO, the release said. That increase was small: the payout grew 0.7% from a year earlier, slower than AFFO.

## The outlook

Chief Executive Sumit Roy said in the release that the company has "fully prefunded our equity needs for the remainder of the year" and kept its $10.0 billion investment target. The new forecast implies fourth-quarter AFFO of about $1.10 to $1.11 a share, after $3.35 in the first nine months, so the year ends a little below this quarter's pace.

## Our take

A steady quarter with no surprises, which is what holders of a monthly-dividend stock pay for. The weak spot is per-share growth: Realty Income's diluted share count rose 7.3% over two years, against a flat median for real estate peers, according to its SEC filings, so growth through buying keeps getting shared among more shares. The stock is up 2.1% this year and 14.8% below its March high; the next test is whether the $10 billion of 2026 deals keeps spreads near the roughly 170 basis points Roy cited.
