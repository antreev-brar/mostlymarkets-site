Apple ($AAPL) closed at a record **$356.27** on Friday, 13 November, the same week Nvidia ($NVDA) lost its founder as chief executive and ended at a 52-week low. Apple is up 7.3% since 3 November, when it fell on a holiday-quarter margin forecast hit by memory-chip costs. This week Bernstein told clients Apple has become the safe "consumer compounder" of big tech, and the market is paying for it.

The argument is about one number: the price of that safety. Our data puts Apple at **39.7 times** its last 12 months of earnings, above its median of 36.1 since November 2024, while analysts quoted this month put it at 36 to 37 times next year's.

- **Why it matters:** Apple guided its December-quarter gross margin down to 45% to 46% from 47.9% because memory chips cost more, so the shares are rising as the profit outlook narrows.

## The bull case

### Mark Newman, analyst, Bernstein
**Apple is the shelter from the AI spending race, and it deserves the premium.** In a note reported by CNBC on 13 November and by Benzinga, Newman argued that Apple's shares no longer move with the mega-cap companies spending heavily on AI data centers, because Apple isn't one of them. "The market has long decoupled Apple from the rest of the mega-cap tech names and reclassified Apple to a consumer compounder," he wrote, keeping an Outperform rating and a **$380 target**, about 7% above Thursday's close.

### Amit Daryanani, analyst, Evercore ISI
**Demand is fine; the margin hit is a cost cycle, not a broken product.** After the 2 November report, Daryanani kept his Outperform rating and $380 target unchanged, pointing to healthy demand for iPhones and Macs, according to AppleInsider and CNBC. Evercore said the debate is moving to margins and earnings power as memory costs rise. The quarter backs the demand half: iPhone revenue rose 17% year on year, with only eight days of iPhone 18 Pro sales, Apple said on its call.

## The bear case

### Craig Moffett, analyst, MoffettNathanson
**The safety is real, but it's already in the price.** In a CNBC interview before Apple's report, reported by Stocktwits and Benzinga, Moffett called the roughly 36-times forward multiple demanding, an earnings yield of about 2.8%. "It's already priced in so much safety that you wonder whether it's overshot somewhat," he said. He also warned that higher iPhone prices could lead owners to keep their phones longer, since older models run current software for years.

### David Vogt, analyst, UBS
**A slowdown that barely touches earnings could still cut the multiple.** Vogt rates Apple Neutral. He argued that a one- or two-point slowdown in Services growth would do little to earnings per share but would weigh on the price investors pay for them, especially if hardware costs stay high, according to TipRanks and Proactive Investors. He called the fourth quarter generally in line, Yahoo Finance reported.

### Laura Martin, analyst, Needham
**Costs are rising for structural reasons, and a new CEO has to choose who pays.** Martin rates Apple Hold. She said Apple's economics are under pressure from structurally rising input costs and supply constraints that won't improve soon, according to Longbridge and Benzinga. Her test is whether Chief Executive John Ternus raises iPhone prices or absorbs the cost, which could slow earnings growth.

## Where they agree

Nobody here doubts the business. Our data shows a 33.0% operating margin over the last 12 months against a 18.2% median for 31 hardware makers, and a 28.4% free cash flow margin, both in the top quarter. Both sides also name the same swing factor: Ternus called memory pricing "unprecedented" on the 2 November call and said purchase prices would keep rising beyond December.

## The number that decides it

The December-quarter gross margin. Apple guided **45% to 46%**, down from 47.9% in the September quarter (about a point of which came from tariff refunds), and said most of the drop comes from memory. Apple usually reports that quarter in late January; a margin at the top of the range, with an outlook that stops falling, supports the bulls, while a figure under 45% would make a near-40 multiple harder to defend.

## Our read

The bulls have the business right: Apple's margins, cash and returns rank near the top of its industry, and it isn't spending its cash on data centers. The bears have the price right. At **39.7 times** trailing earnings, near the top of its 27.4x to 42.6x range since late 2024, Apple earns about 2.5% on its share price while the 10-year Treasury pays 5.14%, so the shares are pricing in a smooth ride through the memory squeeze. We think the evidence favors the bears on price today, and the January margin report is where that gets tested; our [recap of the September quarter](/news/2026-11-02/apple-aapl-beats-q4-estimates-but-memory-costs-squeeze-its-margin-forecast/) and our [breakdown of the business](/news/2026-11-01/apple-aapl-became-wall-streets-ai-haven-is-the-business-worth-the-price/) have the detail.
