The story the stock market is telling right now is that it can ride out a Fed that keeps raising rates: the S&P 500 **rose 2.5%** in the week to Friday, 13 November, even as the 10-year Treasury yield ended above 5% and futures moved to price a hike at the Fed's 9 December meeting as a certainty. Warren Pies of 3Fourteen Research disagrees. The market "is not priced for another two-plus hikes," which he thinks are probably coming, he told CNBC on 11 November.

The news peg was Tuesday's consumer price report. Core prices rose 0.3% in October for a third straight month, and Fed Governor Christopher Waller said he would consider a half-point hike in December if that pace continued, The Hill and the Seoul Economic Daily reported. By Thursday, fed funds futures priced three quarter-point hikes by March, CNBC and 36Kr reported, and Friday's [wholesale price report](/news/2026-11-13/wholesale-prices-rose-less-than-expected-in-october-but-the-annual-rate-hit-6-5/) did nothing to change that.

The stakes: in our data the S&P 500 sits 3.6% below its 52-week high, while existing-home sales just hit their slowest pace since 2010 and gold is within 1% of its 12-month low.

- **What's happening:** After a third straight 0.3% rise in core consumer prices, futures price three Fed hikes by March and the 10-year yield is back above 5%, but the S&P 500 rose 2.5% this week and sits 3.6% below its high.
- **Why it matters:** Each extra hike raises the rate used to value future profits and the cost of every mortgage and corporate loan. Stocks priced for one hike would have to adjust to three.
- **Our read:** Partly supported. Stocks can live with one hike, as they did in September, but housing shows rates already biting, and two more 0.3% core readings would make the bond market's path the base case.

## The story in one sentence

> "The Fed is the real risk going out in the intermediate term." Warren Pies of 3Fourteen Research, to CNBC, 11 November 2026

The chain runs like this: if core inflation keeps rising 0.3% a month, the Fed keeps hiking past December, so bond yields rise and the value investors put on profits years away falls. The stock market's bet is the other half of the chain: that earnings are growing fast enough to absorb it, as they did after the Fed's first hike in September.

## The evidence for more hikes

### 1. Core inflation is stuck at 0.3% a month
October's consumer prices rose 0.4% and core prices, which leave out food and energy, 0.3%, the same as in September, The Globe and Mail and Benzinga reported. A 0.3% monthly pace works out to about 3.6% a year, well above the Fed's 2% goal.

### 2. A Fed governor put a half-point on the table
Waller said he would consider backing a **50 basis-point** hike in December if core inflation keeps coming in at 0.3% or higher, CNBC and The Hill reported. Ed Yardeni of Yardeni Research said in a note that "such a move is warranted," Yahoo Finance and Benzinga reported.

### 3. Bonds moved the most in 19 months
Treasury yields had their biggest jump in 19 months on 10 November, CNN reported, and the 10-year rose about 10 basis points to 4.98%, briefly topping 5%, CNBC and The Globe and Mail reported. The 2-year yield sets the price of short-term borrowing and tracks where traders think the Fed is going.

### 4. Wholesale prices are still rising fast
Producer prices rose 0.3% in October, a little below forecasts, but **6.5%** from a year earlier, the highest since November 2022, the Labor Department said. Core producer prices held at 5.0% a year. Producer prices feed into consumer prices a month or two later.

### 5. Housing already shows the cost
Existing-home sales fell 5.5% in October to a 3.75 million annual pace, the slowest since August 2010, the National Association of Realtors said, with mortgage rates near their highest since 2000 ([our story](/news/2026-11-13/home-sales-fell-to-3-75-million-in-october-the-slowest-pace-since-2010/)). That is the part of the economy that feels rates first.

## The case against it

The doubters are on two fronts. On the Fed, even Waller said he leans toward a quarter-point hike in December, with a half-point only an option, Yahoo Finance and Seeking Alpha reported. On stocks, Goldman Sachs' chief US equity strategist Ben Snider argued around the September hike that the bull market would continue, citing strong earnings and balance sheets, Bloomberg and Investing.com reported.

1. **History favors stocks after a first hike.** Goldman's note found the S&P 500 fell 2% on average in the three months after the start of seven past hiking cycles but rose 9% over the following 12, Investing.com reported. A December hike that is already fully priced adds little new pressure on its own.
2. **The monthly heat is easing in places.** October's producer prices rose less than forecast, and core producer prices didn't speed up.
3. **Oil is falling.** US crude closed the week at $88.92 a barrel in our data, down from about $103 on 30 October, and energy drove much of this year's inflation.

The first is the strongest, but it describes one cycle, not three hikes in four months.

## What would change minds

- **The Fed's October minutes, Wednesday 18 November.** They will show how many officials already favored more than one more hike.
- **November's consumer prices, the next CPI report.** A fourth straight 0.3% core reading would meet Waller's own test for a half-point move; 0.2% or less would weaken the case for March.
- **The 10-year yield against its 52-week high of 5.37%.** A break above it would mean the bond market is pricing a longer cycle than stocks are.

## Our read

**Partly supported.** Stocks have absorbed one hike, as Goldman's strategists expected, and falling oil takes some heat out of next year's inflation. The weakest link is the gap Pies points to: futures price three hikes by March while the S&P 500 sits within 4% of its high, and housing shows what rates above 5% already do. The number we'll watch is November's core CPI: a fourth 0.3% reading would put the bond market's path in charge.
