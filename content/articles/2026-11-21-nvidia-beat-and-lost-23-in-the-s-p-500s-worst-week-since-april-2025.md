This was the week every piece of good news failed. Nvidia ($NVDA) beat its sales forecast by $6.7 billion on Tuesday, Anthropic raised a record $81.3 billion in its IPO on Wednesday, and Walmart ($WMT) raised its outlook on Thursday. The S&P 500 still **fell 8.6%** in five days to 6,910.39, its seventh straight loss and its worst week since April 2025, Reuters reported, and closed Friday in a correction, more than 10% below its 6 October record.

The selling started and ended in AI chips. Nvidia lost 22.9% for the week and memory maker Micron ($MU) 23.6%, as an SEC probe, new China curbs and a short seller's report piled onto worries that the AI buildout is slowing. The Fed did not help: its minutes showed almost every official expects more rate hikes.

Nvidia reported **$115.8 billion** of third-quarter revenue on Tuesday against $109.1 billion expected and guided to $130 billion, LSEG data cited by CNBC and Reuters showed ([our recap](/news/2026-11-17/nvidia-nvda-tops-q3-estimates-and-guides-to-130-billion-in-its-first-report-without-huang/)). ## What's next

All times ET. Markets are closed on Thursday, 26 November, for Thanksgiving.

- **Monday, 23 November:** Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE) and Agilent ($A) report after the close.
- **Tuesday, 24 November:** Analog Devices ($ADI), Medtronic ($MDT), Best Buy ($BBY) and Dollar Tree ($DLTR) before the open; Dell ($DELL), Autodesk ($ADSK), Workday ($WDAY) and HP ($HPQ) after the close.
- **Wednesday, 25 November:** Deere ($DE) before the open.
- **Later:** the Fed decides on 9 December, and Micron reports on 17 December.

We think Dell's report on Tuesday matters most: the AI server maker fell 19.3% this week and is 38.6% down over the past month, so its orders will show whether customers are actually pulling back or the market has run ahead of them.
