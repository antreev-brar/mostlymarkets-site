Alphabet's ($GOOGL) YouTube is finalizing a settlement worth about **$3 billion** with thousands of US school districts that say its app fueled student addiction, anxiety and depression, Bloomberg News reported on Friday, citing people familiar with the matter; Reuters carried the report. The deal would add safeguards for teenage users, and it depends on a majority of districts signing on. Google declined to comment.

The districts want money for costs such as counseling, and they blame features such as autoplay and endless scrolling. Meta ($META), Snap ($SNAP) and TikTok remain defendants in the same federal case, so a YouTube deal would set a price tag for claims the others still face. In May, YouTube, Snap and TikTok settled the first school-district case, brought by Breathitt County in Kentucky, before it reached trial, Bloomberg Law and Reuters reported.

Alphabet closed **2.7%** lower on Friday at $265.29 in a broad tech selloff; Meta fell 5.9% and Snap 11.1%. Any settlement needs approval from US District Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers in Oakland, California, and reports say finalizing it could take a few months.
