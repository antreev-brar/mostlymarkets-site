## How it got here

Take-Two Interactive ($TTWO) is the publisher behind Rockstar Games, maker of Grand Theft Auto and Red Dead Redemption, and 2K, maker of NBA 2K. For most of the last decade it lived off GTA V, released in 2013, which has sold nearly 235 million copies, the company said in its 9 November results.

In May 2022 it closed its $12.7 billion purchase of Zynga, the maker of mobile games such as Words With Friends and FarmVille, to move into phones, Engadget and Game Developer reported. The deal turned a steady earner into a loss-maker: Take-Two wrote down $3.55 billion of goodwill in fiscal 2025, its annual report shows, and our data shows an operating loss in every fiscal year from 2023 to 2026.

On Friday Take-Two said GTA VI took **$3.79 billion** of retail sales on its first day, almost five times GTA V's roughly $800 million debut, CNN and PC Gamer reported ([our brief](/news/2026-11-22/take-two-ttwo-says-gta-vi-took-3-79-billion-on-its-first-day/)). The stock rose 0.6%. **So is Take-Two a great business, or a great game inside an ordinary one?**

## The business in one line

Players pay Take-Two once for a new game, then keep paying, every week, for virtual currency, items and ads inside the games they already own.

## How it makes money

Take-Two reports net bookings, the value of what it sold in a period before accounting rules spread game sales over time. In the year to March 2026, bookings rose 19% to $6.72 billion, GameDev.net and Pocket Gamer.biz reported from the company's results.

The waiting showed in the last quarter. Net bookings for the three months to September fell 18% to $1.60 billion, below the company's $1.62 billion to $1.67 billion forecast, as Borderlands, Mafia and mobile games sold less and NBA 2K27 missed internal targets, GamesBeat and Gaming.net reported.

## The numbers that matter

### Growth
Revenue grew **14.6% a year** over five fiscal years, below the 17.2% median for the 15 consumer internet companies we rank it against, and much of that came from buying Zynga. Over the 12 months to September it rose 3.1%.

### Profitability
The operating margin over the last 12 months was **-2.8%**, against a peer median of 13.1%. In fiscal 2021, before Zynga, Take-Two earned $629 million of operating profit on $3.37 billion of sales, a margin of 18.7%.

### Cash
Free cash flow was 2.4% of sales over two years and **1.5%** over the last 12 months, in the bottom fifth of its peers, whose median over two years is 17%.

### Returns
Return on equity was -6.5%, against a peer median of 22.5%. The Zynga deal tied up billions of dollars of capital that hasn't yet earned a return.

### Balance sheet
Net debt was $2.0 billion at 30 September, with $1.15 billion of cash against $3.16 billion of debt. Diluted shares rose 6.7% over two years, the worst record in its peer group, where the median company shrank its count slightly.

## Why the stock is where it is

The shares closed Friday at $210.00, down 18.0% this year. From the worried investor's side, four things did it:

1. **The delays.** Take-Two pushed GTA VI back twice, the second time in November 2025 to 19 November 2026, and the stock fell close to 10% after that delay, Insider Gaming and Barchart reported.
2. **Fear that AI makes games cheap to build.** Take-Two fell 7.9% on 30 January after Google ($GOOGL) began rolling out Project Genie, an AI tool that turns prompts into explorable 3D worlds, Bloomberg reported. Kotaku reported similar falls across game stocks.
3. **The price tag.** The stock slipped when Take-Two set GTA VI's base price at $80 in June, below the $100 some analysts had hoped for, TipRanks and TS2 reported.
4. **The year before the game.** Bookings fell in the September quarter and Take-Two held its fiscal 2027 forecast at $8.0 billion to $8.2 billion rather than raise it, saying it wanted to see the launch first, GamesBeat and Quartr reported.

## Testing the worry

**The delays: temporary, and now over.** The game is out, and Take-Two's own Q3 forecast of $3.35 billion to $3.50 billion of bookings for the December quarter was set before a first day that brought in $3.79 billion of retail sales. Retail sales aren't bookings, since stores and Sony ($SONY) and Microsoft ($MSFT) take a cut, but the launch plainly beat the base the company planned on.

**AI: not yet visible in the numbers.** Genie generated short, playable scenes without game mechanics, industry coverage noted, and Take-Two President Karl Slatoff dismissed the comparison with real game engines. A launch that outsold every entertainment debut in any medium, by the company's account, is the strongest evidence that players still pay for hand-built worlds.

**The weak core: lasting until proven otherwise.** This is the worry that survives. Weaker mobile games helped pull September-quarter revenue down 15.6%, Zacks and the company's release showed, and even a record GTA year won't show whether the business can earn its pre-Zynga 18.7% margin without a once-a-decade release.

## The moat

1. **The GTA franchise.** GTA V has sold nearly 235 million copies in 13 years, and GTA VI's first day was almost five times GTA V's. No rival publisher has a release that size; the franchise's reviews ahead of launch were near-perfect, Finviz and Business Today reported.
2. **Players who keep paying.** 78% of fiscal 2026 bookings came from spending inside existing games, money that arrives every week rather than with each release. Wedbush expects GTA VI's online mode to reach 24 million monthly players spending about $5 each by fiscal 2029, Finimize reported.
3. **Pricing power on the top title.** GTA VI launched at $80, a third above the old $60 standard, and still set the record.

Against the rivals we cover, the gap is in what reaches the bottom line, not in demand: Netflix ($NFLX) earned a 31.3% operating margin over 12 months and Disney ($DIS) 19.3%, against Take-Two's -2.8%.

## The risks we'd watch

1. **One game carries the year.** The December-quarter bookings forecast of $3.35 billion to $3.50 billion is more than twice the $1.60 billion of the September quarter, and how much of the $3.79 billion first day Take-Two keeps after stores' and console makers' cuts won't be known until February's report.
2. **Mobile shrinking.** Mobile was 49% of fiscal 2026 bookings. A 10% fall would cost about $330 million of bookings a year, close to a tenth of what GTA VI made on day one.
3. **Dilution.** At the two-year pace of 6.7%, the share count would rise by about 3% a year, diluting whatever profit GTA VI brings.

## Our verdict

**Business: Mixed. Price: About its usual price.** Take-Two owns the most powerful franchise in games and a base of players who pay every week, but outside launch years it barely makes money: a -2.8% operating margin, 1.5% of sales in free cash, and a rising share count. At 6.1 times sales, just under its two-year median of 6.5, the stock prices in a GTA VI that succeeds; it doesn't pay up for a business that changes. What would change our mind is a full year of operating margin back near its pre-Zynga 18.7%; the first read comes with the fiscal third-quarter report in early February, the first to include the launch.

The game sold faster than anything ever made; the business still has to show it can keep the money.
