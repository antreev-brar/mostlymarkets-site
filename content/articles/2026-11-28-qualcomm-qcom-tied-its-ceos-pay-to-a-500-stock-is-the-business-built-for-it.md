## How it got here

Qualcomm grew rich on two things: the patents behind every cellular phone standard since 3G, which it licenses to almost every handset maker, and the Snapdragon processors and modems that run most premium Android phones. For a decade that made it the phone industry's toll collector. Revenue first reached $44 billion in fiscal 2022 and has not grown since.

The last two years have tested both halves. Apple, once its biggest chip customer, is replacing Qualcomm modems with its own, and in 2026 memory shortages pushed up phone makers' costs and cut their orders. Revenue was $43.1 billion in the year to 27 September 2026, down 2.6%, and the stock fell 9.5% on 5 November after a quarter in which phone-chip sales dropped 26% ([our recap](/news/2026-11-05/qualcomm-qcom-shares-slide-as-phone-chip-sales-fall-26-and-memory-costs-bite/)).

Then, on 20 November, the board approved special "value creation" awards: a $50 million target for Chief Executive Cristiano Amon and $15 million for Chief Financial Officer Akash Palkhiwala, paid in shares only if the 90-day average price reaches hurdles of $250, $300, $350, $400 and $500, the company's 8-K filing on 24 November and Chip Dispatch reported. Hitting every hurdle pays up to three times the target. The stock **rose 19.6%** this week, much of it in a chip-wide rebound on Monday that Trefis said came with no company news. **Is Qualcomm a business that can carry a $500 share price?**

## The business in one line

Phone makers, carmakers and device makers buy Qualcomm's chips every quarter, and almost every phone maker pays it a royalty on each handset sold.

## The numbers that matter

### Growth
Revenue grew **5.1% a year** over five fiscal years and 5.2% a year over the last two, both below the 14.2% and 25.6% medians for the 25 chipmakers we track. The latest quarter was down 8.4% from a year earlier, in the bottom 4% of the group.

### Profitability
The operating margin was 20.1% in the year to September, against a 29.6% chipmaker median, and it is **9.1 points lower** than five years ago. Qualcomm said in its fourth-quarter release that wafer, packaging and memory costs rose across the board, and that price increases won't fully reach its chip margins until the second half of fiscal 2027.

### Cash
Free cash flow was $8.65 billion last year, 20.1% of sales, down from $12.82 billion the year before. Over two years it averaged 24.6% of sales, a little above the chipmaker median: the cash engine is still good, but it has slowed with profits.

### Returns
Return on capital is about 17.8%, exactly the chipmaker median. Return on equity of 44.8% ranks higher because buybacks have shrunk the equity base: the diluted share count fell **5.0%** in two years, in the top tenth of the group.

### Balance sheet
Net debt was $8.0 billion at 27 September, about 0.9 years of operating profit. That is comfortable, but it leaves less room for buybacks and a big data-center push at the same time than Micron's $35.6 billion of net cash, for example.

## Why the stock is where it is

1. **Phone-chip sales are shrinking.** Handset revenue fell 13% to $24.07 billion in fiscal 2026, and Qualcomm's annual report put that down to customers cutting chip orders to run down stock after memory shortages and price rises.
2. **Apple is leaving.** Qualcomm said in July that its share of modems in Apple's next iPhones would be "materially below" its earlier 20% estimate, MacRumors and Mactrast reported, and in November it said Apple product revenue would fall about 50% from the September quarter to the December quarter, Nasdaq and 24/7 Wall St. reported.
3. **Costs are rising faster than prices.** The chip unit's pre-tax margin fell to 24% in the September quarter from 29% a year earlier, its results showed.
4. **The AI sell-off.** Qualcomm's pitch to own a slice of AI data centers ran into November's rout in AI hardware, and the stock fell with the group.

## Testing the worry

**Phones: partly temporary.** Memory prices are a cycle, and Qualcomm says price rises will reach its margins in the second half of fiscal 2027. But handset revenue has been the bulk of the business for years and fell again this year; even when memory costs ease, the premium Android market it dominates is not growing fast. We'd call the margin squeeze temporary and the growth problem lasting.

**Apple: lasting, but contained.** In November management said fiscal 2027 Apple product revenue would come in below the slightly more than $2 billion it had forecast, so the remaining exposure is under 5% of sales. The loss is real; the damage from here is small.

**Data centers: unproven either way.** Qualcomm set a target of more than $15 billion of data-center revenue by fiscal 2029 at its June investor day, from about $0.3 billion this year, its release and KuCoin reported, and Amon reiterated $5 billion for fiscal 2027 in November. It has named Meta as a customer for server processors, Data Center Knowledge reported. Going from $0.3 billion to $5 billion in one year would be a sixteenfold jump, and none of it shows in reported revenue yet.

The worry that survives is the core: a phone business that shrinks in down years and barely grows in good ones.

## The moat

1. **The patents.** Licensing earns a 72% pre-tax margin on $5.56 billion of revenue, roughly flat year after year, because every cellular phone needs the standards Qualcomm's patents cover.
2. **Premium Android.** Snapdragon is the default processor in high-end Android phones; that is why handset revenue, even after falling 13%, is still $24 billion.
3. **Cars.** Automotive revenue rose 52% to $6.00 billion and hit a record $1.99 billion in the September quarter, from design wins that last the life of a car model.

The rivals in its new markets are much stronger than in its old ones. Nvidia ($NVDA) runs a 65.2% operating margin and Broadcom ($AVGO) 48.0% over the last 12 months, by our data, against Qualcomm's 20.1%, and both already sell billions of dollars of data-center chips a quarter.

## The risks we'd watch

- **The data-center target slips.** Data-center revenue at half the $5 billion target in fiscal 2027 would be a $2.5 billion shortfall, about 6% of fiscal 2026 sales, in the year management says non-phone growth replaces Apple.
- **Memory stays dear.** Another year like 2026 would leave handset revenue falling again; at 13% a year, that is about $3 billion of sales lost.
- **The pay plan meets a flat stock.** The awards pay nothing below a $250 average, 39% above Friday's close, so they cost shareholders little unless the stock rises. The risk is the other way: hurdles that steep can push management toward bigger, riskier bets in data centers.

The trailing P/E flatters Qualcomm: a one-time gain in the March quarter lifted fiscal 2026 net income to $13.6 billion, well above operating profit of $8.7 billion, so the forward multiple is the better guide. At 18.8 times, the market prices Qualcomm like an average chipmaker. A $500 share price would value it at about **$535 billion**; at the same multiple, that needs earnings about 2.8 times what analysts expect for the next year.

## Our verdict

**Business: Mixed. Price: About its usual price.** Qualcomm earns real cash, has a patent business few companies can match, and is shrinking its share count, but revenue has gone nowhere in five years and operating margins are falling. The forward multiple of 18.8 times matches the chipmaker median, so the stock already assumes neither collapse nor a data-center boom. What would change our mind is data-center revenue showing up in the reported numbers: the first quarter of fiscal 2027, reported in early February, is the first chance. Until then, the board's $500 hurdle measures how far Qualcomm hopes to go, not how far the business has come.
