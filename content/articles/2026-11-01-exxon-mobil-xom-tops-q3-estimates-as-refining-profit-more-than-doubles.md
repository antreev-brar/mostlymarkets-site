Exxon Mobil ($XOM) reported third-quarter revenue and other income of $113.2 billion on Friday morning, up 33% year on year. Earnings excluding identified items and timing effects, Exxon's adjusted measure, were **$4.23 a share**, above the $4.08 analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC. The shares closed up 2.2% at $187.25, a record close, on a day the Nasdaq fell 2.9%.

## What stood out

**Refining did the work.** Energy Products, Exxon's refining and fuel business, earned a record **$9.05 billion** on an adjusted basis, more than double the second quarter, the company's results supplement showed. Reuters tied the jump to the wars in Iran and Ukraine: the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz and attacks on refineries in Saudi Arabia and Russia have knocked out fuel-making capacity, so plants that still run earn far more per barrel.

*Verdict: an exceptional quarter for refining, earned from a shortage Exxon didn't create.*

**Pumping oil earned less.** Upstream adjusted earnings fell to $7.31 billion from $9.19 billion, on lower prices and a faster cut to Exxon's share of Guyana output under its contract there, the supplement said. That is the mirror image of the summer, when crude prices did the lifting.

**The headline and adjusted numbers diverge.** GAAP profit was $15.4 billion, double the $7.5 billion of a year earlier, Reuters reported. The adjusted figure leaves out about $2.5 billion of unfavorable timing effects, mostly from fuel trading, which Exxon flagged in a filing on 16 October.

## The outlook

Exxon gave no profit forecast. It set a $20 billion buyback for 2027, assuming reasonable market conditions, the release said, the same pace Reuters reported it was on for 2026.

Chief Executive Darren Woods said Exxon plans to spend about $10 billion on its refineries to raise margins, Seeking Alpha and OPIS reported.

## Our take

We were impressed that refining more than made up for weaker oil earnings, as [Chevron's did](/news/2026-10-30/chevron-cvx-tops-q3-estimates-as-refining-profit-jumps/) the same morning. On the other hand, this quarter's profit rests on fuel shortages caused by war, and Chevron's own refining chief said he expects margins to moderate as refinery runs recover. Overall, a strong quarter whose best part is the least repeatable.

The long run is less flattering. Exxon's revenue grew 2.5% a year over the two years to June, in the bottom quarter of our energy group, and the stock is up 55.6% this year at 12.8 times forward earnings, level with its peers. The number to watch next quarter is Energy Products: if it holds near $9 billion, the war premium is lasting longer than the market expects.
