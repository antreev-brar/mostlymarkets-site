The story investors now tell about online advertising is that AI is moving the money: chatbots and AI summaries pull people away from clicking Google's blue links, while Meta ($META) uses AI to aim ads better inside its own apps. "The bears will argue this is the first quarter that AI is starting to seriously hurt their search business," Pivotal Research Chief Executive Jeffrey Wlodarczak said after Alphabet's ($GOOGL) results on 28 October, as quoted by Yahoo Finance and IBTimes.

On Sunday, CNBC put a number on it: the gap between Meta's ad growth and Google search's growth in the third quarter was **the widest since 2021**. Since 21 October, the day Meta reported, Alphabet has lost 16.4% and Reddit ($RDDT), which counts on Google for visitors, 40.2%, by our price files.

## The story in one sentence

> "Chatbots are becoming an alternative to search, leading consumers to click far fewer blue links."

That's how CNBC summed up Google's problem on Sunday, 1 November, in a piece that quoted Emarketer analyst Nate Elliott on the risk to Google's ad business. The chain runs like this: if people ask a chatbot or read an AI summary instead of clicking links, Google shows fewer paid ads beside the links and sends fewer visitors to the sites that live on search traffic. Meanwhile companies that own their audience, such as Meta, can use the same AI to make each ad work better, so ad budgets drift toward them.

## The evidence for it

### 1. Meta's ad sales grew more than twice as fast as search

Meta's advertising revenue rose 26% in the third quarter, to $63.1 billion, while Google search grew 12% to $63.35 billion, CNBC reported, with the trade site TechBuzz describing the gap as a five-year high. Meta told investors its AI investments were delivering "substantial incremental advertising revenue," CNBC reported.

### 2. Google search fell short of estimates by about $1.6 billion

Search revenue of $63.35 billion came in **about $1.6 billion** short of the roughly $65 billion analysts expected, Yahoo Finance and SiliconANGLE reported ([our story](/news/2026-10-29/alphabet-googl-shares-plunge-after-search-falls-short-and-cash-burn-grows/)). Alphabet fell 12.6% the next day, its worst since Google listed in 2004.

### 3. The websites downstream of Google are losing visitors

Reddit Chief Executive Steve Huffman said search referrals fell by more than 1 million a week on average in October and linked part of the drop to AI-generated summaries in search results, CNBC and Benzinga reported. He warned daily users could fall by a low-double-digit percentage this quarter, and the stock **plunged 29.2%** on Friday ([our story](/news/2026-10-30/why-reddit-rddt-stock-is-sinking-today/)).

### 4. Pinterest found more users but not more money per user

Pinterest ($PINS) reached a record 661 million monthly users, but revenue per user rose only 1% and revenue missed estimates, Investing.com and its results release showed. The stock fell 11.9% after hours on 29 October.

## The case against it

The strongest objection is that Alphabet sells AI as well as losing to it. Google Cloud revenue rose 103% to $30.71 billion in the third quarter, its release showed, so some of the money moving into AI lands back at Alphabet.

1. **Search is slowing, not shrinking.** A 12% gain on a $63 billion business still means about $6.8 billion more than in the same quarter of 2025, by our calculation. Wlodarczak framed the AI threat as "the bears" argument, not his own.
2. **Meta's gain has a cost.** Its free cash flow turned negative, at minus $2.12 billion, as spending on data centers outran cash from operations, and the cost of insuring its debt hit a record after the results, Reuters reported ([our story](/news/2026-10-22/meta-meta-shares-slide-after-free-cash-flow-turns-negative/)). Meta's stock is down 6.8% this year, close to Alphabet's 10.6% fall.
3. **Meta is partly recovering lost ground.** CNBC noted that Meta needed AI to rebuild its ad targeting after Apple's 2021 privacy change limited tracking on iPhones, so some of its faster growth is a rebound, not a share grab from Google.

## What would change minds

- **Alphabet's fourth-quarter report**, due early next year: a second quarter of search below estimates would turn one miss into a trend; a re-acceleration would undercut the story.
- **Reddit's daily users this quarter**: Huffman's warning of a low-double-digit drop is the cleanest gauge of how much traffic AI summaries take from the open web. Its login-wall test, reported by CNBC, shows whether it can hold readers without Google.
- **Meta's 2027 spending plan**, due with its fourth-quarter results: if ad growth holds near 26% while spending keeps rising, investors may decide the AI payoff covers the bill.

## Our read

**Partly supported.** The shift is real in the growth rates: Meta's ads grew more than twice as fast as Google search, and Reddit has given hard numbers on lost search visitors. The weakest link is the leap from "search is slowing" to "search is broken," because a 12% growth rate and a cloud business that doubled are not the numbers of a company losing its market. The number we'll watch is Google search growth in Alphabet's next report: below 10% would make the bears' case, above 12% would break it.
