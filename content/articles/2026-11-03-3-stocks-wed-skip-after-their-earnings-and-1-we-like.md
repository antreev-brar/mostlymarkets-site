Sixteen companies worth over $10 billion that we recapped reported in the seven days to Tuesday 3 November, and most beat estimates, which makes a beat a poor guide on its own. Palantir rose 96% and still fell 7.6% before the open on Tuesday, after its new contract value grew just 6%.

The screen: the 16 companies worth $10 billion or more that reported in the past seven days and that we recapped, leaving out stocks in our lists of the last 14 days. We judged each on its quarter, then on growth, margins, cash and debt against its industry peers from SEC filings, then on what the price assumes.

```picks
skip: PLTR, ABBV, LIN
like: MA
```

## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. Palantir Technologies ($PLTR)
**Forward P/E: 99.5 times, as of 3 November 2026**. Palantir sells data-analysis and AI software to governments and companies.

1. **The quarter was strong, but bookings slowed.** Revenue grew 96% to $2.31 billion, and the fourth-quarter forecast of $2.65 billion topped estimates, [our recap](/news/2026-11-02/palantir-pltr-beats-q3-estimates-as-us-commercial-sales-overtake-government/) showed. But the value of contracts it signed rose only 6% to $2.9 billion, Fortune and FourWeekMBA reported, and [the stock slid](/news/2026-11-03/palantir-pltr-shares-slide-as-its-new-contracts-grow-just-6/).
2. **The business is exceptional.** Its operating margin over the 12 months to June 2026 was **42.8%**, in the top tenth of 32 software companies we cover, and revenue grew 57.6% a year over two years, in the top tenth too.
3. **Shareholders are being diluted.** Its diluted share count rose 6.4% over two years, against a flat median for its peers, so owners get a shrinking slice of that growth.

**The price:** at 99.5 times expected earnings, earnings would have to grow about 3.8-fold for the multiple to fall to the software median of 26.1 times.

**What would change our mind:** fourth-quarter contract value growing back above 30% year on year, due with fourth-quarter results.

### 2. AbbVie ($ABBV)
**Earnings per share: down 2.8% a year over five fiscal years to 2025**. AbbVie makes immunology drugs Skyrizi and Rinvoq, the neuroscience drugs, and Botox.

1. **The quarter beat, and the growth is real now.** Adjusted EPS was $3.82 against $3.74 expected, revenue rose 10.3% to a record $17.40 billion, and Skyrizi and Rinvoq grew 22.7% and 23.9%, [our recap](/news/2026-11-01/abbvie-abbv-tops-q3-estimates-as-skyrizi-and-rinvoq-grow-23/) showed. It raised its 2026 EPS forecast to $13.95 to $13.99.
2. **The long run is weak.** Per-share earnings fell 2.8% a year over five fiscal years, in the bottom quarter of 18 drugmakers we cover, whose median grew 13.4%.
3. **It carries a lot of debt.** Net debt was $64.3 billion at the end of June, **3.8 years** of operating profit, which limits what it can spend on the next drug deal.

**The price:** at 19.1 times expected earnings, a little above the 18.1-times drugmaker median, the price already assumes the new drugs keep growing above 20%; we'd rather pay that for a cleaner record.

**What would change our mind:** 2027 guidance, due with fourth-quarter results, pointing to EPS growth in double digits with net debt falling below three years of operating profit.

### 3. Linde ($LIN)
**Revenue growth: 4.5% a year over five fiscal years to 2025**. Linde supplies industrial gases such as oxygen, nitrogen and hydrogen, piped or delivered to factories, hospitals and chipmakers.

1. **The quarter was steady, not more.** Adjusted EPS of $4.59 beat the $4.52 expected, but underlying sales rose 5%, 3 points of it from higher prices, and the fourth-quarter forecast points to EPS growth of 5% to 7%, [our recap](/news/2026-10-29/linde-lin-tops-q3-estimates-as-higher-prices-lift-sales-9/) showed.
2. **Margins have done the work, and they're high.** Its operating margin widened 14.1 points over five years to 25.3%, in the top tenth of materials stocks which is how EPS grew 25.4% a year on slow sales; at that level there is less left to gain.
3. **Sales growth is slow.** Revenue growth over five years ranks in the bottom quarter of 26 materials stocks we cover.

**The price:** at 27.6 times expected earnings, against a 16.4-times median for materials stocks, and 46.5 times free cash flow, the price assumes margin gains continue that the fourth-quarter forecast doesn't show.

**What would change our mind:** the $11 billion project backlog turning into faster volume growth, visible in its 2027 guidance.

## 1 stock we like

### Mastercard ($MA)
**Free cash flow margin: 48.8% over the last two years, to September 2026**. Mastercard runs a card network and sells fraud, data and security services to banks and merchants.

1. **The quarter beat on both lines.** Net revenue rose 14% to $9.81 billion against $9.63 billion expected, and value-added services grew **19%** to $4.06 billion, now about 41% of revenue, [our recap](/news/2026-10-29/mastercard-ma-tops-q3-estimates-as-value-added-services-grow-19/) showed. The stock jumped 4.2% to a 52-week closing high that day.
2. **It compounds.** Revenue grew 16.5% a year over five years against a 9.6% median for 46 payments and financial-market companies we cover, and EPS grew 21.0% a year.
3. **It turns sales into cash like few others.** Its operating margin of 58.6% over the 12 months to September ranks in the top tenth of that group, and its return on capital of about 82% is the highest of the 21 with data.

**The price:** at 30.4 times expected earnings it costs far more than its peers (17.2 times), but its trailing P/E of 34.4 sits below its own two-year median of 36.9.

**What could go wrong:** payments regulation. Rules that cap card fees would hit network revenue, about 59% of sales; a 10% cut there would take roughly 6% off total revenue.
