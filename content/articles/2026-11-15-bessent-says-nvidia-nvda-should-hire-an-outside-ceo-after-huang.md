Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Sunday, 15 November, that Nvidia ($NVDA) must "go outside the company" to find Jensen Huang's successor and called for a "dramatic shakeup" there, CNBC, Reuters and the South China Morning Post reported. Huang resigned as chief executive on Thursday; Finance Chief Colette Kress is running the company while the board weighs internal and external candidates.

The comments put the administration into the board's search, and they land as Washington questions sales of Nvidia chips to Megaspeed, a Singapore customer that US officials are examining over possible exports to China, Tom's Hardware and Finimize reported. The board had already approached Arm ($ARM) Chief Executive Rene Haas, the Financial Times reported ([our story](/news/2026-11-13/nvidia-nvda-approached-arms-rene-haas-to-be-its-next-ceo-ft-reports/)), while two big shareholders asked it to keep Huang's return on the table ([our brief](/news/2026-11-14/two-nvidia-nvda-investors-ask-its-board-to-keep-jensen-huangs-return-on-the-table/)).

Nvidia closed Friday at $162.85, down 25.0% in a month in our data. It reports third-quarter results after the close on Tuesday, its first call without Huang ([our week ahead](/news/2026-11-15/the-week-ahead-nvidia-anthropics-ipo-the-fed-minutes-and-walmart/)).
