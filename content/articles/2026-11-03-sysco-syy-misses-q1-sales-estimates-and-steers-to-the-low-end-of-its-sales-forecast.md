Sysco ($SYY), the biggest US distributor of food to restaurants, said before the open on Tuesday that sales in its fiscal first quarter, the three months to 26 September, **rose 2.8% to $21.7 billion**, short of the $22.0 billion analysts expected, Reuters and Newsquawk reported. Adjusted earnings per share rose 3.5% to $1.19, matching the consensus. The shares **fell 5.8% to $77.02** in pre-market trading as of 9:19 a.m. ET.

## What stood out

Local case volume, the cases of food Sysco delivers to independent restaurants, its most profitable customers, **rose 2.7%** in the US, after a 0.2% fall a year earlier, the release said. That is the number Sysco's turnaround plan rests on, and it beat the roughly 2.5% the company assumes for the year.

*Verdict: the volume recovery is real.*

Sales still missed because prices are rising more slowly. A food distributor passes food inflation through to its customers, so cooler inflation shrinks sales even when it ships more cases. Sysco said soft restaurant foot traffic persisted, which caps how fast volume can grow.

*Verdict: a pricing problem, not a demand problem, but it shows up in the top line all the same.*

Reported net earnings rose 10.5%, flattered by a $234 million one-off gain on hedges tied to the debt for its purchase of JRD Unico, the parent of the Jetro and Restaurant Depot cash-and-carry stores.

## The outlook

Sysco now expects fiscal 2027 sales growth at the low end of its 6% to 7% range, citing lower food inflation, and kept its target of 9% to 11% adjusted EPS growth and about 2.5% local volume growth, the release said.

## Our take

We were impressed by the volume: independent restaurants are where Sysco earns its margin, and they grew again. On the other hand, the sales guide moved to the bottom of the range, and adjusted operating income came in below forecasts. Overall, a quarter that holds the earnings plan but loses some of the cushion under it.

The reaction is large for this stock: Sysco moved 5% or more on just 2 of the last 252 sessions, by our data. At 22.3 times trailing earnings it was above its own two-year median of 20.2, so a quarter with no upside had room to fall. The next test is whether fiscal second-quarter volume holds near 2.7% while the Restaurant Depot deal adds debt.
