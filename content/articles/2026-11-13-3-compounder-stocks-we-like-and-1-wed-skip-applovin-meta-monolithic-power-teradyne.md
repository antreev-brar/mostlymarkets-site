On Monday Monolithic Power Systems **soared 16.6%**, its biggest one-day rise since April 2025, as buyers returned to the chip stocks that had led the Nasdaq into a correction the week before, Trefis and StockStory reported. Swings like that blur the line between a hot quarter and a compounder, which grows sales and per-share earnings year after year at high margins.

Our screen took the 16 companies in our coverage with an operating margin above 20% in their latest 12 months (the share of each sale left after running the business), ranked by revenue growth. We judged the fastest on five-year growth, margin direction, cash and returns against peers, from SEC filings in our data. We passed on the fastest, Lumentum, which doubled revenue but posted a net loss over the 12 months to September.

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. AppLovin ($APP)
**Revenue growth: 57.8% in the 12 months to September 2026.** AppLovin's software places ads inside mobile games and apps.

1. It has compounded for years: revenue grew **30.4% a year** over five fiscal years, more than twice the 13.6% median of 25 communication-services peers.
2. Its margins are the best we track: an operating margin of 77.8% over the past 12 months, the highest in that group against a median of 18.3%.
3. The latest quarter held the pace: third-quarter revenue rose 55% to $2.19 billion, and fourth-quarter guidance of $2.365 billion to $2.395 billion topped the roughly $2.25 billion analysts expected, Yahoo Finance and Benzinga reported ([our recap](/news/2026-11-05/applovin-app-soars-31-as-q3-sales-rise-55-and-its-forecast-tops-estimates/)).

**The price:** 22.4 times expected earnings, above the 16.3 times group median, but its trailing P/E of 26.5 is less than half its two-year median of 62.7. At 2:15 p.m. ET on Friday the stock was 44% below its December 2025 closing high of $733.60.

**What could go wrong:** the slowdown. Growth slows to about 43% in that guidance; a forecast in the 20s when it reports in February would make today's multiple look full.

### 2. Meta ($META)
**Revenue growth: 27.5% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Meta sells ads on Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp.

1. Growth is speeding up: revenue rose 18.5% a year over five fiscal years and **24.4% a year** over the last two, in the top quarter of 16 consumer-internet peers.
2. It earns well on what it spends: an operating margin of 32.9% over the past 12 months against a peer median of 13.1%, and return on capital of about 18%, in the top quarter of its group.
3. The latest quarter beat: revenue rose 26% to $64.6 billion, above the $63.34 billion expected, the Associated Press reported.

**The price:** 21.9 times expected earnings, near the 19.8 times peer median, and its trailing P/E of 21.3 sits near the bottom of its two-year range of 19.7 to 32.0.

**What could go wrong:** spending. Meta narrowed its 2026 capital-spending range to **$135 billion to $145 billion**, and third-quarter spending of about $34.9 billion outran operating cash flow, leaving free cash flow at minus $2.1 billion, its results showed and VKTR reported. If 2027 spending rises again without faster ad growth, the compounding stops at the cash line.

### 3. Monolithic Power Systems ($MPWR)
**Revenue growth: 40.5% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Monolithic Power designs power chips for AI servers, cars and factories.

1. It compounds on both lines: revenue grew **27.0% a year** over five fiscal years and earnings per share 29.7% a year, against medians of 14.2% and 17.2% for the 25 chipmakers we cover.
2. It gets more profitable as it grows: the operating margin rose 7.3 points over five years to 29.5%, more than the median chipmaker's 4.7-point gain.
3. The forecast keeps rising: third-quarter revenue rose 63.1% to $1.20 billion, and it guided the fourth quarter to $1.315 billion to $1.385 billion, above the roughly $1.23 billion consensus, its filing showed and Zacks and Webull reported.

**The price:** 36.5 times expected earnings, in the most expensive quarter of chipmakers against a median of 22.2 times, and its trailing P/E of 79.8 is above its two-year median of 64.5.

**What could go wrong:** cash. Free cash flow was 16.3% of revenue over the past 12 months, in the bottom quarter of chipmakers,; at 112 times free cash flow, a stall in AI server orders would leave little under the price.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Teradyne ($TER)
**Revenue growth: 74.9% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Teradyne makes chip-testing machines and industrial robots.

1. The growth is a rebound, not compounding: over five fiscal years revenue grew **0.4% a year**, in the bottom quarter of chipmakers, and earnings per share fell 4.1% a year.
2. Margins went backwards: the operating margin is 9.4 points lower than five years ago, against a median chipmaker gain of 4.7 points, and free cash flow was 18.9% of revenue over two years, in the bottom quarter.
3. The newest driver is lumpy: third-quarter revenue rose 70% to $1.31 billion, but chip-test revenue fell to $676 million from $843 million in the second quarter, which management blamed on the timing of compute orders, Quartr and 24/7 Wall St. reported.

**The price:** 26.7 times expected earnings against the chipmaker median of 22.2 times, a premium for a business that hasn't compounded.

**What would change our mind:** orders. Management has pointed to a next wave of compute orders in the first half of 2027, and Jefferies started coverage at Buy on Wednesday, seeing a $2 billion Nvidia opportunity, Barron's and TradingView reported. Chip-test revenue back above $843 million for two quarters would show more than one cycle.
