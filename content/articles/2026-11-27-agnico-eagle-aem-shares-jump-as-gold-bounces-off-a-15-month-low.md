## What happened
Shares of Canadian gold miner Agnico Eagle Mines ($AEM) **jumped 5.4%** to $169.85 by 10:30 a.m. ET on Friday, as gold rebounded from a 15-month low. Gold futures were up 2.6% at $3,627.40 an ounce at 10:20 a.m. ET, and Newmont ($NEM), the biggest US gold miner, rose 3.7%.

FXStreet and Financefeeds tied gold's bounce to calmer stock markets: on Wednesday, gold fell to its lowest since August 2025 as investors sold it to cover losses in AI stocks, and that forced selling eased as tech steadied on Friday. A pause in the dollar's rally helped too, FXStreet reported, though both outlets said a Federal Reserve still raising rates remains the main obstacle for the metal.

## Why it matters
A miner's costs barely move with the gold price, so each dollar gold gains or loses falls almost straight to profit, which is why Agnico swings more than the metal. Over five fiscal years its revenue grew 30.6% a year, against a 6.3% median for the 28 materials companies we track, from its SEC filings.

The rebound doesn't settle the argument in [our gold debate](/news/2026-11-15/bulls-vs-bears-on-gold-can-it-rally-while-the-fed-is-still-hiking/): a rate hike raises the cost of holding a metal that pays no interest. Agnico fell 7% on 18 November when Fed officials [pressed for faster hikes](/news/2026-11-18/agnico-eagle-freeport-mcmoran-and-newmont-shares-slide-what-happened/), and the Fed's 9 December decision is the next test.

## What the market is telling us
Agnico's shares moved 5% or more on 28 of the last 252 sessions, and the last rise this big was on 23 November, when it rose 5.5%. At Wednesday's close of $161.20 it was down 4.9% this year and 36.1% below its 2 March high, and $1,000 invested five years ago would be worth $3,119. At 14.8 times expected earnings, a little under the 16.0 times its peers fetch, the stock is priced for gold to stay low; it moves with the metal, not with news of its own.
