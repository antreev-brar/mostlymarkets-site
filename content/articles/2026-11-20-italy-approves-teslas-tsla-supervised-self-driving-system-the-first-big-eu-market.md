Italy has provisionally approved Tesla's supervised driver-assistance system, sold in Europe as Tesla Assisted Driving and in the US as FSD (Supervised), Reuters and Tesla Mag reported on Friday. The approval came from the transport ministry of Matteo Salvini, and Tesla Europe announced it on X; Italy is the 12th European country to clear the system and the first of the EU's biggest markets, Drive Tesla and Not a Tesla App reported.

It matters because Tesla's price leans on software: the stock traded at 196 times earnings on a 3% margin when [we took it apart](/news/2026-11-07/tesla-tsla-trades-at-196-times-earnings-on-a-3-margin-is-the-business-worth-it/), and driver assistance is software it can sell to owners of cars already on the road. Germany, France and Spain have not approved the system, and an EU-wide approval is still under review, so the gain is one country, not the bloc. Tesla shares were down **1.7%** at $368.24 as of 10:15 a.m. ET. Tesla says the rollout in Italy will start soon; the EU-wide decision is the next test.
