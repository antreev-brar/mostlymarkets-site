In three trading days this week, three of the biggest banks on Wall Street took three different positions on Micron ($MU). Citi said on Monday 23 November that the sell-off in memory stocks was overdone, BNP Paribas Exane downgraded the stock on Tuesday, and UBS cut its price target by a third on Wednesday while keeping its Buy rating. The stock **rose 8.8%** on the week, to $646.95 at Friday's half-day close, after swinging between a 19.0% gain and an 11.0% loss.

The number both sides are arguing about is Micron's valuation: **3.7 times** forward earnings, against a median of 18.9 times for the chipmakers we track, by our data. To the bulls that is a company priced as if its record profits vanish. To the bears it is what memory stocks always look like at the top, when earnings are about to fall.

## The bull case

### Atif Malik, semiconductor analyst, Citi
**The sell-off priced in far more damage than the worst case would do.** In a note on 23 November, Malik kept his Buy rating and $1,300 target and argued that even if every Nvidia-linked financing deal for AI customers stopped, DRAM demand would fall about **12%** and NAND flash demand about 7%, Yahoo Finance and TipRanks reported. Citi pointed to tight supply of DRAM and HBM, the stacked memory beside AI processors, as the reason prices hold, Invezz and TipRanks reported. Micron rose 19.0% that day ([our story](/news/2026-11-23/micron-mu-jumps-19-as-citi-sizes-the-nvidia-financing-risk-at-12-of-dram-demand/)).

### David Tepper, founder, Appaloosa Management
**The hedge fund made Micron its biggest bet.** Appaloosa's filing for the quarter to 30 September, published on 16 November, showed about 1.25 million Micron shares worth roughly $1.33 billion, **17.6%** of its reported stock portfolio and up 28% in the quarter, TIKR and ValueSider reported. That is a position, not an argument, and it is two months old: Micron closed 30 September at $1,065.11; Friday's close is 39% below that, by our data.

### Timothy Arcuri, semiconductor analyst, UBS
**Still a buyer, at a lower price.** Arcuri cut his target to $1,100 from $1,625 on 25 November and kept his Buy rating, Bloomingbit and Price-Target.com reported. Even the new target is 70% above Friday's close. He is a bull who just conceded part of the bear case: the cut came the day after BNP's downgrade, and Micron **fell 4.5%** that day.

## The bear case

### Karl Ackerman, semiconductor analyst, BNP Paribas Exane
**The squeeze ends in 2027 and profits fall in 2028.** Ackerman downgraded Micron to Neutral from Outperform on 24 November and cut his target to $775 from $1,700, Benzinga and TipRanks reported. He now expects memory supply and demand to come into balance from mid-2027, a year earlier than before, citing risks to cloud companies' 2028 data-center plans, falling PC and phone unit sales in 2026 and 2027, and new factories coming online, Yahoo Finance and Finbold reported. His 2028 earnings estimate sits about **45%** below consensus. Micron fell 11.0% that day, in a chip-wide sell-off.

### Kyung Kye-hyun, former head of Samsung's chip division
**China will end the shortage.** Kyung said in May that new Chinese DRAM capacity could push memory prices down from late 2027 or early 2028, Notebookcheck and PC Gamer reported. It is the supply half of Ackerman's argument, from a man who ran the world's biggest memory maker.

The forecasters lean the same way for flash. TrendForce said on 19 November it expects NAND contract prices to fall 0% to 5% in the first quarter of 2027 as Chinese cloud companies cut orders, Evertiq and TrendForce's own release said. That is Sandisk's whole business and a smaller part of Micron's.

## Where they agree

Nobody in this debate thinks 2027 is the problem. Citi's case rests on tight supply now; Ackerman's rests on 2028, and his timeline still has supply and demand roughly balanced only from mid-2027. Micron said in its September results that it had agreed most of its 2027 HBM supply, at higher prices ([our memory narrative](/news/2026-11-21/wall-street-is-betting-the-memory-boom-has-peaked-microns-17-december-report-will-test-it/)). Both sides also agree that flash is weaker than DRAM, which is why flash maker Sandisk ($SNDK) has fallen further from its June high than Micron has.

## The number that decides it

The near-term test is Micron's fiscal first-quarter report on Wednesday 17 December. It guided to an adjusted gross margin of about 86.25%; a figure at or above that, plus comments that 2028 contracts are being signed at today's prices, would undercut Ackerman's timing. The 2028 question itself won't be settled by one quarter: the cloud companies' 2027 spending plans, due with their results from late January, are the first hard data on it.

## Our read

For the next twelve months the bulls have the stronger evidence: signed 2027 contracts outrank forecasts, and Micron's 74.6% operating margin over the last 12 months leaves a lot of room to fall before earnings disappoint at 3.7 times. Ackerman's case is about 2028, and the market has priced much of it: the stock is 46.7% below its June high while profits are at a record. Where the bears look right is flash, where TrendForce and the Chinese cloud buyers point the same way. We'll judge Micron on 17 December on its gross margin against the 86.25% it guided, and on what it says about pricing beyond 2027.
