Bristol Myers Squibb ($BMY) presented the first data from two Phase 3 trials of KarXT, the drug it sells as Cobenfy for schizophrenia, in psychosis linked to Alzheimer's disease, at the CTAD conference in Boston on Wednesday, its release and Investing.com said. In the 12-week open-label stage of the ADEPT-1 trial, patients' score on a hallucinations-and-delusions scale **fell 12.0 points** from 14.4, and the drug was generally well tolerated with no new safety signals.

The catch is in the design: every patient knew they were getting the drug, so these results can't show it beats a placebo, and the double-blind data from all the ADEPT trials remain blinded, the company and The Fly reported. No drug is approved for Alzheimer's psychosis, so a win would widen Cobenfy's market well beyond schizophrenia, where it sold $57 million in the third quarter ([our recap](/news/2026-10-30/bristol-myers-squibb-bmy-tops-q3-estimates-as-eliquis-sales-grow-26/)). The shares were **down 2.1%** at $60.05 as of 10:30 a.m. ET.

What isn't known yet is whether KarXT beats placebo in these patients, the question the blinded results will answer.
