Best Buy ($BBY) reported fiscal third-quarter comparable sales growth of **6.1%** before the open on Tuesday, its highest in five years and far above the 2.3% StreetAccount consensus cited by CNBC. Revenue rose 5.8% to $10.24 billion, beating the $9.90 billion LSEG estimate, and adjusted earnings of $1.62 a share topped the $1.49 expected. The shares **jumped 4.0%** to $101.90 before the open, as of 7:27 a.m. ET, after a raised full-year forecast.

## What stood out

Computing, mobile phones and newer categories such as AI glasses and trading cards led the growth, the release said. Chief Executive Jason Bonfig, in his first report since succeeding Corie Barry at the start of November, said in the release that comparable sales growth "was ahead of our expectations and our highest in five years." Reuters reported that the company is betting on holiday demand for AI-enabled phones, TVs and smart glasses. *Verdict: a broad beat, led by the categories that matter most.*

Part of that growth is price. Memory chips have become far more expensive this year as AI data centers buy them up, and PC and phone makers including Apple and HP have raised prices to pass the cost on, so a laptop sold today brings in more revenue than the same laptop a year ago. That flatters comparable sales even if Best Buy sells fewer units.

The margin gain was real but helped. The adjusted operating margin **rose 0.4 point to 4.4%**, and a $41 million tariff refund added 0.8 point to the US gross margin, the release showed. Refunds like that won't repeat once the backlog of claims is paid.

## The outlook

Best Buy raised its full-year comparable-sales forecast to **3.4% to 4.0%**, from 1.9% to 3.0%, and adjusted EPS to $6.90 to $7.05, from $6.70 to $6.90, Reuters and the release said. CNBC reported revenue guidance of $43.0 billion to $43.3 billion, up from $42.3 billion to $42.8 billion. For the holiday quarter the release guided comparable sales up 2% to 4%, slower than the third quarter.

## Our take

We were impressed that the beat came from sales, not cost cuts: 6.1% is nearly three times the expected pace. On the other hand, the tariff refund props up the margin, and Bank of America's Christopher Nardone, who rates the stock Underperform, wrote last week that memory costs will force more promotions and curb demand next year, Investing.com and MarketScreener reported. Over five years revenue fell 2.5% a year, in the bottom quarter of the 66 consumer discretionary companies in our data, so one strong quarter doesn't change the long-run record; at 13.8 times expected earnings, against a 19.3 times peer median, the price already reflects that. The holiday-quarter report early next year will show whether shoppers keep paying more for gadgets.
