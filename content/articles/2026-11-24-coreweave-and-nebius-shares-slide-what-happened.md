## What happened
Shares of AI cloud providers CoreWeave ($CRWV) and Nebius ($NBIS) **fell 8.0% and 9.4%** by 10:15 a.m. ET on Tuesday, giving back part of Monday's rebound on Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia ($NVDA). TipRanks and Stocktwits pointed to a second weight: on its earnings call, Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE) said its financing arm has not lent to "neocloud or high-risk customers", the label for specialist AI clouds such as these two.

CoreWeave rose 22.3% and Nebius 20.3% on Monday. 24/7 Wall St. and Benzinga reported Tuesday's drops alongside a fall in Anthropic's newly listed shares below their IPO price, which kept the pressure on anything tied to AI spending.

## Why it matters
Neoclouds rent out Nvidia chips they buy largely with borrowed money, so their stocks trade on whether lenders and suppliers keep extending credit. HPE's comment, made while reporting a quarter in which revenue rose 50% ([our story on HPE](/news/2026-11-24/hpe-hpe-slides-11-5-before-the-open-as-it-sees-ai-server-margins-falling-to-14/)), says one big supplier is choosing not to carry that risk itself. It does not say anyone has stopped selling to them.

That is the weak link we tested on 16 November in [our narrative on AI's money loop](/news/2026-11-16/wall-street-is-betting-ais-1-trillion-money-loop-breaks-at-the-neoclouds-nvidias-report-will-test-it/): if financing tightens, the neoclouds are where it shows first.

## What the market is telling us
CoreWeave's shares moved 5% or more on 100 of the last 252 sessions and Nebius's on 104, so both are among the most volatile large stocks we track. CoreWeave closed Monday down 32.5% this year and 65.0% below its 6 May closing high of $137.98; Nebius closed down 38.4% over the past month. We read Tuesday as the market repricing funding risk on a supplier's words, and Dell's results after the close tonight will add another server maker's view of who is paying.
