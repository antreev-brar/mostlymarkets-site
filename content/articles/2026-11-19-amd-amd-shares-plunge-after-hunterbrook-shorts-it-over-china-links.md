## What happened
Shares of chipmaker AMD ($AMD) **plunged 9.4% to $480.56** as of 12:15 p.m. ET on Thursday after Hunterbrook Media published an investigation alleging that AMD's server firmware kept reaching IEIT Systems, a Chinese server maker the Commerce Department put on its Entity List of blacklisted companies in March 2025. Hunterbrook's affiliated fund, Hunterbrook Capital, disclosed that it is short AMD, meaning it profits if the stock falls, CNBC and Reuters reported.

According to the report, IEIT posted at least six newer versions of AMD's server start-up software in its own product updates after it was blacklisted, the latest on 19 October, and near-identical files turned up at three vendors Hunterbrook links to IEIT. Hunterbrook said it does not know how IEIT obtained the code and that its findings do not establish a breach of export law. AMD had not answered the outlet's questions when the report went out and had not commented publicly by midday, according to Benzinga and 24/7 Wall St.

## Why it matters
Hunterbrook aimed the report at the case for AMD as the winner of Nvidia's ($NVDA) troubles, which include an [SEC probe and a Muddy Waters short position](/news/2026-11-17/sec-probes-nvidia-nvda-disclosures-and-muddy-waters-says-it-is-short/) over its China disclosures. AMD **rose 5.3% on Wednesday**, and Thursday's drop more than erased it. A short seller's report is an allegation, not a finding; the risk for holders is a Commerce Department inquiry, which would hang over AMD's server-chip sales while it runs. Intel ($INTC), the other big maker of x86 server chips, was up 1.6% at 12:15 p.m. ET.
