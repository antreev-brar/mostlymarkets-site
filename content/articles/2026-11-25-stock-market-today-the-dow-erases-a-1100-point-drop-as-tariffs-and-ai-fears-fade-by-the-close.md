On Wednesday the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell more than 1,100 points by late morning, as new US tariff proposals, a hotter core inflation reading and a third day of selling in AI stocks hit at once. Then buyers came back. The Dow **closed up 164.25 points, or 0.33%**, at 50,182.95, CNBC and Yahoo Finance reported, while the S&P 500 slipped 0.25% to 7,182.78 and the Nasdaq Composite 0.62% to 23,585.80. Nvidia ($NVDA) fell 3.9% and still did more damage to the S&P 500 than any other stock.

## A 4.6% hole, nearly filled

At their lows, in our data, the Nasdaq was **down 4.6%** and the S&P 500 3.2%, and the Dow touched 48,913.74. Three things landed in one morning. The trade office [proposed tariffs on 14 trading partners](/news/2026-11-25/us-proposes-tariffs-on-14-trading-partners-with-20-on-china-and-a-15-cap-for-taiwan/), 20% on top of existing duties for China. October's [core PCE inflation came in at 3.3%](/news/2026-11-25/core-pce-inflation-rose-to-3-3-in-october-a-tenth-above-forecasts-before-a-fed-hike/), a tenth above forecasts, with a December rate hike already almost fully priced. And the AI hardware stocks kept falling after giving back [most of Monday's record rally](/news/2026-11-24/stock-market-today-chip-stocks-give-back-mondays-rally-while-the-dow-closes-above-50000/) on Tuesday.

The turn came in the afternoon, as investors went looking for bargains, Reuters reported. The Wall Street Journal tied the recovery to a swing in Anthropic, whose shares, listed last week at $83, went from an early 8% drop to trade back above that price, 24/7 Wall St. reported. No single piece of news reversed the morning's worries; the tariff rates are still a proposal, open to comment before they take effect.

## The AI trade still lost, but Dell turned around

The losers were the same names as Tuesday. Lam Research ($LRCX) **fell 5.7%**, Sandisk ($SNDK) 5.6% and Micron ($MU) 4.5%, extending the [chip-equipment](/news/2026-11-25/lam-research-applied-materials-and-asml-shares-slide-what-happened/) and [memory](/news/2026-11-25/micron-astera-labs-and-credo-shares-slide-what-happened/) slides we covered in the morning. Oracle ($ORCL) fell 5.1% to $103.88 as [the cost of insuring its debt hit a record](/news/2026-11-25/why-oracle-orcl-stock-is-down-today/), leaving it 58% below its June high.

Nvidia closed at $149.30, after S&P Global [put its AA- rating on watch for a downgrade](/news/2026-11-25/sp-puts-nvidias-nvda-aa-rating-on-watch-for-a-downgrade-over-governance/) over governance. From Friday's $125.53 close, it has now given back about 40% of Monday's $39.52 jump.

The twist was Dell ($DELL). It traded as low as $265, **down 10.3%**, the day after it [doubled its revenue and lifted its forecast](/news/2026-11-24/dell-technologies-dell-beats-q3-estimates-as-revenue-doubles-and-its-ai-backlog-hits-132-billion/), and closed up 0.6% at $297.06.

## The winners: HP, Analog Devices and the Dow's old names

HP ($HPQ) **jumped 11.5%** to $31.15, its best day since May, after its [fourth-quarter beat](/news/2026-11-24/hp-hpq-beats-q4-estimates-on-tariff-refunds-as-pc-units-fall-29/); MarketWatch called it a standout gainer in the tech rout. Analog Devices ($ADI) rose 6.4%, winning back part of Tuesday's 17.4% fall after its [flat first-quarter forecast](/news/2026-11-24/analog-devices-adi-beats-q4-estimates-but-a-flat-q1-forecast-sends-shares-down-8/).

The Dow's finish came from stocks outside the AI hardware trade. IBM ($IBM) rose 3.3%, Sherwin-Williams ($SHW) 2.4% and Salesforce ($CRM) 2.0%. Smaller companies did better still: the Russell 2000 gained 0.7%, and Meta ($META), up 3.4%, was the biggest lift to the S&P 500.

## Bonds rallied on hot inflation

The 10-year Treasury yield **fell to 4.87%**, down 0.04 point, even after a hotter core inflation number. CNBC said investors bought bonds for protection, and the Journal said yields extended their fall after the day's data. The VIX, Wall Street's fear gauge, hit 34.95 at its high before closing up 7.3% at 28.29. Oil rose 2.5% to $84.12 a barrel.

US markets are closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving, and stocks close early, at 1 p.m. ET, on Friday. The Fed's next rate decision is due on 9 December.
