ConocoPhillips ($COP) reported adjusted earnings of **$3.27 a share** for the third quarter before the open on Thursday, double the $1.61 of a year earlier and 7% above the $3.06 Zacks consensus. Higher oil and gas prices did the work, and the company raised its quarterly dividend 11%. The stock closed **down 1.1% at $135.46**, a day after a 5.2% fall, on a day the S&P 500 lost 4.4%.

## What stood out

**Price did all the work.** The average price ConocoPhillips realized across oil, gas and natural gas liquids rose **32% to $61.40** a barrel of oil equivalent, the release showed, while volumes fell. An oil producer's costs barely move with the price of crude, so most of that rise dropped straight to profit, which more than doubled to $4.0 billion.

*Verdict: a quarter made by the oil market, not by the wells.*

**Volumes went backwards.** Production fell 2% on a like-for-like basis, as growth in the Lower 48 shale fields was more than offset by the Middle East conflict's effect on Qatar and higher royalties at Surmont, its Canadian oil sands project, the company said. The Lower 48 alone produced 1,517 thousand barrels a day.

**The cash went back to owners.** Of $7.2 billion in operating cash, $3.4 billion went to shareholders and $3.1 billion to capital spending, with $1.6 billion more coming in from selling non-core assets. The company said it is on track to return 45% of its operating cash flow this year.

## The outlook

Fourth-quarter production should be **2.32 to 2.36 million** barrels of oil equivalent a day, and full-year capital spending and operating cost guidance are unchanged. For 2027, the company gave a preliminary view of slightly lower capital spending and low-single-digit underlying growth, Benzinga's call transcript and Mizuho's note showed. "We also remain on track to deliver a peer-leading $7 billion free cash flow inflection by 2029. Importantly, 2029 is not the endpoint," Chief Executive Andy O'Brien said in the release. Mizuho raised its price target to $149 from $146 on Thursday and kept an Outperform rating, Investing.com reported.

## Our take

We were impressed by the cash discipline: an 11% dividend raise and $2.4 billion of buybacks in one quarter, with capital spending held flat. On the other hand, production shrank, so the beat depends on prices that [fell almost 8% on Monday](/news/2026-11-02/stock-market-today-oil-sinks-8-but-a-tech-reversal-drags-the-nasdaq-down-1-1/) as hopes for US-Iran talks grew. Overall, a solid quarter, but one built on the price of oil, the same pattern as [Diamondback Energy's beat](/news/2026-11-02/diamondback-energy-fang-beats-q3-estimates-and-raises-its-dividend-7-3/) and [Exxon Mobil's](/news/2026-11-01/exxon-mobil-xom-tops-q3-estimates-as-refining-profit-more-than-doubles/).

That is why a beat could not lift the stock. It sits 12.1% below its 30 October high and is still up 44.7% this year. The next test is fourth-quarter results in early 2027, when a full quarter at lower prices meets the dividend just raised.
