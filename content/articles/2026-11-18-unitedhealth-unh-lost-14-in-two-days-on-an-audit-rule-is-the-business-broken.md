## How it got here

UnitedHealth became America's biggest health company by pairing its insurer, UnitedHealthcare, with Optum, which runs doctors' practices, a pharmacy-benefit manager and health-data software. Until 2023 that machine was steady: operating margins held between 7.0% and 8.8% every year from 2016, and diluted earnings per share more than tripled to $23.86, according to its SEC filings.

Then 2025 broke it. On 17 April 2025 the stock fell 22.4% after the company cut its outlook on higher Medicare Advantage costs; on 13 May, Chief Executive Andrew Witty stepped down, former boss Stephen Hemsley returned, and the outlook was suspended, Yahoo Finance and InsuranceNewsNet reported. The stock closed at **$237.77** on 1 August 2025, 62% below its November 2024 high of $625.25.

This year has been the repair: three raises to the 2026 outlook, then two days of selling this week. This week the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) proposed CRUSH, an anti-fraud rule that would widen Medicare Advantage audits, and UnitedHealth has lost $56.87 a share since Monday's close. **Is this a damaged business, or a mended one with a new bill it can afford?**

## The business in one line

The government, employers and individuals pay UnitedHealth a fixed premium per member every month, and it keeps whatever is left after paying for their care.

Revenue grew about 10% a year from 2016 to 2025, to $447.6 billion, but operating margin halved to 4.2% last year. The third quarter's 5.8% beat the 3.8% of a year earlier, though first quarters run high for insurers before members use more care through the year. *Verdict: the engine is the insurer; the Optum businesses supply most of the extra margin.*

## The numbers that matter

### Growth
Revenue grew **11.7% a year** over five years, in the top quarter of 19 health-care services peers, but the company guided 2026 revenue to more than $439 billion, a 2% fall, because it is shedding unprofitable members, its January release said. *Verdict: shrinking on purpose.*

### Profitability
Operating margin over the 12 months to September was **5.3%**, against 2.7% at Elevance Health ($ELV), 3.4% at Cigna ($CI), 3.6% at CVS Health ($CVS) and 1.5% at Humana ($HUM). The medical care ratio, the share of premiums spent on care, fell to 88.4% in the third quarter from 89.9% a year earlier, the release said. *Verdict: the best in its group, still below its own past.*

### Cash
Free cash flow was **$22.8 billion** in the 12 months to September, 5.1% of revenue, above the 3.5% peer median. *Verdict: strong.*

### Returns
Return on capital of about **12.4%** ranks in the top tenth of its peers, against 6.6% at Elevance and 7.7% at Humana. *Verdict: strong.*

### Balance sheet
Net debt was **$48.5 billion** at 30 September, two years of operating profit. *Verdict: fine for an insurer of this size, with little room to spare for a large repayment.*

## Why the stock is where it is

1. **Medical costs broke 2025.** Operating margin fell from 8.3% in the first quarter of 2025 to 0.3% in the fourth, and earnings per share fell from $23.86 in 2023 to $13.23 in 2025.
2. **Washington's rates.** On 27 January 2026 a near-flat 2027 Medicare Advantage rate proposal cut the stock 19.6%, CNBC and Morningstar reported; the final rate in April was a 2.48% rise.
3. **Shrinking to fix it.** Medicare Advantage membership is set to fall by about 1.1 million this year, the company said in July, as it drops plans that lose money.
4. **Audits and probes.** The Justice Department has criminal and civil investigations into its Medicare Advantage billing, which UnitedHealth confirmed in July 2025, The Hill and Healthcare Finance News reported. This week's CRUSH proposal adds an audit bill on top.

## Testing the worry

**Costs: temporary.** The outlook for 2026 adjusted earnings went from more than $17.75 in January to at least $20.50 in October, the company's releases show The company credited the third quarter's lower care costs to medical cost management, repricing and changes to plan benefits. Elevance and Humana still earn lower margins, so the cost spike hit the whole industry, and UnitedHealth is mending faster than its rivals.

**Rates: lasting, but survivable.** Medicare sets the price every year, so this risk never goes away. The January scare ended in a 2.48% rise, and the 2028 proposal is the next test, due early next year.

**Audits: real, and sizeable.** CRUSH would extrapolate error rates from sampled records across whole contracts back to payment year 2018, without the adjustment insurers fought for, Becker's and Crowell & Moring reported. CMS expects to recover about $9 billion a payment year across the industry, GuruFocus and heygotrade reported. Mizuho analyst Ann Hynes named Humana and UnitedHealth as the most exposed, The Fly reported.

**Probes: can't be sized.** No charges have been filed, and the company has not put a number on the probes.

The worry that survives is Washington: the audit rule, the probes and the yearly rate, not the medical bills that broke 2025.

## The risks we'd watch

1. **The audit back-bill, sized.** UnitedHealth held about 29% of Medicare Advantage enrollment in 2025 and 26% in 2026, according to KFF figures reported by Becker's; we use the higher share as the worst case. If its share of the $9 billion matched that, it would owe about $2.6 billion a payment year before tax. Our worst case: seven years from 2018 to 2024 is $18.3 billion, or about **$16 a share** after a 21% tax; the same $2.6 billion a year going forward would cut the $20.50 earnings floor by 11%, or about $40 a share at today's 17 times. Together that is about **$56 a share**, almost exactly the $56.87 the stock lost since Monday.
2. **The offsets.** Humana won a court ruling in September 2025 that vacated CMS's 2023 version of this rule on procedural grounds, Crowell and Milliman reported, so this one will be fought too. Insurers can also price a known audit cost into next year's plan bids. Comments are open until 19 January 2027.
3. **The Justice Department.** A settlement or charges could cost more than the audit rule. No one has sized it, and the probe has run since 2025.
4. **A break-up push.** Senator Elizabeth Warren called on Monday for UnitedHealth, CVS and Cigna to be split up, as we [reported](/news/2026-11-17/why-unitedhealth-unh-stock-is-down-today/); a law would take years, but it is a threat to Optum Rx's model.

In proportion: the worst case on audits takes a year's profit growth, not the franchise, and the market has already charged it.

## The moat

- **Scale.** UnitedHealth and Humana held nearly half (47%) of Medicare Advantage enrollment in 2024, KFF found, and UnitedHealth guided to about 47 million medical members this year. Size gives it the data to price plans and the leverage to negotiate with hospitals.
- **Optum's own profits.** Optum Insight earned $1.28 billion on $5.5 billion of revenue in the third quarter, a 23% margin; no other insurer owns a business like it.
- **Evidence it works.** Its 5.3% operating margin is 1.7 points above CVS's, the next best of the big insurers, and its return on capital is the top tenth of its group.

Will it be here, and stronger, in 10 years? Here, yes; stronger depends on how much of Optum's model Washington lets it keep.

## Our verdict

**Business: Solid. Price: Below its usual price.** The 2025 break was costs, and costs are mending: margins are back to 5.8%, guidance has been raised three times, and it still out-earns every big rival. The price sits at 22.3 times trailing earnings against a 24.1 times median, after a two-day fall that matches our worst case for the audit rule. What would change our mind is a number from the Justice Department, or an insurer sizing its own audit exposure above that worst case. The next dates are the 2028 Medicare rate proposal early next year and the 19 January close of comments on CRUSH.

The tension: the business is mending, but Washington now sets its margins.
