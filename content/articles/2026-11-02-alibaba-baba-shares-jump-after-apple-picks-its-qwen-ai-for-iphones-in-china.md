## What happened

Shares of Chinese e-commerce and cloud company Alibaba ($BABA) **jumped 5.8%** to $103.69 as of 10:15 a.m. ET on Monday, after it said on Saturday that its Qwen AI model will power Apple Intelligence, Apple's ($AAPL) set of AI features, on iPhones in China. Qwen will sit behind Siri, Writing Tools and Visual Intelligence for users in China, Xinhua reported, and Seoul Economic Daily and StreetInsider reported the same terms.

Developers can test the features from Wednesday 4 November, and a full launch for consumers is planned for December, according to Seoul Economic Daily and TechTimes. Users can opt in or out, and Alibaba said it won't use their requests or the answers to train its models. China's internet regulator approved Apple Intelligence in July, which cleared the way.

## Why it matters

In China, Apple needed a local AI model and the regulator's approval before it could switch these features on. Being that partner puts Qwen in front of China's iPhone users and sends their requests through Alibaba's cloud, which is how the deal could earn money: more demand for its data centers rather than a fee per user. Analysts at Citi said the deal could speed up wider use of Qwen and raise demand for Alibaba Cloud, StreetInsider reported. Neither company has disclosed financial terms.

Other Chinese tech stocks rose with it: PDD Holdings ($PDD) was up 5.2% and Baidu ($BIDU) 2.9% at 10:15 a.m. Apple was down 1.2% ahead of its results after the close today.

## What the market is telling us

Alibaba's shares moved 5% or more on 13 of the last 252 sessions. Through Friday the stock was down 33.1% this year and 44.7% below its 52-week closing high of $177.18 from 22 January, and $1,000 invested five years ago would have been worth $594.

Monday's gain wins back only a small part of this year's fall. We'd judge the deal by usage: the December launch, and whether Alibaba's cloud growth speeds up in its next report, will show whether it brings real revenue or only a headline.
