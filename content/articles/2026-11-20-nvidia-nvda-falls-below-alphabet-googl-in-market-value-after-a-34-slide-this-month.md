Nvidia ($NVDA) **fell 7.2% to $135.47** by 12:15 p.m. ET on Friday, by our quotes, and slipped behind Alphabet ($GOOGL) to become the fourth most valuable US company, after Apple and Microsoft, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters and to Investing.com. The stock is on course for a seventh straight weekly loss, its longest losing streak since 2008, Reuters and MarketWatch reported.

The fall is **33.8% since the end of October**, by our price data, and 43.4% below its 6 October record close of $239.24. It has kept going after Nvidia beat estimates and guided to $130 billion on Tuesday ([our recap](/news/2026-11-17/nvidia-nvda-tops-q3-estimates-and-guides-to-130-billion-in-its-first-report-without-huang/)), as doubts about who will succeed Jensen Huang and how long AI spending lasts outweighed the numbers, Reuters reported. Friday's drop came as the AI sell-off spread after Anthropic's shares slid on their second day of trading ([our story](/news/2026-11-20/micron-arista-and-celestica-shares-slide-what-happened/)).

Alphabet, which pairs its AI spending with search and cloud revenue, was up 0.6% as of 12:15 p.m. ET. The next test for Nvidia is its choice of chief executive: the board has hired search firm Heidrick & Struggles ([our story](/news/2026-11-17/nvidia-nvda-hires-heidrick-for-its-ceo-search-and-talks-to-arms-rene-haas/)), and no date has been set.
