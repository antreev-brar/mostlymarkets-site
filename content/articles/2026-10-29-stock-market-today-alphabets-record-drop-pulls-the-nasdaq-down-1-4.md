Alphabet had its worst day since it went public in 2004, and it dragged big technology stocks down with it on Thursday, even as most of the market rose. The Nasdaq Composite fell 1.38% to 26,184.72, and the S&P 500 slipped 0.30% to 7,720.39, ending a three-day winning streak. The Dow lost 0.25% to 52,342.56, while the Russell 2000 index of smaller companies rose 1.01%.

## Alphabet revived worries about AI spending

Alphabet ($GOOGL) plunged 12.6% to $283.82 after its search revenue missed estimates and its free cash flow came in at negative $9.07 billion, as it spent $51.36 billion on property and equipment in a single quarter ([full story](/news/2026-10-29/alphabet-googl-shares-plunge-after-search-falls-short-and-cash-burn-grows/)). NBC News and Yahoo Finance said the drop erased close to $500 billion of market value, the second-largest one-day loss for a US company after Nvidia's in January 2025.

Software took the next-hardest hit. ServiceNow ($NOW) plunged 27.0%, its worst day on record, after a soft forecast for future bookings ([full story](/news/2026-10-29/servicenow-now-stock-has-its-worst-day-on-record-after-soft-forecast/)), and Fortinet ($FTNT) fell 20.1% after cutting its service revenue forecast ([full story](/news/2026-10-29/fortinet-ftnt-shares-plunge-after-it-cuts-its-service-revenue-forecast/)).

The worry spread to companies that supply the AI build-out. Caterpillar ($CAT) beat profit estimates but kept its sales outlook unchanged and plunged 9.25%, its worst day since March 2020, Yahoo Finance said ([full story](/news/2026-10-29/caterpillar-cat-beats-q3-profit-estimates-but-shares-plunge-on-its-outlook/)). Microsoft ($MSFT), which reported alongside Alphabet on Wednesday, ended down 0.16%.

## Most stocks rose anyway

Eight of the 11 S&P 500 sectors rose; only technology, communication services, and consumer discretionary fell, Reuters and Investing.com reported. Eli Lilly ($LLY) jumped 6.6% to a record close after its revenue beat estimates by 8% and it raised its sales forecast ([full story](/news/2026-10-29/eli-lilly-lly-tops-q3-estimates-as-mounjaro-sales-jump-65/)). Carvana ($CVNA) soared 15.7% on record car sales ([full story](/news/2026-10-29/why-carvana-cvna-stock-is-soaring-today/)).

DoorDash ($DASH) rose 5.6% after it agreed to buy Instacart for about $17 billion, or $65 a share in cash or stock, the companies said.

## The economy grew faster than expected

The economy grew at a 3.4% annual rate in the third quarter, up from 2.2% in the second, the Bureau of Economic Analysis said. That beat the 3.2% median forecast in a Wall Street Journal survey. Inflation stayed high: the PCE price index, which tracks what consumers pay, rose 3.6% in the year to September, and 3.1% excluding food and energy, in line with forecasts.

First-time claims for unemployment benefits fell by 1,000 to 194,000 last week, below the 199,000 economists expected, the Labor Department said. Treasury yields fell anyway: the 10-year yield dropped to 4.94% from 5.05% on Wednesday, CNBC and the AP reported. A day earlier, the Fed had held rates at 3.75% to 4.00%.

In Europe, the European Central Bank held its deposit rate at 2.50%. President Christine Lagarde said she will step down on 7 February 2027, about nine months before her term ends, the AP, Investing.com, and Euronews reported.

## After the bell

Amazon ($AMZN) slid 6.6% after hours by 4:53 p.m. ET after missing estimates for profit and advertising, even as its cloud arm grew 39% ([full story](/news/2026-10-29/amazon-amzn-q3-aws-speeds-up-but-ads-and-profit-miss-estimates/)). Intel ($INTC) fell 15.5% after forecasting fourth-quarter revenue well below estimates ([full story](/news/2026-10-29/intel-intc-q3-profit-tops-estimates-shares-plunge-on-a-weak-forecast/)). Reddit ($RDDT) fell 16.4% after forecasting fourth-quarter revenue below estimates and warning that traffic from Google searches is shrinking, and Stryker ($SYK) fell 11.5% after missing estimates and cutting its growth outlook.
