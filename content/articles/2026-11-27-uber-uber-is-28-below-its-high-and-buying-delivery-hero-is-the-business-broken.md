*Uber is a solid business priced below its usual multiple: the market is paying 13 times its free cash flow because it fears robotaxi makers will go around it, and the next two years of driverless launches will decide whether that fear is right.*

On Friday, Uber ($UBER) said holders of 87.13% of Delivery Hero's shares had accepted its cash offer, [locking up a takeover](/news/2026-11-27/uber-uber-wins-87-of-delivery-hero-in-its-148-billion-takeover-offer/) Bloomberg valued at $14.8 billion. The stock barely noticed: at **$66.69** as of 12:15 p.m. ET, it sits 28% below its closing high of $92.57 from 8 December 2025, even though the company has never made more cash.

## How it got here

Uber spent its first decade buying growth: in 2019 it booked $13.0 billion of revenue and lost $8.6 billion on an operating basis, according to its SEC filings. The turn came in 2023, its first year of operating profit, at $1.1 billion. By 2025 revenue had reached $52.0 billion and operating profit $5.6 billion.

The pitch changed with it, from "can this ever make money?" to "how long can it keep growing?" Then, from the end of 2025, investors began asking a third question: what happens when cars no longer need drivers, and the companies that build them don't need Uber?

This week Uber won control of Delivery Hero, the Berlin-based food-delivery group, adding a big, debt-funded bet on the half of the business that has nothing to do with robotaxis. **Is Uber's network a lasting toll road, or a middleman about to be cut out?**

## The business in one line

Riders, diners and shippers pay Uber a cut of every trip, meal and load booked through its apps, more than 4 billion times a quarter, and about 55 million of them also pay for an Uber One membership.

## How it makes money

| Segment | What it sells | Share of Q3 2026 sales | Revenue growth, Q3 year on year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mobility | Rides with human drivers, plus robotaxi partners on its app | 51% ($7.52 billion) | -2% (bookings +18%) |
| Delivery | Restaurant, grocery and retail delivery | 37% ($5.49 billion) | +23% |
| Freight | Matching trucks with shippers | 12% ($1.76 billion) | +34% |

The Mobility decline is mostly accounting. Uber changed how it books UK rides, which it said cut total revenue growth by 8 percentage points in the quarter; Mobility bookings, the dollar value of rides, rose 18% and the segment's profit rose 27% to $2.36 billion, according to the results release.

## The numbers that matter

Our data groups Uber with 89 industrial stocks, so we also set it against its two closest US rivals.

### Growth
Revenue grew **36.1% a year** over five years, in the top tenth of its peer group, and 16.1% a year over the last two. The latest quarter's 9.6% looks slow only because of the UK change: gross bookings grew 20% to $59.8 billion, the release said.

### Profitability
The operating margin was **12.4%** in the 12 months to September, below the 17.2% median for its group, but up 54 points in five years. That compares with 5.3% at DoorDash ($DASH) and a loss at Lyft ($LYFT), on our SEC data.

### Cash
Free cash flow was **$10.4 billion** in the 12 months to September, an 18.4% margin against a peer median of 13.2%. A year earlier it was $8.7 billion.

### Returns
The return on capital is about **16.5%**, above the 13.4% median for its group, for a business that owns few cars and almost no restaurants.

### Balance sheet
Net debt was **$6.1 billion** at the end of September, under a year of operating profit, and buybacks have cut the diluted share count 4.8% in two years.

## Why the stock is where it is

1. **Robotaxi makers want their own customers.** Waymo, the Alphabet ($GOOGL) unit that leads US driverless rides, ended its Phoenix tie-up with Uber in June. The stock fell 4.3% on 24 July after the Financial Times reported Waymo was exploring an exit, and Waymo has since confirmed it will end Uber's exclusive hold in Austin and Atlanta in January 2028.
2. **Mobility looked soft.** Chief Executive Dara Khosrowshahi told analysts on 4 November that Mobility fell short of the company's own plans because driver supply lagged demand in several international markets, and Uber is spending on incentives to fix it. GAAP operating profit dropped to $1.45 billion in the third quarter from $1.89 billion in the second.
3. **A big, borrowed deal.** Uber lined up a bridge loan of about €14 billion for Delivery Hero, according to its law firm Cooley and The Next Web, and the deal can't close before the second half of 2027.
4. **The market turned on growth.** Uber fell 4.6% on 19 November, the day the [Dow dropped 1,101 points](/news/2026-11-19/stock-market-today-anthropic-jumps-17-in-its-debut-but-the-dow-drops-1101-points/), and hit its 52-week low the next day.

## Testing the worry

**Robotaxis: lasting, but not yet in the numbers.** Uber expects about 700 fully driverless vehicles on its app by the end of 2026 and more than 2,000 by the end of 2027, Khosrowshahi said on the 4 November call. Against 4.1 billion trips a quarter, that is a rounding error today. The worry is what Waymo's choice says about the future, and its co-chief executive was blunt about why it wants its own app.

> "In the early days, we would partner because we didn't have the scale... in that partnering, it was a little bit of a crapshoot." Waymo co-CEO Tekedra Mawakana, to Bloomberg, November 2026

Uber's answer is breadth: Zoox is due to launch on Uber in Las Vegas, Motional is moving to driverless service, and Nuro and Lucid are testing in the Bay Area ahead of a launch in the first quarter of 2027. Uber says its US robotaxi partners get about 30% more trips per vehicle a day on its network than on their own. The Waymo fleet stays on Uber at least until the current contract ends in May 2028, Uber said.

**Mobility softness: temporary.** The revenue drop is the UK accounting change, and the segment's profit still grew 27%. A driver shortage is fixable with money, and it is the cost of that fix, not lost riders, that dented third-quarter profit.

**The deal: lasting, but contained.** If Uber borrowed the full $14.8 billion, net debt would rise to about $21 billion, roughly three years of the $7.0 billion operating profit it made in the past 12 months, before Delivery Hero's own debt and profit. Uber says it will keep its investment-grade rating and gross leverage under two times on its own measure.

The worry that survives is the robotaxi one. The other two show up in this year's numbers, and the numbers are fine.

## The moat

- **The biggest demand pool.** Uber had 215 million monthly active consumers in the third quarter, up 14% in a year, the release said. A robotaxi fleet on Uber starts with riders on day one.
- **Membership.** About 55 million Uber One members, management said on the call. Members who ride and order food are harder to lure to a single-purpose app.
- **Profit at scale.** Uber's 12.4% operating margin compares with 5.3% at DoorDash, which grows faster (29.6% a year over two years) but keeps far less, and a 2.3% operating loss at Lyft.

## The risks we'd watch

1. **Robotaxi bypass in Mobility.** Mobility made $2.36 billion of the $3.39 billion of segment profit in the third quarter, about 70%. If the best driverless fleets sell rides on their own apps in Uber's biggest cities, that is the profit at risk.
2. **Deal execution.** Delivery Hero must sell its businesses in 14 overlapping markets to SSW Partners to win approvals, and closing isn't expected until the second half of 2027. Every quarter of delay keeps the debt cost without the profit.
3. **Paying up for supply.** Driver incentives are how Uber fixes a shortage, and the third quarter showed what they cost: operating profit fell about $440 million from the second quarter.

## What the price assumes

| Measure | Now | Its history | Peers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forward P/E | 16.2x | Not comparable (see trailing) | 20.3x group median; DoorDash 38.5x; Lyft 19.9x |
| Price to free cash flow | 13.1x | 13.5x to 27.2x at quarter ends since September 2024, median 18.7x | DoorDash 27.2x |
| Trailing P/E | 39.3x | Median 16.1x since October 2024, flattered by one-off gains | n/a |

The trailing P/E is distorted both ways: in 2024 Uber's net profit of $9.9 billion was more than three times its operating profit, thanks to gains below the operating line, while in the third quarter a $476 million loss on revaluing its stakes in other companies cut net income to $512 million. Free cash flow is the cleaner measure. At 13.1 times, the price is a 7.7% cash yield, which assumes cash flow grows slowly, if at all, from here; it grew 20% in the past year.

## Our verdict

**Business: Solid. Price: Below its usual price.** Uber grows bookings 20% a year, turns 18% of revenue into free cash and keeps more of each dollar than its rivals, but its operating margin is still below the median for its group and its biggest segment faces a threat it doesn't control. At 13.1 times free cash flow, against a median of 18.7 times since 2024, the price already assumes robotaxis take a real bite. What would change our mind: a second leading driverless fleet following Waymo onto its own app, or Mobility bookings growth falling below 10%. The next tests are the Nuro and Lucid launch in the Bay Area in the first quarter of 2027 and Uber's fourth-quarter results early next year.

Uber has never been more profitable, and investors have rarely paid less for its cash, because they are pricing the business it might lose rather than the one it has.
