HP ($HPQ) said after the close on Tuesday that fiscal fourth-quarter revenue **rose 9.3% to $16.0 billion** and adjusted EPS was $0.83, beating the $0.74 analysts expected, according to Benzinga and Nasdaq's consensus data. But $0.15 of that EPS came from tariff refunds, and the company's PC units fell 29% as memory costs pushed prices up. The stock rose 2.0% after hours to $28.51 as of 4:30 p.m. ET, after falling 3.3% in the regular session.

## What stood out

The beat came from a one-off. Without the $0.15 of tariff refunds, adjusted EPS would have been **$0.68**, below the $0.74 consensus and the bottom of HP's own $0.69 to $0.79 outlook, by our arithmetic from the release. Analysts' estimates ranged from $15.3 billion (Benzinga) to $15.47 billion (Zacks) for revenue, so the sales beat was real.

The PC business sold far fewer machines for more money. Personal Systems revenue rose 14% while units **fell 29%**, and its operating margin dropped to 3.0% from 5.8% a year earlier as memory prices rose. "We continued to grow our higher-value businesses, with AI PCs reaching nearly 60% of shipments, while taking steps to address memory and storage cost pressures," Interim Chief Executive Bruce Broussard said in the release. It is the same pattern we found in [the AI gadget upgrade](/news/2026-11-24/wall-street-is-betting-on-an-ai-gadget-upgrade-the-units-say-its-mostly-price/), and Dell's consumer PC sales fell 15% in its [quarter reported tonight](/news/2026-11-24/dell-technologies-dell-beats-q3-estimates-as-revenue-doubles-and-its-ai-backlog-hits-132-billion/).

## The outlook

HP guided to fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS of $3.15 to $3.45, but the number isn't comparable with this year's $3.33. From the first quarter it will leave stock-based pay out of adjusted earnings, a change it said would have added $0.38 a share in fiscal 2026. If stock pay costs about the same next year, the guide works out to roughly $2.77 to $3.07 on the old basis, below this year's $3.33. Chief Financial Officer Karen Parkhill said the outlook "reflects a prudent view of continued cost and demand pressures." HP raised its quarterly dividend to $0.31 from $0.30.

## Our take

Strip out the refunds and the accounting change and HP is earning less on each PC as memory costs bite, with no sign yet of when that turns. The stock already prices that in: by our data it trades at 9.3 times forward earnings, against 16.5 times for hardware peers. The test is the first quarter, when the refunds fade: HP's own guide of $0.66 to $0.76 already sits below this quarter.

## What the market is telling us

HP's shares have moved 5% or more on 19 of the last 252 sessions. The stock is up 25.4% this year, and at $27.94 it sits 21.2% below its 52-week closing high of $35.48 from 11 September 2026.
