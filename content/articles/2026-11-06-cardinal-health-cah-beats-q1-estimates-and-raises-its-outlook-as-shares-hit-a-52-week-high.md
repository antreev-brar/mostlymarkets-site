Cardinal Health ($CAH) reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of $67.5 billion before the open on Thursday, up 5% from a year earlier and above the $66.6 billion LSEG consensus cited by CNBC. Adjusted earnings rose 28% to **$3.26 a share**, against $2.94 expected, and the drug distributor raised its full-year forecast. The shares **rose 2.3% to $267.82**, a 52-week closing high, on a day the S&P 500 fell 4.4%.

## What stood out

**The core drug business is compounding.** Pharmaceutical and Specialty Solutions, which delivers medicines from manufacturers to pharmacies and hospitals, grew revenue 6% to $62.6 billion and profit **17% to $783 million**, the release said, helped by specialty drugs and its generics program. Distributors keep a thin slice of each sale, so profit growing three times faster than revenue is the number that matters.

*Verdict: strong, and the reason the stock is at a 52-week high.*

**Medical products are still the weak spot.** The segment that makes and distributes medical supplies, including Cardinal's own brand, kept revenue flat at $3.2 billion while profit fell to $33 million, on weaker distribution and currency, the release said. CNBC noted that the smaller "Other" businesses also fell short of its estimates even as their profit rose 16%.

## The outlook

Cardinal raised its fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS range by 40 cents to **$12.80 to $13.00**, which it said is growth of 14% to 15% on fiscal 2026's reported result. It lifted its profit growth forecast for the drug segment to 10% to 12%, from 8% to 11%. It also signed a binding letter of intent to renew its drug distribution agreements with CVS Health, and finished a $500 million buyback in the quarter.

## Our take

We were impressed by the drug segment's profit growth and by the CVS renewal, which removes the biggest single risk to the volume outlook. On the other hand, the medical products turnaround has not shown up yet, and a lower tax rate and fewer shares added to the EPS beat. Overall, a strong quarter that raises the bar for the rest of the year.

The stock is up **30.3% this year**, and at 21.6 times forward earnings it trades above the 17.1 median for health-care services and distributors by our data, so investors are already paying for continued beats. The next test is fiscal second-quarter results early in 2027, and whether drug segment profit keeps growing in double digits.
