Coca-Cola ($KO) reported third-quarter revenue of $13.38 billion, up 7% year on year, beating the $12.92 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC. Comparable earnings, which exclude one-off items, were 91 cents a share, up 11% and ahead of the 88-cent forecast. The results came out before trading opens; the shares closed Monday at $92.70, up 32.6% this year.

## What stood out

Coke sold more drinks almost everywhere except its home market. Unit case volume, a count of drinks sold to consumers, rose 5%, led by India, China, Mexico and Brazil, the company said. Volume grew 6% in Asia Pacific, 6% in Latin America and 5% in Europe, the Middle East and Africa, but was flat in North America, where growth in Coca-Cola itself was offset by declines in water, sports drinks, coffee and tea.

Revenue grew faster than volume. Concentrate sales, what Coke sells to its bottlers, rose 7%, running ahead of volume because of the timing of shipments, and price/mix added 1 point as price increases outweighed a less favorable mix. Among brands, Coca-Cola Zero Sugar volume grew 16% and Diet Coke 6%. The comparable operating margin rose a full point, to 32.9%.

"Our results this quarter demonstrate our system's sustained momentum, giving us confidence in our updated full year guidance," Chief Executive Henrique Braun said in the results release.

## The outlook

Coke raised its full-year targets for the third straight quarter, CNBC and Quartz reported. It now expects organic revenue, which strips out currency moves, acquisitions and divestitures, to grow at least 5%, up from about 5%. It expects comparable EPS to grow at least 11%, up from 9% to 10%, including about 3 points of help from currency, and free cash flow of at least $13.0 billion, up from about $12.4 billion.

Coke will give 2027 guidance with its fourth-quarter results. For now it flagged a headwind of about 8% to next year's comparable revenue from acquisitions and divestitures, and a slight drag from currency; it expects the pending sale of its bottling operations in Africa to close this quarter.

## Our take

The good: a beat on revenue and profit, a wider margin and a third raise to the outlook, with growth coming from many countries at once. The bad: flat volume in North America, its home market, and about 3 points of this year's EPS growth comes from currency, a tailwind that turns into a slight headwind in 2027. What to watch next quarter is whether North American volume returns to growth.
