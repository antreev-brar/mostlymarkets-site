Applied Materials ($AMAT) reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of **$10.41 billion** after the close on Thursday, up 53% year on year and above the roughly $10.3 billion analysts expected, Investing.com and Crypto Briefing reported. Adjusted earnings of $4.16 a share topped the $4.06 consensus, the figure Investing.com and Nasdaq's estimates both show. The shares closed down 3.6% at $380.95 on Thursday before the report, on the day Nvidia's CEO resigned, and were up 0.6% at $383.10 before Friday's open.

## What stood out
The equipment that makes memory chips drove the quarter. Semiconductor Systems, which sells chip-making tools, **grew 64%** to $8.0 billion, and DRAM makers took 33% of it against 28% a year earlier, while foundry and logic fell to 58% from 66%, the release shows. Memory makers are adding capacity for the high-bandwidth memory that sits next to AI chips, and each new DRAM line needs Applied's deposition and etch tools.

China shrank as a share of sales to 25% from 29%, though its revenue still rose 35% to $2.64 billion. Profits grew faster than sales: adjusted operating income rose 89% to $3.68 billion, from about $1.95 billion, as the margin widened by 6.7 points. "AI continues to fuel strong incremental demand for semiconductors," Chief Executive Gary Dickerson said in the release, adding that the chip-systems business is on track to grow more than 40% in calendar 2026.

## The outlook
Applied guided first-quarter revenue to **$11.1 billion**, plus or minus $500 million, and adjusted EPS to $4.35, plus or minus 20 cents. Both midpoints sit above the consensus figures Investing.com and InvestingLive cited, which differ between the two outlets. Applied bought back $2.0 billion of stock in the quarter, its largest quarterly buyback in more than eight years, Chief Financial Officer Brice Hill said.

## Our take
This was a clean beat and a higher guide, and the mix tells the story: memory spending is carrying growth while foundry orders cool. Over two years Applied's revenue grew 7.2% a year against a 22.0% median for 25 chipmakers in our data, so it lagged the AI build-out until these two quarters. Its return on capital of 27.4% ranks in the top quarter of that group. The stock trades at 21.4 times forward earnings, below the 23.1x peer median, and sits 47.3% below its June record; the next test is whether DRAM orders hold into the first quarter.
