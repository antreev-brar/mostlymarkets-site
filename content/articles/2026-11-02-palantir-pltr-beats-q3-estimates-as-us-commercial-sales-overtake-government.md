Palantir Technologies ($PLTR) said after the close on Monday that its third-quarter revenue **grew 96% to $2.31 billion**, ahead of the $2.18 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters. Adjusted earnings per share came to $0.44, against the $0.41 forecast, CNBC reported. The shares, which fell 3.1% in the regular session to $178.34, were up 1.8% at $181.62 in after-hours trading as of 4:15 p.m. ET.

## What stood out

For the first time, Palantir's sales to US companies were bigger than its sales to the US government, the release said. US commercial revenue of **$984 million** grew 148% from a year earlier, more than four times the 20.5% median growth of the 31 software companies in our coverage in their latest quarter. Chief Executive Alex Karp said in the release that "the Sovereign AI movement is imposing the most dramatic dichotomy between haves and have nots that I have witnessed in 30 years of business."

Growth came with profit. Adjusted operating income was $1.43 billion, a 62% margin, and adjusted free cash flow was $1.22 billion, a 53% margin, the release said. Over the 12 months to June, Palantir's operating margin of 42.8% ranked in the top tenth of its software peers, whose median is 9.7%, according to its SEC filings.

## The outlook

The fourth-quarter forecast of about **$2.65 billion** is roughly 8% above the FactSet consensus of $2.46 billion, MarketScreener reported, and implies growth of about 88% on last year's $1.41 billion. Palantir also raised its full-year forecasts for adjusted operating income to $5.245 billion to $5.249 billion and for adjusted free cash flow to $4.8 billion to $5.0 billion. Its call starts at 5 p.m. ET.

## The one debate

The bear case sits in the contract numbers. Total contract value signed in the quarter rose only 6% from a year earlier, to $2.92 billion, and US commercial contract value rose 11%, the release showed, far slower than revenue. Remaining deal value in US commercial, the work customers have signed but not yet paid for, was $6.52 billion, up 79% on the year but only 4% on the quarter. If signings stay this slow, today's 96% growth will be hard to repeat in 2027.

## Our take

This was a clean beat on every line that the market watches, and a fourth-quarter guide that already sits well above where Wall Street was. The price asks a lot: Palantir trades at 104.5 times forward earnings against a software median of 26.5, so it has to keep doing this. The number to watch next quarter is contract value: a pickup there would answer the one weak line in this report.
