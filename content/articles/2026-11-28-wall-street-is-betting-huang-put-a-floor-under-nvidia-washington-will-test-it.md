The story investors told this week is that Nvidia's ($NVDA) crisis was about one man, and that bringing him back ended it. Melius Research's Ben Reitzes put it in four words on Monday, 23 November, when he reversed his downgrade: "Jensen's back — so are we," CNBC and OnInvest reported.

The stock agreed. Nvidia **rose 19.9%** in the week, from $125.53 on 20 November to $150.49 at Friday's shortened close, after the board rehired Jensen Huang before the open on Monday. Three days in Washington, starting Tuesday, 1 December, will show whether that floor is real or just the first good news after the worst of it.

## The story in one sentence

> "A win for AI and a win for investors." Oppenheimer analyst Rick Schafer, on Huang's return, upgrading Nvidia to outperform on 23 November, as reported by OnInvest and IBTimes.

The chain runs like this. If Huang's return keeps customers and staff in place, the $130 billion fourth-quarter forecast holds, and a stock at 10 times forward earnings has room to re-rate. That would lift the suppliers and chip-renting clouds that fell with Nvidia in November. The bet assumes the probes end in fines or nothing, not in a restatement or new limits on where Nvidia can sell.

## The evidence for it

### 1. The brokers who left came back within 11 days

Melius moved to buy with a $220 target, Bernstein's Stacy Rasgon to outperform at $200, and Oppenheimer to outperform at $250, CNBC, OnInvest and IBTimes reported. All three had stepped back after Huang resigned on 12 November. Citi's Atif Malik kept his buy rating and raised his target to **$240**, from $225.

### 2. Demand never left

Nvidia reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of **$115.8 billion** on 17 November against $109.1 billion expected, and guided to $130 billion for the current quarter, its release and Reuters showed ([our recap](/news/2026-11-17/nvidia-nvda-tops-q3-estimates-and-guides-to-130-billion-in-its-first-report-without-huang/)). That was the first report without Huang, so the orders were in place before he came back.

### 3. The stock held most of Monday's jump

Nvidia gained 31.5% on Monday, adding about $952 billion in market value ([our wrap](/news/2026-11-23/stock-market-today-nvidia-adds-952-billion-as-jensen-huang-returns-and-the-nasdaq-jumps-6-5/)). It then fell on Tuesday and Wednesday as Huang admitted to breaking its code of conduct and tariff reports hit chips, but it closed Friday **8.8% below** Monday's close and still 19.9% above the 20 November low.

### 4. The price already charges for a bad outcome

At 10.4 times forward earnings, Nvidia trades at about half the 18.9x median of the 25 chipmakers we track, by our analysis of its SEC filings. Its trailing P/E of 19.0x compares with a median of 45.8x since August 2024, while its free cash flow margin over two years, 42.5%, is in the top tenth of its peers.

## The case against it

The doubters this week were not analysts but institutions: S&P Global Ratings, the lawmakers who kept their hearings, and the officials writing export rules. Three counter-points hold to the same standard.

1. **The governance questions got louder after he came back.** Huang told staff on 24 November that he broke Nvidia's code of conduct ([our brief](/news/2026-11-24/nvidias-nvda-huang-tells-staff-he-broke-its-code-of-conduct-before-december-hearings/)). A day later S&P put Nvidia's AA rating on watch for a downgrade, saying the exit and return did not answer its questions about board oversight ([our brief](/news/2026-11-25/sp-puts-nvidias-nvda-aa-rating-on-watch-for-a-downgrade-over-governance/)).
2. **The problem is wider than one executive.** The Wall Street Journal reported that Jay Puri, Nvidia's former head of worldwide sales, married Cenly Chen, a senior executive at Super Micro Computer ($SMCI), in March after years of dating while the companies did business, Outlook India also reported. Super Micro's co-founder was indicted in March over servers allegedly diverted to China; neither Puri nor Chen is charged, and both say the relationship did not affect business.
3. **The export rules are tightening on its customers.** Nvidia told partners including Dell ($DELL) and Supermicro to stop shipping its servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, the FT reported ([our brief](/news/2026-11-26/nvidia-nvda-halts-server-shipments-to-four-southeast-asian-countries-ft-says/)). Sellers of its chips now bar chip-renting clouds with any Chinese renters, CNBC reported ([our brief](/news/2026-11-26/nvidia-chip-sellers-bar-neoclouds-that-rent-any-capacity-to-chinese-firms-cnbc-says/)).

The third is the strongest, because it reaches revenue. Sales billed in Singapore were 22% of Nvidia's revenue the last time it reported them ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-23/nvidias-crisis-has-moved-from-its-boss-to-its-books-what-the-evidence-says/)), and no rehire changes where the US lets it ship.

## What would change minds

- **Tuesday, 1 December:** Huang gives a closed-door, transcribed interview to three House committees on export controls, CNBC and Tom's Hardware reported, on the day he is due to give a keynote at Nvidia's GTC conference in Washington. General Counsel Tim Teter follows on 2 December.
- **Thursday, 3 December:** Huang appears before the Senate Banking Committee in public, his first formal congressional hearing, Tom's Hardware and CNBC reported. Anything tying the company, not only Huang, to diverted chips would undo the week's re-rating.
- **By about 4 December:** Nvidia's third-quarter 10-Q is due. It is the first filing that must describe the SEC and California probes in Nvidia's own words; a change to past numbers would confirm the bear case.

## Our read

**Partly supported.** Huang's return answered the question the market was asking on 12 November, who runs Nvidia, and the demand numbers say customers never left. The weakest link is that the questions now being asked belong to Congress, the SEC and S&P, none of which answers to the board. The one number we'll watch is Nvidia's 10-Q, due by about 4 December, for any change to past revenue or a new line on the probes.
