On Friday, Arm Holdings ($ARM) fell 4.6% after the Financial Times reported that Nvidia's board had approached its chief executive, Rene Haas, about replacing Jensen Huang ([our story](/news/2026-11-13/nvidia-nvda-approached-arms-rene-haas-to-be-its-next-ceo-ft-reports/)). Arm keeps almost every dollar of revenue after direct costs, and it is a useful reminder that a high gross margin, the share of each sale left after the cost of making it, is where a quality check starts, not where it ends.

Our screen ranked the companies in our coverage by gross margin over their latest 12 months, from SEC filings in our fundamentals data, and left out stocks we listed in the last 14 days. We then judged the leaders on what reaches the owner: operating margin, free cash flow, returns on capital, growth over five years and two, and buybacks, each against their peers.

```picks
like: ADBE, ADSK
skip: ARM
```

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. Adobe ($ADBE)
**Gross margin: 89.3% in the 12 months to August 2026.** Adobe sells subscriptions to Photoshop, Acrobat and its other creative and document software.

1. The margin turns into cash: free cash flow was **41.1% of revenue** over the last two years, in the top tenth of the 32 software companies we cover, against a median of 27.7%.
2. The cash goes back to owners: its diluted share count fell 11.6% in two years, against a peer median rise of 0.3%, and return on capital of about 60% is the highest in the group.
3. It is putting rivals' AI inside its tools: on 10 November Adobe and Google said they would bring Google's AI models into Adobe's creative apps and connect their workflows, Adobe's release said and Dow Jones reported.

**The price:** 9.1 times expected earnings against a software median of 26.6 times, and its trailing P/E of 13.6 is well below its two-year median of 21.8. The stock is down 30.6% this year.

**What would change our mind:** growth. Revenue rose 13.1% a year over five fiscal years, in the bottom quarter of its peers, and Citi's Tyler Radke cut the stock to sell on 4 November, expecting a weaker fiscal 2027 forecast with its fourth-quarter results in December, TIKR and Börse Global reported. A forecast below 10% growth would say the AI image tools are taking customers, not adding them.

### 2. Autodesk ($ADSK)
**Gross margin: 91.2% in the 12 months to July 2026.** Autodesk sells design software, such as AutoCAD and Revit, to architects, engineers, builders and manufacturers.

1. It is speeding up: revenue grew **15.8% a year** over the last two years, faster than its 13.7% pace over five fiscal years.
2. More of it is turning into cash: free cash flow was 36.4% of revenue in the latest 12 months, up from 32.8% over two years and in the top quarter of the 32 software companies we cover.
3. It earns well on little capital: return on capital of about 58% is in the top tenth of its peers, and it holds $604 million more cash than debt.

**The price:** 20.3 times expected earnings against a software median of 26.6 times, and its trailing P/E of 34.2 is near the bottom of its two-year range of 26.3 to 67.8. The stock is 14.8% below its 52-week high.

**What would change our mind:** earnings per share. Diluted EPS shrank 0.8% a year over five fiscal years, the weakest in its group, so the cash has not yet reached reported profit. Autodesk reports its third quarter after the close on 24 November; another quarter of cash without EPS growth would weaken the case.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Arm Holdings ($ARM)
**Gross margin: 97.4% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Arm licenses the chip designs inside almost every smartphone and a growing share of data-center processors, and collects a royalty on each chip sold.

1. Little of the margin reaches profit: the operating margin was **16.7%** over the latest 12 months, in the bottom quarter of the 25 chipmakers we cover, against a median of 29.6%.
2. Returns are thin for a design company: return on equity was 10.3%, in the bottom quarter of its peers, against a median of 23.1%.
3. The latest quarter did not lift the outlook: revenue rose 27% to about $1.44 billion in its fiscal second quarter, but the forecast of $1.54 billion for the December quarter only matched what analysts expected, Benzinga and NewsBytes reported, and the stock **fell 13.1%** on 5 November, in our data.

**The price:** 80.7 times expected earnings against a chipmaker median of 22.3 times. Earnings would have to grow about 3.6-fold for the multiple to fall to that median, and the stock is still up 74.3% this year even after falling 56.6% from its June high.

**What could go wrong:** a faster data-center ramp. If royalties from data-center chips accelerate, the operating margin could rise quickly from a low base and make today's multiple look less stretched; its December-quarter report is the next test.
