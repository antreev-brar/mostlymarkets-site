On Sunday afternoon, Nvidia's ($NVDA) board said a committee of independent directors, helped by outside counsel, will investigate reports that Chief Executive Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The board said it has **no reason to believe** national security or company secrets were compromised, and that Huang supports the review and will cooperate, according to its statement and reports by Reuters and NBC News.

The probe follows Saturday's [Financial Times report](/news/2026-11-07/ft-says-a-us-memo-ties-nvidia-nvda-ceo-to-a-suspected-chinese-spy-days-before-a-house-briefing/) on a US government memo linking Huang to Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive US intelligence suspects of working for China's Ministry of State Security. An Nvidia spokesperson said "today's news will not slow" the company and that Huang is committed to leading it "without interruption," NBC News reported. NBC said it had not confirmed the underlying reports.

For investors the question is leadership, at the world's most valuable chipmaker, a week before its biggest report of the quarter. The stock closed Friday at $186.64, 22.0% below its 6 October high.

The board gave no timetable. Huang has until Wednesday 11 November to accept the House chairs' request for a closed-door briefing on 18 November; Nvidia reports third-quarter results on Tuesday 17 November.
