The story investors are telling this week is simple: a leaderless Nvidia ($NVDA) will lose orders, and AMD ($AMD) and Broadcom ($AVGO) will pick them up. William Blair analyst Sebastien Naji put it in writing on Thursday morning, upgrading AMD and cutting Nvidia hours before Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive, CNBC and Benzinga reported. Barron's ran the day under the headline "Nvidia's loss is AMD and Broadcom's gain."

The peg is the resignation itself. On Thursday Nvidia said Huang, who co-founded it in 1993, had quit with immediate effect after a week of reports about his relationships, and that Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress would serve as interim CEO, according to its SEC filing. Since last Friday's close, in our data, AMD has **risen 30.3%** and Broadcom 24.1%, while Nvidia has fallen 11.0%.

## The story in one sentence

> "Nvidia is facing increased scrutiny and a potential leadership vacuum." — William Blair's Sebastien Naji, in a note on 12 November upgrading AMD to outperform, reported by CNBC and Benzinga

The chain runs like this. If Nvidia's customers worry about who will run the company, its roadmap and its dealings with Washington, they hedge by giving more of their next orders to a second supplier. AMD sells rival AI accelerators and Broadcom designs custom chips for the cloud giants, so any orders that move go to them first, and because both are far smaller than Nvidia, each dollar moved counts for much more of their sales.

## The evidence for it

### 1. Six firms cut Nvidia to a hold rating in one day

By Thursday, Bernstein, Evercore ISI, Oppenheimer, William Blair, KeyBanc and Melius had cut Nvidia to the equivalent of a hold, TIKR and mlq.ai reported. Bernstein's Stacy Rasgon cut his target to $200. The notes centered on leadership and governance rather than demand, TIKR reported, and that doubt is the opening the rivals' bulls are counting on.

### 2. The rivals are already growing fast in AI

AMD's data-center sales reached a record **$8.5 billion** in the third quarter, up 97% year on year, helped by the first sales of its MI450 accelerators, the company said on 3 November. Broadcom's AI chip revenue was $16.7 billion in its quarter to early August, up 221%, its release said.

### 3. Small shifts matter a lot to the smaller companies

Nvidia's revenue was **$96.2 billion** in the quarter to July, according to its filings, against AMD's $8.5 billion of data-center sales. So 1% of Nvidia's sales, about $1 billion, would add about 11% to AMD's data-center business. That arithmetic is why AMD rose nearly three times as much as Nvidia fell.

### 4. Washington is now part of Nvidia's story

Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has agreed to testify to the Senate Banking Committee about chip sales to China, and House committees have summoned Huang, CNBC and Euronews reported. Semafor reported separate Justice Department inquiries touching Nvidia, Megaspeed and Supermicro ($SMCI), Yahoo Finance and Benzinga reported. A buyer weighing a multiyear order has a new reason to want a second source.

## The case against it

The strongest doubt comes from Broadcom's own chief executive. Hock Tan said on 2 September that Broadcom had "secured the supply to again double AI revenue to approximately $115 billion" in fiscal 2027, and that "our demand actually exceeds this outlook," according to the call transcript, as Fierce Network and MarketBeat also reported. A company that can't meet the demand it already has can't quickly absorb Nvidia's.

1. **Supply, not willingness, sets the pace.** Broadcom named leading-edge silicon, substrates and high-bandwidth memory as constraints, MarketBeat reported, the same parts AMD needs. Any order that moves this year waits in the same queue. This is the strongest counter-point.
2. **Customers are locked in for now.** Nvidia said customer commitments and its product roadmap continue without interruption, its filing said. Most buyers reach these chips through contracts with cloud providers, and switching costs are real, CIO Dive reported.
3. **Even the bulls had doubts in July.** Naji started AMD at market perform on 9 July, warning that the cloud giants' spending also pays for their own chip programs, which could cap AMD's share of AI accelerators, MarketBeat and Insider Monkey reported. Those in-house chips are Broadcom's business, not AMD's.

## What would change minds

- **Nvidia's third-quarter report, Tuesday 17 November after the close.** Revenue below its own $108 billion guide, or a weaker outlook for the next quarter, would be the first hard evidence that orders moved.
- **Broadcom's fiscal fourth-quarter report in December.** It guided to about $21.7 billion of AI revenue for the quarter, Pulse 2.0 and Crypto Briefing reported; a raise above its $115 billion fiscal 2027 plan would show new demand finding a home.
- **Nvidia's choice of a permanent CEO and the dates for testimony in Washington.** A fast, credible appointment would weaken the hedge-your-bets argument; a drawn-out search would strengthen it.

## Our read

**Partly supported.** The rivals' AI businesses are real and growing fast, and a leaderless Nvidia under scrutiny in Washington gives buyers a reason to hedge. The weakest link is supply: Broadcom says it can't meet the demand it already has, and AMD needs the same scarce parts, so the shift the stocks have priced can't show up in sales for quarters. With AMD at 48.2 times forward earnings against Nvidia's 13.7, the market is paying up front for orders no one has moved; the number we'll watch is Nvidia's outlook for the quarter to January on Tuesday.
