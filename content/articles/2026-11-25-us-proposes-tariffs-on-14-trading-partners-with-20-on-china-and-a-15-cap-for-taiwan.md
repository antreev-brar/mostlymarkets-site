The US Trade Representative's office on Wednesday proposed new tariffs on **14 trading partners**, including China and the European Union, after its probe into excess factory capacity, CNBC and RTE reported. China faces the highest rate, 20% on top of the duties set in Trump's first term, while the EU, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan would be capped at 15%, in line with their trade deals, FXStreet and TheNextWeb reported. Mexico and Norway, the other two economies in the probe, were left out for now.

That is milder than the worst case the market feared at dawn, when [reports of the plan](/news/2026-11-25/us-readies-new-tariffs-on-16-economies-and-nasdaq-futures-fall-2-2/) sent Nasdaq 100 futures down 2.2%. "When it all nets out, it really will just top up a few percentage points," Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said, according to Reuters reports carried by Business Standard and TimesLive. Taiwan, home to most AI chip production, keeps its 15% ceiling.

The chip stocks haven't taken the relief yet: TSMC's ($TSM) US shares were **down 5.3%** before the open at 9:15 a.m. ET, in our data. The rates are a proposal, open to public comment before they take effect.
