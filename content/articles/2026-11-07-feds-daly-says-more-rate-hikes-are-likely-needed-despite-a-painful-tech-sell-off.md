San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly said on Friday afternoon that higher interest rates "are likely to be needed" to bring inflation down, though how much and when is still undecided, CNBC and Bloomberg reported. She called the tech-stock sell-off "painful" but orderly, Reuters reported, and said she would prefer to move gradually.

Her comments came hours after employers added **165,000 jobs** in October against 65,000 expected, a report that lifted futures-market odds of a quarter-point hike at the Fed's 8-9 December meeting to about 80%, Reuters reported ([our jobs story](/news/2026-11-06/employers-added-165-000-jobs-in-october-keeping-a-december-hike-in-play/)). The Fed raised rates in September to a range of 3.75% to 4% and held them there last week, when two officials dissented in favor of another increase, Quartz and Investing.com reported.

It matters because Daly said the tech slide alone won't hold the Fed back, so a hike would land on a Nasdaq already in a correction ([our narrative on why the Fed may hike into a sell-off](/news/2026-11-06/why-wall-street-thinks-the-fed-will-hike-into-a-stock-sell-off/)). The next test is October's consumer price index on Tuesday, 10 November.
