## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Netflix ($NFLX)

**47.6% below its 52-week closing high of $114.09, at Wednesday's close.** Netflix sells streaming subscriptions and, increasingly, advertising.

1. **It compounds per-share earnings.** EPS grew **33.0% a year** over five fiscal years, against 18.7% for the median communication services stock we cover.
2. **The profit is real cash.** Free cash flow was 22.9% of revenue in the 12 months to September 2026, about twice the peer median of 11.8%.
3. **The fall came from forecasts, not the business.** Third-quarter revenue rose 12% and the operating margin widened to 34.6% from 28.2%, the 20 October release showed, but Netflix trimmed its 2026 advertising revenue outlook to about $2.7 billion from $3 billion, EBC and the call showed. The stock fell 5.3% the next day, after a 7.3% drop on 17 July on a forecast that missed Visible Alpha consensus, S&P Global reported.

**The price:** 17.3 times trailing earnings, at the bottom of its two-year range of 17.2 to 63.2 times and well under its median of 44.0. That is near the 16.2-times forward median for its peers, whose operating margin is 18.3% against Netflix's 31.3%.

**What could go wrong:** advertising is the new growth engine, and at about $2.7 billion it is only about 5% of the roughly $51.1 billion of revenue Netflix expects this year; if ads keep slipping, growth falls back toward the 12.6% five-year pace.

### 2. Caterpillar ($CAT)

**47.5% below its 30 June closing high of $1,064.90, at Wednesday's close.** Caterpillar makes construction and mining machines, plus engines and turbines that power data centers.

1. **It compounds earnings faster than almost any industrial.** EPS grew **28.1% a year** over five fiscal years, in the top tenth of the industrials we cover, whose median is 11.3%.
2. **It is getting more profitable.** The operating margin was 18.3% over the past 12 months, up 5.6 points over five years, against 3.4 points for the median industrial.
3. **Demand did not crack.** Third-quarter sales rose 17% and the backlog hit a record $78.1 billion, the 29 October release showed, but Caterpillar kept its 2026 forecast and the stock plunged 9.25%, [as we reported](/news/2026-10-29/caterpillar-cat-beats-q3-profit-estimates-but-shares-plunge-on-its-outlook/). It touched a 52-week low of about $537 this week, Investing.com and Benzinga reported.

**The price:** 21.9 times trailing earnings, against its own two-year median of 27.0 times.

**What could go wrong:** the June high priced in a long data-center boom; power generation was about a sixth of third-quarter sales. If data-center orders slow, Caterpillar reverts to a cyclical machinery maker, with its margin already near the bottom of its target range.

### 3. Lululemon Athletica ($LULU)

**48.7% below its 6 January closing high of $215.88, at Wednesday's close.** Lululemon sells premium athletic clothing, mostly through its own stores and website.

1. **Its long-run record is strong.** Revenue grew **20.3% a year** over five fiscal years and EPS 24.1% a year, well ahead of the sector medians of 12.8% and 13.6%.
2. **It earns a lot on what it invests.** Return on capital of about 46% is twice the sector median and it holds $1.4 billion of net cash.
3. **But the trend has turned.** Second-quarter revenue fell 4% and comparable sales 9%, and on 3 September Lululemon cut its full-year revenue outlook to $10.35 billion to $10.5 billion from $11 billion to $11.15 billion, CNBC and Quartz reported. The stock plunged 17.4% the next day.

**The price:** 9.1 times trailing earnings, against a two-year median of 13.1.

**What could go wrong:** the company guided for revenue to fall 10% to 11% in the third quarter, Quartz reported, and its earnings outlook was cut by about 13% at the midpoint, to $9.48 to $9.73 a share. If the slide spreads, the low multiple is fair for a shrinking brand.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### ON Semiconductor ($ON)

**49.8% below its 3 June closing high of $133.93, at Wednesday's close.** ON makes power chips and image sensors, mostly for cars and factory equipment.

1. **Its long run is weak.** Revenue grew **2.7% a year** over five fiscal years, against 14.2% for the median of the 25 chipmakers we cover, and EPS shrank 12.3% a year while the median grew 17.2%.
2. **Its margins are among the thinnest.** It keeps 37.2% of each sale after production costs over two years, the lowest gross margin in its group, and its operating margin of 12.3% is down 5.2 points over five years.
3. **The recovery is real but young.** Third-quarter revenue rose 11% from a year earlier, and in early November ON guided fourth-quarter revenue to $1.715 billion to $1.815 billion, MarketScreener and MarketBeat reported. The stock still plunged 11.6% in the 5 November chip sell-off, Stock Titan reported.

In its favor, free cash flow was 25.4% of revenue over 12 months.

**The price:** 18.4 times expected earnings, below the 21.6-times chipmaker median, a modest discount for a business growing well behind them.

**What would change our mind:** a gross margin that keeps climbing toward the peer median. It reached 41.4% in the third quarter, from 37.9% a year earlier; the next report, early in 2027, tests it.
