On Sunday, Nvidia ($NVDA) Chief Executive Jensen Huang told employees in a letter that he had an affair with Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive whom US intelligence reportedly suspects of working for China, CNBC and Data Center Dynamics reported. "I owe my family and all of you an apology," he wrote, according to CNBC.

The letter came the same day Nvidia's board said independent directors and outside counsel would review whether Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, as we [reported on Sunday afternoon](/news/2026-11-08/nvidia-nvda-board-opens-an-independent-probe-10-days-before-its-ceos-house-briefing/). The board's statement did not mention the affair; it said it had no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised, and Nvidia said Huang will keep leading the company "without interruption."

The admission turns a reported allegation into a confirmed relationship at the world's most valuable chipmaker. Singapore-based Megaspeed has bought more than $2 billion of Nvidia hardware and denies involvement in chip smuggling, according to CNBC and Tom's Hardware.

The stock closed Friday at $186.64, so Monday's open is the first full-session test. Huang has until Wednesday 11 November to accept House chairs' request for an 18 November closed-door briefing, and Nvidia reports third-quarter results on Tuesday 17 November.
