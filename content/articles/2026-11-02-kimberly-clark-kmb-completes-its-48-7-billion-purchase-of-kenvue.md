Kimberly-Clark ($KMB) completed its purchase of Kenvue ($KVUE), the maker of Tylenol, Listerine and Neutrogena, on Monday, 2 November, about a year after agreeing to it, the company said in a release filed with the SEC. Each Kenvue share became **$3.50 in cash plus 0.14625 of a Kimberly-Clark share**, and Kenvue stopped trading on the New York Stock Exchange at Monday's open. The deal valued Kenvue at about $48.7 billion including debt when it was struck, NJBiz and Nasdaq reported.

At Monday's close of $108.25, that package was worth about $19.33 a Kenvue share, by our calculation. Former Kenvue holders now own about 46% of the combined company, its release said.

The stake now is execution. Kimberly-Clark reaffirmed about **$2.1 billion** of yearly synergies, most of it cost savings in the first three years, and said it expects about $3.1 billion of pre-tax charges to get there, $2.5 billion of it in cash, according to the release and GuruFocus. Okta will take Kenvue's place in the S&P 500 before the open on Wednesday, S&P Dow Jones Indices said.

Kimberly-Clark's shares **edged up 0.3%** on Monday. It reports third-quarter results on Thursday, 5 November, its first since the deal closed.
