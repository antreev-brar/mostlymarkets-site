The quarters were mostly fine. The forecasts weren't. This week, one big company after another told investors the next few months would be slower than hoped, and their stocks paid for it. The week ended on Friday with Intel ($INTC) losing 28.6%, its worst day since 1974.

The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.86% on Friday and about 5.3% in October, its worst month since March 2025, Yahoo Finance and Investing.com reported. Even gold and bitcoin fell. Here's the week in eight charts, and what's coming next.

## The week in eight charts

### Intel had its worst day since 1974

Intel plunged 28.6% to $65.76 on Friday, Benzinga and CNN reported. On Thursday evening it beat profit estimates and said data-center sales grew 78%, but it forecast fourth-quarter revenue of $15 billion to $16 billion, short of the $17.07 billion analysts expected ([full story](/news/2026-10-29/intel-intc-q3-profit-tops-estimates-shares-plunge-on-a-weak-forecast/)).

Intel blamed a shortage of memory chips that is choking demand for PCs. That warning spread to the companies that build PCs: Dell ($DELL) fell 10.8% on Friday ([full story](/news/2026-10-30/why-dell-technologies-dell-stock-is-sinking-today/)).

### Even after Friday, Intel is up 78% this year

Friday's drop took back less than half of Intel's 2026 gain. The chip rally has been so big that the week's worst losers are still far ahead for the year.

### Alphabet had its worst day since its 2004 listing

Alphabet ($GOOGL) plunged 12.6% on Thursday. Its cloud sales doubled, but search revenue missed estimates, and free cash flow came in at negative $9.07 billion as its spending on property and equipment more than doubled to $51.36 billion ([full story](/news/2026-10-29/alphabet-googl-shares-plunge-after-search-falls-short-and-cash-burn-grows/)).
