## What happened
Shares of UnitedHealth ($UNH), the biggest US health insurer, **rose 4.8%** to $346.42 as of 10:15 a.m. ET on Friday after TD Cowen upgraded the stock to Buy from Hold and named it the firm's best idea for 2027, TipRanks and Investing.com reported. TD Cowen raised its price target to $455 from $402, and upgraded Humana ($HUM) to Buy at the same time.

The upgrade comes straight after the sell-off. UnitedHealth fell from $412.02 on Monday to **$330.48** at Thursday's close, a 19.8% drop in three sessions by our data, after the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services proposed a rule that would let auditors extrapolate Medicare Advantage billing errors back to 2018 ([our story](/news/2026-11-18/unitedhealth-humana-and-centene-shares-slide-what-happened/)). TD Cowen, whose managed-care analyst is Ryan Langston, said the drop went too far, TipRanks and Seeking Alpha reported.

## Why it matters
TD Cowen's case is about earnings, not the rule. It sees a path to growth in earnings per share in the mid-to-high teens a year through 2028, from Medicare Advantage benefit cuts already made, a recovery at Optum Health, its doctor-and-clinic arm, and better margins in commercial and Medicaid plans, Investing.com and Stocktwits reported.

The case leaves the audit rule unsized. CMS expects to recover about $9 billion a year across the industry, and the proposal is open for comment until 19 January 2027, so the bill for 2018 onward is still unknown; we tested how much it could matter in [our breakdown on Wednesday](/news/2026-11-18/unitedhealth-unh-lost-14-in-two-days-on-an-audit-rule-is-the-business-broken/). Humana was up 4.5% and Elevance Health ($ELV) 2.7% at the same time, by our quotes, so the bounce reached the whole group.

## What the market is telling us
UnitedHealth's shares moved 5% or more on 7 of the last 252 sessions, and Thursday's 5.6% fall followed a 10.2% drop on Wednesday. The stock was up just 0.1% this year at Thursday's close of $330.48, 24.3% below its 52-week closing high of $436.35 from 21 July, and $1,000 invested five years ago would be worth $751. We think one upgrade has won back about a fifth of the three-day loss; the rest waits on the first insurer to put a dollar figure on its audit exposure.
