Chip stocks bounced on Tuesday, Election Day, after a month-long rout, and that was enough to end the S&P 500's three-day losing streak. The Nasdaq Composite **rose 0.83%** to 25,372.01 and the S&P 500 0.36% to 7,568.92, while the Dow fell 0.38% to 51,786.72 as Apple ($AAPL) weighed on it, CNBC and Anadolu Agency reported.

The calm didn't last into the night. As early results showed Democrats winning Senate races, stock futures and the dollar slid, Reuters and NBC News reported, and the 10-year Treasury yield fell back to 4.91% by 10:30 p.m. ET in our data, from 4.97% at the close.

## The chipmakers got a bounce, not a turn

Credo Technology ($CRDO) **rose 7.8%**, Monolithic Power ($MPWR) 6.8%, Broadcom ($AVGO) 3.1%, Micron ($MU) 2.6% and Nvidia ($NVDA) 2.3% in the regular session, a day after the Marvell-Lumentum deal helped knock the Nasdaq down 1.07%. Yahoo Finance and InvestingLive described it as bargain hunting after the slide; Yahoo Finance put the value chip and memory stocks have lost since 6 October at about $3.7 trillion. Our [story on the morning's AI-hardware jump](/news/2026-11-03/astera-labs-monolithic-power-and-credo-shares-jump-what-happened/) has the detail.

AMD ($AMD) rose 3.7% to $483.90 into its results, then reported record third-quarter revenue of about $13.4 billion, up 45% from a year earlier, and a fourth-quarter forecast above estimates, CNBC and KuCoin reported. The shares were **up 5.0%** at $508.20 by 7:59 p.m. ET in our data. Even after Tuesday, AMD is 25.5% below its 6 October closing high.

## Apple's margin warning stuck; Palantir's drop didn't

Apple **fell 1.9%** to $331.90, a smaller drop than the 4.1% it showed before the open, after forecasting a lower gross margin for the holiday quarter as memory chips get dearer ([our analysis of the memory bill](/news/2026-11-03/memorys-boom-has-become-the-device-makers-bill-what-the-evidence-says/)). Palantir ($PLTR) recovered more: down 7.6% before the open on slow new contracts ([our story](/news/2026-11-03/palantir-pltr-shares-slide-as-its-new-contracts-grow-just-6/)), it closed down just 1.7% at $175.40.

## Earnings set the day's biggest moves

Gartner ($IT) **sank 13.2%**, the S&P 500's worst performer in our data, after its contract value grew just 2.5% ([our story](/news/2026-11-03/gartner-it-shares-sink-after-its-contract-value-grows-just-2-5/)). Sysco ($SYY) fell 9.3% on a sales miss, Zebra Technologies ($ZBRA) 8.8% on a soft forecast, and Shopify ($SHOP) 8.7%, less than its 15% drop before the open ([our story](/news/2026-11-03/shopify-shop-q3-gmv-and-subscriptions-miss-and-the-stock-sinks-15/)).

On the other side, Fiserv ($FISV) **jumped 12.5%** after doubling its savings target ([our story](/news/2026-11-03/fiserv-fisv-shares-jump-as-it-doubles-its-savings-target-despite-a-6-45-billion-charge/)), Bloom Energy ($BE) rose 9.1% on a 1-gigawatt fuel-cell pact ([our story](/news/2026-11-03/bloom-energy-be-shares-soar-on-a-1-gigawatt-pact-with-california-resources/)), and DuPont ($DD) 8.6% after raising its forecast ([our roundup](/news/2026-11-03/ametek-ame-and-dupont-dd-jump-keurig-dr-pepper-kdp-slides-tuesdays-earnings/)).

## After the bell, the AI hardware names split

Arista Networks ($ANET) was **up 9.5%** and Supermicro ($SMCI) 7.2% by 8 p.m. ET in our data after their results, while Skyworks Solutions ($SWKS) was down 17.4% and Astera Labs ($ALAB) 3.6%. Our recaps follow tonight. On Wednesday, Qualcomm ($QCOM), Arm Holdings ($ARM), McDonald's ($MCD) and Toyota ($TM) report, and the final count of the midterm vote may take days in close Senate races.
