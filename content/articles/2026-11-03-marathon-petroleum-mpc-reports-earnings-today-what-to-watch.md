Marathon Petroleum ($MPC), the US oil refiner, reports third-quarter results before the open on Tuesday, 3 November. The stock is **up 164.6%** this year, as the war with Iran squeezed fuel supplies and widened refining margins.

Last quarter, reported on 4 August, Marathon earned $17.73 a share against the $13.73 analysts expected, its filing and Zacks show, on revenue of $52.0 billion, up 53.8% from a year earlier. The shares rose 1.8% that day.

## What analysts expect

The Zacks consensus, which matches the Nasdaq calendar, is earnings of **$25.74 a share**, up from $3.01 a year earlier. Revenue forecasts are scattered, from $33.6 billion at Zacks to $46.6 billion at MarketBeat, so the profit line is the one to judge. Marathon guided to about $290 million of turnaround costs, the planned maintenance shutdowns that cut a refinery's output, 24/7 Wall St. noted.

## What to watch

- **A refinery sale.** Carl Icahn's CVR Energy is near a deal worth more than $4 billion to buy refineries from Marathon, The Wall Street Journal reported late on Monday, and the companies could announce it on Tuesday morning; Reuters said it could not verify the report, and OilPrice.com and Yahoo Finance carried it. What it sells, and what it does with the cash, matters more than one quarter's margin.
- **The margin outlook.** US crude fell 7.9% on Monday on Iran diplomacy ([our market wrap](/news/2026-11-02/stock-market-today-oil-sinks-8-but-a-tech-reversal-drags-the-nasdaq-down-1-1/)); any comment on how long wide fuel margins last sets the next leg of the stock.
- **Buybacks.** Marathon's diluted share count fell 17.1% over two years, the most among 21 energy peers in our data; the pace of buybacks shows how much of the windfall comes back to holders.

## How the stock has done

At $430.39 on Monday, Marathon sat 11.2% below its 52-week closing high of $484.92 from 14 October, and it fell 7.4% on 27 October, its last big drop. It trades at 6.0 times forward earnings, against a 12.2 times median for its energy peers, which says the market expects these margins to fade. As of 5:52 a.m. ET the shares were up 2.4% before the open ([the story Wall Street is telling about $100 oil](/news/2026-10-31/the-story-wall-street-is-telling-about-100-oil-and-the-fed/)).
