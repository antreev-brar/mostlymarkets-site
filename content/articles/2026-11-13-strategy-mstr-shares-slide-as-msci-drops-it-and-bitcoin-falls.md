## What happened
Shares of Strategy ($MSTR), the software company turned bitcoin holder, **slid 6.5%** on Thursday after index provider MSCI confirmed late on Wednesday that it will drop the stock from its indexes, including the MSCI USA index, at the end of November, Cointelegraph and Yahoo Finance reported. Bitcoin fell below $72,000 on the same morning, Benzinga and KuCoin News reported, which hit a stock that trades as a leveraged bet on the coin.

MSCI judged that Strategy now looks more like an investment fund than an operating business, CoinDesk and Yahoo Finance reported, and funds are left out of its equity indexes. The shares closed at $114.41, down 6.5% from the previous close, on 1.1 times their usual volume in our data.

## Why it matters
Index funds that track MSCI benchmarks will have to sell their shares by the end of the month, so the stock loses a group of buyers that never looked at its price. CoinDesk argued the larger cost is to Strategy's model: it buys bitcoin by selling new shares and preferred stock, and that works best when the shares trade above the value of the coins behind them.

The selling also landed in a weak week for bitcoin. As of 7:16 a.m. ET on Friday, bitcoin was at **$69,427**, down 2.8% on the day in our data, and Strategy was down 1.0% before the open at $113.26.

## What the market is telling us
Strategy's shares moved 5% or more on 71 of the last 252 sessions. The last move at least this big in the same direction was on 5 November 2026, when the stock fell 9.3%. The stock is down 24.7% this year, and at $114.41 it sits 49.1% below its 52-week closing high of $224.61 from 12 November 2025. $1,000 invested in Strategy five years ago would be worth $1,409 today.

The exit date was known on Wednesday, so Thursday's drop says as much about bitcoin as about MSCI. The next test is the rebalancing at the end of November, when the forced selling actually happens, and whether other index providers follow.
