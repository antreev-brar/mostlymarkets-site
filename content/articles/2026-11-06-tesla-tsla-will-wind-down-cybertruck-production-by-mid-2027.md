Tesla ($TSLA) will wind down Cybertruck production at its Texas factory by mid-2027 to free up room for the Cybercab, its planned two-seat robotaxi, Chief Executive Elon Musk said in a post on X on Friday, Reuters and Bloomberg reported. It was not clear whether the truck will be built elsewhere; the Austin plant is the only Tesla factory known to make its specialized parts, Reuters reported.

Musk put a number on the failure. Tesla installed tooling to build **125,000 Cybertrucks a year** but sold 70,000 over three years, he said, and the project lost a net $1.9 billion after about $2 billion of research and development, according to Reuters and NBC News. US sales fell 48% in 2025 to 20,237, Kelley Blue Book data cited by Reuters show.

The truck is a small part of Tesla's sales, so the cost is already sunk; what matters is the factory space it hands to the Cybercab, the product that carries much of the stock's valuation at 188 times forward earnings in our quote data. Tesla shares were up 4.9% at $360.67 as of 10:15 a.m. ET, after falling 10.8% on Thursday ([our story](/news/2026-11-05/why-tesla-tsla-stock-is-sinking-today/)). The next test is when Cybercab volume production starts, which Tesla has not dated in these reports.
