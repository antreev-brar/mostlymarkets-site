Fiserv ($FISV), the payments and bank-software company behind the Clover card terminals, said before the open on Tuesday that third-quarter revenue **fell 1% to $5.20 billion** and that it took a $6.45 billion non-cash charge to write down past acquisitions. Adjusted earnings per share of $1.75 were in line with to slightly above estimates, Reuters and Chartmill reported. The shares **jumped 9.0% to $56.59** in pre-market trading as of 9:30 a.m. ET, on a doubled cost-savings target and a new buyback.

## What stood out

The charge is an admission about the past, not a cash cost. Fiserv wrote down **$6.45 billion** of goodwill, the premium it paid for past acquisitions, because of the "sustained decreases" in its share price and market value, the release said. It turned the quarter into a GAAP loss but leaves adjusted EPS and cash untouched.

*Verdict: an accounting catch-up to a stock that has lost about half its value over five years.*

The business itself is still shrinking at the margin. The adjusted operating margin **fell to 31.2%** from 37.0% a year earlier, the release said. Fiserv said it expects Clover revenue to return to double-digit growth in the fourth quarter.

*Verdict: no turn yet; the fourth quarter is the test.*

## The outlook

Fiserv kept its 2026 forecasts of flat to 1% lower organic revenue and adjusted EPS of $7.20 to $7.40, Seeking Alpha and Chartmill reported. What changed is the cost plan: Project Elevate, its savings program, now targets $1 billion instead of $500 million, and Fiserv plans $500 million of share buybacks in the fourth quarter.

## Our take

We were impressed by the self-help: doubling a savings target and buying back stock with the shares at 7.1 times forward earnings, against a peer median of 17.6, by our data. On the other hand, revenue isn't growing and margins are six points below a year ago, so the rally rests on costs and Clover, not demand. Overall, the market is paying for a floor, not a recovery.

Some investors were already making that case: Michael Burry, the hedge fund manager, argued over the weekend that buyout math tied to the Global Payments deal puts Fiserv's value between $100 and $120 a share, Benzinga and Yahoo Finance reported. Even after Tuesday's pre-market jump, the stock is down about 16% this year. Clover's fourth-quarter growth, due in February, is the number that decides whether the floor holds.
