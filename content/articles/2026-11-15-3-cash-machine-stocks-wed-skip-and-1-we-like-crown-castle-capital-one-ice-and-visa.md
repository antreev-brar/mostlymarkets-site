Cash flow matters more when cash pays 5%. The 10-year Treasury yield closed at **5.14%** on Friday, 13 November, in our data, and futures traders expect another Fed hike on 9 December. A company that throws off cash can fund itself without borrowing at those rates.

Our screen: free cash flow (cash from operations minus capital spending) as a share of revenue over the latest 12 months, from SEC filings, among the stocks in our coverage that we haven't listed in the last 14 days. The 12 with positive margins ran from Visa's 48.1% to SBA Communications' 36.9%, with a median of about 42.7%. We then judged each on growth, returns, debt and price against its own industry.

```picks
skip: CCI, COF, ICE
like: V
```

## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. Crown Castle ($CCI)

**Free cash flow margin: 42.2% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Crown Castle rents space on its US cell towers to the wireless carriers.

1. Weak: revenue shrank **6.1% a year** over five years, the worst record of the 30 real estate stocks we cover, and the latest quarter was down 7.2% on a year earlier.
2. Weak: earnings per share fell 15.5% a year over five years, in the bottom quarter of the group, while net debt stands at 9.2 years of operating profit.
3. The stock has already moved on hope: it is up 13.5% in a month, after soaring 9.1% on 23 October when Bloomberg reported that SpaceX was in talks for access to at least 80,000 US cell sites ([our story](/news/2026-10-23/att-t-shares-slide-after-report-spacex-is-seeking-80000-cell-sites/)). Nothing has been signed.

**The price:** 22.4 times free cash flow, 3.9% below its 52-week high.

**What would change our mind:** a signed SpaceX lease that turns revenue growth positive in the 2027 guidance it gives with fourth-quarter results.

### 2. Capital One ($COF)

**Free cash flow margin: 41.7% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Capital One is the biggest US credit-card lender, and it now owns Discover and its payments network.

1. For a lender, cash flow says little; return on equity says more. Capital One's was **9.9%**, in the bottom quarter of the 46 financial firms we compare it with, against a median of 18.0%.
2. Weak: earnings per share fell 4.9% a year over five years, near the bottom of the group, even as revenue grew 28% a year over the last two with Discover added.
3. Policy risk is live. The stock fell about 7% on 12 January when President Trump called for a one-year 10% cap on card interest rates, Axios and The Motley Fool reported; card lending drives about 70% of revenue, Morningstar estimated. The shares are 24.4% below their 6 January high.

**The price:** 1.1 times book value and 8.9 times expected earnings, about half its group's multiple, which is what a 9.9% return earns.

**What would change our mind:** the **$2.5 billion** of Discover savings management promised by mid-2027 showing up as a return on equity above the group's 18% median.

### 3. Intercontinental Exchange ($ICE)

**Free cash flow margin: 37.6% in the 12 months to September 2026.** ICE runs the New York Stock Exchange, energy futures markets and a mortgage-data business.

1. Behind most peers: earnings per share grew **8.9% a year** over five years against a 15.7% median for exchanges, brokers and payment firms, and its operating margin rose 2.2 points in that time, less than most.
2. Behind most peers: return on capital was about 8.2%, below the 10.6% median for its group.
3. The quarter was good, which is the problem for the price: exchange revenue rose 22% in the third quarter ([our recap](/news/2026-10-30/intercontinental-exchange-ice-tops-q3-estimates-as-exchange-revenue-grows-22/)), and the stock is up 12.7% in a month to 1.2% below its high.

**The price:** 21.5 times expected earnings against a 17.7-times median for its group, for slower per-share growth than the median.

**What would change our mind:** real volume at OKXICE, the NYSE affiliate that plans to open 24-hour trading in tokenized stocks on 23 November, Reuters and Dow Jones reported, or two more quarters of trading growth above 20%.

## 1 stock we like

### Visa ($V)

**Free cash flow margin: 48.1% in the 12 months to September 2026, the highest in our screen.** Visa runs the card network that moves money between shoppers' banks and merchants' banks, and takes a small fee on each payment.

1. Strong: it keeps **61.2%** of revenue as operating profit, in the top tenth of 46 financial firms we cover, and earns about 49% on the capital tied up in it, against a 10.6% median.
2. Better than most: revenue grew 13.7% a year over five years against a 9.4% median, and 13.0% a year over the last two. The fiscal fourth quarter kept the pace, with net revenue up 13% and cross-border volume up 14% ([our recap](/news/2026-10-27/visa-v-beats-q4-estimates-but-its-2027-outlook-points-to-slower-growth/)).
3. Little capital is needed to grow, so cash goes to owners: Visa raised its quarterly dividend 16% to 78 cents in October.

**The price:** 29.4 times expected earnings, well above its group's 17.7 times but below Mastercard's 31.1 times, for the higher margin of the two.

**What could go wrong:** Visa guided fiscal 2027 earnings growth to the low double digits to low teens, slower than the fourth quarter's 17%. At 35.8 times free cash flow, the stock yields about **2.8%** in cash, roughly half the 10-year Treasury's 5.14%, so a growth miss would cost it more than a cheaper stock. Its operating margin is also 4.3 points lower than five years ago.
