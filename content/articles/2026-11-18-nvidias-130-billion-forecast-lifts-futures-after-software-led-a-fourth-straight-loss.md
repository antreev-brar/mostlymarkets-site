Wall Street spent Tuesday selling the companies it thinks AI will replace. Salesforce ($CRM) **sank 9.5%** and dragged software down with it two days before Anthropic starts trading, and the S&P 500 fell 1.0% for its fourth straight loss, its close now 6.4% below its 52-week high ([our wrap](/news/2026-11-17/stock-market-today-salesforce-leads-a-software-rout-as-the-dow-drops-849-points/)).

Then, after the bell, the company that sells AI its chips said demand is still growing faster than anyone forecast. Nvidia's ($NVDA) fourth-quarter guide of $130 billion lifted Nasdaq futures overnight and pulled the cloud and chip stocks up with it, Reuters and the WSJ reported. Tonight Anthropic sets its price, and this afternoon the Fed's minutes show how close officials are to a December hike.

## Wednesday in seven takes

### Nvidia's guide came in $5 billion above forecasts

Nvidia forecast fourth-quarter revenue of **$130 billion**, plus or minus 3%, against an LSEG consensus of $125.25 billion, after third-quarter sales rose 103% to $115.8 billion, its release and Reuters said ([our recap](/news/2026-11-17/nvidia-nvda-tops-q3-estimates-and-guides-to-130-billion-in-its-first-report-without-huang/)). The stock was up 3.1% at $160.25 at 6:15 a.m. ET in our data, still about a third below its October high close of $239.24. Chair Harvey Jones said the board will not bring back Jensen Huang, so the leadership question now runs through the CEO search, not the numbers.

Microsoft ($MSFT) **fell 2.3%**, a quarter of Salesforce's drop, but at its size that took more off the index than any other stock, by our data. Nearly nine in ten S&P 500 members fell, the AP reported.

### Salesforce had its worst day since May 2024 without any news

Salesforce's drop was its **biggest since 30 May 2024** by our price data, and Intuit ($INTU) lost 7.2% and ServiceNow ($NOW) 6.5%, with none of them reporting news ([our story](/news/2026-11-17/salesforce-mongodb-and-sap-shares-slide-what-happened/)). Seeking Alpha and Kiplinger tied the selling to Anthropic's listing: the fear is that AI agents do the work that per-seat software licenses charge for, and fund managers need cash for a $64 billion sale. Salesforce was flat at 6:15 a.m. ET, so the Nvidia bounce has not reached software.

Arm ($ARM) **rose 4.8%**, Astera Labs ($ALAB) 4.6% and Coherent ($COHR) 3.6% on a day the S&P 500 lost 1.0%, with no single news cause we could find. Before the open they added to it: Coherent was up 5.2% and Astera Labs 4.2% at 6:15 a.m. ET in our data. The market is drawing a line between AI's suppliers, which Nvidia's guide supports, and the software it may displace.

These are the borrowers at the center of the worry that AI's money loop breaks first at the neoclouds ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-16/wall-street-is-betting-ais-1-trillion-money-loop-breaks-at-the-neoclouds-nvidias-report-will-test-it/)), so a bigger Nvidia guide reads to the market as more customers paying their bills.

Lowe's ($LOW) said before the open that comparable sales **rose 1.8%** and that it now expects fiscal-year sales of about $92.5 billion, up from $92.0 billion, its release and Reuters said. Adjusted EPS of $3.10 topped the $2.88 LSEG consensus. The stock fell anyway, as Home Depot's did on Tuesday after its own beat ([our recap](/news/2026-11-17/home-depot-hd-tops-q3-estimates-and-names-cfo-richard-mcphail-ceo/)), which says a modest beat is already in the price of the home-improvement chains.
