Target ($TGT) said before the open on Wednesday that third-quarter sales **rose 7.1%** to $27.07 billion, ahead of the $26.56 billion analysts expected in an LSEG survey cited by CNBC and Yahoo Finance. Adjusted earnings of $2.65 a share topped the $2.07 consensus, but $0.50 of that came from refunds of tariffs charged under the IEEPA emergency-powers law, the company said. The stock slipped 1.3% to $157.41 as of 7:15 a.m. ET, a pre-market dip Investing.com also reported.

## What stood out

The driver was **more visits**: comparable traffic rose 5.0%, the release showed, so most of the 5.6% comparable growth came from more shoppers rather than bigger baskets. That is the opposite of Home Depot ($HD), which grew on bigger tickets with fewer visits on Tuesday ([our recap](/news/2026-11-17/home-depot-hd-tops-q3-estimates-and-names-cfo-richard-mcphail-ceo/)). The 7.1% sales gain was Target's strongest in more than five years, the Associated Press and Quartz reported, led by a nearly 27% jump in its "Fun 101" toys, games and electronics unit and more than 9% in beauty.

Without the $301 million of refunds, the gross margin would have widened about **1.2 percentage points** from 28.2%, the company said, helped by last year's heavy markdowns and growth in advertising, membership and credit-card income, which rose nearly 20%. Chief Executive Michael Fiddelke said the company's "investments are building on one another—creating momentum in our business." Higher incentive pay and store hours lifted the adjusted cost rate to 21.9% of sales from 21.3%.

## The outlook

Target now expects full-year sales growth of around 6%, one point more than before, and EPS of **$10.65 to $11.40**, up from $9.90 to $10.90, CNBC and Quartz reported. The new range carries $2.14 of refunds against $1.65 in August, so on our arithmetic the underlying midpoint rose about $0.14, to roughly $8.89. November sales so far are in line with the plan and faster than October, the release said, and the forecast assumes no further refunds.

## Our take

This is a real beat underneath the refunds: about $2.15 of underlying EPS against $2.07 expected, on traffic, not price. The pre-market dip says the bar was higher after a 63.2% run this year; options had priced a move of about 9% either way, TipRanks reported. The test is the holiday quarter, and Walmart ($WMT) reports on Thursday ([our preview of the week](/news/2026-11-17/wall-street-is-hiding-in-the-shopper-this-weeks-retailers-will-test-it/)), a day after Lowe's ($LOW) beat on refunds too ([our story](/news/2026-11-18/lowes-low-raises-its-sales-forecast-to-92-5-billion-but-tariff-refunds-made-its-beat/)).
