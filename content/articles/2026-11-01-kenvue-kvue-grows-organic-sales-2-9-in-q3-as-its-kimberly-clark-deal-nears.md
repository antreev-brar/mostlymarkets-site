Kenvue ($KVUE), the maker of Tylenol, Listerine and Neutrogena, reported third-quarter net sales of $3.89 billion on Friday, 30 October, up 3.2% from a year earlier, with organic sales up 2.9%, its release showed. Adjusted earnings of 29 cents a share rose from 28 cents but came in about 3.5% below a consensus near 30 cents, Chartmill calculated; the Nasdaq consensus of 28 cents had it as a beat. The stock **slipped 1.3%** to $19.31 on Friday.

## What stood out

It was the **fourth quarter in a row** of growth in both net and organic sales, the company said, after a 2025 in which sales stalled. Self Care, the over-the-counter medicines business that includes Tylenol, grew fastest, with organic sales up 5.4%, MarketChameleon and the release showed.

Growth came from volume as well as price: shoppers bought 1.6% more, and prices and product mix added 1.3 points. The adjusted operating margin widened 0.4 point to 21.9%.

Kenvue's gross margin over the last two years was 58.2%, against a 39.3% median for the 35 consumer staples companies we cover, and its free cash flow was 15.1% of sales over the last 12 months, against a peer median of 10.3%, by our calculation from its filings.

## The outlook

Kenvue gave no forecast and held no call because of its pending sale to Kimberly-Clark ($KMB), the release said. Under the deal agreed in November 2025, Kenvue holders get $3.50 in cash plus 0.14625 of a Kimberly-Clark share for each share, the companies said at the time, and it was expected to close in the second half of 2026.

At Kimberly-Clark's Friday close of $107.94, that package is worth about **$19.29** a share, by our calculation, two cents below Kenvue's close. A gap that small says the market sees little risk that the deal fails. Kimberly-Clark reports its own results on 5 November.

## Our take

A steady quarter: growth from both volume and price, wider margins, and Self Care pulling ahead. But with the stock pinned to Kimberly-Clark's price, Kenvue's own results now matter mainly as a gauge of what Kimberly-Clark is buying; the next number to watch is Kimberly-Clark's report on Thursday.
