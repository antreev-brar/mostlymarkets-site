## What happened

Shares of cybersecurity companies Rubrik ($RBRK), Zscaler ($ZS) and CrowdStrike ($CRWD) **jumped 6% to 9%** in the morning session on Thursday, extending Wednesday's rally after Palo Alto Networks ($PANW) beat estimates and raised its forecast after the close. As of 10:30 a.m. ET, Rubrik was up 8.5% at $119.29, Zscaler 6.9%, Cloudflare ($NET) 6.6% and CrowdStrike 6.0% at $251.17.

Palo Alto's fiscal first-quarter revenue rose 39% to $3.44 billion, and it lifted its full-year revenue forecast to $14.52 billion to $14.57 billion, from $14.10 billion to $14.20 billion, its results release shows ([our recap](/news/2026-11-18/palo-alto-networks-panw-beats-q1-estimates-raises-its-forecast-and-buys-applied-compute/)). Reuters, in a report carried by Investing.com, said the raised forecast powered the rally in its peers, and Invezz and Benzinga tied CrowdStrike's gain to the same results.

Palo Alto itself rose less, up 3.6% at 10:30 a.m. ET after a 9.9% jump on Wednesday. Fortinet ($FTNT) was up 3.9%.

## Why it matters

Palo Alto is the biggest company in the industry, so its order book is the closest thing investors have to a reading on the whole group's demand. Its next-generation security business, the cloud and AI subscriptions, reached $9.73 billion of annual recurring revenue, up 66%, and Chief Executive Nikesh Arora tied the demand to "an unprecedented threat environment" in the release.

That cuts against the fear that sank software stocks earlier this week: that AI agents will shrink the number of seats companies pay for ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-18/wall-street-is-betting-ai-will-empty-softwares-seats-3-numbers-will-decide-it/)). Security spending grows with the number of systems to protect, and a string of US tech outages this week has put "digital resilience" spending in focus, CNBC reported.

## What the market is telling us

Rubrik's shares moved 5% or more on 51 of the last 252 sessions, and the last rise this big came on 14 September, when the stock gained 15.6%. It is up 43.8% this year, and at Wednesday's $110.00 close it sat 19.1% below its 52-week closing high of $136.00 from 28 October. CrowdStrike sat 15.0% below its October high and Zscaler 32.5% below its high of a year ago.

We think the group is trading on Palo Alto's numbers, not its own: CrowdStrike reports on 1 December, and its recurring revenue growth is the next test of whether the demand is broad.
