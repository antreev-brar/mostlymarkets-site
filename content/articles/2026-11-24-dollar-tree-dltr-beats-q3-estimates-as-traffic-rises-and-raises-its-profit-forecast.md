Dollar Tree ($DLTR) reported fiscal third-quarter adjusted earnings of **$1.16 a share** before the open on Tuesday, well above the $0.91 analysts expected, according to FactSet data cited by Finimize. Net sales rose 6.9% to about $5.1 billion, ahead of the $5.04 billion LSEG consensus cited by Reuters, and the company raised its full-year profit forecast. The shares **soared 12.3%** to $156.25 before the open, as of 6:40 a.m. ET, above their 52-week closing high of $147.00 from 4 November.

## What stood out

The quality of the comparable-sales number is the story. Same-store sales **rose 4.6%**, and 3.8 points of that came from more shopping trips rather than bigger baskets, on top of 4.2% growth a year earlier, the release showed. Chief Executive Mike Creedon said in the release that traffic growth "accelerated to 3.8%" and took comparable sales "above the high end of our outlook." *Verdict: real demand, not just higher prices.*

The profit beat is partly planned generosity. Dollar Tree had guided third-quarter EPS to $0.80 to $0.95, including about 50 cents of cost from handing tariff refunds back to shoppers through lower prices and other spending, its August release said. Even so, the operating margin fell 1.1 points to 6.1%, so the business earned less per dollar of sales than a year ago.

The format shift keeps going. About 800 stores were converted to or opened in the multi-price format, which sells items above $1.25, bringing the total to about 7,400, according to the release and GuruFocus.

## The outlook

Dollar Tree raised its full-year adjusted EPS forecast to **$8.05 to $8.25**, from $7.70 to $8.05, including a net benefit of about 65 cents from tariff refunds, Reuters and the release said. Net sales guidance of $20.6 billion to $20.7 billion was essentially unchanged. For the fourth quarter it expects comparable sales up 3% to 4% and adjusted EPS of $2.46 to $2.66, including about 25 cents of reinvestment cost.

## Our take

We were impressed by the traffic: more trips is the hardest number for a discounter to fake, and it says shoppers looking for value are choosing Dollar Tree. On the other hand, roughly 65 cents of the raised forecast is tariff refunds that won't recur, and adjusted EPS still fell from a year ago. Over five years revenue shrank 5.3% a year in our data, but that includes selling Family Dollar; the 7.0% growth of the latest reported quarter is the better guide. At 20.2 times expected earnings before today's jump, against a 17.4 times median for consumer staples peers, the stock already priced in a good holiday, so the fourth-quarter comparable-sales number, due early next year, is the next test.
