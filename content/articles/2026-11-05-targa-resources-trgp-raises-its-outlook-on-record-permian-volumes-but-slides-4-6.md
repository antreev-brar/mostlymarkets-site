Targa Resources ($TRGP) reported third-quarter adjusted EBITDA of **$1.50 billion** before the open on Thursday, up 18% from a year earlier on record volumes in the Permian Basin, and raised its 2026 outlook to at least $6.0 billion. But that profit measure fell 6% from the second quarter as marketing and export margins shrank. The stock opened 2.7% higher, then closed **down 4.6% at $271.82** as the S&P 500 fell 4.4%.

## What stood out

**The pipes are full.** Targa gathers and processes natural gas in the Permian Basin of west Texas and New Mexico, then moves and separates the natural gas liquids that come with it. Inlet volumes hit a record **7.54 billion cubic feet a day**, up 14%, and NGL transport and fractionation volumes set records too, the release showed. Those are fee-based businesses, so volume growth is the steady part of Targa's earnings.

*Verdict: the core business grew as fast as the bulls wanted.*

**The trading side shrank.** In its logistics segment, lower marketing margin, from fewer chances to profit from price differences, and lower LPG export margin, on lower volumes and fees, offset the record pipeline margin, the company said. That is why adjusted EBITDA fell 6% from the second quarter even as volumes rose. Adjusted free cash flow fell 81% from a year earlier to $34 million, as growth spending ran at $1.19 billion in the quarter.

## The outlook

Targa now expects 2026 adjusted EBITDA of **at least $6.0 billion**, with net growth capital spending of about $5.0 billion, unchanged. It plans to recommend raising the dividend 20% to $6.00 a share for 2027, from $5.00 now. It gave no 2027 EBITDA guidance.

## Our take

We were impressed by record volumes across the system and a 20% dividend raise backed by EBITDA up 18% from a year earlier. On the other hand, the quarter-on-quarter dip in profit and thin free cash flow after $5 billion of annual growth spending leave little room for a slip. Overall, a solid quarter; no outlet tied Thursday's drop to a specific line in the report, and it came on a day of broad selling.

The price asks a lot: Targa trades at 23.9 times forward earnings, about twice the 11.6 median for energy peers by our data, and has fallen 14.1% from its 30 October high after rising 47.3% this year. The next test is fourth-quarter results early next year and the first 2027 profit outlook.
