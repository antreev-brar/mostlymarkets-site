The Nasdaq fell into a correction on Thursday, 5 November, and in a sell-off we look first at companies that pay their own way, funding growth, buybacks and dividends from cash they generate rather than from investors. But a business that turns a big share of its sales into cash is only worth owning if that cash keeps growing and the price doesn't already assume it.

Our screen ranked the companies in our coverage by free-cash-flow margin, the cash left after running the business and paying for equipment, as a share of revenue, over the 12 months to September 2026, from their SEC filings. All four below keep about 40 cents of every dollar of sales; three of them have a catch.

```picks
skip: GEV, EQT, EXR
like: FTNT
```

## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. GE Vernova ($GEV)

**Free-cash-flow margin: 38.9% in the 12 months to September 2026.** GE Vernova makes gas turbines, grid equipment and wind turbines.

1. **The cash is running ahead of the profit.** Its operating margin was **6.4%** over the same 12 months, in the bottom quarter of the 89 industrial companies in our data, where the median is 17.5%. Third-quarter free cash flow rose $4.7 billion from a year earlier, to $5.4 billion, "mainly" from working capital, the company said in its results release.
2. **The demand is real.** Orders rose 96% organically to $29.3 billion in the third quarter, the backlog reached $197 billion, and the company raised its 2026 free-cash-flow forecast to $16 billion to $17 billion from $11.5 billion to $12.5 billion, the release showed.
3. **The cash margin was much lower before.** Over two years it averaged 24.0%, so the latest year leans on working capital, cash that can flow back out as orders are delivered.

**The price:** 36.8 times expected earnings, against 21.0 times for the median industrial, even after falling 34.1% from its 30 June high.

**What would change our mind:** an operating margin closing on the industrial median as the backlog turns into sales; its next quarterly report is the first check.

### 2. EQT ($EQT)

**Free-cash-flow margin: 40.7% in the 12 months to September 2026.** EQT produces natural gas in Appalachia.

1. **Owners keep getting diluted.** Its diluted share count rose **12.4%** over two years, in the bottom quarter of energy companies, where the median is up 0.3%.
2. **More shares are coming.** In late October EQT agreed to buy Range Resources in an all-stock deal, paying 0.987 of its shares for each Range share, its filing shows; EQT's current owners will hold about 73% of the combined company when it closes in the first quarter of 2027.
3. **The returns don't justify the deals yet.** Its return on capital is about 10.0%, below the energy median of 11.8%, and revenue fell 9.6% from a year earlier in the latest quarter while the median energy company's rose.

**The price:** 18.4 times expected earnings against 11.5 times for its energy peers, though it trades at 11.7 times trailing earnings, below its own two-year median of 20.5 times.

**What would change our mind:** evidence that the $325 million of yearly savings EQT promises from the Range deal within two years lifts cash flow per share, not just in total.

### 3. Extra Space Storage ($EXR)

**Free-cash-flow margin: 44.7% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Extra Space Storage owns and runs self-storage sites.

1. **The fast growth is in the past.** Revenue grew 20.0% a year over five fiscal years, but only **3.7%** a year over the last two, below the 6.1% median for real estate stocks in our data.
2. **The latest quarter leaned on cost cuts.** Same-store revenue rose 2.7% in the third quarter while same-store expenses fell 1.8%, the results release shows, and Finviz noted the raised forecast implies roughly flat same-store revenue in the fourth quarter.
3. **Per-share profit has lagged.** Earnings per share grew 4.3% a year over five years, against a sector median of 7.3%.

**The price:** 34.1 times expected earnings, about its sector's median, after a 19.6% rise in a month that left it 1.2% below its 4 November record close.

**What would change our mind:** same-store revenue growth back above 4% without help from lower expenses.

## 1 stock we like

### Fortinet ($FTNT)

**Free-cash-flow margin: 43.5% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Fortinet sells network firewalls and the security subscriptions that run on them.

1. **It has compounded through the cycle.** Revenue grew **21.3%** a year over five fiscal years, faster than the software median of 15.9%, and third-quarter revenue rose 20% to $2.08 billion ([our story](/news/2026-10-29/fortinet-ftnt-shares-plunge-after-it-cuts-its-service-revenue-forecast/)).
2. **It is far more profitable than most software companies.** Its operating margin was 33.0% over the last 12 months against a median of 9.7%, and its free-cash-flow margin averaged 37.8% over two years against 27.7%.
3. **It returns the cash.** Diluted shares fell 3.8% over two years, and it held $3.3 billion more cash than debt at the end of September.

What changed: the shares plunged 20.1% on 29 October after Fortinet cut its 2026 service revenue forecast to $5.15 billion to $5.17 billion, though it raised its billings and earnings forecasts. They have fallen further since, and at $118.84 on Friday were down 37.2% in a month and 39.0% below their 9 October high.

**The price:** 38.6 times trailing earnings, below its own median of 42.9 times since November 2024; at 32.9 times expected earnings it is still above the software median of 26.7 times.

**What could go wrong:** services, about 63% of sales, grew only about 13% last quarter while product sales rose 37%. If the recurring part keeps slowing, the premium shrinks further; its next quarterly report will show which way services are going.
