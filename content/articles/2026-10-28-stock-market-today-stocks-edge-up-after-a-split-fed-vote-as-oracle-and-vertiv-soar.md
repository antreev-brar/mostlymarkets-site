The Federal Reserve held interest rates on Wednesday, but two of its 12 voters wanted to raise them, and stocks shrugged it off. The S&P 500 edged up 0.28% to 7,743.98, its first gain on a Fed decision day since December 2025. The day's big moves came from companies: Oracle ($ORCL) soared 10.7% on its long-term targets, Vertiv ($VRT) 16.5% on its orders, and F5 ($FFIV) plunged 20.4% on its forecast.

The Nasdaq Composite added 0.27% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average 0.19%. Under the surface the day was split: 247 S&P 500 stocks rose and 256 fell, by our count.

## The Fed held, and two officials wanted a hike

The Fed kept its target range at 3.75% to 4.00% in a 10-2 vote, six weeks after its September hike. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favor of another quarter-point increase. "We just came out of a heck of a family fight," Chair Kevin Warsh told reporters ([our Fed story](/news/2026-10-28/fed-held-rates-but-two-officials-wanted-a-hike/)).

Stocks dipped after the 2 p.m. ET statement and recovered while Warsh spoke. The S&P 500 had fallen on six straight Fed days before Wednesday, Yahoo Finance noted. Traders now see about an 85% chance of a hike at the 8–9 December meeting, according to Bloomberg futures data cited by Yahoo Finance.

The 10-year Treasury yield ended at 5.05%, up 6 basis points, while the 2-year, which tracks Fed expectations, edged down to 4.67%, Treasury data show.

## Oracle and Vertiv led the AI trade

Oracle had its best day since May after it set a fiscal 2030 revenue target of $225 billion or more and capped new outside funding at $20 billion ([full story](/news/2026-10-28/oracle-orcl-shares-soar-after-it-sets-a-225-billion-revenue-target/)). Investors had worried about how much the company is borrowing to build AI data centers.

Vertiv, which makes the power and cooling gear inside data centers, was the S&P 500's best stock. Its organic orders more than doubled, and it raised its 2026 sales forecast by $650 million at the midpoint ([full story](/news/2026-10-28/vertiv-vrt-shares-soar-as-its-orders-more-than-double/)).

## Memory chips rallied on SK hynix

SK hynix, the South Korean memory-chip maker, posted its first quarter with more than 100 trillion won in revenue and said demand will outrun supply through 2030, and Korea's KOSPI index jumped 6.6%. In the US, Sandisk ($SNDK) jumped 7.7% and Micron ($MU) 5.9% ([full story](/news/2026-10-28/sandisk-sndk-and-micron-mu-shares-jump-after-sk-hynixs-record-quarter/)). The PHLX Semiconductor index rose 1.75%, though Nvidia ($NVDA) fell 1.2%.

## F5 had the day's worst fall

F5, whose software and hardware keep big websites running, was the worst stock in the S&P 500. It forecast revenue growth of 8% to 9% for its new fiscal year and lower margins on higher component costs, and Piper Sandler cut the stock to Neutral ([full story](/news/2026-10-28/f5-ffiv-shares-plunge-after-a-soft-forecast-and-a-piper-sandler-downgrade/)). It was the stock's worst day in more than a decade, GeekWire reported.

## Oil climbed back above $100

US crude for December delivery settled at $102.78 a barrel, up 2.9%. Warsh named energy prices when explaining why inflation is still too high, and energy stocks were among the best of the S&P 500's 11 sectors, by our count.

## After the bell

Microsoft ($MSFT) and Alphabet ($GOOGL) reported after the close and went opposite ways. Microsoft's revenue rose 19% to $92.3 billion and its Azure cloud business grew 48%; the stock rose about 4% in early after-hours trading, SiliconANGLE reported. Alphabet's Google Cloud revenue more than doubled, but search revenue fell short and free cash flow was negative for a second straight quarter, and its shares plunged about 10% after hours, Investing.com reported.
