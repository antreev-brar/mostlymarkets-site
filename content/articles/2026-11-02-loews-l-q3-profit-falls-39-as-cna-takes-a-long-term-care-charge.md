Before the open on Monday, Loews ($L) reported third-quarter net income of $306 million, or **$1.50 a share**, down from $2.43 a year earlier, on revenue of $4.76 billion, up 1.9%, the company said in its results release. The drop came almost entirely from CNA Financial, the insurer it controls, which took a $125 million charge to top up its long-term care reserves. We could find no consensus forecast from a named data provider, so we judge the quarter against last year; the shares closed Friday at $111.32, before the release.

## What stood out

The charge is CNA's annual review of what it will pay on long-term care policies, insurance for nursing homes and home care sold decades ago. Loews said the charge "will almost entirely be offset by higher investment yields from recent investment portfolio actions," a benefit that will arrive through investment income over time rather than this quarter.

The weaker sign is in CNA's everyday underwriting. Its property and casualty **combined ratio rose 4.2 points to 97.0%**, meaning it kept only 3 cents of each premium dollar as underwriting profit, down from about 7 cents, on higher catastrophe losses and an underlying loss ratio up 2.2 points. CNA blamed rising claims costs and "lower-than-expected rate in certain lines," insurance-speak for prices that haven't kept up.

The rest of Loews did better. Loews Hotels earned **$16 million**, against a $3 million loss a year earlier, as room rates and occupancy rose, helped by the renovated Loews Miami Beach Hotel. Boardwalk Pipelines' profit slipped to $85 million on two one-off charges; without them, it rose on higher gas transportation revenue.

## The outlook

Loews gives no profit forecast. What it controls is the buyback: it has cut its share count to 202.9 million from 206.0 million at the end of 2025 and kept buying in October, with $4.4 billion of cash and investments at the parent against $1.8 billion of debt.

## Our take

A bad headline quarter from a one-off charge, but the underwriting slippage at CNA is the part to watch, since it recurs. Loews earns a return on equity of 8.8%, in the bottom quarter of 23 insurers we track and about half the 17.4% median, per its filings, and the stock trades at 13.6 times trailing earnings, close to its own two-year median of 13.7. The next test is whether CNA's underlying loss ratio, flat at 64.1% from the second quarter, starts to come down in its next quarterly report.
