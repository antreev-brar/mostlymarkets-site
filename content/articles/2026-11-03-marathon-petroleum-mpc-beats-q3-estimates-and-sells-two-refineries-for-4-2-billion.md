Marathon Petroleum ($MPC) said before the open on Tuesday that it earned **an adjusted $26.93 a share** in the third quarter, up from $3.01 a year earlier and above the $24.47 analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters. Net income rose to $7.4 billion from $1.4 billion as fuel margins widened, and Marathon agreed to sell two Midwest refineries to CVR Energy for $4.2 billion. The shares were down 0.1% at $429.79 in pre-market trading as of 9:29 a.m. ET.

## What stood out

The refining margin, what Marathon earns on each barrel after paying for the crude, **rose to $45.70** from $17.60 a year earlier, the release said. Higher crack spreads, the gap between fuel and crude prices, in every region drove it, the release said; Reuters reported refining margins at multi-year highs. A refiner's costs barely move with that gap, so most of the extra $28 a barrel fell to profit.

*Verdict: a windfall quarter, run at full tilt.*

Marathon ran its refineries at **100% of capacity**, so it caught every barrel of that margin. Net income of $7.4 billion was more than the $4.0 billion it earned in all four quarters of 2025 combined, by its filings.

The sale is the bigger long-run news. Marathon gets $3.45 billion for the St. Paul Park, Minnesota, and Mandan, North Dakota, refineries, which can process 177,000 barrels a day between them, and its pipeline arm MPLX gets $750 million for the pipes, storage and terminals that serve them, both companies said. Chief Executive Maryann Mannen said the sale advances "our strategic priorities of maximizing value."

## The outlook

Marathon expects fourth-quarter crude throughput of 2.83 million barrels a day, below the third quarter's 3.2 million, and refining operating costs of $5.60 a barrel, the release said. It gives no margin forecast; that depends on fuel and crude prices.

## Our take

We were impressed by the execution: full utilization in the best margin quarter in years, and a sale agreed in the year refining margins hit multi-year highs. On the other hand, the quarter's profit came from a margin the market expects to fade: the stock trades at about 6 times forward earnings against a 12.2 median for its energy peers, by our data, and US crude **fell 7.9% on Monday** on Iran diplomacy ([our market wrap](/news/2026-11-02/stock-market-today-oil-sinks-8-but-a-tech-reversal-drags-the-nasdaq-down-1-1/)).

That explains the flat pre-market reaction to a big beat: the stock is up 164.6% this year to Monday, and the question is how long the margin lasts, not how big it was. The next read is the fourth-quarter margin in late January or early February, and what Marathon does with $3.45 billion of sale proceeds, a question [we raised in our preview](/news/2026-11-03/marathon-petroleum-mpc-reports-earnings-today-what-to-watch/).
