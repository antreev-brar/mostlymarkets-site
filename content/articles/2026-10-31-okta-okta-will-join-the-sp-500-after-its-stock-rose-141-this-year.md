After the close on Friday, 30 October, S&P Dow Jones Indices said Okta, whose software controls who signs in to company systems, will join the S&P 500 before the open on Wednesday, 4 November. Worth about **$36.4 billion** at Friday's close, Okta replaces Kenvue ($KVUE), the Tylenol maker Kimberly-Clark ($KMB) is buying, and hands its S&P MidCap 400 seat to Glaukos.

Funds tracking the S&P 500 must own its members and usually buy at the last close before a change. Harvard Business School's Robin Greenwood and Marco Sammon estimated that by 2020 they bought **7% to 8%** of an added company's shares, roughly $2.4 billion to $2.8 billion of Okta's listed stock. But MidCap 400 funds must sell simultaneously, shrinking the net buying.

That offset is one reason the pop has faded: in their study (Journal of Finance, April 2025), moves up from the MidCap 400 **averaged -1.8%** against the market in the 2010s. When Okta entered the MidCap 400 directly in April 2025, with no index fund forced to sell, it jumped 7.4% the next day.

On Friday, Okta slid 6.1% in a [tech selloff](/news/2026-10-30/stock-market-today-intels-worst-day-since-1974-sinks-the-nasdaq-2-9/), then rose 2.1% in extended trading after the news, Investing.com reported.

Our read: Okta's results should matter more than the index trade. Its two biggest gains this year, 30.1% and 28.6%, came the day after its May and August reports.

Index buying should land at the close on Tuesday, 3 November. Okta's next report covers the quarter ending 31 October; it reported the same quarter a year ago on 2 December.
