Eli Lilly ($LLY) said on Saturday that EloraTZP, its experimental pairing of the amylin drug eloralintide with tirzepatide, the medicine in Zepbound, helped adults with obesity and type 2 diabetes lose an average of up to **23.3%** of their weight, about 54.1 pounds, after 48 weeks in a Phase 2 trial. Patients on the highest dose of tirzepatide alone lost 14.8%, CNBC reported, and Lilly's release said blood-sugar readings (A1C) fell by up to 2.9 points.

The result raises the bar for weight loss in people with type 2 diabetes, Fierce Biotech wrote, at a time when Lilly and Novo are both betting on amylin, a hormone that curbs appetite, as the next step after GLP-1 drugs, CNBC reported in October. Lilly, worth more than $1.3 trillion and up 34.3% this year, needs a follow-up to tirzepatide, the molecule [our breakdown](/news/2026-11-08/eli-lilly-lly-is-worth-1-3-trillion-on-one-molecule-how-long-can-it-last/) found its value rests on and the closest rival is Novo Nordisk ($NVO).

The two drugs were given as separate injections in this trial, and Lilly has not yet released the full results, Reuters reported; it is presenting them at the ObesityWeek 2026 meeting.
