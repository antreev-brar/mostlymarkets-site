## What happened
Shares of AI cloud provider Nebius ($NBIS) **slid 13.5%** to $111.33 by 10:30 a.m. ET on Friday, a day after they plunged 16.8% on its third-quarter report. Analysts lowered their price targets overnight, pointing to delays at the outside data centers that Nebius depends on to bring new capacity online, Investing.com and Stocktwits reported.

Baird and DA Davidson both cut their targets citing the delays, Investing.com reported. On Thursday, Nebius said those delays would push part of this year's revenue into 2027 and that 2026 sales would land at the low end of its $3 billion to $3.4 billion range, while it raised planned spending for 2026 to about $27 billion, as we [reported](/news/2026-11-13/nebius-nbis-q3-sales-jump-571-but-it-trims-its-2026-outlook-and-plunges-17/).

## Why it matters
The two days together have taken the stock **down 28%** from Wednesday's close of $154.69. Nebius is spending ahead of revenue that now arrives later, so every quarter of delay stretches the gap between cash going out and cash coming in. It fell with the rest of AI hardware on Friday, but by more: CoreWeave ($CRWV), its bigger rival, was down 11.0%, and AMD 8.7%.

## What the market is telling us
Nebius's shares moved 5% or more on 105 of the last 252 sessions. Unless it recovers above last Friday's close of $129.49, this will be its sixth straight weekly loss, in our data. The stock is still up 53.7% this year at Thursday's close, but at $111.33 it is about 61% below its June high of $286.69. The thing that would change the story is evidence that the delayed Microsoft ($MSFT) capacity in New Jersey is coming on line; until then, the market is pricing the delays, not the 571% revenue growth.
