Nvidia ($NVDA) rose 7.6% to $135.03 before the open on Monday, as of 7:15 a.m. ET, after President Donald Trump wrote overnight that he hopes its board rehires Jensen Huang ([our brief](/news/2026-11-23/trump-urges-nvidia-nvda-to-rehire-jensen-huang-and-the-shares-rise-8-before-the-open/)). That bounce rests on a simple story: Nvidia's problem is who runs it, and bringing back its founder would fix it.

The evidence of the past week points somewhere else. On Friday, 20 November, Reuters reported that the Securities and Exchange Commission's probe of Nvidia is **examining its accounting**, and the stock fell 14.0% that day. The question has moved from the boss to the books, and Nvidia is now priced like a company whose earnings are in doubt.

The short version
- **What's happening:** Nvidia trades at 10.1 times forward earnings, about half the chipmaker median, after an SEC probe widened to its accounting and California said its own probe covers possible criminal violations.
- **Why it matters:** A discount for a missing founder can close with a hire. A discount for doubtful numbers closes only when regulators finish, and that can take years.
- **Our read:** Partly supported. Demand is not the problem yet; trust in the numbers is, and the dates that test it fall in the next two weeks.

## The story in one sentence

> "Did Nvidia know their client?" Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, on CNBC on 13 November, saying that was the real question rather than Huang's personal conduct.

The chain runs like this. If the SEC finds that Nvidia booked or described sales wrongly, the earnings that make it look cheap are less certain. If California or Washington finds it sold chips that reached China against US rules, it faces fines and tighter curbs on a market it already excludes from its forecasts. Either way, rehiring Huang does not end the probes, and Bessent has said the board should pick an outsider.

## The evidence for it

### 1. Demand beat forecasts, and the stock fell anyway

Nvidia reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of **$115.8 billion** on 17 November against the $109.1 billion analysts expected, and guided to $130 billion for the fourth quarter, $4.9 billion above the LSEG consensus, its release and Reuters showed ([our recap](/news/2026-11-17/nvidia-nvda-tops-q3-estimates-and-guides-to-130-billion-in-its-first-report-without-huang/)). The stock lost 22.9% that week. A sell-off that ignores a beat this size is not about this quarter's orders.

### 2. The probes have moved to the numbers

The SEC's inquiry was first reported on 17 November as a look at whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors ([our brief](/news/2026-11-17/sec-probes-nvidia-nvda-disclosures-and-muddy-waters-says-it-is-short/)). By Friday, Reuters reported that enforcement staff were looking at **potential accounting issues**, and the New York Times reported on Saturday that the inquiry is at an early stage. California's attorney general said on Saturday that his probe covers possible criminal violations ([our brief](/news/2026-11-21/californias-nvidia-nvda-probe-covers-possible-criminal-violations-bonta-says/)).

### 3. Nvidia stopped showing where a fifth of its sales were billed

Singapore was **22% of Nvidia's revenue**, about $10.2 billion, in the quarter to July 2025, the last time it was broken out; Nvidia then switched to reporting sales by customer headquarters, Fortune and the Wall Street Journal reported. It had put Singapore-based Megaspeed on an internal block list in June 2025, the Journal said; Megaspeed has been under US investigation over suspected smuggling of Nvidia chips to China ([our brief](/news/2026-11-22/nyt-says-us-intelligence-tracked-megaspeed-executives-contacts-with-chinas-spy-agency-nvda/)). Nvidia has not said why it made the change, and it is not known whether the SEC is looking at it.

### 4. Its new boss is pulling back from the deals bears questioned

Interim Chief Executive Colette Kress said on the 17 November call that Nvidia would be "disciplined and selective" in backing neoclouds, the chip-renting clouds it has invested in and guaranteed, Axios and Morningstar reported. Commitments tied to those agreements fell to **$32 billion** at the end of October from $36 billion three months earlier, Bisnow and Capacity reported. Those arrangements are the circular financing [we examined on 16 November](/news/2026-11-16/wall-street-is-betting-ais-1-trillion-money-loop-breaks-at-the-neoclouds-nvidias-report-will-test-it/).

## The case against it

Three groups read the discount differently, and each has a point.

1. **It's the boss after all.** Trump said Huang's return "would be the best thing for the Company," Investing.com and Forbes reported, and the stock jumped. Pershing Square's Bill Ackman said the board breached its duty by ruling out Huang ([our brief](/news/2026-11-18/bill-ackman-says-nvidias-nvda-board-breached-its-duty-to-shareholders-over-huang/)), and Tesla's Elon Musk said Nvidia will lose "critical" handshake deals without him ([our brief](/news/2026-11-15/musk-says-nvidia-nvda-will-lose-critical-deals-without-jensen-huang/)). Musk named no deal, and the $130 billion guide shows none lost so far.
2. **It's demand, arriving late.** The fourth quarter has an extra week and excludes China, so the guide flatters, and the gross margin guide of 71% to 72% is three points below the third quarter's. Analyst Ming-Chi Kuo said Google cut its 2027 TPU orders by about 20% ([our story](/news/2026-11-20/broadcom-avgo-shares-sink-after-a-report-that-google-cut-its-2027-tpu-orders/)), a sign the biggest buyers are watching what they spend.
3. **An inquiry is not a finding.** SEC inquiries often end without action, and no regulator has accused Nvidia of misstating a number. At 10.1 times forward earnings, the market already charges for a bad outcome.

The third is the strongest. The thesis is that the books are in question, not that they are wrong, and the price may already assume the worse answer.

## What would change minds

- **Around Wednesday, 25 November:** the roughly 10-day window Bessent gave Nvidia's board on 15 November to answer export-compliance questions closes, Fortune reported. A public answer, or a named outside chief executive, would be the first sign the board is ahead of Washington.
- **1 and 2 December:** House committees interview Huang behind closed doors on 1 December and General Counsel Tim Teter on 2 December. Anything that ties the company, not only Huang, to Megaspeed would widen the discount.
- **By about 4 December:** Nvidia's third-quarter 10-Q is due within 40 days of its 25 October quarter end. It is the first filing in which Nvidia has to describe the probes itself, and any change to past numbers would confirm the bear case.

## Our read

**Partly supported.** The demand side is well documented: a $6.7 billion beat and a $130 billion guide say customers have not left. The weakest link is that nobody outside the SEC knows what it is looking at, so a 10 times multiple may price either too much fear or too little. The number we'll watch is the legal note in Nvidia's 10-Q, due by about 4 December, because it is the first filing that has to describe the probes in Nvidia's own words.
