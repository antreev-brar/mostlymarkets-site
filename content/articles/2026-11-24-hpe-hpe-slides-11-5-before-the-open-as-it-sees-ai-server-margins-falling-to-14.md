Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE) **slid 11.5% to $42.50** before the open on Tuesday, as of 6:15 a.m. ET, after Chief Financial Officer Marie Myers said the operating margin of its Cloud & AI segment, home of its servers, will fall to about 14% in fiscal 2027 from 18.4% in the fourth quarter, Seeking Alpha and GuruFocus reported. She tied the drop to a bigger share of lower-margin AI systems and to server price increases that are expected to slow.

That overshadowed a quarter in which revenue rose 50% and HPE guided fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS to $5.05 to $5.35, above estimates ([our recap](/news/2026-11-23/hewlett-packard-enterprise-hpe-beats-q4-estimates-as-server-sales-nearly-double/)). Investing.com called it a sell-the-news reaction after Monday's 17.4% jump, the stock's second-biggest gain of the past year in our data. HPE also said it is steering clear of financing neocloud or other high-risk customers, Stocktwits reported.

Dell ($DELL) reports after the close tonight, and its margin on AI servers will show whether HPE's squeeze is its own or the industry's ([our Dell preview](/news/2026-11-23/what-to-expect-from-dell-technologies-dell-q3-earnings/)).
