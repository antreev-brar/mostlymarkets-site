The AI sell-off turned into a market-wide correction on Friday. The Nasdaq Composite **sank 5.3%** to 22,617.73 and the S&P 500 fell 3.7% to 6,910.39, a seventh straight loss that left it more than 10% below its 6 October record, Reuters and CNBC reported. Nvidia ($NVDA) plunged 14.0%, its worst day since 27 January 2025, as a report that regulators are examining its accounting landed on top of Anthropic's slide toward its IPO price.

## A correction, and a year's gains gone

The S&P 500's seven-day losing streak is its longest since February 2020, Reuters and the Globe and Mail reported, and the Dow, down 1.9% to 48,147.33, also closed in a correction. The Nasdaq turned **negative for 2026**, AP and CNBC said. All three indexes had their biggest weekly percentage losses since April 2025, according to Reuters.

This was the Nasdaq's second drop of 5% or more this month, after the [5.5% fall on 5 November](/news/2026-11-05/stock-market-today-nasdaq-sinks-5-5-into-a-correction-on-midterm-ai-fears/) on fears about data-centre limits after the midterms. The VIX, Wall Street's fear gauge, jumped 26.2% to 35.89 after touching 40.03 during the day, its highest level in at least a year by our data.

## Nvidia's worst day in almost two years

Nvidia **fell 14.0% to $125.53**, by our price data, and is now 47.5% below its 6 October closing high of $239.24. It had been down about 7% at midday, when it slipped [behind Alphabet in market value](/news/2026-11-20/nvidia-nvda-falls-below-alphabet-googl-in-market-value-after-a-34-slide-this-month/). The losses deepened after Reuters reported at about 2 p.m. ET that the SEC's investigation of the company is [examining potential accounting issues](/news/2026-11-20/sec-probe-of-nvidia-nvda-is-examining-its-accounting-reuters-reports/), widening a probe first reported on Tuesday.

The selling hit every part of the AI supply chain, extending the [morning's slide in data-centre suppliers](/news/2026-11-20/micron-arista-and-celestica-shares-slide-what-happened/). Micron ($MU) fell 13.9%, AMD ($AMD) 13.3% and Broadcom ($AVGO) 12.2%, while chip-renting neoclouds Nebius ($NBIS) and CoreWeave ($CRWV) lost 16.1% and 12.0%. Alphabet ($GOOGL), the midday bright spot, gave up its gain to close 2.7% lower.

## Anthropic's debut stopped helping

Anthropic, which closed 17% above its $83 offer price in [Thursday's record debut](/news/2026-11-19/stock-market-today-anthropic-jumps-17-in-its-debut-but-the-dow-drops-1101-points/), traded as low as $83.05 on its second day, a nickel above the IPO price, Fortune and the Motley Fool reported. A listing that bulls had hoped would steady the AI trade instead gave investors a reason to sell the rest of it, as we set out in [our bulls-and-bears piece on the stock](/news/2026-11-20/bulls-vs-bears-on-anthropic-after-its-debut-can-growth-justify-the-price/). "The market is no longer looking for proof of AI demand alone," Saxo strategist Charu Chanana told Reuters.

## Where money went instead

Investors moved into defensive stocks, Reuters reported. General Mills ($GIS) rose 3.7% and hospital operator HCA Healthcare ($HCA) 3.3%, and UnitedHealth ($UNH) added 2.8% after [TD Cowen named it its best idea for 2027](/news/2026-11-20/unitedhealth-unh-shares-jump-after-td-cowen-upgrade/). Global Payments ($GPN) jumped 9.9% on a Bloomberg report that [GTCR is weighing a $50 billion buyout](/news/2026-11-20/global-payments-gpn-jumps-12-as-gtcr-weighs-a-50-billion-buyout/).

Bonds rallied: the 10-year Treasury yield fell **0.12 percentage point to 4.94%**, even with the Fed minutes showing [almost every official expects more hikes](/news/2026-11-18/fed-minutes-show-almost-all-officials-expect-more-rate-hikes-and-flag-ai-as-an-inflation-risk/). There was no shelter in gold or crypto: gold fell 4.4% and bitcoin 6.2%, both to their lowest levels in at least a year by our data.

## What it means

Friday's pattern was the market pricing in a slower AI buildout: the biggest falls came in the companies whose sales depend most on AI spending, while food, health and bonds rose. We think the next real evidence is Micron's report on 17 December, the first big supplier to show whether orders are holding; before then, markets close on Thursday for Thanksgiving.
