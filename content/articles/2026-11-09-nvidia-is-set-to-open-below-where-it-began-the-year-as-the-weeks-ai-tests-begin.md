Over the weekend the story around Nvidia ($NVDA) changed from a reported allegation to a confirmed one. On Sunday its board said independent directors would investigate whether Chief Executive Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, and that night Huang told staff he had had an affair with a Megaspeed executive whom US intelligence reportedly suspects of spying for China ([our brief](/news/2026-11-08/nvidia-nvda-ceo-jensen-huang-admits-an-affair-in-a-letter-to-staff-9-days-before-earnings/)). By 6:15 a.m. ET on Monday the stock was **down 5.1%** before the bell, at $177.20.

The rest of the market is steadier: stock futures were steady to higher, and the companies that buy AI chips rose before the open, Reuters, the Wall Street Journal and Benzinga reported. That leaves this week's tests, CoreWeave ($CRWV) and October's consumer prices on Tuesday and Applied Materials ($AMAT) on Thursday, to land on an AI trade whose biggest name now carries a question about its leader.

## The week ahead in six takes

### Nvidia is set to open below where it began the year

At $177.20, Nvidia would erase this year's gain: it ended 2025 at $186.50 and Friday at $186.64, in our data, already **22.0% below** its 6 October record close. Huang said in his letter that he takes "full responsibility," and President Trump called him "honorable" on Sunday and said they had not discussed the allegations, CNN and Yahoo Finance reported. The board gave no timetable for its review, so the next fixed dates are Wednesday, the House chairs' deadline for Huang to accept their briefing, and the 17 November report.

CoreWeave **fell 4.1%** and Nebius ($NBIS) 3.3% before the bell, both renters of Nvidia-powered computing, while Microsoft ($MSFT), Amazon ($AMZN) and Alphabet ($GOOGL), the biggest buyers of AI chips, each rose more than 1%, in our quotes. AMD ($AMD), Nvidia's nearest rival in AI chips, rose 2.7%. The split says investors are pricing a problem with one supplier, not with AI spending as a whole.

Banks and credit investors are stress-testing AI borrowers' cash flows and asking whether customers can cut their commitments, Reuters reported on Monday, after a sell-off that took **$2.5 trillion** off the Nasdaq 100. ING has put about $500 million of data-center loans up for sale in one package, including a piece of the Stargate site in New Mexico, CNBC and Data Center Dynamics reported, citing Bloomberg. CoreWeave borrows heavily to build, so its backlog on Tuesday, $104 billion at the end of June, is now also a test of whether lenders keep funding it ([our neocloud narrative](/news/2026-11-07/wall-street-is-betting-the-neoclouds-are-ais-weak-link-coreweaves-report-will-test-it/)).
