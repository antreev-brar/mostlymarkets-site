Oil fell hard on Monday on hopes for a diplomatic end to the war with Iran, and for one morning that looked like enough to end last week's selloff. US crude **sank 7.9%** to $95.87 a barrel by 4:05 p.m. ET in our data, and the Dow closed up 0.41% at 51,983.98. But technology shares turned lower after the open: the Nasdaq Composite closed down 1.07% at 25,164.32 and the S&P 500 fell 0.51% to 7,541.40, near its low of the day, CNBC and Yahoo Finance reported.

The swing was the story. At its high the S&P 500 was up about 1.1% and the Nasdaq about 1.4%, by our count from the day's highs; both closed in the red, and the VIX, Wall Street's gauge of expected swings, rose 8.9% to 21.15. Apple ($AAPL) fell 1.8% and Palantir ($PLTR) 3.1% ahead of their results after the close.

## Oil's drop paid the airlines and charged the drillers

Iranian and Qatari officials held a third straight day of talks over the weekend, and Iran signaled room for diplomacy with Washington, OilPrice.com and Finimize reported, though Iran's foreign minister said Tehran was not holding direct talks with the US. Brent crude fell 8.3% to $104.00 in our data.

The trade split cleanly along fuel costs. United Airlines ($UAL) **rose 4.8%**, Delta ($DAL) 4.1%, and Royal Caribbean ($RCL) 4.5%, while Occidental ($OXY) fell 5.1%, ConocoPhillips ($COP) 4.7%, and Exxon Mobil ($XOM) 4.1%. Our [story on the airline and aerospace jump](/news/2026-11-02/united-ual-howmet-hwm-and-ge-aerospace-ge-shares-jump-what-happened/) sets out why jet fuel matters so much. No ceasefire has been agreed, and the midterm elections are on Tuesday.

## The Marvell deal hit the optical-networking stocks

Laser maker Lumentum ($LITE) **plunged 18.6%** to $684.40, its worst day since April 2025, on the day it agreed to be bought by Marvell ($MRVL) in an all-stock deal valued at about $115 billion. Marvell fell 12.7%, which cut the value of its 4.5-share offer, and rivals in AI networking fell with them: Ciena ($CIEN) lost 11.3%, Astera Labs ($ALAB) 11.2% and Coherent ($COHR) 4.9%. Our [story on why the target fell](/news/2026-11-02/lumentum-lite-and-marvell-mrvl-shares-slide-on-the-day-their-deal-is-signed/) explains the arithmetic, and our [bulls-vs-bears piece](/news/2026-11-02/bulls-vs-bears-on-marvell-mrvl-is-lumentum-worth-a-third-of-the-company/) weighs the deal.

The wider chip trade slid too. AMD ($AMD) fell 7.5%, Intel ($INTC) 8.8% and Micron ($MU) 5.8%, and Investing.com reported the Nasdaq 100 fell 1.39% as selling continued across semiconductor and AI shares. We found no single new cause for the chip drop beyond the Marvell deal; it extends October's slide, the Nasdaq's worst month since March 2025.

## The AI build-out's bills came due

Oracle ($ORCL) **fell 8.8%** to $119.22 after filing to raise $20 billion in stock and bonds, five days after telling investors that was its limit through fiscal 2030 ([our story](/news/2026-11-02/oracle-orcl-shares-slide-after-it-files-to-raise-20-billion-in-stock-and-bonds/)). Meta ($META) is also tapping the bond market for at least $20 billion, Bloomberg reported ([our brief](/news/2026-11-02/meta-meta-plans-a-bond-sale-of-at-least-20-billion-bloomberg-reports/)), and cloud provider CoreWeave ($CRWV) fell 8.6%. A day of AI companies raising money is a day investors are reminded how much the build-out costs.

## The winners had their own news

Idexx Laboratories ($IDXX) **soared 21.9%** after it beat estimates and raised its 2026 outlook ([our recap](/news/2026-11-02/idexx-idxx-beats-q3-estimates-and-raises-its-2026-outlook/)), and Rivian ($RIVN) rose 8.5% after a UBS upgrade ([our story](/news/2026-11-02/rivian-rivn-shares-jump-after-ubs-upgrades-the-stock-to-buy-on-its-r2-ramp/)). Tonight Apple, Palantir, Vertex ($VRTX), Williams ($WMB) and Diamondback Energy ($FANG) report; AMD follows on Tuesday.
