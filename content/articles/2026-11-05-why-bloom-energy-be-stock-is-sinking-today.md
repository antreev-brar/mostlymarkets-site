## What happened

Shares of fuel-cell power maker Bloom Energy ($BE) **sank 16.4%** on Thursday, one of the S&P 500's biggest losers, as investors worried that the midterm results will stall the data-center buildout that buys its power systems, MarketWatch and Seeking Alpha reported. The shares closed at $167.52, down from $200.41 on Wednesday.

The selling came on the same day Bloom announced that NTT DATA will take 900 megawatts of on-site power for its data centers, its largest international deal, with first shipments expected in 2027, according to 24/7 Wall St. and GuruFocus. The deal did not stop the fall. Texas's incoming governor, Democrat Gina Hinojosa, has promised a moratorium on new data-center approvals, and Wells Fargo estimates the state holds about 27% of planned US data-center capacity, CNBC and The Daily Reporter reported.

## Why it matters

Bloom's pitch is speed: data centers that can't wait years for a grid connection put its fuel cells on site. A pause on new approvals hits that pitch at the source, because a data center that never gets permitted needs no power at all. Cooling-equipment maker Vertiv ($VRT), which sells into the same buildings, fell 14.0%; see our [story on the chip sell-off](/news/2026-11-05/sandisk-micron-and-amd-shares-plunge-what-happened/).

## What the market is telling us

Bloom Energy's shares moved 5% or more on 108 of the last 252 sessions. The last fall at least this big was on 26 June 2026, when the stock fell 18.5%. It is still up 92.8% this year, but at $167.52 it sits 51.6% below its 52-week closing high of $345.85 from 22 June, and $1,000 invested five years ago would be worth $5,235 today.

Our read: Thursday priced a policy risk, not a lost order, since Bloom's backlog grew this week. The tell will be whether Texas turns the promise into a rule once Hinojosa takes office.
