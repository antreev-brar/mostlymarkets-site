Companies that sell Nvidia ($NVDA) chips have told neoclouds, the firms that rent out AI computing such as CoreWeave ($CRWV) and Nebius ($NBIS), that they won't supply the chips if **any** of the resulting capacity is rented to Chinese companies, CNBC reported on Thursday, citing two people familiar with the matter; Reuters carried the report. Some sellers are now looking for new buyers for chips meant for data centers that served Chinese renters, CNBC said.

The zero-tolerance line is stricter than Nvidia's own: the company told customers this month it wouldn't sell to buyers renting more than half their chips to Chinese firms, [CNBC reported on 17 November](/news/2026-11-17/nvidia-nvda-board-sets-up-export-control-committee-hours-before-its-q3-report/). It follows the Commerce Department's 16 November rule on remote access to advanced chips through overseas data centers, the same rule behind Nvidia's [halt of server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries](/news/2026-11-26/nvidia-nvda-halts-server-shipments-to-four-southeast-asian-countries-ft-says/) reported earlier on Thursday. Chips that can't go to China-facing data centers have to find other buyers, which matters for a stock already down 31.5% in a month.
