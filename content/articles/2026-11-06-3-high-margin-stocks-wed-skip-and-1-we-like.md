A high gross margin, what's left of each sales dollar after the direct cost of making the product, is where great businesses start. On Thursday evening Atlassian showed why it is only the start: a gross margin above 80% and a sales beat still left the stock down 16.2% on Friday, because growth and the bottom line are what investors pay for.

The screen: gross margin over the last 12 months, from SEC filings, across our coverage, leaving out the stocks in our lists of the last 14 days. From the top of that list, we checked whether the margin reaches operating profit and free cash, how fast the company grows and what the price assumes, each against its industry peers.


## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. Atlassian ($TEAM)

**Gross margin: 84.8% in the 12 months to June 2026.** Atlassian makes Jira and Confluence, the software teams use to track work.

1. **Little of the gross margin reaches operating profit.** Its GAAP operating margin was **0.2%** in the 12 months to June 2026, against a median of 9.7% for the 32 software companies in our coverage, and it has fallen 6.6 points over five years.
2. **Growth is slowing.** On Thursday it beat estimates but guided fiscal 2027 subscription growth to about 18.5% and revenue growth to about 13.75%, its release said, below the 24% it grew in the quarter, Seeking Alpha and SiliconANGLE reported ([our recap](/news/2026-11-06/atlassian-team-beats-q1-estimates-but-sinks-as-it-forecasts-slower-subscription-growth/)).
3. **Returns on capital are thin.** Its return on capital was about 1.0%, in the bottom quarter of its peers, whose median is 9.0%.

**The price:** 27.9 times forward earnings, about the software median of 26.7, after falling 32.5% from its 23 October high.

**What would change our mind:** cloud revenue grew 31% in the quarter. A rise in GAAP operating margin toward 10% while growth holds near 20% would make the gross margin count.

### 2. Incyte ($INCY)

**Gross margin: 92.7% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Incyte sells Jakafi, a blood-cancer drug, and a growing list of newer medicines.

1. **The margin stops at the gross line.** Its operating margin was 10.4% over 12 months, below the median of 19.6% for the drugmakers we compare it with, because research spending takes most of the gross profit.
2. **The shareholders are being diluted.** Its share count rose 3.8% over two years, the most of 15 peers, whose median was flat.
3. **The price is far above its own history.** At **63.6 times** trailing earnings, against its median of 17.9 since October 2024, the stock prices in years of growth on top of the 21.4% a year it managed over the last two.

**The price:** 64.6 times forward earnings, more than three times the drugmaker median of 18.2. Analysts are split: Leerink Partners cut its price target to $150 and Bernstein holds a Market Perform rating with a $108 target, TradingView reported.

**What would change our mind:** an operating margin above 20%, the peer median, which would mean its newer drugs pay for the research.

### 3. Verisign ($VRSN)

**Gross margin: 88.6% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Verisign runs the registries for .com and .net web addresses.

1. **The margins are the best in our data, but growth is slow.** Its operating margin was 67.6% and its free-cash-flow margin 60.4% over two years, both first in its group, but revenue grew only **5.5%** a year over five years, below the peer median of 8.0%.
2. **EPS barely compounds.** Earnings per share grew 4.5% a year over five years, in the bottom quarter, even with a 7.1% cut in its share count over two years.
3. **The stock has already re-rated.** It was up 28.5% this year by Thursday and closed at a record $318.03 on 4 November.

**The price:** 30.2 times forward earnings against an industry median of 18.0, and 33.2 times trailing earnings, near the top of its 20.5 to 36.5 range since October 2024.

**What would change our mind:** a fall back toward its median of 30.4 times trailing earnings, or faster growth in .com registrations, which would lift its 5.7% quarterly growth rate.

## 1 stock we like

### Fair Isaac ($FICO)

**Gross margin: 85.1% in the 12 months to June 2026.** Fair Isaac sells the FICO credit score that lenders use to price mortgages, cards and car loans.

1. **The margin reaches the bottom line.** Its operating margin was **51.7%** over 12 months, first of the 32 software companies we track, and it turned 41.0% of sales into free cash over two years. In fiscal 2026, free cash flow rose 40% to $1.03 billion, its results release said.
2. **The quarter was still strong.** Fourth-quarter revenue rose 27% to $657.0 million, and adjusted EPS of $11.10 beat the $10.86 analysts expected, the release and Zacks reported.
3. **The price has fallen far more than the business.** The stock is 61.8% below its December 2025 high, after Wells Fargo in October and RBC Capital this week downgraded it over its decision to hold mortgage-score prices flat for lenders, Investing.com and TradingView reported ([our story](/news/2026-10-26/why-fair-isaac-fico-stock-is-sinking-today/)).

**The price:** 15.9 times forward earnings against a software median of 26.7, and 20.4 times trailing earnings, against its median of 65.2 since July 2024.

**What could go wrong:** Fair Isaac gave no fiscal 2027 forecast while it negotiates mortgage pricing for 2027, and RBC said it has paused buybacks, Investing.com and TradingView reported. If rival VantageScore wins mortgage share, the pricing power that built the 51.7% margin would be the thing that breaks.
