On Friday, Sandisk ($SNDK) **plunged 13.5%**, a day after it beat estimates, and Micron ($MU) slid 5.3%. Bank of America kept its Buy rating on Sandisk but cut its price target and the valuation multiple behind it, on concern that the memory cycle may be nearing a late stage, Invezz and Stocktwits reported. The selling asks the oldest question in memory chips: will this boom end like the others, or have AI and long contracts changed the business?

The argument comes down to timing: when new supply catches up with demand. Jensen Huang, chief executive of Nvidia ($NVDA), says not until after 2029. Counterpoint Research expects prices for DRAM, the working memory in computers and servers, to **fall about 23% in 2028**, CNBC reported.

## The bull case

### Jensen Huang, chief executive, Nvidia

**AI will keep memory scarce for years.** In a CNBC interview at Nvidia's GTC event in Berlin, reported on 22 October, Huang said he doesn't expect an oversupply until sometime after 2029: AI data centers will keep needing more memory, and Nvidia will keep finding new ways to use it. "One of the things that's really great about memory is that you can always use more of it," he said.

### Kwak Noh-jung, chief executive, SK hynix

**Long contracts already lock in more than half of 2027 and 2028.** SK hynix said in its results release on 28 October that demand will exceed supply through 2030, because the newest chips are hard to make and new factories take years to build. Its contracts with **14 major customers**, typically five years long, cover more than half of its planned capacity for both years, at prices that move with the market inside an agreed range. "The customer trust built through our technological competitiveness is leading to long-term contracts," Kwak said.

### Jeongku Choi, senior research analyst, Counterpoint Research

**Contracts make this a "New Normal," so the next downturn will be milder.** Choi told CNBC on 15 October that buyers now sign multi-year contracts with substantial upfront payments and upper and lower price limits, so a downturn shouldn't be as sharp as past ones. Micron's annual report puts a number on those payments: customers paid **$12.75 billion** in deposits in fiscal 2026, nearly all due back between 2029 and 2031, and Micron may keep some or all of a deposit if a buyer misses its minimum purchases. Even after the drop Counterpoint forecasts for 2028, DRAM prices would still be **about 230% above 2025 levels**, CNBC reported.

## The bear case

The bears lean on history: fat margins pay for new factories, and new factories end booms. The last bust nearly halved Micron's revenue between fiscal 2022 and fiscal 2023. Pzena Investment Management, a value-fund manager that still owns Samsung but has trimmed it, wrote on 15 May that memory margins were "likely to mark the peak of the current cycle," and that HBM, the stacked memory in AI chips, is only about 12% of DRAM demand this year, measured in bits.

### Lip-Bu Tan, chief executive, Intel

**High memory prices are already cutting PC demand.** After Intel reported on Thursday, Tan said customers "began signaling a PC slowdown based on the memory prices and availability," Yahoo Finance reported, and finance chief David Zinsner said in the results release that he sees the slowdown lasting at least through the first half of 2027. That's the mechanism bears expect to end the boom: buyers of PCs and phones buy less when memory gets too expensive. Intel ($INTC) **plunged 28.6%** on Friday, its worst day since 1974.

### Kye-hyun Kyung, senior adviser and former head of the chip division, Samsung Electronics

**China's new factories could end the shortage by late next year.** Speaking at a National Academy of Engineering of Korea forum in May, Kyung said a surge in Chinese capacity could start pushing prices down in the second half of 2027 or early 2028, Notebookcheck and PC Gamer reported. In October, CNBC reported fresh worries about CXMT, China's leading memory maker, after it announced plans for server memory built on a new design.

### James Mackintosh, Streetwise columnist, The Wall Street Journal

**A low price-to-earnings ratio is no comfort at the top of a memory cycle.** In a column on 16 May, Mackintosh argued that "high profits encourage CEOs to expand supply," and that the cycle turns when the extra supply pushes prices down. His warning sign is the valuation: Micron's forward price-to-earnings ratio was under 10, close to the nine it traded at when the stock peaked in early 2022, before the shares halved that year.

## Where they agree

Neither side disputes the price data: memory prices are still rising, only more slowly. TrendForce forecast on 30 September that contract prices for conventional DRAM would **rise 10% to 15%** this quarter, down from an estimated 93% to 98% in the first quarter, with enterprise SSDs, the storage drives in data centers, the only category where increases would speed up.

And the suppliers are adding capacity. Micron put **$30.7 billion** into plants and equipment in fiscal 2026, nearly double the year before, and SK hynix says its investment will rise significantly next year as the first phase of its Yongin complex enters full-scale operation, though it will set the scale and timing after weighing customer demand and profitability. The earliest date anyone here puts on falling prices is Kyung's: the second half of 2027.

## The number that decides it

Watch Micron's gross margin, the share of sales left after production costs. On a GAAP basis it has risen for six straight quarters, to **86.8%** in the quarter to 3 September, up from below zero in late 2023.

## Our read

The evidence favors the bulls on timing: more than half of SK hynix's 2027-28 output is under contract, Micron's buyers have put cash behind their orders, and prices are still rising, so we think a price collapse in 2027 is unlikely. But the bears have the direction right: price rises are shrinking and Micron's margin guide has turned down.

Micron's share price already agrees with the bears: at Friday's close it was under 6 times this quarter's guided adjusted earnings per share multiplied by four, a price that assumes today's profits won't last. The contracts that should soften a downturn also cap the upside, since TrendForce says their terms will make some suppliers' server-memory price rises lag the market this quarter. Micron's December guide will tell us whether the turn has started.
