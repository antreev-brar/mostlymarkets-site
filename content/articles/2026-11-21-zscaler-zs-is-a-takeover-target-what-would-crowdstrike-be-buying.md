## How it got here

Jay Chaudhry founded Zscaler in 2007 on one idea: instead of guarding a company's office network with boxes in the server room, send every employee's internet traffic through Zscaler's own cloud and inspect it there. Remote work made that the default for big companies, and revenue went from $673 million in fiscal 2021 to $3.35 billion in the year to July 2026, our analysis of its SEC filings shows.

The stock has not followed. It closed at $289.73 on 25 November 2025 and fell 13% the next day despite beating forecasts, Yahoo Finance and Barchart reported. Then on 27 May 2026 it **plunged 31.5%** in one session after a preliminary fiscal 2027 forecast of 16% to 17% growth, the departure of two sales leaders and a lower free-cash-flow target, The Motley Fool and Nasdaq reported.

On Friday, Bloomberg reported that CrowdStrike ($CRWD) is in talks to buy it; Reuters said the talks could still end without a deal, and CrowdStrike declined to comment. That makes the question concrete: **is Zscaler a great business that slipped, or a maturing one that a buyer would be paying up for?**

## The business in one line

Large companies pay Zscaler a yearly subscription, per user, to route their staff's and their apps' traffic through its cloud, where it blocks attacks and enforces who may reach what.

## How it makes money

Zscaler does not report segments; nearly all its revenue is subscriptions. The growth story is in the yearly totals:

Growth fell from 62% to the mid-20s as the company got bigger, and fiscal 2026 was lifted by Red Canary, a threat-detection firm it bought, which brought $141 million of annual recurring revenue (ARR, the yearly value of its subscriptions). Excluding Red Canary, ARR grew 20% to $3.63 billion, the 3 September release said, as Zacks and Quartr reported. Guidance of $3.908 billion to $3.938 billion for fiscal 2027 means about 17%.

*Verdict: still compounding, but each year's step is getting smaller.*

## The numbers that matter

### Growth
Revenue grew **37.9% a year** over five fiscal years, against a 15.9% median for the software companies we cover, and 24.4% a year over the last two, still in the top quarter. *Verdict: top-quarter growth on every horizon, but slowing.*

### Profitability
On the accounts' GAAP basis, the operating margin was **minus 4.0%** over the 12 months to July, in the bottom quarter of its peers. On the company's adjusted basis it was 23% for the year, the release said; the gap of about $900 million is costs it excludes, chiefly stock-based pay. *Verdict: profitable only before stock pay.*

### Cash
Free cash flow was **25.4% of revenue** in fiscal 2026, about the peer median. In the July quarter it fell to $60.8 million on the company's definition, down 65% from a year earlier, as capital spending rose, Proactive Investors and the release reported; the company guides to 23% to 23.5% for fiscal 2027. *Verdict: real cash, but the margin is drifting down.*

### Dilution
Diluted shares rose **6.9% in two years**, against a 0.3% median for its peers. *Verdict: owners pay for growth with a slice of the company each year.*

### Balance sheet
Cash was $928 million at the end of July against book value of $2.6 billion, and the stock trades at 12.2 times book. *Verdict: nothing in our data points to a balance-sheet problem; the question is growth, not survival.*

## Why the stock is where it is

1. **Growth is slowing.** The fiscal 2027 outlook of about 17% sits well below the 25% just reported, and it caused the 31.5% fall in May.
2. **Cash margins are falling.** The free-cash-flow target was cut from 26.5%–27% to about 23% because of heavier spending on capacity, Nasdaq and The Motley Fool reported.
3. **Sales leadership turned over.** Two senior sales executives left in the April quarter, and the company tied part of its caution to that, Yahoo Finance and Nasdaq reported.
4. **The whole group was sold.** Fortinet's 20.1% fall on 29 October and the 5 November tech rout, when Zscaler fell 9.2%, hit subscription security stocks as a group, TIKR reported, with no Zscaler news either day.

## Testing the worry

**Slowing growth: partly temporary, partly the size of the business.** A sales team rebuilt mid-year usually costs a few quarters, and management tied part of its caution to the departures. But organic ARR growth of 20% and net new ARR growth of 17% show the slowdown had started before the departures. *This worry survives, in smaller form.*

**Falling cash margins: temporary if the spending is capacity.** Capital spending rose to $199.8 million in the July quarter from $60.0 million a year earlier, our filings data shows, and the company blamed the lower cash target on that spending. That is a choice, not lost pricing power: gross margin held at 76.8%, the software median.

**The selloff: contained.** CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks ($PANW) and Fortinet ($FTNT) fell on the same days, so it was the group, not Zscaler.

## The moat

1. **It sits in the traffic.** Every connection a customer's staff make passes through its cloud, so ripping it out means rewiring the company's network. Customers paying more than $1 million a year rose 18% to 785, the release said.
2. **It was built for this one job.** Its two biggest rivals in the field, Palo Alto and Fortinet, grew up selling hardware firewalls, and Zscaler's pitch is that a cloud built for the purpose beats one bolted on.
3. **Cash without capital-heavy growth, until now.** Free cash flow of 25% of sales funds its buildout without borrowing.

| Rival | Revenue growth, last 12 months | Free cash flow margin, last 12 months |
|---|---|---|
| Zscaler | 25.4% | 25.4% |
| CrowdStrike | 24.3% | 29.8% |
| Palo Alto Networks | 30.3% | 34.3% |
| Fortinet | 20.2% | 43.5% |

Zscaler grows about as fast as its rivals but converts less of each sale into cash. Will it be here and stronger in ten years? Here, very likely; stronger depends on whether platform sellers squeeze a single-product specialist.

## The risks we'd watch

1. **The deal falls apart.** Friday's after-hours price was $34.74 a share above the close; if talks end, that gain is at risk, about **15% of the after-hours price**. Reuters said the talks could still end without an agreement.
2. **Dilution eats the growth.** If free cash flow grows 17% a year but the share count keeps rising about 3.4% a year (6.9% over two), free cash flow per share grows about 13%.
3. **Platform pressure.** Palo Alto's revenue grew faster over the last 12 months on a base nearly four times larger, and bundling is how it competes.

In proportion: none of these threatens the business; they threaten the growth rate the price needs.

## What the price assumes

| Measure | Now | Its history | Peers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price to free cash flow | 37x | 21x to 60x since September 2024, median about 39x | n/a |
| Forward P/E | 39.9x | n/a (GAAP losses) | software median 23.7x |
| Price to sales, last 12 months | 9.5x | 6.4x to 18.2x since September 2024 | CrowdStrike 43.5x, Fortinet 11.6x |

Our history column uses the stock price about 40 days after each quarter ended, against that period's 12-month free cash flow and sales. At 37 times free cash flow, the market is assuming the 17% path: five years of 17% growth would lift free cash flow about 2.2-fold and bring the multiple at Friday's close to about 17 times. CrowdStrike trades at 43.5 times sales against Zscaler's 9.5, so a stock-based deal would be paid in a much richer currency.

## Our verdict

**Business: Solid. Price: About its usual price.** Zscaler grows faster than most software companies and turns a quarter of its sales into cash, but it still reports GAAP losses, dilutes owners about 3% a year, and has told investors growth will drop to about 17%. At Friday's close it traded near the middle of its two-year range on free cash flow, which assumes that 17% holds. What would change our mind is net new ARR, the subscriptions added in a quarter: growth well below the 17% it reported in September when it reports on 2 December, after the close, would mean the slowdown is not done. The tension: CrowdStrike may be about to pay a premium for a business the market had just marked down for growing slower.
