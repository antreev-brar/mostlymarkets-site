Walmart ($WMT) reports its fiscal third-quarter results before the market opens on Thursday, 19 November. The bar is Walmart's own forecast: in August it guided to adjusted earnings of 62 to 64 cents a share, and analysts now sit at the top of that range.

Last quarter, on 20 August, the shares **fell 9.2%**, their worst day since May 2022 in our price data. Walmart raised its full-year sales growth forecast to 4% to 5%, but its third-quarter profit guide came in below the 68 cents analysts wanted, and US comparable sales growth slowed to 2.6%, Yahoo Finance and Stocktwits reported. About $2.9 billion of tariff refunds lifted that quarter's profit, and Walmart said it would spend the rest on lower prices ([our breakdown](/news/2026-11-14/walmart-wmt-reports-thursday-at-46-times-earnings-is-the-business-worth-it/)).

## What analysts expect

The Zacks consensus is **64 cents** of adjusted earnings per share, up from 62 cents a year earlier, and it rose a penny over the past 30 days. Revenue estimates run from $186.5 billion at Zacks to $187.25 billion in the consensus Seeking Alpha cites, against $179.5 billion a year ago, a rise of about 4%.

## What to watch

- **US comparable sales.** After 2.6% last quarter, a figure back above 3% would say the slowdown was a blip.
- **Where the tariff refunds went.** Walmart said it would use them to cut prices; the gross margin will show what that cost, and whether advertising and membership fees made up for it.
- **The holiday quarter.** Its fourth-quarter guide is the first read on Christmas spending, a day after Target ($TGT) [beat estimates as traffic jumped 5%](/news/2026-11-18/target-tgt-beats-q3-estimates-as-traffic-jumps-5-and-raises-its-forecast/).

## How the stock has done

Walmart closed at $127.36 on Wednesday, **up 14.3% this year** and 8.1% over the past month. It sits 5.1% below its 52-week closing high of $134.20 from 19 May, and at 42.5 times forward earnings, the price leaves little room for another miss on the guide.
