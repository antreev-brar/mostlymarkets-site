Analog Devices ($ADI) reports results for its fiscal fourth quarter, the three months to October, at **7 a.m. ET on Tuesday, 24 November**, with a call at 10 a.m., the company said. The shares **jumped 9.5%** to $378.75 on Monday as chip stocks rallied on Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia, their biggest gain since April 2025.

Last quarter, the three months to 1 August, revenue rose 40% to a record $4.02 billion and adjusted earnings of $3.45 a share beat the Zacks consensus by 3.6%, the release and Zacks said. Its forecast for this quarter, $4.3 billion of revenue and $3.86 a share, give or take 15 cents, topped estimates, but the shares rose before the open and then closed **0.9% lower** that day in our data.

## What analysts expect

Zacks puts consensus at **$3.89 a share**, up 72.1% from $2.26 a year ago, a touch above the company's own midpoint. Revenue is expected at about $4.32 billion, up roughly 41%, according to Hudson Labs and MarketBeat. Analog Devices has beaten the Zacks consensus in each of the last four quarters, by 4.8% on average.

## The one question

Can a recovery in factory and car chips, plus data-center power demand, carry growth of 40% into next year? Analog Devices sells chips that turn real-world signals into data, with factories its biggest market, so its guidance for the January quarter is the read on whether that recovery is broadening or peaking. We named it [one of three AI infrastructure stocks we like](/news/2026-11-16/3-ai-infrastructure-stocks-we-like-and-1-wed-skip-micron-western-digital-analog-devices-and-amd/) on 16 November for its cash: a 35.6% free cash flow margin over the last 12 months, in the top quarter of the 25 chipmakers we cover.

## What to watch

- **The January-quarter forecast.** A midpoint above $4.3 billion would mean growth is still rising quarter to quarter; a flat or lower figure would say the cycle has crested.
- **Industrial revenue,** its biggest market, and whether customers are restocking or only buying what they use.
- **Gross margin.** Higher factory use pushes it up; a slip would say price or mix is turning.

## How the stock has done

The shares are up 39.7% this year but down 3.2% over the past month, and at $378.75 they sit 15.0% below their 52-week closing high of $445.48 from 22 June. They trade at 23.4 times expected earnings, above the 19.9 times median for chipmakers we cover, so the bar for the guidance is high.
