Nvidia ($NVDA) has told the cloud companies that rent out its AI chips to sell **at least 51%** of their services to non-Chinese companies, the Financial Times reported on Tuesday, citing five people with knowledge of the matter; Reuters and AFP carried the report. The rule applies worldwide, including in data centers outside Asia, and covers new contracts rather than existing leases, according to the FT. Nvidia passed it on by word of mouth through its sales teams, the reports said.

Nvidia did not confirm the figure. In a statement quoted by AFP, it said data centers and clouds built on American chips are the foundation American model makers need to compete worldwide. The reports did not say whether the 51% is measured by revenue, computing capacity or customer count.

The rule closes a route by which Chinese companies could rent Nvidia's chips abroad. Southeast Asian data-center operators, some of which rely heavily on Chinese customers, are the most exposed and may struggle to find enough other buyers quickly, The Standard and AFP reported. Nvidia shares were up 1.4% at $204.94 before the open as of 6:14 a.m. ET; Nasdaq’s earnings calendar has its next results after the close on 17 November.
