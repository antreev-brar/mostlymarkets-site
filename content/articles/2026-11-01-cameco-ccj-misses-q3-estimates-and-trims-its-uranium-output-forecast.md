Cameco ($CCJ) reported third-quarter revenue of C$619 million on Friday morning, up 1% year on year, and an adjusted net loss of C$2 million, down from adjusted earnings of C$32 million a year earlier. Analysts had expected adjusted earnings of about 23 cents a share, according to Nasdaq's earnings calendar, and Investing.com and Seeking Alpha both reported a miss. The uranium miner's shares **sank 7.9%** to $76.91 at the close.

## What stood out

**Maintenance cut output in half.** Production at McArthur River and Key Lake, Cameco's main mine and mill in Saskatchewan, fell to 1.6 million pounds from 3.2 million a year earlier, the company's report showed. Cameco said the annual maintenance shutdown started in the third quarter this year rather than the second, so the drop is mostly about timing.

*Verdict: a timing miss, not a broken mine, but it came with a lower ceiling for the year.*

**Westinghouse weighed on the bottom line.** Cameco's share of the nuclear-reactor maker's adjusted EBITDA rose to C$143 million, but on a net basis its stake lost C$24 million, the company reported, and Cameco blamed lower equity earnings from Westinghouse for most of the 14% fall in adjusted EBITDA. Westinghouse is Cameco's main exposure to the construction of new reactors.

## The outlook

Cameco narrowed its 2026 production range to 19.5 million to 20.5 million pounds, from 19.5 million to 21.5 million, cutting the top end, and trimmed McArthur River and Key Lake to 10.0 million to 10.5 million pounds, the Motley Fool transcript and the company's filing showed. It tightened deliveries to 29.5 million to 30.5 million pounds. The company headlined its release "Year-to-Date Performance as Expected" and said the industry's fundamentals continue to strengthen.

## Our take

We were not impressed: the profit miss, the trimmed output range and a loss at the Westinghouse stake all landed in the same report, even if the mine shortfall is mostly timing. On the other hand, deliveries are on plan and Westinghouse's adjusted EBITDA grew, which is the long-term story. Overall, a weak quarter for a stock priced for strong ones.

That is why the stock fell so hard. Even after a 15.9% drop this year, Cameco trades at 55.5 times forward earnings, against a 12.8 times median for our energy group, so the price assumes years of rising uranium prices and reactor orders. The fourth-quarter report in February, with the 2027 production plan, is the next test.
