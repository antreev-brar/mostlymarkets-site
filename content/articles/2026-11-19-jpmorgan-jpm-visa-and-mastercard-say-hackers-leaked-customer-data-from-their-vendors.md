JPMorgan Chase ($JPM), Visa ($V) and Mastercard ($MA) said on Thursday that hackers had reached customer data held by outside vendors, after a ransomware group called n0n posted files tied to all three, the Wall Street Journal and Yahoo Finance reported. JPMorgan said the stolen marketing data may include customer names and email addresses, and that no payment credentials, card numbers or passwords were taken. Visa said the breach began at a provider in Asia Pacific and its own systems were not involved; Mastercard said a limited set of email addresses from one card-benefits program was exposed.

The stake is trust, not money: card networks earn their fees on being the safe way to pay, and so far nothing touches the card data itself. Outlets that went through the leaked files counted about **16,000** JPMorgan customer email addresses, a number the bank hasn't confirmed. Visa closed **down 3.1%** and Mastercard down 2.6%, but on a day the Dow lost 1,101 points ([our wrap](/news/2026-11-19/stock-market-today-anthropic-jumps-17-in-its-debut-but-the-dow-drops-1101-points/)), so the leak can't be separated from the selloff. JPMorgan said the attack had no connection to the separate tech outages that hit the US this week.

None of the three has named the vendor. JPMorgan said it will contact affected customers directly; what to watch is whether the count grows, or a regulator or lawsuit follows.
