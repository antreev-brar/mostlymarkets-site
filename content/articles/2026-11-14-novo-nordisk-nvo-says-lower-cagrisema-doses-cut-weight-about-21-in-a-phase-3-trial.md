Novo Nordisk ($NVO) said on Saturday that adults without diabetes on two lower doses of CagriSema, its experimental weekly shot pairing the amylin drug cagrilintide with semaglutide, lost an average of **20.7%** and 21.0% of their weight after 68 weeks in its REDEFINE 9 trial, against 2.0% on placebo. The Phase 3 study of 309 adults was presented at the ObesityWeek 2026 meeting and published in The Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology, Novo's release said, and Reuters carried the figures.

It is the first Phase 3 weight trial to test CagriSema below its 2.4 mg top dose, and the 1.0 mg and 1.7 mg doses landed within half a point of each other. Our read: the trial shows dose flexibility rather than a better dose, since it was not built to rank the two. The same day, Eli Lilly ($LLY) reported [23.3% weight loss for its own amylin pairing](/news/2026-11-14/eli-lilly-lly-says-its-eloratzp-combination-cut-weight-23-3-in-a-phase-2-trial/) in a Phase 2 trial of people with type 2 diabetes, so the two results don't compare directly. Novo shares are down **18.2%** this year even after it [raised its 2026 forecast a third time](/news/2026-11-04/novo-nordisk-nvo-raises-its-2026-forecast-a-third-time-as-the-wegovy-pill-beats/) on 4 November.

What's next: CagriSema is under review at the FDA, which Novo has said it expects to decide on in the fourth quarter of 2026, Zacks and LifeScienceDaily reported; no decision date has been made public.
