After last Wednesday's rate decision, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said that one of the Fed's eight meetings in 2027 will "focus exclusively on broad monetary policy strategy, with neither a vote on the federal funds rate nor a policy statement anticipated," and that 2028 will have **two** such meetings and six that set policy, CNBC and NBC News reported. The Fed has voted on rates at eight scheduled meetings a year since 1981.

The change was overshadowed by the split vote that day ([our story](/news/2026-10-28/fed-held-rates-but-two-officials-wanted-a-hike/)), but it changes how markets price the Fed. Warsh argued that longer gaps between decisions let the committee gather more data. Fewer dates means each decision can carry a bigger move, or a move between meetings, analysts quoted by CNBC and an FXStreet analysis argued, and borrowing costs could stay where they are for longer when the economy turns, CNBC wrote on Monday.

Warsh did not say which 2027 meeting loses its vote, and it isn't yet clear what, if anything, the strategy meetings will publish. The next rate decision is due at 2 p.m. ET on 9 December, the Fed's calendar shows.
