CrowdStrike ($CRWD) agreed early on Monday to buy Zscaler ($ZS) in an all-stock deal valued at about **$43.9 billion**, the companies said, as CNBC and Reuters reported. Each Zscaler share becomes 1.135 CrowdStrike shares, worth $260.41 at Friday's close, a 34% premium. Zscaler rose 13.4% to $221.00 before the open and CrowdStrike fell 9.3% to $208.00, as of 6:15 a.m. ET.

The deal adds Zscaler's zero-trust network security, which checks every user before letting them reach company systems, to CrowdStrike's endpoint software. CrowdStrike targets $1 billion of extra yearly revenue within 30 months and $500 million of yearly cost savings within 18 to 24 months. It also reported a record $419.2 million of net new annual recurring revenue for its third quarter, up 58%, and revenue of $1.58 billion against the $1.529 billion LSEG consensus, its release showed.

At today's pre-market prices the offer is worth about $236 a share, so Zscaler trades **6.4% below** it: the market's discount for the deal not closing ([what CrowdStrike is buying](/news/2026-11-21/zscaler-zs-is-a-takeover-target-what-would-crowdstrike-be-buying/)). Regulators and both sets of shareholders still have to approve it.
