The Cigna Group ($CI) reported third-quarter revenue of $73.7 billion before the open on Thursday, up 6% year on year, and adjusted earnings of **$7.98 a share**, 7% above the $7.46 Zacks consensus. It nudged its 2026 profit forecast higher. The shares opened at $318.00, within cents of the session's 52-week high, then slid with the market to close **down 1.3% at $301.48**, on a day the S&P 500 fell 4.4%.

## What stood out

**Specialty drugs carried the quarter.** Evernorth's Specialty and Care unit, which dispenses expensive drugs for conditions such as cancer and arthritis, grew profit **15% to $1.06 billion** on revenue up just 1%, the release showed. The gain came from biosimilars and specialty generics, cheaper copies of biologic drugs that earn the pharmacy a wider margin than the branded originals.

*Verdict: the strongest part of the company, and the reason the beat was 7% rather than 1%.*

**The pharmacy-benefit business is paying to keep clients.** Pharmacy Benefit Services, the Express Scripts arm that runs drug plans for employers, grew revenue 9% to $37.3 billion, but its profit fell **22% to $765 million**. Cigna put that down to renewals of large client contracts and investment spending.

*Verdict: volume is holding up, but at thinner margins, and that is the line the next two years hinge on.*

**Medical costs stayed in check.** The medical care ratio, the share of premiums paid out in claims, was 85.0%, slightly worse than a year ago but better than the 85.18% LSEG estimate cited by Reuters.

## The outlook

Cigna raised its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast to **at least $30.60**, from at least $30.45. For 2027, it said it expects adjusted EPS to grow at least 10% from a base of about $30, while naming the headwinds: shrinking drug-rebate pools, fewer employers paying for GLP-1 weight-loss drugs, and its exit from the Affordable Care Act individual market on 1 January 2027, Seeking Alpha and Healthcare Finance News reported.

The rebate shift is the bigger change. Express Scripts will move fully insured clients to its rebate-free pharmacy model in 2027 and make it the default for other clients in 2028, Fierce Healthcare reported. Chief Executive Brian Evanko said in the release that the quarter showed Cigna "making health care more affordable and personalized" while it pushes a cost-cutting program called Accelerate to One.

## Our take

We were impressed by the size of the beat and by specialty pharmacy, which is growing profit far faster than revenue. On the other hand, the pharmacy-benefit profit drop and a 2027 plan built around fewer rebates mean the earnings mix is moving under investors' feet. Overall, a solid quarter: the stock trades at 9.5 times forward earnings, against a peer median of 17.3 by our data, so the market is already pricing in doubt about that transition.

The fade from the open owed more to Thursday's selloff than to the report, which gave no new reason for alarm. Revenue has grown 11.4% a year over five years, ahead of the 10.3% peer median, but EPS has not kept up, slipping 0.7% a year. The next test is the full 2027 guidance with fourth-quarter results in early 2027, and how much pharmacy-benefit profit falls.
