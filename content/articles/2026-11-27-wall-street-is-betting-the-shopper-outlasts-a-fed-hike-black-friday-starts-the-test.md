The story investors are telling about the American shopper is simple: people keep spending, so the stores they spend in are the safe place to sit while AI stocks fall and the Fed raises rates. The National Retail Federation set the tone in October with a forecast of record holiday sales, and in the last ten days the biggest retailers backed it with their best quarters in years: Target's ($TGT) sales grew the most in more than five years, and Best Buy's ($BBY) comparable sales the most in five.

Black Friday, today, starts the part of the season that decides whether that holds. Adobe expects a record **$12.9 billion** of US online sales today, up 9.2% from a year earlier. The stocks have already priced a good answer: in our data Target is up 76.2% this year and Dollar Tree ($DLTR) 23.1% in the past month, while Nvidia ($NVDA) fell 31.5% over the same month.

## The story in one sentence

> Consumers are the "unsung heroes of our economy," NRF Chief Executive Matthew Shay said on 15 October, as the group forecast holiday sales up 4.2% to a record $1.013 trillion.

The chain runs like this: if jobs and wages hold, households keep shopping despite 3.8% inflation and a rising Fed rate; if they keep shopping, retailers' sales grow steadily while AI earnings look uncertain, so investors pay up for them. What has changed since we [first tested this story](/news/2026-11-17/wall-street-is-hiding-in-the-shopper-this-weeks-retailers-will-test-it/) on 17 November is the evidence: then the worry was that growth was all price, and the quarters that followed showed more shoppers coming through the door.

## The evidence for it

### 1. More people came through the doors

Target said comparable traffic **rose 5.0%** in its third quarter, so most of its 5.6% comparable-sales gain came from more visits, not bigger baskets ([our recap](/news/2026-11-18/target-tgt-beats-q3-estimates-as-traffic-jumps-5-and-raises-its-forecast/)). Dollar Tree's traffic rose 3.8% ([our recap](/news/2026-11-24/dollar-tree-dltr-beats-q3-estimates-as-traffic-rises-and-raises-its-profit-forecast/)), and Ross Stores ($ROST) said its 8% comparable gain was "primarily driven by customer traffic." Traffic is the hardest number for a retailer to buy with price increases.

### 2. The forecasts went up, not down

Walmart ($WMT) raised its full-year outlook for the third time this year, to adjusted earnings of $2.84 to $2.89 a share ([our recap](/news/2026-11-19/walmart-wmt-beats-q3-estimates-and-raises-its-outlook-for-the-third-time/)). Target lifted its sales forecast to about 6% from about 5%, and Best Buy raised its comparable-sales range to **3.4% to 4.0%** from 1.9% to 3.0% ([our recap](/news/2026-11-24/best-buy-bby-beats-q3-estimates-with-its-best-comparable-sales-in-five-years/)). Target added that November sales so far were running in line with its plan and faster than October.

### 3. Online spending is running ahead of forecast

Adobe said US shoppers spent **$82.5 billion** online from 1 to 22 November, up 9.1% from a year earlier, Digital Commerce 360 and MLQ.ai reported. That is ahead of the 6.7% growth Adobe forecast in September for the whole season, which it put at a record $275.1 billion.

### 4. The card networks agree on the dollars

Visa forecasts holiday retail sales up **4.5%**, the strongest since 2021, its 5 November release said, and Mastercard's SpendingPulse expects 5.5% growth from 1 November to 24 December excluding cars and gas, which would be the best in four years. Mastercard, like the NRF, expects about half of that growth to come from higher prices.

## The case against it

The doubters fall into three camps: economists who say the dollars hide thin volumes, the companies' own holiday guides, and the price investors are paying. Three points, strongest first.

1. **The stocks are priced for perfection.** Walmart trades at **42.7 times** expected earnings and Costco ($COST) at 47.6 times, against a median of 17.8 times for consumer-staples peers, in our data; Nvidia now trades at 10.4 times. Walmart's own trailing multiple of 47.6 is near the top of its two-year range of 33.9 to 49.4. A shopper who merely holds up does not justify those prices, which makes this the strongest objection.
2. **The holiday guides are slower, and the beats were padded.** Best Buy guided holiday-quarter comparable sales up 2% to 4%, against 6.1% last quarter; Dollar Tree 3% to 4%, against 4.6%; and Ross 4% to 5%, against 8%. Target's $2.65 of adjusted earnings included $0.50 of refunds of tariffs collected under the IEEPA emergency law, and refunds also lifted Best Buy, Lowe's ($LOW) and Williams-Sonoma's margins. Those will not repeat, and the trade office [proposed new tariffs](/news/2026-11-25/us-proposes-tariffs-on-14-trading-partners-with-20-on-china-and-a-15-cap-for-taiwan/) on 14 economies on Wednesday.
3. **Real growth is thin, and stretched buyers are borrowing.** Oxford Economics, which builds the NRF forecast, expects holiday sales up 4.2% in dollars but only **2.2%** after inflation, in a season it calls "deeply bifurcated" with high-income shoppers doing the lifting. Adobe expects a record $21.3 billion of buy-now-pay-later spending, and 47% of those borrowers told LendingTree they had paid late in the past year, up from 34% two years earlier. Walmart's finance chief John David Rainey warned on 19 November that diesel and transport costs are starting to feed into the cost of goods.

## What would change minds

- **Adobe's Black Friday and Cyber Monday tallies this weekend.** Online sales well below the $12.9 billion forecast for today, or the $15.1 billion forecast for Monday, 30 November, would be the first crack in the holiday numbers.
- **The November jobs report on Friday, 4 December.** Spending holds only if hiring does; a weak report days before the Fed would hit the lower-income shopper story hardest.
- **The Fed on Wednesday, 9 December.** A hike is about 99% priced, CME FedWatch data cited by Reuters show. New projections showing several more hikes in 2027 would raise borrowing costs for the households already leaning on card and BNPL credit.

The rally since results has favoured the chains that showed traffic gains. Ross is the telling case: its shares sank 9.0% after hours on its holiday guide on 19 November, and closed on Wednesday at $223.26, above where they stood before the report.

## Our read

**Partly supported.** The traffic is real: Target, Dollar Tree and Ross grew on more visits, not only higher prices, which answers the main doubt we raised ten days ago. The weakest link is the price: Walmart and Costco cost four to five times what Nvidia does per dollar of expected earnings, while the companies themselves guide the holiday quarter slower and part of last quarter's profit came from refunds that will not recur. The number we will watch is Adobe's Cyber Monday tally against its $15.1 billion forecast, then Walmart's holiday comparable sales in February.
