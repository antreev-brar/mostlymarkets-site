The story on Wall Street this weekend is that the Federal Reserve will not catch falling stocks this time. Strategists at Barclays led by Emmanuel Cau wrote in a note reported by Investing.com and Yahoo Finance that beyond the turmoil in AI stocks, the "bigger challenge for equities is the perception that the Fed put has disappeared." The Fed put is the old belief that the central bank eases when markets fall hard enough.

The peg came on Friday, 20 November: Fed Governor Lisa Cook said in Santiago that rates must rise to beat inflation, and hours later the S&P 500 closed in a correction after its worst week since April 2025. Futures still price a hike on 9 December, and Nvidia ($NVDA), Dell ($DELL) and Strategy ($MSTR) lost a fifth to a third of their value in the week, while Walmart ($WMT) held level.

## The story in one sentence

> "Waiting to act when a policy adjustment is required has a cost." — Fed Governor Lisa Cook, in a speech at the Central Bank of Chile on Friday, 20 November

The chain runs like this: inflation is still rising, so the Fed keeps hiking even as stocks fall, so the cost of money for every future dollar of profit keeps climbing, which hurts the stocks priced furthest into the future, AI hardware first. The only way out the bulls see is a Fed that blinks, and the officials spent last week saying they won't.

## The evidence for it

### 1. A Fed governor called for hikes on the day of a 3.7% drop

Cook said on Friday that the Fed's 3.75% to 4.00% range is **not high enough** to deal with inflation she views with "great concern," and that financial conditions are still not restraining the economy, Reuters, via Kitco, and Seeking Alpha reported. She warned that if the Fed falls behind, "additional and larger" increases may be needed later. The S&P 500 fell 3.7% that day, and no official has since suggested a pause.

### 2. Almost all officials already expect more hikes

The minutes of the October meeting showed that **almost all** officials expect further increases, and that many saw the AI buildout as a source of inflation risk, CNBC and Reuters reported ([our story](/news/2026-11-18/fed-minutes-show-almost-all-officials-expect-more-rate-hikes-and-flag-ai-as-an-inflation-risk/)). J.P. Morgan's economists now expect a quarter-point hike at each of the next three meetings, the same call as BofA Global Research and Deutsche Bank, Reuters reported ([our brief](/news/2026-11-19/jpmorgan-now-expects-fed-rate-hikes-at-each-of-the-next-three-meetings/)).

### 3. The market's panic barely dented the hike bets

The odds of a December hike slipped only to **88.6%** from 93.9% after Friday's rout, according to CME FedWatch data cited by Benzinga on Yahoo Finance and by BigGo Finance. Goldman Sachs's trading desk put its panic index at 9 out of 10 on Friday and said more selling by trend-following funds could follow this week, Yahoo Finance and KuCoin News reported. The worst week for stocks since April 2025 moved the hike odds about five points.

### 4. Inflation is expected to rise again on Wednesday

RBC Economics and the Cleveland Fed's nowcast both put October's PCE inflation, the Fed's preferred gauge, at about **3.8%** a year, up from 3.6% in September, with core inflation at 3.2% to 3.3%. A number like that is the reason Cook gave for hiking, and it lands two weeks before the Fed decides.

## The case against it

The bond market is the doubter. The 2-year Treasury yield, which tracks where traders think the Fed's rate is heading, **fell 0.20 point** last week to 4.65%, and the 10-year yield fell 0.19 point to 4.95%, in our data. Barclays itself noted that markets still price about three hikes over the coming year, which it called at odds with what stocks are doing; a falling 2-year yield says traders are trimming the later ones.

Three points are held against the story, strongest first:

1. **The Fed has said it fears AI debt.** The same October minutes flagged fast-growing, opaque AI-related borrowing that could amplify losses if returns disappoint, CNBC and Quartz reported. A sell-off that starts to hit lenders is the kind of stress the Fed responds to, even mid-hiking cycle.
2. **Some investors think the backstop has only changed form.** Darius Dale, founder of research firm 42 Macro, said on Yahoo Finance before the sell-off that "the Fed put is very much back," pointing to the chance of Fed and Treasury action on the bond market and a "liquidity rescue in 2027." Edward Jones still expects a standard quarter point in December but said the Fed will weigh the risk an AI slowdown poses as volatility rises.
3. **The White House wants cuts.** President Trump said at a news conference on Friday that rates are too high because the Fed is "political," and that the US should have the lowest rate in the world, Reuters, via MarketScreener, and Business Standard reported. He said he did not blame Chair Kevin Warsh. Pressure alone has not moved the votes so far.

The first is the strongest: it is the Fed's own stated worry, not an outside hope. The second rests on a policy no official has proposed.

## What would change minds

- **Wednesday, 25 November, 8:30 a.m. ET:** October PCE inflation. A reading at or below September's 3.6% would give the doves their first argument; 3.8% or above locks in December and keeps January in play.
- **Tuesday, 24 November, after the close:** Dell's results. A forecast cut from the AI hardware leader would be the "real AI slowdown" Barclays says would ease the pressure on the Fed, at the cost of the stocks themselves ([our week-ahead edition](/daily/2026-11-22/)).
- **Wednesday, 9 December:** The Fed's decision and its new rate projections. A projection showing fewer hikes in 2027 than J.P. Morgan's three would be the Fed signaling where the net is; a half-point move, which Governor Christopher Waller has argued for, would end the debate the other way ([the debate](/news/2026-11-18/the-debate-over-decembers-hike-waller-says-50-williams-says-no-dramatic-action/)).

One more sign that this is a scramble for cash and not just a rate story: gold **fell 9.0%** last week in our data even as Treasury yields fell, the opposite of its usual move, and Newmont ($NEM) lost 16.3%. Yahoo Finance and Stocktwits called it a "great cash grab," with investors selling what they could to cover losses elsewhere ([our rotation narrative](/news/2026-11-19/the-average-stock-is-up-while-the-s-p-500-is-down-wall-street-is-betting-techs-lead-is-over/)).

## Our read

**Partly supported.** The Fed won't rescue stocks in December: four officials have called for hikes since the October inflation report, Cook did so on the day of a 3.7% drop, and futures barely moved. The weak link is the story's second half, that there is no net at all: the 2-year yield's 0.20-point drop says the bond market expects the Fed to stop sooner than it says, and the Fed's own minutes name AI debt as a risk it is watching. The number we'll watch is Wednesday's PCE reading against 3.8%, because it decides whether the doves have anything to argue with on 9 December ([the bulls and bears on the correction](/news/2026-11-21/bulls-vs-bears-on-the-s-p-500s-correction-is-the-bottom-close-or-still-ahead/)).
