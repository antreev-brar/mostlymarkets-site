On Monday morning, Estée Lauder ($EL) reported fiscal first-quarter sales of $3.72 billion for the three months to September, **up 7%** from a year earlier, ahead of the roughly $3.64 billion analysts expected. Adjusted earnings of $0.72 a share beat the $0.52 consensus of 11 analysts in Nasdaq's earnings calendar by 38%, and the company raised its forecasts for the year. The stock rose 2.7% before the open, then turned lower: it was **down 0.7%** at $108.47 as of 10:15 a.m. ET.

## What stood out

Profit grew far faster than sales. The adjusted operating margin **widened by 4.8 percentage points** to 12.1%, the company said in its release, helped by an adjusted gross margin of 75.7%, up from 73.3%. That is the number that matters most for Estée Lauder: over the 12 months to June its operating margin was 5.2%, against 13.6% for the median consumer-staples company in our coverage, according to its SEC filings.

Asia's airports did much of the lifting on sales. Shipments to duty-free shops at Beijing and Shanghai airports rose as new retailers there restocked, the company said, a rebound the Moodie Davitt Report also highlighted. Chief Executive Stéphane de La Faverie said on the call that the company had moved beyond restoring growth and was now building several drivers of steady, profitable growth, Investing.com and TheIndustry.beauty reported.

## The outlook

The company now expects organic sales to grow 4% to 5.5% this fiscal year and adjusted earnings of $3.20 to $3.45 a share, both up from its August forecast. It said it expects more of the year's growth in the first half than the second, partly because of travel-retail restocking, and kept its caution about geopolitical and economic uncertainty.

## Our take

The quarter was good on the number that matters most: the margin is recovering, and management raised its profit forecast only three months after setting it. The fade from a pre-market gain says the market already expected a beat, and the company's own warning that growth slows in the second half, as airport restocking ends, gives investors a reason to wait. Over five fiscal years its sales have shrunk 1.5% a year, so the test next quarter is whether organic growth holds near 6% once the travel-retail lift fades.
