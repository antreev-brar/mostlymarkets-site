President Donald Trump on Thursday called the upward revision to third-quarter US economic growth a "record setting error" and demanded an immediate investigation, CNBC and the Associated Press reported. The Commerce Department said on Wednesday that the economy grew at a **4.3%** annual rate in the quarter, [not the 3.4% in its first estimate](/news/2026-11-25/us-third-quarter-gdp-growth-is-revised-up-to-4-3-from-3-4/); Trump alleged, without evidence, that the earlier figure was deliberately low before the 3 November midterm elections, the AP said.

Revisions are routine: the Bureau of Economic Analysis publishes three estimates for each quarter as fuller data arrive, and Wednesday's came on its announced schedule, the AP noted. The demand matters because markets set rates on these numbers, and it echoes August 2025, when Trump fired the head of the Bureau of Labor Statistics after weak jobs revisions. A stronger economy also supports the [Fed hike priced for 9 December](/news/2026-11-25/core-pce-inflation-rose-to-3-3-in-october-a-tenth-above-forecasts-before-a-fed-hike/).

Whether the Commerce Department will open a review isn't known yet. The third estimate of third-quarter growth is due in December.
