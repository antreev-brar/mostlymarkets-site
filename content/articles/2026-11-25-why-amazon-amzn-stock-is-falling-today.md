## What happened
Shares of Amazon ($AMZN) **fell 5.9%** to $237.50 by 10:30 a.m. ET on Wednesday, on course for the company's worst day since 1 August 2025, as the AI sell-off reached the biggest cloud companies. Alphabet ($GOOGL) was down 4.9% and Microsoft ($MSFT) 3.5% in our data, with the Nasdaq Composite down 3.5%.

Amazon had one worry of its own. Bernstein analysts said activity on AWS, Amazon's cloud arm, measured by Similarweb's count of AWS sign-ins, was running about 16% below its third-quarter average in mid-to-late November, as companies freeze system changes before the holidays, Investing.com and Longbridge reported. They said that could take several hundred basis points off AWS's growth in the first quarter of 2027. The rest is the market: CNN and Yahoo Finance tied Wednesday's sell-off to doubts that AI spending will pay off, new US tariff proposals and October inflation data that points to a December rate hike.

## Why it matters
AWS earns most of Amazon's operating profit, and where its AI spending has to earn its return. Investors are now testing every AI spender's cash flow: the cost of insuring the debt of Oracle, Alphabet, SpaceX and CoreWeave against default hit records this week, Reuters reported, and [Oracle fell 8% today](/news/2026-11-25/why-oracle-orcl-stock-is-down-today/). Amazon also owns a large stake in Anthropic, whose shares [fell below their $83 IPO price](/news/2026-11-24/anthropic-falls-below-its-83-ipo-price-for-the-first-time/) on Tuesday.

Several Amazon executives, including AWS chief executive Matthew Garman, sold shares on 23 and 24 November under preset trading plans, Investing.com reported, about $16.6 million in all. Sales under such plans are scheduled in advance and say little about the outlook.

## What the market is telling us
Amazon's shares have moved 5% or more on only 4 of the last 252 sessions, so today's drop stands out against its usual calm. At Tuesday's close of $252.29, the stock was up 9.3% this year and 11.2% below its 3 August high. We read the Bernstein note as a seasonal blip that it says itself happens around Thanksgiving; the bigger driver today is the market marking down everyone who spends heavily on AI, and Amazon's fourth-quarter report early next year is the next real test of AWS growth.
