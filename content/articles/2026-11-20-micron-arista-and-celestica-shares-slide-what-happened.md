## What happened
Shares of the companies that supply AI data centres fell hard on Friday morning. As of 10:15 a.m. ET, contract manufacturer Celestica ($CLS) was **down 9.5%**, cable-chip maker Credo ($CRDO) 8.0%, network-switch maker Arista Networks ($ANET) 7.7%, memory maker Micron ($MU) 7.0% and connectivity-chip maker Astera Labs ($ALAB) 6.8%, by our quotes.

There was no company news behind the drops. The cause was a sell-off in AI stocks that started in Asia overnight, after Anthropic's shares fell back on their second day of trading, Reuters and The Standard reported. The Nasdaq Composite opened **1.2% lower**, and Reuters also pointed to hawkish signals from Federal Reserve officials this week. Benzinga and Invezz both put Micron's fall down to investors pulling back from AI-linked stocks rather than any news from the company.

Chipmaking-equipment makers fell with them: Applied Materials ($AMAT) was down 6.2%, Lam Research ($LRCX) 6.1% and Teradyne ($TER) 5.1%, by our quotes, while Broadcom ($AVGO) lost 6.3% and Nvidia ($NVDA) 4.0%.

## Why it matters
The selling had already been global. Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan were headed for an 8% weekly loss, their worst week since March 2020, and Taiwan's market fell more than 6% on Friday as TSMC dropped, Reuters reported, while The Standard put Taiwan on track for its worst week since 1990. "The market is no longer looking for proof of AI demand alone," Saxo strategist Charu Chanana told Reuters.

That quote is the problem for this group. Nvidia beat estimates and guided to $130 billion this week ([our recap](/news/2026-11-17/nvidia-nvda-tops-q3-estimates-and-guides-to-130-billion-in-its-first-report-without-huang/)), and Anthropic raised a record sum, yet the suppliers whose sales depend on that spending have kept falling. Investors are now asking whether the spending pays off, the question we put to the bulls and bears [on Monday](/news/2026-11-16/bulls-vs-bears-on-ai-spending-semianalysis-cut-28-morgan-stanley-says-the-cycle-is-intact/), and with the Fed minutes showing [almost every official expects more hikes](/news/2026-11-18/fed-minutes-show-almost-all-officials-expect-more-rate-hikes-and-flag-ai-as-an-inflation-risk/), high-multiple stocks have less support.

## What the market is telling us
Before Friday, Micron was up 141.9% this year but 43.1% below its June closing high, and Credo was 61.1% below its June high, by our data; Arista was the exception, up 44.7% this year and only 12.5% below its October high. This is the second sweep of the group in a week: on Monday, 16 November, Arista fell 5.2% and Micron 7.6% in an [Asia-led chip rout](/news/2026-11-16/lumentum-coherent-and-arista-shares-slide-what-happened/). We think the market is now selling the stocks with the most AI in their sales regardless of their latest results, so the next test is Micron's own report on 17 December, the first big supplier to show whether orders are holding.
