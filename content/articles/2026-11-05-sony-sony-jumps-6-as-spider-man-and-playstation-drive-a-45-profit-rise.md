Sony Group ($SONY) said on Thursday that operating income for July to September, its fiscal second quarter, rose 45% year on year to **¥622.3 billion**, a record for the period and well above the ¥505 billion average of 12 analysts polled by LSEG, Reuters reported. Sales rose 10% to ¥3.43 trillion, and Sony raised its full-year profit forecast by about 9%. Its US-listed shares closed **up 6.1% at $27.54**, on a day the S&P 500 fell 4.4%.

## What stood out

**PlayStation made more money on fewer consoles.** Profit in Game & Network Services nearly doubled, up **83% to ¥220.1 billion**, even though Sony sold just 2.6 million PS5s. The money now comes from games, subscriptions and the cut Sony takes on other publishers' sales, which carry far higher margins than the hardware.

*Verdict: the strongest sign yet that PlayStation's profit no longer depends on the console cycle.*

**Spider-Man filled the cinema arm.** Sony Pictures' revenue rose 48% and its operating income reached a second-quarter record of $581 million, The Hollywood Reporter and The Irish Times reported, on the run of *Spider-Man: Brand New Day*. Film hits are lumpy, which is why the beat was bigger than the forecast raise: Sony lifted the Pictures full-year profit target by ¥50 billion, to ¥200 billion.

**Music kept compounding.** Sony Music's operating income rose 6% to a record ¥122.8 billion for the quarter, Hits Daily Double and Music Week reported.

## The outlook

Sony raised its operating income forecast for the year to March 2027 to **¥1.87 trillion**, from ¥1.72 trillion, and its sales outlook to ¥13 trillion. The open question for the console business is the cost of memory chips, which are in short supply. "We have been able to offset the rise in memory prices through adjustments to promotions and pricing, as well as additional cost-reduction," Chief Financial Officer Lin Tao said on the call, as reported by GamesRadar and Video Games Chronicle; he added that work on the next PlayStation is "progressing as scheduled."

## Our take

We were impressed by the size of the beat and that it came from three businesses at once: games, film and music. On the other hand, part of the quarter rests on one blockbuster, and console sales are shrinking as Sony prices through higher memory costs. Overall, a strong quarter that supports the shift investors have been paying for, from a hardware maker to an entertainment company with recurring revenue.

MST Financial analyst David Gibson called it "a huge beat, with an upgrade to guidance to match," while questioning how much is already priced in, Bloomberg reported in a story carried by The Japan Times. Even after Thursday's jump, the US shares sit 9% below their November 2025 high. The next test is the holiday quarter, reported in February, when PS5 volumes and memory costs meet the busiest selling season.
