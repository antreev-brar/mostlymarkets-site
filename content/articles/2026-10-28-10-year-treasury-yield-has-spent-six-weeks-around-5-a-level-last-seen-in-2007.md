The 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.05% on Wednesday, up from 4.99% on Tuesday, according to the Treasury's daily yield-curve data. That came after the Federal Reserve held its target range at 3.75% to 4.00%, with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack voting for a hike.

The yield began the year at 4.19%. It first closed above 5% on 16 September, for the first time since July 2007, and peaked at 5.31% on 5 October, its highest close since May 2002. Since 15 September it has finished at 5% or higher on 26 of 31 trading days.

The 10-year sets the tone for mortgage rates, and it is the safe return that every stock's earnings get measured against. At 5%, a Treasury pays more than the free-cash-flow yield of many big stocks: Meta's was 1.8% at Monday's close, by [our count](/news/2026-10-27/3-cash-machine-stocks-we-love-and-1-we-hate/).

What to watch: the Fed's next meeting, on 8 and 9 December.
