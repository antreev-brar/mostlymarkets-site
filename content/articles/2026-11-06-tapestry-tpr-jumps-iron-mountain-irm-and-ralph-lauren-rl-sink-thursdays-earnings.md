Seven companies worth $22 billion to $35 billion each reported before the open on Thursday 5 November, on a day the S&P 500 fell 4.4%. Tapestry ($TPR) **jumped 7.1%** close to close after Coach sales grew 16%, and Verisk ($VRSK) rose 6.9%. Iron Mountain ($IRM) and Ralph Lauren ($RL) beat estimates and raised their full-year outlooks, yet fell 11.1% and 7.4% because their forecasts for the current quarter came in light.

## Iron Mountain ($IRM): a beat, a raise, and its worst day in a year

The storage and data-centre landlord earned **$1.53 a share** of adjusted funds from operations (AFFO, the cash measure REITs are judged on), up from $1.32 and 4% above the Zacks consensus, Zacks and GuruFocus reported. Data-centre revenue grew 34% to $274 million, and the company raised its 2026 AFFO-per-share forecast to $5.94 to $6.00, from $5.87 to $5.93, and its dividend by 15%. But it guided fourth-quarter revenue to about $2.03 billion, below the roughly $2.05 billion analysts expected, Yahoo Finance and Investing.com reported. The stock fell 11.1% to $105.50, its biggest one-day move in a year, on more than three times its usual volume.

## DTE Energy ($DTE): a $3 billion sale and a bigger spending plan

The Michigan utility earned **$2.30 a share** on an operating basis, above the $2.10 Zacks consensus, Zacks and Alphastreet reported, and said that puts it at the high end of its reaffirmed 2026 range of $7.59 to $7.73. The bigger news was strategic: DTE agreed to sell most of DTE Vantage, its non-utility energy business, for about $3 billion to investors advised by J.P. Morgan's infrastructure group, closing in the first half of 2027. It lifted its five-year capital plan by nearly $10 billion, to about $44 billion, and said the sale cuts the new stock it needs to issue. The shares fell 1.9%, less than half the market's drop.

## Verisk Analytics ($VRSK): organic growth of 7.4%

The insurance-data company earned an adjusted **$1.96 a share**, up 14% from $1.72 and above the $1.92 FactSet consensus, on revenue of $809 million, up 5.3% and about in line, the release and GuruFocus reported. Organic constant-currency revenue, which strips out deals and exchange rates, grew 7.4%, with subscriptions up 8.2% and transactional revenue up 3.2%. Verisk held its 2026 outlook of $7.45 to $7.75 of adjusted EPS and warned that the quarter's stronger transactional revenue won't repeat in the fourth. The stock rose 6.9%, its biggest gain since May 2023, then gave back 4.0% on Friday as of 2:15 p.m. ET.

## Global Payments ($GPN): growth mostly bought with Worldpay

We couldn't find a consensus from a named provider, so here is the quarter against last year. Adjusted EPS rose 10% to **$3.58**, on adjusted net revenue of $3.18 billion, the release said; reported growth of about 31% mostly reflects the Worldpay purchase, and on a normalized basis, counting Worldpay's earlier results, it grew a little over 4%. The company reaffirmed its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast of $13.60 to $13.80 and raised its Worldpay cost-savings target to $650 million a year. The stock slipped 1.2% on Thursday and was up 2.7% on Friday as of 2:15 p.m. ET.

## Tapestry ($TPR): Coach carries the quarter

The owner of Coach and Kate Spade earned an adjusted **$1.82 a share** in its fiscal first quarter, against the $1.57 LSEG consensus, on revenue up 11% to $1.89 billion against $1.86 billion expected, Reuters and Trefis reported. Coach grew 16%, with international sales up 30%, while Kate Spade fell 10%. Tapestry raised its fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS forecast to $8.25 to $8.35 and its revenue forecast to $8.5 billion to $8.6 billion, from $8.4 billion to $8.5 billion, and lifted its planned buybacks by $350 million to $1.7 billion. The stock rose 7.1% to $134.17, still 18.6% below its August closing high.

## Ralph Lauren ($RL): beat and raise, then the next quarter

Ralph Lauren's fiscal second-quarter revenue rose 9% to $2.19 billion against the $2.12 billion LSEG consensus, and adjusted EPS of **$4.64** beat the $4.26 expected, Reuters reported in Business of Fashion and RTE. It raised its fiscal 2027 revenue growth forecast to 6% to 7%, from 5% to 6%. But it guided third-quarter revenue growth to about 3% to 4%, below the 4.2% analysts wanted, citing the timing of wholesale shipments and fewer off-price sales. The stock fell 7.4% to $381.78, from a Wednesday close just short of its 52-week high, its biggest drop since April 2025, and slipped another 1.5% on Friday as of 2:15 p.m. ET.

## Viatris ($VTRS): supply problems, a higher profit range

Without a consensus we could confirm from a named provider, this is the quarter against last year. The generic-drug maker earned an adjusted **$0.71 a share**, up 6%, on revenue of $3.75 billion, the release said, after supply constraints cost it about $100 million of sales; it expects a similar hit in the fourth quarter. It raised its 2026 adjusted EPS range to $2.56 to $2.63, from $2.45 to $2.59, excluding its pending $1.65 billion purchase of Pacira BioSciences. The stock fell 1.3% on Thursday from Wednesday's 52-week closing high, and was up 3.6% on Friday as of 2:15 p.m. ET.

## What they say together

On a day the market sold off hard, a beat and a higher full-year number were not enough: Iron Mountain and Ralph Lauren had both, and both fell far more than the [S&P 500's 4.4% drop](/news/2026-11-05/stock-market-today-nasdaq-sinks-5-5-into-a-correction-on-midterm-ai-fears/), because each guided next-quarter revenue below what analysts expected. Tapestry rose because its beat was big, about 16% on EPS, and its raise was large. The utility, the payments company and the drugmaker, with steadier quarters and no forecast surprise, fell less than half as much as the market. The next test for the two fashion houses is the holiday quarter, which Ralph Lauren has already told investors to expect to be slower.
