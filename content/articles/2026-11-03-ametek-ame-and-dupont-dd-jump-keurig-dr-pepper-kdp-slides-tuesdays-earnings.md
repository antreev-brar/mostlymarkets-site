Six more big companies reported before the open on Tuesday, and the market sorted them by one question: did the full-year forecast go up? Ametek ($AME) and DuPont ($DD) raised theirs and **rose 4.6% and 4.7%** in pre-market trading, while Keurig Dr Pepper ($KDP) beat estimates but fell 4.2% on its coffee margins, and Apollo Global Management ($APO) missed.

## Ametek ($AME): a record quarter and a higher forecast

The maker of electronic instruments and motors earned an adjusted **$2.21 a share**, up 17% and ahead of the roughly $2.11 analysts expected, on sales up 17% to $2.22 billion against about $2.12 billion, Alphastreet and Newsquawk reported. The adjusted operating margin widened 0.6 points to 26.8%. Ametek raised its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast to $8.53 to $8.57, from $8.20 to $8.30, above the $8.38 consensus, crediting the quarter and its purchase of Indicor's instrumentation business. Shares were up 4.6% at $253.00 as of 9:27 a.m. ET.

## DuPont ($DD): a 12% profit beat and a higher forecast

DuPont earned an adjusted **$2.09 a share**, up 62% from $1.29 and well above the $1.87 LSEG consensus, the release and Reuters reported. Organic sales rose 5%, led by health care, water and aerospace, and lower interest costs and a smaller share count lifted EPS further. It raised its 2026 adjusted EPS range by 25 cents at the midpoint to $7.47 to $7.52, guided the fourth quarter to $1.90 to $1.95, and plans $250 million of buybacks this quarter. Shares were up 4.7% at $148.00 at 9:29 a.m. ET.

## Waters ($WAT): its first big test since the BD deal

The lab-instrument maker's revenue of $1.77 billion, swollen by the Biosciences and Diagnostics businesses it took over from Becton Dickinson, was about in line, and adjusted EPS **rose 21% to $4.10**, slightly above estimates, StockStory and Chartmill reported. The acquired businesses grew just 1%, but Waters raised every part of its 2026 guidance and agreed to buy MOBILion Systems. Shares were up 3.4% at $465.00 at 8:48 a.m. ET.

## Yum Brands ($YUM): a beat with a Taco Bell problem

Yum earned an adjusted **$1.56 a share** against the $1.49 LSEG consensus, CNBC reported, on revenue of $2.05 billion, in line. But Taco Bell's US same-store sales fell 4% against the 0.9% decline expected, after a cyclosporiasis outbreak cut weekly traffic by as much as 20% in early July, CNBC and Quartz reported. KFC's grew 1% against 2.1% expected. Shares were up 1.2% at $159.45 at 9:29 a.m. ET.

## Apollo ($APO): a miss on the headline measure

The private-markets firm's adjusted net income came to **$2.07 a share**, short of the roughly $2.19 analysts expected, Newsquawk and GuruFocus reported; excluding one-off items it was $2.15, against $2.14 a year earlier, Reuters said. Inflows of $53 billion and record loan origination of $114 billion took assets to about $1.07 trillion, but mark-to-market losses left a GAAP loss of $1.06 billion. Shares were down 2.1% at $117.00 at 8:48 a.m. ET.

## Keurig Dr Pepper ($KDP): sodas strong, coffee squeezed

Sales **rose 70.6% to $7.34 billion**, mostly from the JDE Peet's coffee business it bought, and adjusted EPS of $0.66 beat estimates, GuruFocus and Investing.com reported. The US soft-drink business was strong, Seeking Alpha said, but the gross margin fell 5.9 points to 48.7% on the lower-margin JDE Peet's mix, US coffee prices fell 4.2%, and management trimmed its expectations for JDE Peet's on the call. Shares were down 4.2% at $35.29 at 9:30 a.m. ET, from Monday's close at a 52-week high of $36.82.

## What they say together

The pattern is a market paying for forecasts, not quarters: all six met or beat on profit except Apollo, but only the companies that raised their full-year numbers by a clear margin, Ametek and DuPont, got a big lift. Keurig Dr Pepper is the counterexample: a beat with a weaker margin and a lower view of its biggest acquisition was worth less than the stock's run into Monday's close.
