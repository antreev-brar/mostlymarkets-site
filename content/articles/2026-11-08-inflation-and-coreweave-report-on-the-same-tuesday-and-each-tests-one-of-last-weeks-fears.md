Last week the market found two things to fear. On Thursday the Nasdaq Composite **sank 5.5%** on bets that Democrats' midterm sweep would slow the building of AI data centers, and on Friday a strong jobs report put a Federal Reserve rate hike in December back in play. The Nasdaq ended the week down 3.4%, its fourth weekly loss in a row ([our weekend edition](/daily/2026-11-07/)).

This week both fears get a test, on the same day. At 8:30 a.m. ET on Tuesday the government publishes October's consumer prices, and after the close CoreWeave ($CRWV), the AI cloud renter that has lost half its value in a month, reports its third quarter. Thursday brings the chip-equipment and networking test, with Applied Materials ($AMAT) and Cisco ($CSCO).

## The week in seven takes

### Inflation and CoreWeave report on the same Tuesday

Economists in Bloomberg's survey expect consumer prices to have risen **0.3%** in October, after a 0.6% jump in September that was the largest since April, Bloomberg and Morningstar reported. A cooler reading would ease the rate worry before the Fed's 8-9 December meeting; a hot one would land on top of a jobs report that already pushed hike odds to about 80% ([our Daly brief](/news/2026-11-07/feds-daly-says-more-rate-hikes-are-likely-needed-despite-a-painful-tech-sell-off/)).

### October's inflation report will have no yearly rate

```stat
[{"value": "0.6%", "label": "September CPI, monthly", "note": "The largest rise since April", "tone": "down"},
 {"value": "0.2%", "label": "October core CPI, expected", "note": "Bloomberg survey; smallest in three months"},
 {"value": "None", "label": "12-month rate for October", "note": "October 2025 prices were never collected"}]
```

The government shutdown of 2025 meant the Bureau of Labor Statistics never collected most October 2025 prices and cancelled that month's report, Bloomberg and Al Jazeera reported at the time. With no base a year back, Tuesday's report will carry **no 12-month rate** for overall or core prices, StockTitan and FourWeekMBA reported, so the monthly change has to carry the whole read. Core prices, which leave out food and energy, are expected to rise 0.2%, Bloomberg's survey found.

### CoreWeave reports after halving in a month


CoreWeave **fell 49.4%** between 6 October and Friday, and Nebius ($NBIS) 48.2%, as Microsoft ($MSFT) reviewed the outside AI capacity it rents, while Microsoft's own stock was flat. The yield on CoreWeave's 2032 bond rose above 16% on Friday, CNBC reported. The number to watch is its backlog of signed contracts: $104 billion at the end of June, and anything less, or a change to a Microsoft deal, would back the bears ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-07/wall-street-is-betting-the-neoclouds-are-ais-weak-link-coreweaves-report-will-test-it/)).

### Applied Materials reports 37% below where it was a month ago

Applied Materials, which makes the machines that build chips, has **fallen 37.3%** in a month and Lam Research ($LRCX) 40.0%, while Cisco, the networking company, is down 20.4%, in our data. Both report after the close on Thursday, and Applied's orders are the first read on whether chipmakers plan to spend less on new factory capacity next year.

### Nvidia trades on Monday for the first time since the FT memo

On Saturday the Financial Times reported that a US government memo describes Nvidia ($NVDA) Chief Executive Jensen Huang as romantically involved with an executive whom US intelligence suspects of spying for China; Nvidia declined to comment on the memo ([our brief](/news/2026-11-07/ft-says-a-us-memo-ties-nvidia-nvda-ceo-to-a-suspected-chinese-spy-days-before-a-house-briefing/)). House committee chairs have asked Huang to brief them on **18 November**, the day after Nvidia reports. The stock fell 9.3% on Thursday alone and closed Friday at $186.64.

### The power producers that broke first report on Monday


Constellation Energy ($CEG) and NRG Energy ($NRG) report before Monday's open, five days after the Texas governor's race raised the risk of a pause on new data-center permits. NRG **fell 13.5%** on Wednesday and is still 8.3% below its election-day close, even after a 9.6% gain on Friday, its fourth-biggest move of the past year, in our data. Vistra ($VST), which raised its outlook last week, is the bar they're measured against ([our Vistra recap](/news/2026-11-06/vistra-vst-jumps-as-q3-profit-rises-50-and-it-raises-its-2026-outlook/)).

On Saturday, Berkshire Hathaway ($BRK.B) said it spent **$9.6 billion** on its own shares in the third quarter, more than twice the second quarter, while operating earnings fell 5.8% as insurance underwriting profit dropped, CNBC and the Wall Street Journal reported ([our recap](/news/2026-11-07/berkshire-hathaway-brk-b-q3-profit-falls-6-as-buybacks-hit-a-record-9-6-billion/)). We think the buyback is the weekend's clearest signal: Berkshire says it buys its own stock only below what it judges the business is worth, and it did so at a record pace with the shares 1.4% below their record close.
