On Friday, 13 November, Nvidia ($NVDA) closed at $162.85, its sixth straight weekly loss and its lowest close in a year, a day after Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive. This week it reports for the first time without him, on Tuesday after the close, and on Wednesday Anthropic plans to price the biggest AI share sale yet. Between them sit October's retail sales and the Fed's minutes, with the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.14%.

The rest of the calendar belongs to the shops. Home Depot ($HD), Lowe's ($LOW), Target ($TGT), TJX ($TJX) and Walmart ($WMT) all report, after a month in which investors bought home-improvement stocks even as existing-home sales fell to their slowest pace since 2010.

## Monday, 16 November: Warsh speaks

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh gives opening remarks at the San Francisco Fed at noon ET, Admiral Markets and Newsquawk reported, two days before the minutes of the last meeting. Baidu ($BIDU), down 40.2% this year in our data, and Tyson Foods ($TSN) report before the open.

## Tuesday, 17 November: retail sales, Home Depot and Nvidia

October retail sales come out at **8:30 a.m. ET**, Newsquawk and Global View reported. Home Depot reports before the open, with analysts expecting adjusted EPS of about $3.86 on revenue of $42.7 billion, according to Benzinga, close to the $3.87 Nasdaq's data shows. Home Depot rose 18.6% between 13 October and Friday in our data even as home sales sank ([our story](/news/2026-11-13/home-sales-fell-to-3-75-million-in-october-the-slowest-pace-since-2010/)). Disney ($DIS) and JD.com ($JD) also report before the open.

After the close comes Nvidia. Analysts polled by LSEG expect revenue of about **$109 billion** for the quarter that ended on 25 October, Invezz and BigGo Finance reported, a little above the company's own $108 billion forecast. Interim Chief Executive Colette Kress presents; the number that matters is the fourth-quarter forecast, the test of last week's bet that customers are moving orders to AMD ($AMD) and Broadcom ($AVGO) ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-13/wall-street-is-betting-nvidias-loss-is-amd-and-broadcoms-gain-tuesday-will-test-it/), [our bulls and bears](/news/2026-11-13/bulls-vs-bears-on-nvidia-nvda-is-13-times-earnings-cheap-without-jensen-huang/)).

## Wednesday, 18 November: Lowe's, Target, the minutes and Anthropic

Lowe's, Target and TJX report before the open. Lowe's soared 20.7% between 13 October and Friday in our data, the most of the week's big reporters; Target is up 65.2% this year and TJX is 20.2% below its high, so the three go in with very different bars.

At **2 p.m. ET** the Fed publishes the minutes of its 27-28 October meeting, the Fed's calendar shows; they will show how many officials already wanted to raise rates ([our narrative on the hikes](/news/2026-11-14/wall-street-is-betting-stocks-can-ride-out-more-fed-hikes-3-numbers-will-decide-it/)). Palo Alto Networks ($PANW), down 18.8% since 13 October, reports after the close.

The same evening Anthropic plans to price 825 million shares at $70 to $78 each, raising up to about $64 billion at a value of up to $1.4 trillion, CNBC and The Next Web reported ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-15/wall-street-is-betting-anthropics-ipo-can-rescue-the-ai-trade-3-numbers-will-decide-it/)). Pricing at or above the top of the range would say investors still pay up for AI growth after the sell-off.

## Thursday, 19 November: Walmart, Alibaba and Trump-Xi

Walmart reports before the open, with analysts expecting adjusted EPS of about $0.64 against $0.62 a year earlier, according to Fiscal.ai estimates cited by Yahoo Finance, at 46 times trailing earnings ([our breakdown](/news/2026-11-14/walmart-wmt-reports-thursday-at-46-times-earnings-is-the-business-worth-it/)). Alibaba ($BABA), 46.7% below its high, and PDD ($PDD) report the same morning, as President Trump and President Xi Jinping meet in Shenzhen at the APEC summit, Voice of America and CNBC reported ([our Alibaba story](/news/2026-11-13/alibaba-baba-shares-slide-on-fears-over-its-access-to-nvidia-chips/)).

Anthropic's shares are due to start trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, CNBC reported, and weekly jobless claims come out at 8:30 a.m. ET. After the close, Intuit ($INTU), down 48.4% this year, reports with Ross Stores ($ROST) and Keysight ($KEYS).

## Friday, 20 November: a quiet close

No big company reports, so the week's verdict will be in by Thursday night: Walmart on the consumer, Alibaba and the Trump-Xi meeting on China, and Anthropic's first day of trading on AI.

## What we'll be watching

The order matters again. Retail sales and Home Depot on Tuesday morning say whether the consumer can carry stocks while rates rise; Nvidia that evening says whether AI spending can. If Nvidia's forecast holds and Anthropic prices at the top of its range on Wednesday, last week's rotation from chips into shops and Berkshire Hathaway ($BRK.B) has its first reason to reverse ([our Berkshire breakdown](/news/2026-11-15/berkshire-hathaway-brk-b-is-up-19-since-october-is-the-business-worth-the-price/)).
