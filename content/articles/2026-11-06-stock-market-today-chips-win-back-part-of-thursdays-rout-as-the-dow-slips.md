Chip stocks won back part of Thursday's rout on Friday, but the wider market gave up most of its early gain. AMD ($AMD) **rose 11.0%** and the Nasdaq Composite 1.1%, while the S&P 500 closed up just 0.3% at 7,376.86 after being up 1.2% at its high, and the Dow fell 0.5% to 51,285.73. A strong October jobs report pushed Treasury yields up and kept a December Fed rate hike in play.

## Chips took back part of the rout

Investors bought back the AI hardware stocks that fell hardest on Thursday, when the Philadelphia Semiconductor index had its worst day since March 2020 on fears that the new Democratic Congress will slow data-center building. Intel ($INTC) **rose 7.7%**, Micron ($MU) 5.1% and Nvidia ($NVDA) 2.8% at the close, and Reuters and TheStreet both put the chipmakers at the head of the rebound. The bounce was smaller than the drop: AMD and Micron had each lost more than 17% on Thursday, and AMD is still 27.7% lower than a month ago in our data. Our [morning chip story](/news/2026-11-06/amd-micron-and-lam-research-shares-jump-what-happened/) has the detail.

The VIX, Wall Street's fear gauge, fell 9.0% to 25.64, still about twice its 12-month low. The Nasdaq still ended the week lower, Reuters and Yahoo Finance reported, and Reuters counted it as the index's fourth weekly loss in a row.

## The jobs report lifted yields, and the rally faded

Employers added **165,000 jobs** in October against the 65,000 economists expected, and unemployment fell to 4.1%, CNBC and CNN reported ([our jobs story](/news/2026-11-06/employers-added-165-000-jobs-in-october-keeping-a-december-hike-in-play/)). Treasuries fell and the 10-year yield closed at 4.86%, up 0.02 point, as traders raised their bets on a rate hike at the Fed's 8-9 December meeting, Reuters and the Wall Street Journal reported. The two-year yield, which tracks Fed expectations more closely, rose 0.035 point to 4.51%.

The S&P 500 hit 7,444.20 in the morning, then spent the rest of the day giving it back. Microsoft ($MSFT) fell 1.0% on a day when Bloomberg reported it is winding down a 2023 deal to rent AI computing power from Oracle ($ORCL) and reviewing its other outside cloud contracts, which CNBC also reported; Oracle recovered to close up 0.8%. Our [narrative on the hike](/news/2026-11-06/why-wall-street-thinks-the-fed-will-hike-into-a-stock-sell-off/) sets out why rising rates and a falling Nasdaq can go together.

## Forecasts decided the earnings losers

The biggest falls in the S&P 500 came from companies whose outlooks disappointed. [Axon Enterprise ($AXON) plunged 18.2%](/news/2026-11-06/why-axon-enterprise-axon-stock-is-sinking-today/) after cutting its sales-growth forecast, [Atlassian ($TEAM) 16.2%](/news/2026-11-06/atlassian-team-beats-q1-estimates-but-sinks-as-it-forecasts-slower-subscription-growth/) on slower subscription growth and [Microchip ($MCHP) 10.0%](/news/2026-11-06/microchip-mchp-beats-q2-estimates-but-slides-on-a-softer-margin-forecast/) on a softer margin forecast. [DraftKings ($DKNG) sank 21.3%](/news/2026-11-06/draftkings-dkng-sinks-27-as-predictions-spending-guts-its-profit-outlook/) as spending on prediction markets cut its profit outlook.

On the other side, IonQ ($IONQ) **soared 23.4%** after the quantum-computing company raised its 2026 revenue forecast to $505 million to $515 million, from $450 million to $460 million, in its results release; CNBC listed it among the day's biggest movers. [Airbnb ($ABNB) jumped 11.7%](/news/2026-11-05/airbnb-abnb-jumps-after-hours-as-bookings-grow-21-and-it-raises-its-outlook/) after raising its outlook, and [Vistra ($VST) 10.6%](/news/2026-11-06/vistra-vst-jumps-as-q3-profit-rises-50-and-it-raises-its-2026-outlook/).

## What's next

October's consumer price index comes out on Tuesday, 10 November, the last big inflation reading the Fed sees before December. Our read: Friday's bounce shows buyers think Thursday's 12% chip drop overshot, but it fixed neither of the two worries behind it, the midterm hit to AI spending and a Fed that may hike. Nvidia's results on 17 November are the next real test of the first.
