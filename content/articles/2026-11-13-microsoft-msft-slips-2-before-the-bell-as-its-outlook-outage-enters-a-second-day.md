Microsoft ($MSFT) **fell 2.1%** to $511.93 before Friday's open as an outage that took down Outlook and other Microsoft 365 services ran into a second day, CNBC and Forbes reported. Microsoft said it was seeing early signs of stabilization, Reuters and ABC News reported, but capacity had not returned to normal by Friday morning, Forbes said.

The outage also hit Teams, SharePoint Online and the Defender XDR security product, with complaints on Downdetector peaking near 130,000 for Outlook on Thursday, Forbes reported. Microsoft has found no sign of malicious activity and has not given a final cause, Forbes said; ABC7 reported that a process handling secure connections on affected servers became overloaded and stopped processing requests.

Office software is the steady, subscription half of Microsoft's business, so the cost of a day's outage is mainly to trust with corporate customers rather than to this quarter's sales. The stock closed Thursday at $523.00, 5.9% below its 4 November record close. The next marker is Microsoft's post-incident report, which will say what failed and whether customers are owed service credits.
