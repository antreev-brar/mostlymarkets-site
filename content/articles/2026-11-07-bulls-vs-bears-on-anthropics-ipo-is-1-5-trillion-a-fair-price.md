Anthropic's IPO is about to test what public investors will pay for an AI company whose revenue rose 47% in a single quarter, and the answer moved this week. After Thursday's AI sell-off, three large investors who plan to buy shares told The Wall Street Journal the roughly $1.5 trillion valuation the company had been targeting will probably have to come down, one of them to nearer $1 trillion, Decrypt and Yahoo Finance reported. Its roadshow is expected to start in the week of 9 November, CNBC reported.

The number at the center is what that price says about sales. At $1.5 trillion, Anthropic would be valued at about **22 times** its third-quarter revenue of $16.96 billion at an annual rate, by our arithmetic from its amended filing; at $1 trillion, about 15 times. The arguments below are about whether growth can carry that multiple.

- **What's happening:** Anthropic's IPO roadshow is due next week, and investors are pushing its value down from about $1.5 trillion, about 22 times its third-quarter sales at an annual rate, toward $1 trillion after the AI sell-off.
- **Why it matters:** It is the biggest test yet of public money for an AI company that spends more on computing than it earns, and its compute bill is revenue for Amazon, Alphabet and the chipmakers.
- **Our read:** The bulls have the stronger case on the business and the bears on the price: at $1.5 trillion the valuation already assumes the best-case decade.

## The bull case

### Aswath Damodaran, finance professor, NYU Stern

**The business is worth about what the bankers hoped, but not more.** In a blog post on 28 October, Damodaran valued Anthropic's equity at about **$1.475 trillion**, assuming revenue reaches roughly $1 trillion a year by 2036 with a 27.5% operating margin, and counting its computing commitments as debt. His scenarios ran from a worthless equity to $4.04 trillion, and he said he would sit out an IPO priced well above his number, as FourWeekMBA and Invezz reported. He set out the call on X:

### Denny Fish, portfolio manager, Janus Henderson Investors

**This is a once-in-a-generation listing.** Fish told CNBC on 27 October that a decade from now this period will be seen as the biggest paradigm shift in technology, calling the IPO a "generational moment." His case rests on growth: revenue rose from $11.56 billion in the second quarter to $16.96 billion in the third, the filing shows.

## The bear case

### David Trainer, chief executive, New Constructs

**Anthropic is worth a tenth of the talk.** In a note in early October, Trainer's research firm put Anthropic's fair value at about **$150 billion** and called the listing the "most ridiculous IPO of 2026," CNBC and Benzinga reported. "We don't think Anthropic has a viable business," he told CNBC, citing rising operating losses and competition from open-source models. The firm made the same call on WeWork in 2019, which pulled its IPO, and on DoorDash in 2020, which has since grown to an $83 billion company, Benzinga noted.

### Roger McNamee, co-founder, Elevation Partners

**The prospectus is a story, not a forecast.** McNamee, an early investor in Google and Facebook, told CNBC on 27 October that the filing should be read as "speculative fiction" and called the IPO a "very dangerous thing for investors." The third quarter gives him a number: computing costs rose 78% in a quarter to $9.86 billion, turning a $974 million operating profit into a **$567 million loss**, the filing shows and Reuters reported ([our story](/news/2026-11-02/anthropics-ipo-filing-shows-17-billion-of-q3-sales-and-a-swing-to-an-operating-loss/)).

## Where they agree

Neither side disputes the growth or the cost. The amended filing puts Anthropic's computing and infrastructure commitments at about $895 billion, and growth from the prior quarter slowed to 47% in the third quarter from 144% in the second. Even the bulls price the company on 2036, not 2026.

Gil Luria, an analyst at D.A. Davidson, put the middle ground plainly to CNBC on 6 November: investors expect the deal to price at least at the low end of earlier talk, and "the best way to set Anthropic up for success is to price the deal well."

## The number that decides it

The price range comes first: Anthropic has to set it in a further filing before the roadshow, CNBC reported, and a range near $1 trillion would mean buyers won the argument before trading starts. After that, quarter-on-quarter revenue growth is the figure to watch, because Damodaran's base case needs revenue to rise about 15-fold from the third quarter's annual pace in about a decade, by our arithmetic.

## Our read

The evidence favors the bulls on the business and the bears on the price. Revenue up 47% in a quarter at a $68 billion annual pace is real, but the best-known valuation built on optimistic assumptions lands just under $1.5 trillion, so at that price buyers pay for the success case before it happens. Below about $1 trillion, about 15 times sales, the argument changes; the price range in the coming filing will tell us which side the bankers think is winning. For Amazon ($AMZN) and Alphabet ($GOOGL), both investors and both promised more than $100 billion of its computing spending through the 2030s ([our earlier brief](/news/2026-10-31/anthropics-100-billion-ipo-got-harder-to-price-in-the-ai-selloff/)), a weak debut would hit the story behind their AI spending as much as the stake.
