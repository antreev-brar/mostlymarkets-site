Nvidia ($NVDA) has told manufacturing partners to stop shipping servers built with its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, the Financial Times reported on Thursday, citing three people familiar with the matter, according to Reuters. Partners including Dell ($DELL) and Supermicro ($SMCI) told buyers in the four countries that Nvidia would not let their orders ship.

The halt follows a Commerce Department rule, issued on 16 November and in force last week, that requires an export license to ship advanced chips to those countries and Brazil. It aims to stop Chinese firms reaching Nvidia hardware through data centers outside China, a route at the center of the [Megaspeed affair](/news/2026-11-23/us-blacklists-alibabas-baba-singapore-unit-and-megaspeed-over-chip-diversion-risk/) that briefly cost Jensen Huang his job. Until a license is granted, Nvidia-based servers bound for the four countries sit in the queue.

It isn't clear whether the halt is temporary while Nvidia and its partners work out how to comply. US markets are closed for Thanksgiving; the first trading read is Friday's half session, which ends at 1 p.m. ET.
