On Thursday 29 October ServiceNow ($NOW), whose software runs IT, HR and customer-service work inside big companies, had its worst day on record after forecasting slower growth in contracted revenue. It fell again on Friday and ended the week **down 33.9%**, by our price files, the second-biggest weekly loss among the stocks we track, after Reddit's.

The question splitting Wall Street is whether that was a one-quarter timing problem or the first hard evidence for the "SaaSpocalypse": the fear that AI agents let companies do the same work with fewer employees, and so buy fewer of the per-person licenses, or seats, that business software is sold by. The number both sides argue over is cRPO, the contracted revenue ServiceNow expects to book over the next 12 months: it grew 21% in the third quarter, adjusting for currency, and the company guided to **18.5%** for the fourth.

The damage was uneven: Microsoft ($MSFT) and Intuit ($INTU) were nearly flat, while Salesforce ($CRM) lost 7.8%. Fortinet ($FTNT) and F5 ($FFIV) fell on their own weak forecasts, so the selloff wasn't one story.

## The bull case

### Thomas Blakey, analyst, Cantor Fitzgerald

**The selloff is an AI-driven buying opportunity.** In a note on 29 October, Blakey kept his overweight rating and $201 price target, pointing to seat growth, AI demand and government business, StreetInsider and StockTwits reported. His target sits nearly twice Friday's close.

### Billy Fitzsimmons, analyst, Piper Sandler

**ServiceNow beat and raised, and the stock sank anyway.** Fitzsimmons kept his overweight rating and $175 target after the results, TIKR and TradingView reported. TIKR calculated that target at about 37 times forward earnings, against a five-year average of 53 times: even the bull's number assumes a lower multiple than the stock used to carry.

### Bill McDermott, chief executive, ServiceNow

**The fourth quarter will be strong.** "ServiceNow always closes strong. We have no intention of delivering anything less than the best year in our history," McDermott said on the 28 October call, according to transcripts published by Investing.com and The Motley Fool. ServiceNow raised its target for new annual AI contract value in 2026 to $1.7 billion and raised its full-year subscription revenue forecast. Chief Financial Officer Gina Mastantuono said early renewals pulled business out of the fourth quarter into the third, which is why cRPO beat its own guidance by a percentage point last quarter and the next quarter looks slower.

## The bear case

### Jackson Ader, analyst, KeyBanc Capital Markets

**AI will shrink the seat count software is priced on.** Ader cut ServiceNow to underweight in December 2025, arguing that generative AI puts structural pressure on seat counts, Yahoo Finance and TipRanks reported. On 29 October KeyBanc kept the underweight rating and raised its target to $110 from $85, citing a rebound in software multiples, Investing.com and GuruFocus reported. That target is close to Friday's $105.12 close: in KeyBanc's view, the crash only brought the stock to fair value.

### UBS

**Next year's estimates are too high.** UBS kept its neutral rating and cut its target to $140 from $150 on 29 October, MarketBeat and TradingView reported. The analyst behind the note wasn't named in either report.

### Guggenheim Securities

**Not broken, but no longer exceptional.** Guggenheim called ServiceNow "a high-teens grower at scale that will likely face AI pressure over time," while keeping a buy rating and a $175 target, Investing.com and Rolling Out reported. It estimates organic subscription growth at about 17% to 18% once the Moveworks and Armis acquisitions are stripped out.

## Where they agree

No one in this debate says the business stopped growing: subscription revenue rose 21% last quarter, and no firm we found cut its rating after the results. Citi analyst Tyler Radke, who kept a buy rating while cutting his target to $157 from $170, told CNBC the quarter was "a little bit uncharacteristic for ServiceNow standards, just in terms of the lack of upside," and that investors would have to "wait until next year to really see that inflection" from AI. The disagreement is about the slope, not the direction: high-teens growth with AI pressure, or low-20s growth with AI help.

## The number that decides it

cRPO growth in the fourth quarter, reported in late January. If the CFO is right about timing, it should come in at or above the 18.5% guide and pick up again in early 2027 as renewals return to their usual timing. If seats are shrinking, it will keep sliding toward the mid-teens, and the AI contract value won't make up the gap.

## Our read

The evidence favors the bulls on the business today: subscription revenue grew 21%, cRPO beat its own forecast by a point, and ServiceNow raised its full-year outlook. The bears' seat-count thesis may prove right, but it isn't in the reported numbers yet. At 23.9 times expected earnings, below the 26.6x median of the 32 software companies we track and with a 30.8% free-cash-flow margin over 12 months, the stock is priced for a slowdown that has so far been a half-point miss on one forecast.

We said after Thursday's drop that we'd want cRPO growth near 18% before deciding whether this was timing or a real slowdown ([our story](/news/2026-10-29/servicenow-now-stock-has-its-worst-day-on-record-after-soft-forecast/)). That's still the test, and it comes in late January.
