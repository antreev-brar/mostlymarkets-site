Wall Street split in two on Tuesday as the chip stocks that led Monday's record rally gave a large part of it back. The Nasdaq Composite **fell 1.5%** to 23,733.54 and the S&P 500 slipped 0.5% to 7,201.09, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 423 points, or 0.9%, to 50,018.70, the Associated Press and CNBC reported. Nvidia ($NVDA) fell 5.9% a day after its best session since 2003, and Analog Devices ($ADI) had the worst day of the big stocks we track after a flat forecast.

## Nvidia gave back a third of Monday's jump

Nvidia **fell 5.9% to $155.38**, about a third of Monday's 31.5% gain on [Jensen Huang's return](/news/2026-11-23/nvidia-nvda-shares-soar-29-before-the-open-after-it-reinstates-jensen-huang/), and it was down about 9% at midday, the Motley Fool reported. Memory and connectivity suppliers fell harder: Micron ($MU) dropped 11.0%, Astera Labs ($ALAB) 13.9%, and Credo ($CRDO) 13.1%. We covered the slide in [Nvidia and Micron](/news/2026-11-24/nvidia-and-micron-shares-slide-what-happened/), [AMD, Credo and Monolithic Power](/news/2026-11-24/amd-credo-and-monolithic-power-shares-slide-what-happened/), and the [storage makers](/news/2026-11-24/sandisk-seagate-and-western-digital-shares-slide-what-happened/).

Huang's return calmed the leadership panic, but the open questions over Nvidia's books and AI-cloud financing still stand, as we laid out in [what the evidence says](/news/2026-11-23/nvidias-crisis-has-moved-from-its-boss-to-its-books-what-the-evidence-says/). Anthropic also closed below its [$83 IPO price](/news/2026-11-24/anthropic-falls-below-its-83-ipo-price-for-the-first-time/) for the first time, in its fourth session as a public company, CNBC reported.

## Two warnings hit the AI hardware chain

Analog Devices **plunged 17.4%**, its biggest drop of the past year by our price data, even though fiscal fourth-quarter revenue rose 42% to $4.37 billion. Its forecast for next quarter implied almost no growth, as we covered in [the recap](/news/2026-11-24/analog-devices-adi-beats-q4-estimates-but-a-flat-q1-forecast-sends-shares-down-8/) and took apart in [our breakdown](/news/2026-11-24/analog-devices-adi-is-30-below-its-high-after-a-flat-forecast-is-the-business-broken/). Its peers went with it: Texas Instruments ($TXN) fell 11.3% and ON Semiconductor ($ON) 7.9%.

Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE) fell 16.3% after its finance chief said margins in its AI server unit will drop to about 14% next fiscal year from 18.4%, outweighing a beat and a higher outlook ([our story](/news/2026-11-24/hpe-hpe-slides-11-5-before-the-open-as-it-sees-ai-server-margins-falling-to-14/)). HPE also said it won't finance neocloud customers, which hit [CoreWeave and Nebius](/news/2026-11-24/coreweave-and-nebius-shares-slide-what-happened/). Dell ($DELL) fell 11.8% into its own results tonight.

## The Dow's 28 winners

The selling stayed inside the AI supply chain: Tradingnews counted 28 of the Dow's 30 members higher, and BusinessToday named Amgen ($AMGN), Nike ($NKE) and Salesforce ($CRM) as the biggest lifts. Amgen **rose 5.5%**, Nike 5.9% and Salesforce 4.2% by our price data, and Merck ($MRK) added 4.5%. Because the Dow weights members by share price rather than market value, a big move in a $474 stock such as Amgen counts for far more than one in Nvidia.

Shoppers' stocks had their own good news. Dollar Tree ($DLTR) soared 11.2% after it beat estimates on more store traffic and raised its profit forecast ([our recap](/news/2026-11-24/dollar-tree-dltr-beats-q3-estimates-as-traffic-rises-and-raises-its-profit-forecast/)), and Best Buy ($BBY) rose 2.7% after its [best comparable sales in five years](/news/2026-11-24/best-buy-bby-beats-q3-estimates-with-its-best-comparable-sales-in-five-years/). Pinterest ($PINS) jumped 9.5% on a filing traders read as a hint of [a sale](/news/2026-11-24/why-pinterest-pins-stock-is-soaring-today/).

## What's next

Dell, Autodesk ($ADSK) and Workday ($WDAY) reported after the close. As of 4:15 p.m. ET, Dell was down 2.1% after hours, Autodesk 5.0% and Workday 4.9%; our recaps follow tonight. Gold fell 2.8% to $3,550.20 an ounce and bitcoin 2.4%, while WTI crude rose 1.5% to $81.40 ahead of the OPEC+ meeting on 29 November. The market is shut on Thursday for Thanksgiving and closes early on Friday.
