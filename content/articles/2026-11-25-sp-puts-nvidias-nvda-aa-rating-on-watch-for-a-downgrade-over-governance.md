S&P Global Ratings placed Nvidia's ($NVDA) **AA** credit rating on CreditWatch Negative on Wednesday, signaling a high likelihood of a downgrade, Reuters and ROIC.ai reported. S&P said Chief Executive Jensen Huang's departure and reinstatement did not sufficiently answer its questions about the board's oversight of the executive code of conduct.

It is the second agency in a week to flag governance: Moody's kept Nvidia's Aa1 rating on 18 November but cut its outlook to stable from positive, citing the abrupt exit and the lack of a formal succession plan. A one-notch cut would change little for a company whose net debt is about a tenth of a year's operating profit, by our analysis of its filings; what matters is that the ratings agencies, like shareholders, now treat Nvidia's leadership as a risk to price, the thread running through [our coverage of Huang's return](/news/2026-11-24/nvidias-nvda-huang-tells-staff-he-broke-its-code-of-conduct-before-december-hearings/).

Nvidia was **down 5.1%** at 2:15 p.m. ET, leading a broad AI sell-off. CreditWatch placements are usually resolved within about 90 days, and Huang faces lawmakers from 1 December.
