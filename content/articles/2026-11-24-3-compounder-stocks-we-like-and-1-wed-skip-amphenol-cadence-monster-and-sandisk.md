On Tuesday the AI hardware trade gave back much of Monday's rally: Analog Devices fell 17.4% on a flat forecast and Sandisk 11.1%, while the Dow rose 0.9% ([our wrap](/news/2026-11-24/stock-market-today-chip-stocks-give-back-mondays-rally-while-the-dow-closes-above-50000/)). Days like this separate companies that grow every year from companies riding one boom. This list looks for the first kind.

Our screen ranked companies by revenue growth over the 12 months to their latest quarter, from SEC filings, among those with an operating margin above 20%. We then checked whether that growth held up over five years, against each company's peers, and what the price assumes. All figures are from our SEC filing data unless we say otherwise.

```picks
like: APH, CDNS, MNST
skip: SNDK
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Amphenol ($APH)
**Revenue growth: 57.6% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Amphenol makes the connectors, cables and sensors that link everything from AI server racks to cars and jets.

1. Sales grew **21.8% a year over five years**, almost four times the 5.6% median for hardware makers, so the AI surge sits on top of a long record rather than replacing it.
2. Its operating margin of 27.7% is up 6.4 points in five years, against an 18.2% peer median, and EPS has compounded 27.8% a year, twice the peer pace.
3. Third-quarter revenue rose 65.1% to $10.23 billion, and the stock still fell 5.1% on Tuesday with the rest of AI hardware.

**The price:** 21.1 times forward earnings, above the 16.5 peer median, but its trailing P/E of 32.0 is well under its own two-year median of 42.4.
**What would change our mind:** quarterly growth falling below its five-year pace of about 22%, the sign that AI orders are fading rather than adding.

### 2. Cadence Design Systems ($CDNS)
**Revenue growth: 17.6% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Cadence sells the software every chip designer uses to lay out and test a chip before it is made.

1. Growth is speeding up, not slowing: revenue rose 22.0% in the September quarter, against 14.6% a year over five years.
2. It turns 28.6% of sales into free cash over two years, above the 27.7% software median, and earns a 17.7% return on capital, almost twice the 9.7% median.
3. Its customers pay for design tools whether a chip sells well or not, so its sales don't swing with chip prices.

**The price:** 31.4 times forward earnings, above the 23.7 software median, but its trailing P/E of 59.0 sits near the bottom of its two-year range of 54.5 to 100.9.
**What would change our mind:** a quarter of growth under its 14.6% five-year pace, which would mean chip designers are cutting projects.

### 3. Monster Beverage ($MNST)
**Revenue growth: 20.2% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Monster sells energy drinks around the world.

1. Sales grew **12.5% a year over five years**, almost three times the 4.4% median for consumer staples, and 16.7% in the latest quarter.
2. It keeps 55.5 cents of each sales dollar after direct costs, against 38.7 cents for its peers, and turns 22.2% of sales into free cash, more than twice the median.
3. It is one of the few staples growing faster now than over five years.

**The price:** 33.5 times forward earnings, nearly double the 17.9 staples median, though in line with its own two-year median trailing P/E of 40.3 (now 38.0).
**What would change our mind:** the operating margin, already down 6.3 points over five years to 28.5%, slipping further, which would mean it is buying growth with discounts.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Sandisk ($SNDK)
**Revenue growth: 272.4% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Sandisk makes flash memory chips and drives for phones, PCs and data centers.

1. The growth is a price spike, not compounding: annual revenue rose only from $6.09 billion to $7.36 billion between fiscal 2023 and 2025, about 10% a year, before jumping to $20.25 billion in fiscal 2026 as AI demand pushed flash prices up.
2. An operating margin of 72.5%, against an 18.2% peer median, is a margin no hardware maker keeps through a cycle.
3. The stock has moved 5% or more on 118 of the last 252 sessions and fell 11.1% on Tuesday, as we covered in [the storage slide](/news/2026-11-24/sandisk-seagate-and-western-digital-shares-slide-what-happened/).

**The price:** 2.7 times forward earnings, a sixth of the peer median, which tells us the market already treats these as peak earnings.
**What could go wrong:** flash prices could stay high through 2027 if AI data centers keep buying, in which case 2.7 times earnings would prove too cheap. Its [fiscal first-quarter recap](/news/2026-10-30/sandisk-sndk-tops-fiscal-q1-estimates-as-data-center-sales-jump-61/) shows how strong the current demand is.
