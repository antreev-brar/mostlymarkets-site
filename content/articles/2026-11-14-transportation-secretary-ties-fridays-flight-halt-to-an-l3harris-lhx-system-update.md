Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said on Saturday that Friday's nationwide ground stop began while contractor L3Harris ($LHX) was switching lines from copper wire to fiber at a remote FAA site, when a router "started flooding other routers in our air traffic control system," NPR and CNBC reported. The FAA has disabled the faulty function on the roughly **60 routers** of the same type in use nationwide, and officials said they saw no sign of a cyberattack.

The finding puts a defense and communications contractor at the center of a halt that stopped every US departure for hours on Friday evening and led to thousands more cancellations on Saturday, Yahoo News reported. Duffy said "we're going to hold people accountable" without saying how, and called on Congress to fund upgrades to the FAA's ageing network. L3Harris shares closed at $272.35 on Friday, before the comments; United Airlines ($UAL) and Delta ($DAL) had slipped after hours as [the stoppage spread](/news/2026-11-13/faa-grounds-every-us-flight-for-hours-and-airline-stocks-slide-after-hours/).

The FAA says it is still investigating whether an installation error or a faulty router caused the failure, and L3Harris had not commented by Saturday, NPR reported. Monday's open is the first test for both the contractor and the airlines.
