Four big companies reported in the past week into a market that is punishing anything tied to AI hardware: HP ($HPQ) and Workday ($WDAY) on Tuesday, Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE) on Monday and Deere ($DE) before the open on Wednesday. On Wednesday the Nasdaq Composite was down 3.5% by 10:30 a.m. ET, yet HP was up 11.1% and Deere 2.2%.

Our screen: companies worth $10 billion or more that reported in the last seven days and that we recapped. We judged each on the quarter, then on five years of SEC-filed numbers against industry peers, then on the price. Prices are as of 10:30 a.m. ET on Wednesday.

```picks
skip: HPQ, HPE, DE
like: WDAY
```

## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. HP ($HPQ)
**Fourth-quarter adjusted EPS of $0.83, of which $0.15 came from tariff refunds, reported 24 November.** HP sells personal computers and printers.

Revenue rose 9.3% to $16.0 billion as memory costs pushed up PC prices, but PC units fell 29% and the PC unit's operating margin was 3.0% ([our recap](/news/2026-11-24/hp-hpq-beats-q4-estimates-on-tariff-refunds-as-pc-units-fall-29/)). The stock was up 11.1% at $31.05 as investors read its fiscal 2027 forecast as a sign it is handling memory costs.

1. **It has not grown in five years:** revenue fell 0.5% a year over five fiscal years, in the bottom tenth of the 31 hardware makers we cover, where the median grew 5.6%.
2. **Thin margins:** gross margin averaged 20.4% over the last two years, against a peer median of 43.2%, so rising chip costs land hard on profit.
3. **Per-share growth is slow despite buybacks:** EPS grew 6.0% a year over five years, in the bottom quarter, even as the share count fell 6.4% in two years.

**The price:** 9.9 times forward earnings against a peer median of 15.1 times, and a trailing P/E of 10.7, close to its own 10.1 median since August 2024.

**What would change our mind:** PC units growing again without tariff refunds in the profit, which its next quarterly report will show.

### 2. Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE)
**Fourth-quarter revenue up 50% to $14.55 billion, reported 23 November.** HPE sells servers, storage and networking gear to companies and AI clouds.

Server sales nearly doubled, but finance chief Marie Myers said the Cloud & AI segment's operating margin will fall to about 14% in fiscal 2027 from 18.4% in the fourth quarter, Seeking Alpha and GuruFocus reported, and the stock [slid 11.5% before Tuesday's open](/news/2026-11-24/hpe-hpe-slides-11-5-before-the-open-as-it-sees-ai-server-margins-falling-to-14/). It was down 3.8% at $38.68.

1. **Weak returns on the money it uses:** return on capital of about 5.1%, in the bottom tenth of its peers, where the median is 16.5%.
2. **Growth is being bought, and diluted:** the share count rose 8.8% in two years, in the bottom quarter, while the operating margin over the last 12 months was 6.2% against an 18.2% median.

**The price:** 7.5 times forward earnings, in the cheapest tenth of its peers, with net debt of $14.0 billion, 5.4 years of operating profit.

**What would change our mind:** a Cloud & AI margin holding above 14% in the first half of fiscal 2027.

### 3. Deere ($DE)
**Fourth-quarter EPS of $3.89 against the $4.03 consensus cited by Investing.com, reported 25 November.** Deere makes tractors, combines and construction machines.

Sales of large farm machines fell 15%, and Deere forecast fiscal 2027 net income of $4.50 billion to $5.25 billion, short of the $5.91 billion LSEG consensus ([our recap](/news/2026-11-25/deere-de-misses-q4-estimates-as-big-farm-sales-fall-15-but-sees-a-2027-recovery/)). The stock was nonetheless up 2.2% at $627.49.

1. **Sales are shrinking:** revenue fell 7.4% a year over the last two years, in the bottom tenth of the 89 industrial stocks we cover, where the median grew 6.5%.
2. **The long run is middling:** 5.2% a year over five fiscal years, against an 8.0% median.

**The price:** 30.0 times forward earnings against an industrial median of 19.6 times, and a trailing P/E of 34.1 against its own 24.8 median since August 2024.

**What would change our mind:** a price nearer its usual multiple, or large-farm orders turning up in the spring.

## 1 stock we like

### Workday ($WDAY)
**Third-quarter adjusted EPS of $2.82 against $2.74 expected, reported 24 November.** Workday sells human-resources and finance software to large companies on subscription.

Revenue rose 11.2% to $2.705 billion and Workday raised its full-year subscription forecast to $9.958 billion, but guided to 10% subscription growth next quarter ([our recap](/news/2026-11-24/workday-wday-beats-q3-estimates-but-guides-subscription-growth-down-to-10/)). The stock fell after hours on that forecast, but was up 1.7% at $182.27 on Wednesday. Its AI products drove nearly $150 million of new annual contract value, more than three times a year earlier, it said in the release.

1. **Steady compounding:** revenue grew 17.2% a year over five fiscal years, above the 15.9% median of the 32 software companies we cover.
2. **It turns revenue into cash:** free cash flow averaged 28.3% of revenue over two years, in line with the software median, and its operating margin of 11.1% over the last 12 months beat the 9.7% median.
3. **It is shrinking its share count:** diluted shares fell 8.4% in two years, in the top quarter of its peers, where the median rose 0.3%.

**The price:** 14.5 times forward earnings against a software median of 23.1 times, and a trailing P/E of 36.2 against its own 59.9 median since December 2024.

**What could go wrong:** growth keeps slowing below 10% as AI agents cut the number of employee seats its customers pay for; its next quarterly report will show it.
