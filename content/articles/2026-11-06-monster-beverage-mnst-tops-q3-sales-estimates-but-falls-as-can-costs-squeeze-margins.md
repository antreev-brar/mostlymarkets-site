Monster Beverage ($MNST) reported record third-quarter net sales of $2.56 billion after the close on Thursday, up 16.7% from a year earlier and about 3% above the $2.48 billion Zacks consensus. Adjusted earnings of 30 cents a share only matched the Zacks estimate, as higher aluminum can and freight costs cut its gross margin. The shares fell 5.8% after hours on Thursday and were at **$46.13, down 3.6%**, as of 2:15 p.m. ET on Friday.

## What stood out

**Volume is doing the work.** Monster sold **18.0% more cases** of energy drinks than a year earlier, the release showed, but average net sales per case slipped 5 cents to $8.30. International sales grew 28.1%, and Latin America rose 48.8% "primarily driven by our operations in Brazil," Chief Executive Hilton Schlosberg said in the release. By our arithmetic from the release, US sales grew about 8%.

*Verdict: demand is strong everywhere, and fastest where each can earns less.*

**The margin went the other way.** Gross margin, the share of each sales dollar left after making and shipping the product in, fell to **54.7%** from 55.7%. The release blamed higher aluminum can costs, the mix of countries and higher freight, partly offset by price rises. On the call, Schlosberg said faster growth in lower-margin international markets was a big factor, and management expects aluminum costs to keep rising modestly each quarter through at least the first quarter of 2027, according to call summaries from Yahoo Finance and Defense World.

*Verdict: a cost squeeze with no clear end date yet.*

**Spending rose faster than sales.** Operating expenses climbed 24% as Monster spent more on freight, fuel and marketing, and they included a $19.9 million equipment write-down. Adjusted operating income rose only 7.1% on sales up 16.7%. That gap is why a sales beat turned into an earnings match, and why the shares fell, as Investing.com and Seeking Alpha both reported.

## The outlook

Monster gave no forecast, as usual, and declined to give margin guidance for the fourth quarter or 2027 on the call, citing aluminum, geographic mix, pricing and cost savings. It bought back about $100 million of stock in the quarter at an average of $43.87 a share and has $800 million left under its authorizations.

## Our take

We were impressed by the volume: 18% more cases on a base of more than $9 billion of yearly sales is rare in consumer staples. On the other hand, Monster is now growing fastest in markets that earn less per can while its can costs rise, and nobody on the call would say when that stops. Overall, a strong sales quarter with a weaker profit engine underneath.

The reaction was about the price paid for that growth. The stock had climbed 11.5% in the month before the report and closed Thursday 4.3% below its 52-week high, and at 37.7 times expected earnings it sells for more than twice the median consumer staples stock in our coverage, so a margin slip was hard to forgive. The long-run record still stands: by our analysis of SEC filings, sales grew 12.5% a year over five fiscal years, against a 4.6% median for 32 staples peers, and operating margin is about double the peer median, though it has fallen 6.3 points over five years. The next test is fourth-quarter results early next year, and the number that matters is whether gross margin holds above 54%.

On Thursday, defensive stocks such as [Kimberly-Clark](/news/2026-11-06/kimberly-clark-kmb-matches-q3-estimates-as-a-china-diaper-scare-cuts-sales/) held up while the [Nasdaq fell 5.5%](/news/2026-11-05/stock-market-today-nasdaq-sinks-5-5-into-a-correction-on-midterm-ai-fears/); Monster fell 1.9% in the regular session before its report.
