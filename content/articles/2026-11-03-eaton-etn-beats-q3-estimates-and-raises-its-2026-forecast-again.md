Eaton ($ETN), the maker of power-distribution gear for data centers, utilities and buildings, said before the open on Tuesday that third-quarter sales **rose 28% to a record $8.95 billion**, ahead of the roughly $8.5 billion analysts expected. Adjusted earnings per share of $3.68 beat the $3.54 Zacks consensus and Eaton raised its full-year profit forecast. The shares were **up 3.0% at $377.00** in pre-market trading as of 9:14 a.m. ET. Orders were the number [we flagged in our preview](/news/2026-11-02/what-to-expect-from-eatons-etn-q3-earnings/).

## What stood out

Organic growth, sales growth without acquisitions or currency moves, came in at **19%**, up from 14% in the second quarter, and above the top of the range Eaton gave in July, the release and Seeking Alpha reported. A faster pace in the third quarter of a boom is the opposite of what the market feared last week.

*Verdict: demand is still accelerating.*

Orders are the forward signal. Rolling 12-month organic orders rose 39% in Electrical Americas and 51% in Electrical Global, and the combined electrical backlog **grew 46%** from a year earlier, with record order dollars in all three segments. "Record orders grew sequentially, backlog continued to expand," Chief Executive Paulo Ruiz said in the release.

*Verdict: the order book covers next year's growth.*

The segment margin, profit from the businesses before corporate costs, reached a record 25.3%. Eaton is adding factories for data-center gear, and the margin shows the volume is outrunning those costs.

## The outlook

Eaton raised its 2026 organic growth forecast to 13% to 15%, from 11% to 13% in July, and its adjusted EPS range to $13.70 to $13.80 from $13.40 to $13.60, Reuters and dpa-AFX reported. It guided fourth-quarter adjusted EPS to $4.05 to $4.15.

## Our take

We were impressed by the acceleration: faster organic growth, a record margin and a bigger backlog in the same quarter. On the other hand, the shares had already fallen 16.1% in the past month to Monday, including **6.0% on 30 October** in the [AI-stock sell-off](/news/2026-10-30/stock-market-today-intels-worst-day-since-1974-sinks-the-nasdaq-2-9/), so the debate is less about this quarter than about how long data-center spending lasts. Barron's argued on Tuesday that solid results won't be enough to ease those AI concerns.

Overall, this quarter answers the worry with orders, not hope. Even after Tuesday's pre-market gain, the stock sits about 18% below its 12 August closing high of $459.96. The next test is the big cloud companies' spending plans for 2027, which they set out with their next results early in 2027.
