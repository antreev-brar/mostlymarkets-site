Vistra ($VST), the Texas-based power producer, said on Friday morning that third-quarter adjusted EBITDA from ongoing operations rose to $2.38 billion from $1.58 billion a year earlier, **up 50%**, and it raised and narrowed its 2026 forecast. Revenue of $6.35 billion beat the $6.02 billion analysts expected in LSEG's I/B/E/S survey, Reuters reported. The stock was **up 7.6%** at $121.47 as of 10:15 a.m. ET, after losing almost a fifth of its value on Wednesday and Thursday.

## What stood out

Prices did the work. Vistra got higher prices for the power it sold and for the capacity it keeps on standby, and it had a full quarter from the gas plants it bought from Lotus, and had Martin Lake Unit 1, a Texas coal unit, back in service, the release said. It also closed the purchase of Cogentrix, about 5,500 megawatts of gas-fired plants, in October.

## The outlook

The new 2027 range of **$7.7 billion to $8.5 billion** would be about 11% growth at its midpoint over the middle of the 2026 range. Vistra also guided 2027 adjusted free cash flow before growth spending to $4.3 billion to $5.1 billion.

## Our take

Vistra fell **13.8%** on Wednesday, when the result of the Texas governor's race raised the risk of a pause on new data-center permits, and another 6.2% on Thursday ([our narrative on the midterm AI trade](/news/2026-11-05/wall-street-is-betting-the-midterms-will-slow-the-ai-buildout-3-numbers-will-decide-it/)). Friday's report shows the business it already has is earning more, and the 2027 guide plus a bigger buyback is management saying so with money. The price no longer assumes much: at 13.7 times forward earnings, Vistra is cheaper than the 19.0 median for the 33 utilities in our data, against a trailing multiple of 19.1 that compares with a median of 30.6 since August 2024. The cost is debt, with net debt of $19.2 billion at the end of June, 5.4 years of operating profit. The open question is the upside, not the base: the next data-center supply deal it signs, or the first word from Austin on permits, will decide whether the stock earns back the $191 it closed at a year ago, its 52-week high.
