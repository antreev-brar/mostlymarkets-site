Intuit ($INTU) reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of $4.35 billion after the close on Thursday, up 12% year on year and above the roughly $4.31 billion analysts expected, Reuters and Investing.com reported. But it forecast second-quarter revenue of $4.93 billion to $4.95 billion, below the $5.11 billion Wall Street wanted, trimmed its full-year outlook and agreed to sell Mailchimp. The shares **sank 14.6% to $264.87** in after-hours trading as of 4:15 p.m. ET, after closing at $310.02.

## What stood out

The small-business side carried the quarter. Global Business Solutions, which holds QuickBooks, grew **15% to $3.1 billion**, and its Online Ecosystem of cloud products grew 20% to $2.4 billion, the release showed, as reported by Investing.com and Panabee. Consumer revenue, mostly TurboTax, rose 7% to $958 million in a quarter with little tax filing in it.

Adjusted earnings of $2.91 a share were well above the $2.44 to $2.48 Intuit had guided, and GAAP earnings rose 63% to $2.59. From this fiscal year Intuit no longer strips stock-based pay out of its adjusted figures, so the outside consensus numbers quoted after the report ($2.64 to $2.70) are not all on the same basis as the $2.47 [we cited in our preview](/news/2026-11-18/intuit-intu-reports-thursday-after-a-53-fall-this-year-what-to-watch/).

## The outlook

The guidance did the damage. Intuit cut its fiscal 2027 revenue range to $23.08 billion to $23.37 billion, growth of 8% to 9%, from the $23.28 billion to $23.51 billion it reaffirmed at its September investor day; it tied the change to Credit Karma, its credit-score and loan-matching app, Reuters and Investing.com reported. It kept its adjusted EPS range of $22.88 to $23.12, below the $23.62 analysts expected, according to Reuters.

Separately, Intuit agreed to sell Mailchimp, its email-marketing business, to Bending Spoons for roughly **$3.68 billion**: $3.31 billion in cash and $368 million in Bending Spoons stock. Mailchimp's revenue fell 1% to $313 million in the quarter. The deal should close in early 2027, and Intuit plans to use the cash for buybacks, Investing.com and Panabee reported. The guidance excludes the sale.

## Our take

The quarter itself was solid, with QuickBooks growing faster than the company overall. The trouble is a second disappointing outlook in three months, after August's [slower 2027 forecast](/news/2026-11-18/intuit-intu-reports-thursday-after-a-53-fall-this-year-what-to-watch/), from a stock already down 53.2% this year on fears that AI agents will take over tax and bookkeeping work ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-18/wall-street-is-betting-ai-will-empty-softwares-seats-3-numbers-will-decide-it/)). At about 13 times forward earnings before Thursday's drop, against a software peer median near 25 times in our data, the price already assumed slow growth; the test now is the second quarter's $4.95 billion ceiling and the start of tax season in January.
