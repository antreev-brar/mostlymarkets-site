Ferguson Enterprises ($FERG), the distributor of plumbing, heating and cooling supplies, reports third-quarter results for July to September before the open on Monday, 9 November. Results go up on its website at 6:45 a.m. ET and its call starts at 8:30 a.m. ET, the company said.

Last quarter, on 10 August, Ferguson's sales rose **4.6% to about $8.8 billion**, with 3.8% organic growth, and non-residential revenue grew 8% while residential returned to 2% growth, the release showed. Diluted EPS of $3.39 beat the roughly $3.23 Earnings Whispers consensus. Ferguson raised its 2026 sales outlook to mid-single-digit growth and agreed to buy FloWorks, a valve and flow-control distributor, for about $1.6 billion, Modern Distribution Management reported. The stock rose 2.8% that day and gave it back the next.

## What analysts expect

EPS of about **$3.28 to $3.31 a share**, up roughly 16% from $2.84 a year earlier, according to Barchart's preview and the consensus TradingView publishes; neither names its data provider. TradingView's revenue consensus is about $8.74 billion. Ferguson has beaten EPS estimates in each of the last two quarters, Barchart noted.

## What to watch

- **Non-residential growth.** It carried the second quarter at 8% growth, helped by large capital projects, MDM reported; holding that pace is the clearest test of demand.
- **Residential.** About half of revenue comes from homes, Barchart said, and the 2% return to growth was the first sign of a turn; another quarter of it would matter more than the beat.
- **Margin and the outlook.** Ferguson's 2026 range for the adjusted operating margin is 9.5% to 9.8%, MDM and Quartr reported; any change, and a first word on 2027, sets the stock's direction.

## How the stock has done

Ferguson closed Friday at $233.70, up 5.0% this year and 12.7% below its closing high of $267.71 on 30 April. It rose 7.8% in the past month, then fell 5.2% on Thursday, the day the Nasdaq [sank into a correction](/news/2026-11-05/stock-market-today-nasdaq-sinks-5-5-into-a-correction-on-midterm-ai-fears/). It trades at 19.5 times expected earnings.
