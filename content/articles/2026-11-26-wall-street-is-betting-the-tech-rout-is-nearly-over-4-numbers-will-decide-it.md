The story now spreading on Wall Street is that the AI sell-off is close to done. Deutsche Bank strategist Binky Chadha made the case in a note this week, reported by Bloomberg and MarketWatch on Wednesday: the Nasdaq 100's roughly 12% fall since mid-October is a "tech volatility shock", such shocks have historically bottomed within six to eight weeks, and the S&P 500 will reach **9,000 by the end of 2027**.

The peg is Wednesday itself. The Nasdaq was down 4.6% at its low on new tariff proposals and hot inflation, then [closed down just 0.6%](/news/2026-11-25/stock-market-today-the-dow-erases-a-1100-point-drop-as-tariffs-and-ai-fears-fade-by-the-close/), and Europe rallied 1.7% on Thursday. The stakes: Nvidia ($NVDA) is 37.6% below its October high, Micron ($MU) 50.5% below its June high and Broadcom ($AVGO) 37.5% below its June high, by our data.

## The story in one sentence

> The Nasdaq 100's fall since mid-October is a "tech volatility shock" that should bottom within six to eight weeks, with the S&P 500 at 9,000 by the end of 2027. Deutsche Bank's Binky Chadha, in his 2027 outlook, as reported by Bloomberg and MarketWatch on 25 November

The chain runs like this. If fund managers have already sold their tech and prices already assume AI profits will fall, then the next surprise is more likely to be good than bad, which means the most-punished stocks, Nvidia, Micron and the server makers, rebound first and drag the index with them.

## The evidence for it

### 1. Fund managers made their biggest tech cut since 2006

Bank of America's survey, taken from 6 to 12 November, found the share of fund managers overweight technology fell to a net **12% from 37%** in October, the biggest one-month cut since 2006, CNBC and Fortune reported, as [we set out on 19 November](/news/2026-11-19/the-average-stock-is-up-while-the-s-p-500-is-down-wall-street-is-betting-techs-lead-is-over/). Chadha makes the same point from his own data: investors' stock holdings have fallen to levels that have come before rebounds when there was no recession, Oninvest and Investinglive reported.

### 2. AI hardware is priced as if profits have peaked

Nvidia trades at **10.4 times** forward earnings against an 18.1 median for chipmakers, and Micron at 3.4, by our data from their filings, while Apple ($AAPL), the month's safe haven, trades at 38.2. Paying three to ten years of next year's profit only makes sense if that profit is expected to fall, as [our 25 November narrative](/news/2026-11-25/wall-street-is-pricing-ai-hardware-at-its-peak-dells-record-didnt-change-that/) argued. A bottom doesn't need good news, only news that is less bad than that.

### 3. The orders haven't cracked

Dell ($DELL) said on 24 November that its AI server backlog reached a record **$132.3 billion** and its server unit's operating margin rose to 17.0% ([our recap](/news/2026-11-24/dell-technologies-dell-beats-q3-estimates-as-revenue-doubles-and-its-ai-backlog-hits-132-billion/)). Chadha's target rests on earnings: Deutsche expects S&P 500 earnings per share to rise 19% in 2027 to $430, Oninvest and Investinglive reported. The sell-off so far has been about what investors will pay for AI profits, not about the profits themselves.

### 4. Buyers showed up on the worst morning

On Wednesday three shocks landed at once, the tariff proposal, 3.3% core inflation and a third day of AI selling, and the Dow still erased a drop of more than 1,100 points. The [tariff plan](/news/2026-11-25/us-proposes-tariffs-on-14-trading-partners-with-20-on-china-and-a-15-cap-for-taiwan/) turned out not to stack on most existing duties, and Europe's STOXX 600 rose 1.7% on Thursday. A market that can absorb bad news is acting the way markets do near a low.

## The case against it

Christian Mueller-Glissmann, head of asset allocation research at Goldman Sachs, said on Bloomberg Television on Wednesday that it is too early to buy the AI sell-off and that markets are in a "pretty fragile regime", Bloomberg and Oninvest reported. Bank of America's Savita Subramanian set an end-2027 target of 7,800, which only takes the index back to its October level, CNBC and Investinglive reported. Three counter-points:

1. **The Fed is pushing the other way.** Core PCE inflation rose to [3.3% in October](/news/2026-11-25/core-pce-inflation-rose-to-3-3-in-october-a-tenth-above-forecasts-before-a-fed-hike/), and futures put about 96% odds on a hike on 9 December. Bloomberg reported Goldman's view that the correction may persist without a shift by central banks, the clearest route to stabilizing markets. This is the strongest point: it names the one thing that usually ends sell-offs, and it's pointing the wrong way.
2. **Nobody has seen the profits.** Mueller-Glissmann's condition for buying is evidence of real returns on AI spending, and Goldman's risk-appetite gauge isn't yet at levels typical of a bottom, Oninvest and Roic reported. Oracle ($ORCL), 58.1% below its June high, shows what happens when the spending is funded with debt: [the cost of insuring its bonds hit a record](/news/2026-11-25/why-oracle-orcl-stock-is-down-today/) on Wednesday.
3. **More stock is coming.** Subramanian points to rising share issuance and new listings, and fading buybacks, CNBC and Investinglive reported; her bear case is 6,600, 8% below Wednesday's close. Anthropic's listing last week, at $83 a share, is the kind of new supply she means.

## What would change minds

- **Marvell ($MRVL) reports on Tuesday, 1 December**, after the close. A raised outlook for custom AI chips would be the first hard evidence the orders are intact since Dell's; a cut would undercut point 3.
- **The November jobs report on Friday, 4 December.** A cooler number is the only route to the softer Fed that Goldman says the market needs.
- **The Fed on Wednesday, 9 December**, and Micron's report on 17 December. A hike with a signal that it's the last would support the bottom; Micron holding its 2027 outlook would test whether a 3.4 times multiple was too low.

## Our read

**Partly supported.** The positioning and price evidence is strong: managers have cut tech harder than at any time since 2006, and Nvidia trades at about half the chipmaker multiple while Dell's orders keep rising. The weakest link is the calendar. The six-to-eight-week window Deutsche describes runs into a Fed hike on 9 December, and a bottom needs either a softer Fed or proof of AI profits. The number we'll watch is the 4 December payrolls figure, because it decides whether 9 December is the last hike or the start of more.
