## What happened

Shares of data-software maker Palantir ($PLTR) **slid 9.7%** on Thursday, their eighth straight loss, as the market's AI sell-off hit software too and investors went back to the company's slowing bookings, Seeking Alpha and Benzinga reported. The shares closed at $154.99, down from $171.70 on Wednesday and 22.1% below where they stood before the streak began on 27 October.

The pressure came from three directions. The total value of new contracts grew just 6% in the third quarter, down from 49% the quarter before, as we [reported on Tuesday](/news/2026-11-03/palantir-pltr-shares-slide-as-its-new-contracts-grow-just-6/), and Benzinga said investors added political risk to military spending after the Democratic sweep. Other software stocks that had led on AI fell with it, Yahoo Finance and StockStory reported.

## Why it matters

At about 88 times forward earnings in our quote on Thursday morning, Palantir is priced for years of fast growth, so a slowdown in bookings, the pipeline for future sales, matters more than a beat on this quarter.

## What the market is telling us

Palantir's shares moved 5% or more on 38 of the last 252 sessions. The last fall at least this big was on 4 February 2026, when the stock fell 11.6%. It is down 12.8% this year, and at $154.99 it sits 25.9% below its 52-week closing high of $209.05 from 9 October.

$1,000 invested in Palantir five years ago would be worth $5,961 today. Our read: the streak reflects a re-rating of an expensive stock more than new bad news, and the next test is whether US commercial bookings pick up again in the fourth quarter.
