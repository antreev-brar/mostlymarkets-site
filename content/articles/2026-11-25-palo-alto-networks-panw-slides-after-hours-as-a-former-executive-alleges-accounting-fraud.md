Palo Alto Networks ($PANW) was sued by Dan McGill, a former regional vice president, who alleges the cybersecurity company's leaders pushed resellers to issue purchase orders before customers had formally approved the deals, so they could be booked as sales, Bloomberg reported on Wednesday. McGill, who says he was fired after raising concerns, filed the complaint in federal court in Manhattan, Investing.com reported. The company called the allegations "completely meritless." The shares **fell 5.7% to $336.00** after hours as of 5:15 p.m. ET, after closing up 0.9% at $356.14.

The claim goes to how a sales-driven software company counts its orders, the number investors pay for: the stock is up 93.3% this year and trades at about 81 times forward earnings in our data. It came a week after Palo Alto [beat first-quarter estimates, raised its forecast and agreed to buy Applied Compute](/news/2026-11-18/palo-alto-networks-panw-beats-q1-estimates-raises-its-forecast-and-buys-applied-compute/). These are allegations in a complaint, not findings by a court.

What to watch: Palo Alto's formal answer in court, where it will have to respond to the specific deals the complaint describes.
