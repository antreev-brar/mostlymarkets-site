Air Products ($APD) reported adjusted earnings of **$3.61 a share** for its fiscal fourth quarter, the three months to September, before the open on Thursday, a cent above the $3.60 Zacks consensus and up 6% from a year earlier. Sales jumped 12% to $3.56 billion, helped by a one-off asset sale, and the industrial gas supplier guided to adjusted EPS of $14.15 to $14.50 for fiscal 2027. The stock closed **down 2.9% at $309.49**, a day after a 52-week high, as the S&P 500 fell 4.4%.

## What stood out

**The sales jump was mostly a one-off.** Volumes rose 9%, but the release said that included "a significant asset sale associated with an early contract termination in the Americas," where sales rose **27%**. When a customer ends a long-term supply contract early, Air Products can sell it the plant built to serve it, which shows up as a one-time lift to sales and profit.

*Verdict: a sales beat that says little about underlying demand, since the release does not say how much the sale added.*

**Margins slipped.** The adjusted operating margin fell 1.5 points to 24.1%, on higher costs, the release showed, including fixed-cost inflation and incentive pay over the year.

**The clean-up is mostly done.** GAAP EPS of $2.62 was well below adjusted because of charges tied to project exits, mainly two coal gasification plants in China, the company said. It cut capital spending by $1.8 billion in fiscal 2026, after walking away from projects including its Louisiana clean energy complex in June.

## The outlook

Air Products guided to fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS of **$14.15 to $14.50**, 5% to 8% above the $13.45 it earned in fiscal 2026, after 12% growth this year. On the call, management said a higher tax rate of about 19% and interest costs from the cancelled projects would drag on growth by about 2%, according to Motley Fool's transcript and BigGo. Capital spending is set at $3.0 billion to $3.5 billion. Chief Executive Eduardo Menezes said the results "demonstrate the progress we are making to improve our core industrial gas business."

## Our take

We were impressed that fiscal 2026 came in above the midpoint of guidance and that the capital spending cuts are real. On the other hand, the quarter's sales beat came from a one-off, margins fell, and growth is set to slow by about half next year. Overall, a mixed quarter for a stock priced for a clean recovery.

No outlet tied Thursday's fall to a line in the report, and it came on a day of broad selling. The stock trades at 21.7 times forward earnings against a peer median of 17.8 by our data, after rising 25.3% this year. The next test is fiscal first-quarter results early next year, against the $3.35 to $3.45 guidance.
