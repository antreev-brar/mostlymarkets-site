CrowdStrike ($CRWD) said early on Tuesday that it had agreed to buy Fiddler, a company that monitors how AI models and agents behave, and The Wall Street Journal reported that it is also buying Dreadnode, which tests AI models for weaknesses before they go live, for about **$495 million** combined; GuruFocus and Seeking Alpha carried the report. The deal is mostly cash, with some stock that vests over time, and is expected to close in CrowdStrike's fiscal fourth quarter, which ends in January, the company said.

Fiddler will become Falcon Observe, part of CrowdStrike's main security platform, and CrowdStrike also announced a feature it calls an AI kill switch, for shutting down AI agents that go wrong. The price is small, about 0.2% of CrowdStrike's $248 billion market value, but it is a bet that companies running AI agents will pay their security vendor to watch them.
