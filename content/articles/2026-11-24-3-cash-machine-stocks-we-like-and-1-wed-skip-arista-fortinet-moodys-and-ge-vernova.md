A company's free cash flow, the money left after running the business and paying for new equipment, is the part of profit that pays for buybacks and dividends and survives a sell-off. After the swings in tech stocks on Friday and Monday, we screened the companies we cover for the highest free cash flow margin, cash as a share of sales, over the 12 months to September 2026, using their SEC filings, and then asked a second question: does the cash come from the business itself?

Three of the strongest pass that test. One, GE Vernova, has a cash margin that looks better than the business behind it.

```picks
like: ANET, FTNT, MCO
skip: GEV
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Arista Networks ($ANET)
**Free cash flow margin: 42.2% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Arista makes the network switches that connect servers in data centers, including the ones built for AI.

1. **The cash rests on growth.** Revenue grew 31.2% a year over five years, in the top tenth of the 31 hardware makers we cover, and 58.5% in the third quarter to about $3.66 billion, the fastest since its listing, the results release and 24/7 Wall St. reported.
2. **The margins are rare for hardware.** Its operating margin of 43.8% is more than twice the 18.2% peer median, and it rose 12.6 points over five years.
3. **The outlook keeps rising.** Arista raised its 2026 revenue forecast to about $13.3 billion, its fourth raise this year, and guided to growth of 38% to 40% in 2027.

**The price:** 31.6 times expected earnings, nearly twice the 17.3 times hardware median, but below its own median of 52.0 times trailing earnings since 2024.

**What could go wrong:** a few big cloud customers buy most of its gear, so one of them slowing AI spending would show up quickly in the quarterly guide.

### 2. Fortinet ($FTNT)
**Free cash flow margin: 43.5% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Fortinet sells firewalls and the security software that runs on them.

1. **Long-run compounding.** Revenue grew 21.3% a year over five years, above the software median, and earnings per share 33.1% a year, in the top tenth of the software companies in our data.
2. **Cash is rising, not falling.** The 12-month cash margin of 43.5% is above its 37.8% two-year average, and the company has net cash of $3.3 billion.
3. **The sell-off was about pace, not trouble.** Third-quarter revenue rose 20% to $2.08 billion and billings 29%, but product sales growth slowed to 37% from 52%, and the shares fell after the 28 October report, Zacks and Yahoo Finance reported.

**The price:** 34.9 times expected earnings against a 24.0 times software median; on trailing earnings it sits at 42.6 times, its own median since 2024, and the shares are 32.7% below their 52-week high.

**What could go wrong:** its fourth-quarter revenue guide implies another slowdown, and a third straight step down would make the multiple hard to defend.

### 3. Moody's ($MCO)
**Free cash flow margin: 36.4% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Moody's rates bonds and sells credit data and software to banks and investors.

1. **Returns at the top of its group.** Return on capital of about 33.2% ranks in the top tenth of the financial-data, exchange and asset-management companies we cover, against a 10.6% median.
2. **The recurring half is growing.** Third-quarter revenue rose only 1% to $2.02 billion and missed estimates, because leveraged-finance deals slowed, but Moody's Analytics grew 8% on an organic basis, with recurring revenue up 8% to $3.7 billion, the release and Grafa reported.
3. **Buybacks shrink the share count.** Diluted shares fell 5.0% over two years.

**The price:** 29.3 times trailing earnings, below its own median of 39.6 times since 2024 and near the bottom of its 28.0-to-48.2 range, after the stock fell 5.9% this year.

**What could go wrong:** growth is slow, at 7.5% a year over five years, below the peer median, so the cheaper multiple may simply be the new normal if issuance stays weak.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### GE Vernova ($GEV)
**Free cash flow margin: 38.9% in the 12 months to September 2026.** GE Vernova builds gas turbines, grid equipment and wind turbines.

1. **The cash comes before the profit.** Third-quarter free cash flow was $5.4 billion, and the company said most of the jump came from working capital, mainly customer down payments and fees to reserve turbine production slots. That cash is real, but it is paid for future work.
2. **The business behind it earns thin margins.** Its operating margin of 6.4% over the last 12 months is in the bottom quarter of the 89 industrials we cover, and its two-year gross margin of 20.7% is about half the 39.0% median.
3. **The cash margin is far above its own norm.** Over two years it averaged 24.0%, so the 38.9% figure leans on one strong run of deposits.

**The price:** 31.9 times expected earnings, against a 19.8 times median for industrials.

**What would change our mind:** its record $197 billion backlog turning into operating margins in the double digits, which would show up first in the power segment's margin in its next report.
