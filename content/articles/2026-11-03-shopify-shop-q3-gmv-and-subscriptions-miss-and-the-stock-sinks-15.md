Shopify ($SHOP) said before the open on Tuesday that third-quarter revenue **rose 32% to $3.74 billion**, about what analysts expected, but the value of goods sold through its merchants' stores and its subscription revenue both fell short. Adjusted earnings per share of $0.49 beat the $0.47 consensus, according to Investing.com. The shares **fell 15.2% to $132.51** in pre-market trading as of 9:15 a.m. ET. GMV growth was the number [we flagged in our preview](/news/2026-10-29/what-to-expect-from-shopifys-shop-q3-earnings/).

## What stood out

GMV, the total value of everything sold through Shopify's stores, is the number that drives most of its revenue, because Shopify takes a cut of the payments it processes. It grew **29%**, down from 32% in the second quarter, and came in about $900 million short of the $120.1 billion analysts wanted, Investing.com and 24/7 Wall St. reported. A miss of under 1% would not usually cost a stock 15%; the slowdown going into the holiday quarter is what changed.

*Verdict: still fast, but a step down at the wrong moment.*

Subscription revenue, the monthly fees merchants pay for their stores, rose 20% to $836 million against the $850.2 million expected. It's the stickiest part of the business and the best read on how many merchants are signing up.

*Verdict: a soft quarter for new merchants.*

The profit lines were the good news. Gross profit grew **28% to $1.78 billion**, slower than revenue, so the gross margin slipped to 47.6% from 48.9% a year earlier, by our math from the release and Shopify's filings. But costs grew more slowly still: the free cash flow margin rose to 22% from 18%, the release said.

## The outlook

Shopify guided fourth-quarter revenue to grow at a low-thirties percentage rate and gross profit at a high-twenties rate, with operating expenses at 29% to 30% of revenue, Benzinga and Investing.com reported. That is the same shape as this quarter: gross profit growing more slowly than sales, because more of the mix comes from payments, which earn a thinner margin than subscriptions.

## Our take

We were impressed by the cash: a 22% free cash flow margin while revenue grew 32% is a rare combination. On the other hand, the stock was priced for faster growth, at 74 times forward earnings against a peer median of 17.6, by our data, and it got a slowdown in GMV and in subscriptions at once. Overall, a good quarter met a price that needed a great one.

The thesis isn't broken: over five years Shopify's revenue grew 31.6% a year, in the top tenth of its peers, and one quarter rarely changes that. The test is the holiday quarter, due in February. GMV growth back above 30% would settle the worry; another step down would confirm it.
