Becton Dickinson ($BDX) reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of **$5.27 billion** before the open on Thursday, up 5.4% from a year earlier and above the roughly $5.1 billion analysts expected, and adjusted earnings of $4.15 a share against the $4.06 Zacks consensus. Its first forecast for fiscal 2027, adjusted EPS of $13.10 to $13.30, came in just under the $13.25 consensus cited by Finimize (FactSet) and Zacks. The shares rose 2.1% to $213.55, a 52-week closing high, on a day the S&P 500 fell 4.4%.

## What stood out

**This was the first full year of the "New BD".** In February BD spun off its biosciences and diagnostics business into a combination with Waters, leaving a pure medical-device maker selling syringes, infusion pumps, stents and surgical products. Growth of **5.6%** on a currency-neutral basis was broad, led by Interventional and BioPharma Systems, the prefilled syringes drugmakers use. Chairman and Chief Executive Tom Polen said on the call that BD has "confidence in the underlying trajectory of New BD and our path towards sustainable mid-single-digit growth".

*Verdict: a clean quarter that shows the slimmer company can grow at a medtech pace.*

**Cash is the quiet improvement.** Free cash flow from continuing operations rose 42.5% to **$2.9 billion** in fiscal 2026, the release said. By our data BD's free cash flow margin over two years, about 12%, still sits in the bottom quarter of 19 medical device makers, so there is room to climb.

## The outlook

BD guided to low single-digit revenue growth and adjusted EPS of **$13.10 to $13.30** in fiscal 2027, against $12.70 in fiscal 2026. Coverage by BigGo and Yahoo Finance tied the soft revenue guide to a tough comparison for Alaris, its infusion pump, which they said will cut about 2 percentage points from growth. BD also said it plans to invest $19 billion in the US over several years under a deal with the government that gives it relief from future Section 232 tariffs.

## Our take

We were impressed by the breadth of the quarter and the cash flow. On the other hand, a guide that implies about 4% EPS growth at the midpoint, after 27% in the fourth quarter, puts the promised mid-single-digit growth off until at least fiscal 2028. Overall, solid; Jefferies raised its price target to $242 from $210, according to Investing.com.

The stock is up **37.5% this year**, but at 16.4 times forward earnings it is still cheaper than the 19.7 median for medical device makers by our data, a discount that reflects years of below-peer margins. The next test is BD's investor day on 10 December at the New York Stock Exchange, the first chance to see whether it puts numbers on the mid-single-digit growth Polen promised.
