Wall Street spent Wednesday paying for what it already owned. A proposed Medicare audit rule sent UnitedHealth ($UNH) **down 10.2%**, Nvidia ($NVDA) turned a beat into a 2.9% loss, and the S&P 500 slipped 0.2% for its fifth straight fall, its longest losing streak since June, Reuters reported ([our wrap](/news/2026-11-18/stock-market-today-health-insurers-drag-the-dow-down-561-points-as-nvidia-fades/)).

Then, after the bell, Anthropic showed what the market will pay for what it doesn't own yet. It sold 980 million shares at $83, above its $70 to $78 range, raising about $81.3 billion in the largest IPO on record, its release and CNN said. Nasdaq-100 futures rose 1.9% by about 4 a.m. ET, Reuters reported, and the companies that own a piece of Anthropic led the move.

The offering was upsized from 825 million shares, and at **$83** it priced 6% above the top of the range, by our arithmetic from the release ([our brief](/news/2026-11-18/anthropic-prices-its-ipo-at-83-a-share-above-its-70-to-78-range/)). It beat SpaceX's $75 billion listing in June, CNN and CNBC reported. The shares start trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT today; we weighed whether $1.5 trillion is a fair price in [our bulls-vs-bears piece](/news/2026-11-07/bulls-vs-bears-on-anthropics-ipo-is-1-5-trillion-a-fair-price/).

Alphabet ($GOOGL) and Amazon ($AMZN) will own more than 28% of Anthropic between them after the offering, the Motley Fool reported from the filing, so every dollar on the IPO price lifts the value of their stakes. Reuters named both, plus AMD ($AMD) and Nvidia, among the investors rising before the open. S&P 500 futures were **up 1.1%**, BigGo and MLQ reported, citing Reuters.

Amazon **rose 3.8%**, its biggest gain since 4 November, as traders priced in its 14.7% stake in Anthropic, Investing.com and Benzinga reported. By our estimate that added about 0.16 point to the index, the same amount Nvidia took off. - **Also today:** Novartis ($NVS) set a 5% yearly sales-growth target through 2031 at its investor day, below the 5% to 6% it set last year, CNBC and Investing.com reported; its US shares were down 2.2% at 6:15 a.m. ET.

We think today's test is where Anthropic's money comes from: Intuit, down 53% this year, reports tonight, and if funds keep selling software to buy ANT, the 1.1% gain in S&P 500 futures will be narrower than it looks.
