## What happened

Shares of optical-parts maker Coherent ($COHR), data-centre connectivity chipmaker Astera Labs ($ALAB) and Intel ($INTC) **jumped 5% to 9%** in the morning session on Thursday, as Anthropic's record IPO, priced on Wednesday night, lifted chip stocks around the world. As of 10:30 a.m. ET, Coherent was up 8.5% at $230.98, Astera 5.7% at $229.15, Intel 5.1% at $57.47 and Qualcomm ($QCOM) 3.4%.

Anthropic sold 980 million shares at $83, above its $70 to $78 range, raising $81.3 billion in the largest IPO on record, CNBC reported ([our brief](/news/2026-11-18/anthropic-prices-its-ipo-at-83-a-share-above-its-70-to-78-range/)). Yahoo Finance and a Bloomberg markets wrap carried by Swissinfo reported that chip stocks led the rally on it, and Yahoo Finance noted that Nvidia ($NVDA) and AMD ($AMD) are among the investors buying Anthropic shares in a private placement alongside the offering.

Coherent rose the most of the group. Yahoo Finance and CNBC reported that optical stocks, which carry data between AI servers over light, were extending their rebound; Lumentum ($LITE) was up 2.7% by 10:30 a.m. ET. Intel had news of its own: on Wednesday it said it had signed its first outside customer for its 18A-P manufacturing process, which Investing.com and Invezz cited alongside the IPO ([our story](/news/2026-11-18/intel-intc-says-it-has-signed-its-first-outside-customer-for-18a-p-chips/)).

## Why it matters

Chip stocks fell this month on one worry: that the money funding AI data centres was running short. An AI lab raising $81.3 billion from public investors at a value of about $1.5 trillion is the plainest evidence against it so far ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-15/wall-street-is-betting-anthropics-ipo-can-rescue-the-ai-trade-3-numbers-will-decide-it/)).

The market isn't fully convinced. The Nasdaq Composite opened 1.6% higher in our data but was up only 0.5% at 10:30 a.m. ET, and the S&P 500 was flat, so these gains are smaller than at the open.

## What the market is telling us

Coherent's shares moved 5% or more on 92 of the last 252 sessions, so a move this size is common for it. The stock is up 15.3% this year, and at Wednesday's $212.84 close it sat 50.1% below its 52-week closing high of $426.89 from 2 June. Intel sat 61.2% below its June high and Astera 55.1% below its own.

We read this as a bounce that has to hold through the close to mean much: Intel's 5% gain today barely dents a 41% slide over the past month, and only the spending plans of the big data-centre builders, not an IPO, can settle the demand question.
