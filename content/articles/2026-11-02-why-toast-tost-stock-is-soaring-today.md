## What happened

Shares of restaurant software and payments company Toast ($TOST) **soared 8.8%** to $34.47 as of 11:30 a.m. ET on Monday. We found no company news, filing or analyst call that two outlets tied to the jump, so the cause is no clear news. The move comes a day before Toast reports third-quarter results after the close on Tuesday, with its call at 5 p.m. ET, according to its listing on Yahoo Finance and StockStory's preview.

The last big news on the company cut the other way. On Thursday 29 October the Federal Trade Commission and eight states sued Toast in federal court in California, alleging it hid payment-processing fees behind its $0-a-month "Pay-as-you-Go" plan and made it hard for restaurants to cancel, the FTC said and Restaurant Dive and the Boston Globe reported. Toast said it "strongly disagrees" with the allegations and would defend itself. The stock fell 1.4% that day.

## Why it matters

The lawsuit goes at the heart of the model: Toast gives small restaurants hardware and software cheaply and earns its money on the fees for each card payment. Tuesday's report is the first chance for management to say whether the suit changes how it prices those plans, and how fast restaurants are signing up.

Block ($XYZ), which owns the rival Square point-of-sale system, was up 3.2% at the same time.

## What the market is telling us

Toast moved 5% or more on only 18 of the last 252 sessions, so a day like this is rare for it. If the gain holds to the close, it would be the stock's biggest one-day rise since 5 November 2025, when it rose 9.5% after its last third-quarter report. Even so, it went into Monday down 10.8% this year and 18.8% below its 52-week closing high of $39.02, and $1,000 invested five years ago would have been worth $595 at Friday's close.

A move this big a day before results, with no news, looks like positioning for the report; Tuesday's numbers will show whether it was right.
