## What happened

Shares of electric-car maker Tesla ($TSLA) **plunged 10.8%** on Thursday as investors bet that Democratic wins in Congress and the Texas governor's race will slow Elon Musk's data-center plans and invite more scrutiny of his companies, Reuters and Bloomberg reported. Musk's rocket company SpaceX fell about 9%.

Texas, where both companies are based, is the center of the worry. Democrat Gina Hinojosa won the governor's race after campaigning for a moratorium on new data centers, CNBC and The Daily Reporter reported, and Reuters noted that more than half of SpaceX's third-quarter sales came from renting out computing capacity. Investors also fear a Democratic Congress will look harder at Musk's federal contracts after he spent heavily backing Republicans, Quartz and Yahoo Finance reported.

The shares closed at $343.69, down from $385.19 on Wednesday and $398.50 on Election Day.

## Why it matters

The bull case for Tesla had moved well beyond cars, to robotaxis, robots and its links with Musk's AI and space businesses. Special committees at Tesla and SpaceX have been exploring a merger, Reuters and Autoevolution reported, and Reuters said the election could make that harder. The midterms put a political price on the parts of the story that depend on Washington and Texas.

## What the market is telling us

Tesla's shares moved 5% or more on 16 of the last 252 sessions, so a day like this is rare. The last fall at least this big was on 23 July 2026, when the stock fell 14.5%. The stock is down 23.6% this year, and at $343.69 it sits 29.8% below its 52-week closing high of $489.88 from 16 December 2025.

$1,000 invested in Tesla five years ago would be worth $844 today. Our read: nothing in Tesla's car or energy business changed this week, so the next real test is whether Texas acts on data centers and whether the merger committees say anything about timing.
