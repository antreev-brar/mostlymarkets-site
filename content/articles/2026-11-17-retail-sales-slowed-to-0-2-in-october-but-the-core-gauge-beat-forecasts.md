US retail sales **rose 0.2%** in October, the Commerce Department's Census Bureau said at 8:30 a.m. ET on Tuesday, in line with the forecast of economists polled by Reuters and down from a 0.8% gain in September. Car buyers did most of the slowing: sales at auto dealers fell 1.6%, the Associated Press and Bloomberg reported. The narrower measure that feeds into GDP did better than expected.

## Big purchases fell, the rest held up

Sales in the control group, which leaves out cars, gasoline, building materials and restaurants, **rose 0.6%**, above the 0.4% economists had forecast, Reuters and Bloomberg reported. Economists use this measure to estimate consumer spending in GDP, so the beat points to a firmer fourth quarter than the headline suggests. Leaving out only cars and gas stations, sales rose 0.5%, Breitbart and MLQ.ai reported.

The weak spots were big-ticket goods. Furniture and home furnishings stores sold 2.3% less than in September and clothing stores 0.7% less, while online retailers sold **1.2% more**, the AP and Reuters reported. Carl Weinberg, chief economist at High Frequency Economics, called the release "a mixed report card on the health of the consumer" in a note cited by the AP, saying it hinted that spending is weakening as prices rise.

## Price, not volume

These figures are in dollars and are not adjusted for inflation. Consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, Wolf Street and Inflation Guy reported on 10 November, so a 0.2% rise in sales suggests shoppers bought slightly less in volume in October than in September, though the price index also covers services that retail sales leave out.

That matters for the trade investors have made this month. Money has moved out of AI stocks and into retailers on the bet that the shopper keeps spending, as we set out in [this morning's narrative](/news/2026-11-17/wall-street-is-hiding-in-the-shopper-this-weeks-retailers-will-test-it/). In our data, Costco ($COST) rose 12.6% and Home Depot ($HD) 12.2% in the month to Monday's close while Nvidia ($NVDA) fell 27.1%.

## What it means for the Fed

Our read: October's report does little to change the case for a rate increase at the Federal Reserve's meeting on 8-9 December. The part of spending that feeds GDP beat forecasts, and prices are still rising 0.4% a month. As we [reported on 13 November](/news/2026-11-13/wholesale-prices-rose-less-than-expected-in-october-but-the-annual-rate-hit-6-5/), CME FedWatch data then put the odds of a December hike at about 87% to 88%.

Before the open, the big retailers were lower. As of 9:15 a.m. ET, Walmart ($WMT) was down 1.5% in pre-market trading and Target ($TGT) down 1.1%, both ahead of their own results this week, and Home Depot was down 3.2% after its [third-quarter report](/news/2026-11-17/home-depot-hd-tops-q3-estimates-and-names-cfo-richard-mcphail-ceo/).

## What's next

Target, Lowe's ($LOW) and TJX ($TJX) report before Wednesday's open and Walmart before Thursday's. The final University of Michigan sentiment reading for November comes on Friday, 20 November.
