## How it got here

Google built its business on one habit: people type what they want into a search box, and advertisers pay to appear next to the answer. It listed in 2004, reorganized under the Alphabet holding company in 2015, and spent the next decade adding YouTube, Android, subscriptions and a cloud business on top of search.

AI turned that model into a question. Chatbots answer questions directly, so investors worry fewer people will click on ads, and Alphabet's answer has been to spend heavily to lead in AI itself: its own Gemini models, its own TPU chips and the data centers to run them.

On 28 October the bill arrived. Third-quarter search revenue grew 12% to $63.35 billion, short of the roughly $65 billion expected, Yahoo Finance and SiliconANGLE reported, and capital spending more than doubled from a year earlier, the results release showed ([our story](/news/2026-10-29/alphabet-googl-shares-plunge-after-search-falls-short-and-cash-burn-grows/)). **Is Alphabet's core business breaking, or is it paying up front for its next one?**

## The business in one line

Advertisers pay Alphabet per click and per view on search and YouTube, businesses pay Google Cloud for computing by usage and under multi-year contracts, and consumers pay monthly subscriptions.

## The numbers that matter

### Growth: 24.7% last quarter, against 17.2% a year over five years

Revenue grew **24.7%** from a year earlier in the third quarter, faster than its five-year pace of 17.2% a year, which matched the median of the consumer internet companies in our data. Over two years it grew 17.7% a year, above the 13.4% median. Most of the speed-up is Cloud.

### Profitability: a 33.3% operating margin, top of its peers

Alphabet kept **33.3%** of its sales as operating profit over the last 12 months, the highest among its peers, whose median is 13.1%, and 9.4 points more than five years ago. Meta ($META), the closest rival in ads, was at 32.9%. Google Cloud's own operating margin rose to 36.2% from 23.7% a year earlier, according to the call, as reported by Yahoo Finance and the Motley Fool.

### Cash: free cash flow of 4.2% of sales

Free cash flow, the cash left after paying for data centers and equipment, was just **4.2%** of sales over the last 12 months, in the bottom quarter of its peers, against 10.9% over two years. It was negative in each of the last two quarters, at -$5.86 billion and -$9.07 billion, its filings show.

### Returns: 34.8% on shareholders' equity

Return on equity was **34.8%**, in the top quarter of its peers, and return on capital about 16.6%, above the 12.1% median. The equity figure is flattered by gains on its stakes in private companies.

### Balance sheet: $71.2 billion of net debt

Alphabet ended September with $56.3 billion of cash against **$127.5 billion** of debt, its filing shows: $71.2 billion of net debt, about half a year of operating profit. It sold a seven-part euro bond on 2 November, its second this year, Bloomberg and MarketScreener reported ([our story](/news/2026-11-02/alphabet-googl-returns-to-the-euro-bond-market-days-after-its-record-drop/)).

## Why the stock is where it is

1. **Search fell short of forecasts.** The 12% growth fell short of forecasts, and Pivotal Research Chief Executive Jeffrey Wlodarczak said "the bears will argue this is the first quarter that AI is starting to seriously hurt their search business," Yahoo Finance and IBTimes reported.
2. **The spending keeps rising.** Alphabet kept its 2026 capital spending forecast at $195 billion to $205 billion, and Chief Financial Officer Anat Ashkenazi said "we continue to expect our CapEx to increase significantly in 2027," IBTimes and Investing.com reported.
3. **Its reported profits are partly paper.** Gains on its stakes in SpaceX and Anthropic supplied $6.26 of its $9.11 of second-quarter earnings per share, Fortune and MediaPost reported, and in the third quarter an $8.66 billion loss on such stakes cut 55 cents, the release showed. The headline P/E of 14.9 is therefore not a guide to its value.
4. **The AI trade broke this week.** The stock fell 2.2% on Thursday, 5 November, as the Democrats' midterm sweep hit AI stocks, a smaller drop than chipmakers' ([our market wrap](/news/2026-11-05/stock-market-today-nasdaq-sinks-5-5-into-a-correction-on-midterm-ai-fears/)).

## Testing the worry

**Search: contained, so far.** A business losing its customers doesn't grow 12% in a year; the miss was against hopes, not a decline. The test is the trend: one point of search growth is worth about $2.5 billion of yearly revenue at the third quarter's pace, by our arithmetic, so a slide toward single digits would show up quickly in the total.

**Spending: lasting, but it has buyers.** Google Cloud's backlog, revenue customers have contracted but not yet received, reached about **$561 billion**, and Alphabet expects about 55% of it to become revenue within 24 months, the call and 10-Q show, as reported by Yahoo Finance and SEC-API. That is roughly $300 billion over two years, against $122.8 billion of Cloud sales at the third quarter's annual pace, by our arithmetic. On 6 November Google also took over a Wyoming data center site that a smaller developer, Crusoe, couldn't fill, Bloomberg reported ([our brief](/news/2026-11-06/alphabet-googl-takes-over-crusoes-wyoming-data-center-site-a-day-after-its-17-billion-pledge/)): the giants with cash are picking up projects, not dropping them.

**The paper profits: real money, wrong yardstick.** The stakes are worth a lot, but their value moves with private-market marks, and Anthropic's IPO price is now under pressure ([our bulls-vs-bears piece](/news/2026-11-07/bulls-vs-bears-on-anthropics-ipo-is-1-5-trillion-a-fair-price/)). They belong outside any judgment of the operating business.

The worry that survives is the cash. Operating cash flow was **$179.6 billion** over the last 12 months, below the midpoint of this year's spending forecast, so Alphabet is spending more than its operations bring in and borrowing the difference while it promises more in 2027.

## The moat

1. **Search habit.** Search still brought in $63.35 billion in a quarter, about half of sales, and grew 12% in the year AI chatbots went mainstream.
2. **Its own chips.** Alphabet designs its TPU processors and now sells TPU systems through Cloud, according to the call reported by Yahoo Finance; owning the design gives Cloud an alternative to buying all its AI processors from Nvidia.
3. **Paying users.** YouTube Music and Premium passed 150 million subscribers including trials, the company said on the call, as reported by Yahoo Finance and the Motley Fool.

The rivals: Amazon's ($AMZN) AWS grew 39% last quarter, to $45.98 billion ([our recap](/news/2026-10-29/amazon-amzn-q3-aws-speeds-up-but-ads-and-profit-miss-estimates/)), and Microsoft's ($MSFT) Azure 48% ([our recap](/news/2026-10-28/microsoft-msft-beats-q1-estimates-as-azure-growth-speeds-up-to-48/)); Google Cloud's 103% beat both, from a smaller base. In ads, Meta grew revenue 24.4% a year over two years against Alphabet's 17.7%.

## The risks we'd watch

1. **A search slowdown.** At the third quarter's pace, each point of lost search growth costs about $2.5 billion of yearly revenue, and search carries most of the group's profit.
2. **Spending without a return.** If Cloud's backlog converted more slowly than the 55% in two years Alphabet expects, the company would be left with debt-funded data centers earning less than its 16.6% return on capital.
3. **Marks on its stakes.** Its holdings in Anthropic and SpaceX swing reported earnings by billions a quarter in either direction; a lower Anthropic IPO price would show up as a loss.

The operating-profit multiple is our calculation from Alphabet's filings and our price files. On that measure the market prices Alphabet about as usual: it isn't assuming the business breaks, and it isn't paying for the spending to work.

## Our verdict

**Business: Strong. Price: About its usual price.** Sales are growing faster than in any quarter in our data, margins lead its peers, and Cloud has $561 billion of contracts behind it. The cash is the catch: Alphabet now spends more than its operations generate and will spend more in 2027. We'd change our mind on the business if search growth fell into single digits or Cloud growth halved; its fourth-quarter report, early next year, is the next test of both. Alphabet isn't broken, it's betting the cash machine on the cloud.
