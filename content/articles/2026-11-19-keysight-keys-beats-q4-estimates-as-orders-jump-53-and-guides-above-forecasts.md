Keysight Technologies ($KEYS) reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of **$1.99 billion** after the close on Thursday, up 41% from $1.42 billion a year earlier and above the $1.95 billion analysts expected, MarketBeat reported. Adjusted earnings of $3.61 a share beat the $3.37 LSEG (I/B/E/S) consensus, Reuters reported, and orders rose 53%. The shares slipped 1.1% to $278.22 after hours as of 4:30 p.m. ET, after closing down 1.7% at $281.42 in a day when the Nasdaq fell 2.3%.

## What stood out

Keysight sells the test and measurement equipment that chip, network and phone makers use to design new products, so orders are an early read on what those customers plan to spend. Orders **rose 53% to $2.34 billion** in the quarter, ahead of revenue, and to $8.13 billion for the year, up 49%, the release's tables show. That keeps the order book growing faster than sales, the pattern [our preview](/news/2026-11-18/what-to-expect-from-thursdays-earnings-ross-stores-keysight-and-williams-sonoma/) said would matter most after the 56% jump last quarter.

The Communications Solutions Group, which sells to network and data-center builders, grew revenue 51% to $1.50 billion, with commercial communications up 73%. The Electronic Industrial Solutions Group rose 16% to $498 million on semiconductor, automotive and energy customers. "Keysight delivered the strongest year in the company's history," Chief Executive Satish Dhanasekaran said in the release.

## The outlook

Keysight guided first-quarter revenue to $2.04 billion to $2.06 billion, above the $1.99 billion it just reported, and adjusted EPS to $3.63 to $3.69. Consensus for the quarter was $3.16 a share at MarketBeat and $3.25 at StockAnalysis, so the bottom of the range clears both by at least 12%.

## Our take

This was a clean beat: revenue, earnings and the forecast all came in above estimates, and orders still outran sales. The muted reaction says more about the day than the report, with chip and AI hardware stocks falling and Keysight already 27.5% below its 6 October high. At 21.2 times forward earnings against a peer median of 17.2 times in our data, the stock prices continued growth; the next test is whether orders hold above $2 billion in the first quarter when it next reports.
