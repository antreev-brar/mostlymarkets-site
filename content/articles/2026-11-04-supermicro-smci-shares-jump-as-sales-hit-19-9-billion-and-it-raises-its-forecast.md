Supermicro ($SMCI) reported fiscal first-quarter sales of **$19.9 billion** after the close on Tuesday, almost four times the $5.0 billion of a year earlier and ahead of the $16.39 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters. Adjusted earnings of $1.81 a share topped the $1.23 consensus, MarketBeat and Benzinga reported. The shares, which closed up 3.1% at $31.39, were **up 9.1%** at $34.26 before the open on Wednesday, by 6:15 a.m. ET in our data.

## What stood out

The size was known; the margin wasn't. Supermicro had already told investors in October that sales would land between $18.5 billion and $19.5 billion, so the surprise was finishing above that range with a 12.8% adjusted gross margin, above its preliminary 12% to 12.5%, Benzinga reported. AI systems were 71% of sales, up from 59% the quarter before, Startup Fortune reported.

"Supermicro's transformation into a Total AI and IT Datacenter Solution provider continues to accelerate," Chief Executive Charles Liang said in the release. The catch is cash: building racks for giant customers ties up money in inventory, and the company used **$434 million** in operations, with $6.9 billion of cash against $8.6 billion of bank debt and convertible notes.

## The outlook

Supermicro guided December-quarter sales to $19.5 billion to $21.5 billion, above the roughly $17.1 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters, and raised its fiscal-year range to **$72 billion to $82 billion**. The midpoint of $77 billion is above the $69.17 billion analysts had forecast, MarketBeat reported.

## Our take

On the numbers this was a blowout, and it lands the same night [AMD](/news/2026-11-03/amd-amd-beats-q3-estimates-and-guides-q4-sales-to-17-billion/) and [Arista Networks](/news/2026-11-03/arista-networks-anet-beats-q3-estimates-as-sales-grow-58-5/) said AI spending is still growing. But the market values Supermicro at about $20.6 billion, barely one quarter's sales, because thin margins and cash going out the door make its profits hard to trust. Before the report the stock had fallen 27% in a month, Startup Fortune noted; what to watch next quarter is whether operating cash flow turns positive.
