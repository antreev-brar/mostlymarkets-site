Nvidia's ($NVDA) board said early on Tuesday that it has created a **National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee**, chaired by director Suzanne Nora Johnson, a former Goldman Sachs executive, with directors Stephen Neal and John Dabiri, the company said in a release reported by Bloomberg and Reuters. Its national security and trade compliance teams will now report to the committee, and to interim Chief Executive Colette Kress day to day. The stock was up 0.3% at $156.62 at 6:30 a.m. ET.

The timing matters: it came a day after the Commerce Department moved to stop Chinese firms renting Nvidia chips in overseas data centers ([our brief](/news/2026-11-16/us-moves-to-cut-off-chinas-remote-access-to-nvidia-nvda-chips-abroad/)), and hours before Nvidia's first report since Jensen Huang resigned. Separately, Nvidia has warned buyers that a proposed law on remote access could expose them to legal risk, and says it won't sell to customers who rent more than half their chips to Chinese companies, CNBC reported and Reuters relayed. The board repeated that it has no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised.

Nvidia closed Monday **34.7% below** its 6 October high. Its results come after the close, with a call at 5 p.m. ET ([our preview](/news/2026-11-16/what-to-expect-from-nvidias-nvda-q3-earnings/)).
