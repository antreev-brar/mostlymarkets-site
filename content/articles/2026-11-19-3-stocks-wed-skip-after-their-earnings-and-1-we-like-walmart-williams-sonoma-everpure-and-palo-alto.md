Walmart ($WMT) raised its outlook on Thursday and slipped, Williams-Sonoma ($WSM) matched estimates with help from tariff refunds and slid, and Everpure ($P) jumped after a 51% revenue gain. Palo Alto Networks ($PANW) raised its forecast on Wednesday.

Our screen: companies worth $10 billion or more that reported in the last seven days and that we recapped. We judged each on the quarter, then on five years of SEC-filed numbers against industry peers, then on the price. Prices are as of 10:30 a.m. ET on Thursday.

```picks
skip: WMT, WSM, P
like: PANW
```

## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. Walmart ($WMT)
**Third-quarter revenue up 5.2% to $188.8 billion, reported 19 November.** Walmart is the biggest US retailer by sales, and its global e-commerce sales grew 17% in the quarter.

Revenue and adjusted EPS of 65 cents beat LSEG estimates, and Walmart raised its outlook for the third time this year, but its sales-growth guide of 4.9% to 5.2% sat below the 5.5% analysts wanted, CNBC and Quartz reported ([our recap](/news/2026-11-19/walmart-wmt-beats-q3-estimates-and-raises-its-outlook-for-the-third-time/)). The stock was down 1.3% at $125.76.

1. **Little of the revenue becomes cash:** free cash flow was 1.8% of revenue in the 12 months to July 2026, in the bottom tenth of the 33 consumer-staples stocks we cover, where the median is 10.1%.
2. **The margin is thin and flat:** a 4.4% operating margin over the same 12 months, in the bottom quarter of its peers, and just 0.1 points higher than five years ago.
**The price:** 42 times forward earnings, against a staples median of 18 times, and 74 times free cash flow. Its trailing P/E of 46 sits near the top of its 34 to 49 range since August 2024.

**What would change our mind:** a free cash flow margin back above 3%, or a price nearer its own 40.6 times trailing median.

### 2. Williams-Sonoma ($WSM)
**Third-quarter adjusted EPS of $2.19, a penny above the Zacks consensus, reported 19 November.** Williams-Sonoma sells furniture and kitchenware under its own name and the Pottery Barn and West Elm brands.

Tariff refunds supplied about 1.4 of the 1.5-point gain in gross margin, the release shows, and the company only reiterated its outlook ([our recap](/news/2026-11-19/williams-sonoma-wsm-q3-matches-estimates-with-tariff-refund-help-and-the-stock-slides/)). The stock was down 7.8% at $239.69.

1. **Growth is slow over the long run:** revenue rose 2.9% a year over five fiscal years, in the bottom tenth of the 62 consumer-discretionary stocks we cover, where the median is 12.8%.
2. **The quarter's margin gain was a one-off:** without the refunds, gross margin was close to flat, so the earnings beat doesn't repeat.
**The price:** a trailing P/E of 26.6 before Thursday's fall, against its own median of 21.7 since August 2024, and 24 times forward earnings against a peer median of 19.6 times.

**What would change our mind:** a holiday-quarter gross margin that rises without refunds, due in March, or a price back near its usual multiple.

### 3. Everpure ($P)
**Third-quarter revenue up 51% to $1.46 billion, reported 18 November.** Everpure, formerly Pure Storage, sells flash-storage systems and subscriptions.

It beat the LSEG revenue estimate by 9% and raised its full-year forecast for a third straight quarter ([our recap](/news/2026-11-18/everpure-p-beats-q3-estimates-as-product-sales-jump-77-and-raises-its-forecast/)). The stock was up 12.4% at $113.22.

1. **The growth is real:** revenue rose 16.8% a year over five fiscal years, in the top quarter of the 27 hardware makers we cover, against a median of 5.6%.
2. **The profit isn't there yet:** a GAAP operating margin of 5.3% over the 12 months to August 2026, in the bottom quarter of hardware peers, and free cash flow of 3.0% of revenue.
3. **The mix is getting lumpier:** product sales, which are one-off, did most of the lifting while subscription services grew 19%, and GAAP gross margin fell to 68.0% from 72.3%.

**The price:** 34.5 times forward earnings against a hardware median of 17.7 times, and 294 times free cash flow.

**What would change our mind:** a fourth-quarter report early next year with gross margin holding near 68% and free cash flow keeping pace with revenue.

## 1 stock we like

### Palo Alto Networks ($PANW)
**First-quarter revenue up 39% to $3.44 billion, reported 18 November.** Palo Alto sells network, cloud and AI security software.

It beat the $3.31 billion LSEG estimate, its next-generation security annual recurring revenue reached $9.73 billion, up 66%, and it raised its full-year revenue forecast to $14.52 billion to $14.57 billion ([our recap](/news/2026-11-18/palo-alto-networks-panw-beats-q1-estimates-raises-its-forecast-and-buys-applied-compute/)). The stock was up 3.6% at $351.98.

1. **The growth is compounding:** revenue rose 22.0% a year over five fiscal years, better than most of the 29 software companies we cover, and the latest quarter's growth is in the top quarter.
2. **The profits are cash:** free cash flow was 36.6% of revenue over two years, in the top quarter of software peers, against a median of 27.7%.
3. **It is getting more profitable:** the GAAP operating margin rose 13.2 points over five years, about twice the software median's gain.

**The price:** 84 times forward earnings against a software median of 25 times, and 70 times free cash flow. The price assumes its cloud and AI growth runs for years, so it leaves little room for a slip.

**What could go wrong:** diluted shares rose 14.7% over two years, in the bottom quarter of software peers, and the GAAP operating margin of 6.1% over the 12 months to July 2026 is behind most of them, so per-share profit lags the headline growth.
