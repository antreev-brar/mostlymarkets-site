EchoStar ($ECHO) reported third-quarter revenue of $1.13 billion in its quarterly filing at 8:15 a.m. ET on Tuesday, **down 69%** from a year earlier because it no longer counts its pay-TV and Hughes satellite businesses in its results. Boost Mobile, the wireless carrier that is now most of what it reports, lost 182,000 customers, and GAAP earnings of $7.97 a share came from accounting gains, not from selling phone plans. The stock was up 5.5% at $94.82 on Friday as of 2:15 p.m. ET, after a 3.8% gain on Tuesday and two days of losses.

## What stood out

The revenue drop is bookkeeping, not lost sales. EchoStar stopped consolidating "substantially all" of its pay-TV business as of 30 June, when its DISH DBS unit went into a prepackaged Chapter 11 plan, and most of its Hughes broadband and satellite business as of 31 July, the 10-Q said. It still owns and runs the pay-TV operations, and it has stopped reporting pay-TV subscribers.

The number that tells you about the business is Boost's. A net loss of **182,000 customers** means more people left than signed up: the filing blamed fewer new activations, fewer government-subsidized sign-ups, and higher churn, the share of customers who leave each month. Fierce Network and PhoneArena both put the base at about 7.2 million.
*Verdict: weak; the one operating business left in the results is shrinking.*

The profit is the same story in reverse. Operating income was **$148 million**, against a loss of $16.6 billion a year earlier that was swollen by write-downs, but the $2.78 billion net profit leaned on gains from deconsolidation and its deals, as summaries of the filing by Quartr and Yahoo Finance noted.
*Verdict: a profit on paper; it says little about what Boost earns.*

## The outlook

EchoStar gave no forecast. It put out no results release and held no call, Fierce Network reported, so the 10-Q is all investors got. The next tests are the Dish Wireless bankruptcy case and any agreement on Boost's future.

## Our take

The debate is not this quarter. The stock trades on what EchoStar owns: SpaceX shares from its 2025 sale of AWS-4 and H-block spectrum, cash from selling spectrum to AT&T, and a possible buyer for Boost. On Monday 2 November, the Washington Post reported SpaceX was in early talks to buy Boost or use its network, TechCrunch and Deadline said, and AST SpaceMobile ($ASTS) fell 11% that day on the competition worry. UBS valued EchoStar's SpaceX stake at about **$110 a share** at SpaceX's price in September, Proactive Investors and Goldesel reported, above Friday's price.

We were impressed by the cash pile and the clean-up. On the other hand, the customer losses make Boost a weaker asset to sell at the very moment a buyer is said to be circling. Thursday's 8.8% drop came on [a day the Nasdaq sank 5.5%](/news/2026-11-05/stock-market-today-nasdaq-sinks-5-5-into-a-correction-on-midterm-ai-fears/), and Trefis found no company news behind it; we found none behind Friday's rebound either, which came as [chip stocks bounced](/news/2026-11-06/amd-micron-and-lam-research-shares-jump-what-happened/). EchoStar's shares moved 5% or more on 31 of the last 252 sessions, and at Thursday's close they were down 17.3% this year and 36.6% below their 20 May high of $141.80. The number to watch is whether SpaceX makes an offer for Boost, and at what price.
