Roku ($ROKU) said after the close on Monday that its third-quarter revenue **grew 24% to $1.50 billion**, ahead of the roughly $1.41 billion analysts expected, Benzinga and Seeking Alpha reported. Earnings per share were $1.20, up from $0.16 a year earlier and against the $0.53 consensus Benzinga cited. The shares barely moved: they closed up 0.3% at $155.52 and were down 0.5% at $154.81 after hours as of 4:15 p.m. ET, because Fox ($FOXA) has agreed to buy the company.

## What stood out

Advertising did the heavy lifting. Ad revenue grew 23% to **$727 million**, Roku's ninth straight quarter of double-digit growth, and its gross margin rose 4.7 points to 63.2% on a shift to higher-margin ad products, the shareholder letter said. Political advertising on Roku beat the same quarter of the 2024 presidential cycle, and media companies' ad spending grew at its fastest pace in four years, the letter said.

Profit grew much faster than sales because costs fell. Sales and marketing spending dropped 21% from a year earlier, and operating income rose to $164 million from $9.5 million. Trailing 12-month free cash flow reached $796 million.

## The outlook

Roku gave no forecast and held no call "in light of the pending transaction", the letter said. It is on track for the second-half 2026 forecast it set out in its merger proxy, Deadline and Benzinga reported. The Justice Department's antitrust division is reviewing the deal, which is expected to close in the first half of 2027, Deadline reported.

## Our take

The quarter was strong, but it doesn't set the stock price. Under the June agreement each Roku share gets $96 in cash plus 0.9693 Fox Class A shares, Financier Worldwide and TIKR reported. At Fox's Monday close of $62.76 that comes to **$156.83**, just 0.8% above Roku's close: the market sees little risk that the deal fails. The things that move Roku now are Fox's share price and the antitrust review, not Roku's own numbers.
