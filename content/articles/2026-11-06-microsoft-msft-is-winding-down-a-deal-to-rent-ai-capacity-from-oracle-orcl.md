Microsoft ($MSFT) is winding down a 2023 agreement to rent AI computing capacity from Oracle ($ORCL), switching it off over recent months after deciding not to renew, Bloomberg reported on Friday, and Reuters carried the report. The capacity ran on older Nvidia ($NVDA) chips that no longer fit Microsoft's needs, TradingKey reported, and the deal is part of a wider review of its outside cloud contracts. Microsoft **fell 1.0%** to $530.61, while Oracle recovered from an early drop to close up 0.8% at $116.10.

The deal's size wasn't disclosed, and Oracle said demand for its AI infrastructure, including older chips, remains strong, Benzinga reported. The bigger signal is the review: Microsoft is still deciding which neoclouds, the specialist AI cloud renters, it will stop using, CNBC reported. It has committed more than $80 billion to outside providers including CoreWeave ($CRWV) and Nebius ($NBIS), both of which rose more than 6% on Friday in the chip rebound.

Microsoft has not commented. Its next quarterly report should show whether it is cutting outside rentals or shifting the money to its own data centers ([our AI spending narrative](/news/2026-11-05/wall-street-is-betting-the-midterms-will-slow-the-ai-buildout-3-numbers-will-decide-it/)).
