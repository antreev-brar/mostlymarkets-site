Jensen Huang said on Tuesday morning that he would be "honored to return as CEO" if Nvidia's board decided it was in the best interest of shareholders, employees, the company and America, in his first public statement since resigning on 12 November, Reuters and CNBC reported. He said he would cooperate with the board's investigation and has agreed to transcribed interviews with three House committees on 1 December, Fortune and NBC News reported.

The decision now sits plainly with Nvidia's ($NVDA) board, which set up a national-security committee early on Tuesday ([our story](/news/2026-11-17/nvidia-nvda-board-sets-up-export-control-committee-hours-before-its-q3-report/)) and is still investigating. President Trump said on Monday he "would not mind" a return ([our story](/news/2026-11-16/trump-says-he-wouldnt-mind-jensen-huang-returning-as-nvidia-nvda-ceo/)); his Treasury secretary has urged an outside hire. The stock was **down 1.4%** at $154.02 at 12:15 p.m. ET, below Monday's close, which was its lowest in a year.

What's next: Nvidia reports its third quarter after the close, with a call at 5 p.m. ET, where interim Chief Executive Colette Kress is likely to face questions on the statement ([our preview](/news/2026-11-16/what-to-expect-from-nvidias-nvda-q3-earnings/)).
