EOG Resources ($EOG) reported third-quarter revenue of **$8.55 billion** after the close on Thursday, up 46% from a year earlier and well above the $7.54 billion five analysts surveyed by Zacks expected, according to the AP. Adjusted earnings of $4.87 a share beat the $4.45 Zacks consensus and the $4.46 LSEG estimate cited by Reuters, and the Texas shale producer raised its regular dividend 8%. The shares were at **$147.28, up 1.7%**, as of 2:15 p.m. ET on Friday.

## What stood out

**Volume did the work.** Production of 1.433 million barrels of oil equivalent a day was a record, with oil, natural gas liquids and gas each above the midpoint of EOG's guidance, the company said on its call. Oil output of **550,400 barrels a day** compared with a guidance midpoint of 548,500. That matters because revenue barely slipped from the second quarter even though the oil price EOG realized fell by about $11 a barrel.

*Verdict: strong. More barrels from the same rigs offset a weaker oil price.*

**The cash went back to owners.** Free cash flow, the cash left after paying for new wells, was **$2.18 billion**. Buybacks and the dividend took about 71% of it, and net debt fell to $2.18 billion from $4.54 billion at the end of 2025. The share count has fallen 8.1% in two years, the best in our energy group of 25 stocks, by our analysis of SEC filings.

*Verdict: solid, and the balance sheet now has little debt left to pay down.*

## The outlook

EOG pointed to modest growth next year rather than a new spending plan. "If current oil supply demand fundamentals persist, we are well positioned to deliver low single digit oil production growth in 2027," Yacob said on Friday's call. The 2027 capital budget usually comes with fourth-quarter results in February.

## Our take

We were impressed by the record output and a beat on both lines. On the other hand, the quarter was booked at $87 oil, down from $98 in the second quarter, so the oil price sets how much of this cash flow repeats. The stock sank 5.2% on Wednesday and sits about 11% below its 30 October closing high. Overall, a strong quarter that the oil price, not EOG, will decide the value of.

The longer record backs it up. EOG's operating margin over the past 12 months was 35.8%, against an energy median of 22.5%, and the stock trades at 8.2 times forward earnings against a peer median of 11.6, by our data. That price assumes oil stays well below this quarter's level. Peers told a similar story this week: [Devon Energy ($DVN) beat on profit but missed on oil volumes](/news/2026-11-06/devon-energy-dvn-beats-q3-profit-estimates-but-eagle-ford-oil-falls-short/), and [ConocoPhillips ($COP) beat and raised its dividend 11%](/news/2026-11-05/conocophillips-cop-beats-q3-estimates-and-raises-its-dividend-11/). The next test is the 2027 budget in February, and whether low single-digit growth holds at a lower oil price.
