*Broadcom is a strong business going through a margin squeeze it chose, and at 20 times expected earnings the price assumes the squeeze stops near 73%; December's report decides it.*

On Friday, Broadcom ($AVGO) **fell 5.3%** to $345.14 by 12:15 p.m. ET, giving back part of a 24.1% four-day rebound that ran from 6 to 12 November as investors bet Nvidia's leadership turmoil would send it orders ([our story on today's slide](/news/2026-11-13/amd-broadcom-and-credo-shares-slide-what-happened/)). The stock is still 28.3% below its record close of $481.57 on 2 June, even though its sales grew 86% in its latest quarter.

## How it got here

Broadcom is the product of Chief Executive Hock Tan's deal machine: buy chip and software companies, cut their costs and keep the products customers can't easily replace. The latest was VMware, the data-center software company it bought in late 2023, which more than doubled its software business.

The AI boom turned the chip half into the growth engine. Broadcom co-designs custom AI chips, which it calls XPUs, for the cloud giants, and it has six such customers, including Google, Meta, Anthropic and OpenAI, according to its call transcripts reported by MarketBeat and TIKR. Google's TPU, its in-house AI chip, is the biggest of those programs.

The turn came on 4 June, when the stock **fell 12.6%**, its worst day since January 2025 in our data, after Broadcom guided its gross margin down and Tan said it would sell chips but not full server racks: "No racks. We are only chips," he said on the call, TechTimes and TheStreet reported. **So is Broadcom becoming a worse business, or just a bigger one with a different mix?**

## The business in one line

The world's biggest cloud and AI companies pay Broadcom for custom chips and networking chips, ordered years ahead, and thousands of companies pay it every year for VMware software that runs their data centers.

The software is the margin anchor: it earned a **94% gross margin** and an 84% operating margin last quarter, the release and the call showed. The chips are the growth: Broadcom guided to $34.8 billion of revenue for the quarter to early November, up 93% from a year earlier, with about $21.7 billion of it from AI chips.

## The numbers that matter

### Growth
Revenue grew **21.7% a year** over five fiscal years and 38.0% a year over the last two, against chipmaker medians of 14.2% and 22.0%, from its SEC filings. In the quarter to 2 August it grew 85.5%, the top quarter of the 23 chipmakers we track.

### Profitability
Its operating margin was **48.0%** over the 12 months to August 2026, in the top tenth of 21 chipmakers and 23 points wider than five years ago. Its GAAP operating margin hit 53.9% last quarter, the highest of any quarter in our data, which starts in late 2023, even as its gross margin fell.

### Cash
Broadcom turned **43.2% of revenue** into free cash flow over two years, the best of the 23 chipmakers we track, against a median of 23.7%. Last quarter alone it generated $13.7 billion.

### Returns
Return on equity was 38.4% and return on capital about 25.0%, both above the chipmaker medians of 23.1% and 17.8%. The returns are held down by the VMware purchase, which still sits on its balance sheet as goodwill and intangible assets.

### Balance sheet and shares
Net debt was **$35.4 billion** at 2 August, 0.8 years of operating profit, which is light for a company of its cash flow. The weak spot is dilution: its diluted share count rose 4.8% in two years, in the bottom fifth of chipmakers.

## Why the stock is where it is

1. **The margin guide.** In June Broadcom guided its adjusted gross margin to about 74%, down from 77%, because custom AI chips earn less than software, The Motley Fool and TechTimes reported. It came in at 75% in the quarter to August, and it guides to about 73% for the quarter to November, Yahoo Finance reported.
2. **Google's second supplier.** Broadcom said on its calls that it expects "some modest supplier diversification" at Google as TPU volumes grow, after Google added MediaTek to the program, TIKR and INDmoney reported.
3. **No raise.** Investors wanted a higher long-term AI forecast in June and didn't get one, and AI revenue guidance of about $16 billion fell short of what some had hoped for, INDmoney and TechTimes reported.
4. **The AI chip selloff.** The stock **sank 10.4%** on 5 November, with no company news, as AI chip stocks fell on worries that the midterm results would slow data-center building, Seeking Alpha and TIKR reported ([our market wrap that day](/news/2026-11-05/stock-market-today-nasdaq-sinks-5-5-into-a-correction-on-midterm-ai-fears/)). That close of $291.53 was its 52-week low.

## Testing the worry

**The margin squeeze is lasting but contained.** It is lasting because AI chips will keep growing faster than software: they go from 56% of sales last quarter to about 62% in the guide for this one. It is contained because it is a mix effect, not a price war. Software margins rose, and operating income still grew faster than revenue: GAAP operating margin went from 36.9% a year earlier to 53.9%. Each point of gross margin on $34.8 billion of quarterly sales is about $350 million a quarter, against $13.7 billion of quarterly free cash flow.

**Google's diversification is real but slow.** Broadcom itself called it modest, and its AI guide still rose from $16 billion for the quarter to August to $21.7 billion for the quarter to November. Tan said on 2 September that Broadcom had "secured the supply to again double AI revenue to approximately $115 billion" in fiscal 2027, and that "our demand actually exceeds this outlook," Fierce Network and MarketBeat reported.

**The selloffs were about the sector, not Broadcom.** The 5 November drop came on no company news, and the stock rose 24.1% in the next four sessions without any either. That worry does not survive; the margin worry does, in a smaller form.

## The moat

1. **Design partnerships that last years.** Custom chips are co-designed with each customer over several generations, so switching partner means redoing work. Evidence: six XPU customers, with AI chip revenue up 221% in a year.
2. **Software customers can't easily leave.** VMware runs inside most large companies' data centers. Evidence: a 94% gross margin and an 84% operating margin in the software segment.
3. **Scale that turns into cash.** Evidence: a 43.2% free cash flow margin over two years, first of 23 chipmakers.

The rivals: Nvidia ($NVDA) earns a 65.2% operating margin selling its own GPUs, more than Broadcom's 48.0%; Marvell ($MRVL), the closest rival in custom chips, earns 16.5%; and AMD ($AMD) earns 17.7%, from their SEC filings.

## The risks we'd watch

1. **Too few buyers.** One distributor took 32% of Broadcom's revenue in fiscal 2025, and its top five end customers about 40%, its annual report said. With AI chips heading to 62% of sales, a pause by one cloud giant would show quickly.
2. **Margins below 73%.** If the gross margin keeps falling a point or two each quarter, that is $350 million to $700 million of quarterly gross profit each time, on current sales.
3. **China.** Sales delivered to China, including Hong Kong, were 17% of revenue in fiscal 2025, the annual report said, exposing it to any new trade restrictions.

| Price to free cash flow | 41.8x | n/a in our data | Marvell 78.0x |

The trailing history is inflated by VMware's purchase costs, which cut GAAP earnings per share to $1.23 in fiscal 2024 from $3.30 the year before, so the gap overstates how cheap the stock is. On expected earnings, the market pays less than the median chipmaker for a company guiding to 93% growth next quarter, so it assumes either that growth fades sharply after 2027 or that margins keep falling. Neither is in the company's guidance.

## Our verdict

**Business: Strong. Price: Below its usual price** (46.5 times trailing earnings, against a median of 89.2 times since September 2024). Broadcom's sales and profits are compounding faster than almost any chipmaker's, and the margin decline is the cost of a mix it chose, not a sign of weak pricing. What would change our mind is a gross margin guide below 73% or a cut to the $115 billion fiscal 2027 AI target when it reports its fiscal fourth quarter in December. The tension is plain: Broadcom is selling more than ever, and keeping less of each dollar, and the stock prices in only the second half of that.
