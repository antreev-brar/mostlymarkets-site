Analog Devices ($ADI) reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of **$4.37 billion** on Tuesday morning, up 42% from a year earlier and above the $4.32 billion Zacks consensus. Adjusted earnings of $4.06 a share beat the roughly $3.88 to $3.89 analysts expected. But its forecast of $3.82 a share for the January quarter fell short, and the shares **slid 7.7%** to $349.70 before the open, as of 7:14 a.m. ET, giving back most of Monday's 9.5% jump.

## What stood out

Industrial, the chips that run factory machines, test equipment and power systems, **grew 55% to $2.19 billion**, half of all sales, the release showed. It is the business most tied to the factory cycle, so its growth says that recovery was still running in the fall. Communications, which includes data-center networking, grew 81%, while automotive rose 21% and consumer only 7%. *Verdict: broad growth, with the cyclical industrial business doing the heavy lifting.*

Chief Executive Vincent Roche pushed back on the idea that this is an AI story, saying in the release that "more than two-thirds of our growth in fiscal 2026 came from non-AI-centric market opportunities." That matters for the stock: it was swept up in both the AI sell-off last week and Monday's AI rebound, as [our story on the chip rally](/news/2026-11-23/tsmc-intel-and-marvell-shares-jump-what-happened/) showed.

Profitability jumped with volume. The adjusted operating margin rose **8.8 points to 52.3%**, because a chipmaker that owns its factories spreads fixed costs over more output. Free cash flow for the year was $5.5 billion, 36% of revenue.

## The outlook

For the January quarter, Analog Devices expects revenue of **$4.4 billion**, give or take $150 million, about 0.6% above the quarter just reported at the midpoint, and adjusted EPS of $3.82, give or take 20 cents. Investing.com reported the EPS guide was below the Bloomberg consensus, and other tallies put the analyst average at $3.86. The adjusted operating margin is guided to about 50.5%, nearly 2 points below the fourth quarter's.

## Our take

We were impressed by the quarter: 42% growth with a 52% operating margin gave the company its biggest quarter of revenue ever. On the other hand, the guide says sequential growth stalls and margins slip, and that lands on a stock that rose 9.5% the day before the report. At 23.4 times expected earnings against a 19.9 times median for the chipmakers we cover, Analog Devices was priced for another step up. One quarter doesn't change the long run, a 14.5% five-year growth rate in line with peers and a top-quarter cash margin, but the February report now has to show the pause is seasonal and not the top of the cycle.
