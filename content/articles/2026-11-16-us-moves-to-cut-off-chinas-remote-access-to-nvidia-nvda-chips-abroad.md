The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule on Monday that aims to stop Chinese companies from **renting advanced AI chips**, including Nvidia's ($NVDA) Blackwell line, through data centers outside China, CNBC and others reported. The rule requires a licence to send such chips to overseas data centers that serve companies headquartered in China, with conditional exemptions for US-operated sites that limit access from embargoed countries.

It is the first US policy response to the scandal that led Jensen Huang to resign as Nvidia's chief executive on Thursday, CNBC reported; that scandal centered on Megaspeed, a Singapore customer US intelligence reportedly suspects of spying for China ([our story](/news/2026-11-13/nvidia-nvda-shares-slide-after-jensen-huang-resigns-as-ceo/)). Renting computing power in third countries was the loophole that let Chinese firms use chips they cannot buy, CNBC and Gizmodo reported. Nvidia was **down 3.5%** at $157.13 in pre-market trading as of 9:15 a.m. ET, and AMD ($AMD) was down 5.0%, amid a wider AI selloff ([our morning story](/news/2026-11-16/asias-chip-rout-opens-nvidias-week-and-morgan-stanley-goes-max-overweight/)).

Nvidia reports third-quarter results after the close on Tuesday, and any comment on how much of its demand came through overseas cloud operators will show what the rule costs it.
