By Friday's close, at the end of the Nasdaq Composite's [worst month since March 2025](/daily/2026-10-31/), the 41 AI stocks we track sat a median **32.3%** below their 52-week closing highs. The list runs from chip designers to power suppliers and the biggest cloud spenders.

The selling didn't sort strong businesses from weak ones, and the fastest-growing third of the list ended it as its cheapest, priced below the median stock we cover. We read that as doubt about how long AI profits last, not about the demand behind them.

## October's selling landed on the hardware makers

Chip, memory, networking, equipment, and server makers took the brunt of the month, while the cloud spenders and power suppliers, as groups, fell far less.

Among the spenders, cash counted, but not on its own. Microsoft ($MSFT), whose free cash flow stayed positive in every quarter this year, **rose 1.8%** in October, while Alphabet ($GOOGL) and Meta ($META), which burned cash in the September quarter, fell more than 15%. Amazon ($AMZN) burned more cash than either and fell far less, a sign investors are weighing growth alongside cash ([our story](/news/2026-10-31/free-cash-flow-is-now-the-scorecard-for-ai-spenders-what-the-evidence-says/)).

## The drop ignored growth and margins

The fastest-growing third of our list ended October a median **30.1%** below its 52-week highs, about as far down as the slowest-growing third, at 32.4%. Wide margins didn't help either: the third with the widest operating margins sits about as far below its highs as the third with the thinnest.

Sandisk ($SNDK) and Intel ($INTC) make the point. Over their latest four quarters, Sandisk kept **72.5 cents** of operating profit per dollar of sales and Intel 2.9 cents, yet both stocks are about half off their highs.

## Low multiples are how markets price a peak

Investors who expect earnings to fall back once a boom ends pay little for this year's, so the multiple shrinks as the profits swell.

Memory is the clearest case: Micron ($MU) trades at **5.0 times** forward earnings and Sandisk at 5.4, the lowest on our list, on Wall Street's view that memory prices will turn ([our story](/news/2026-10-31/why-memory-stocks-have-fallen-for-four-months-while-profits-hit-records/)).

The discount extends to the AI chip leaders: Nvidia ($NVDA), at **16.9 times**, and Broadcom ($AVGO) trade below the median stock we cover, though their revenue grew more than five times as fast as the median company's.

## Nvidia grows like Palantir at less than a sixth of the price

The starkest gap is between Nvidia and Palantir Technologies ($PLTR). Over their latest four quarters, Palantir grew revenue about as fast as Nvidia at a narrower operating margin, yet investors pay **108.1 times** its expected earnings, more than six times Nvidia's multiple.

We read the gap as a bet on durability: the market treats Palantir's software sales as recurring and Nvidia's chip sales as tied to its customers' building budgets, which can be cut.

## Where investors still pay up

Eight of the ten highest multiples on our list belong to companies keeping less than 25 cents of operating profit per dollar of sales. Their prices pay for profits still to come.

Arm Holdings ($ARM) trades at **86.3 times** forward earnings even after losing more than half its value since June ([our chip list](/news/2026-10-31/3-chip-stocks-wed-skip-and-1-we-like-after-fridays-rout/)), and AMD ($AMD) at 44.8 times.

Power gear and grid work cost a premium for the opposite reason to memory: their sales are booked well ahead. Vertiv ($VRT) said its organic orders more than doubled in the September quarter, and Quanta Services ($PWR) said its backlog of signed work hit a record **$63.7 billion**; both stocks fell less than 4% in October.

GE Vernova ($GEV) trades at 40.8 times for revenue growth of 16.9%, a price for its orders, which nearly doubled last quarter.

## Our read

We think the doubt about how long profits last is overdone at Nvidia and Broadcom. Both turn more than 40 cents of each sales dollar into free cash, and Goldman Sachs expects the five biggest US cloud spenders to put about **$1.2 trillion** into AI infrastructure in 2027, half as much again as this year, Bloomberg reported in September.

At about 5 times, memory stocks are priced for a bust that long-term contracts make less likely: SK hynix said on 28 October that they cover more than half its 2027 and 2028 capacity.

The prices that worry us are at the other end: Palantir and Arm are priced for years of fast growth without a stumble, leaving little room for a soft forecast when they report this week.

What would change our mind on Nvidia is a cut to next year's data-center budgets. Its forecast on 17 November is the next hard read on them.
