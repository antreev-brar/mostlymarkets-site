## What happened
Shares of optical-networking maker Coherent ($COHR) **jumped 10.7%** to $217.21 by 10:30 a.m. ET on Friday, as buyers came back to the AI hardware stocks sold hardest in November. Rival laser-component maker Lumentum ($LITE) rose 5.7% at the same time. Coherent had no news of its own; Investing.com put the move down to renewed AI optimism, and Benzinga and Stocktwits described dip-buying across beaten-down AI names.

The rebound comes after a large insider bet. Director Stephen Pagliuca bought 82,000 shares for about $15.2 million on 20 November, the day the stock fell about 13%, then about $34.7 million more on Monday and Tuesday, Benzinga and 24/7 Wall St. reported from his filings. That is roughly **$50 million** at an average near $198 a share, just above Wednesday's close of $196.19. Coherent's board has also authorized a $2 billion share buyback this month.

## Why it matters
Coherent sells the optical transceivers that link AI chips inside data centers, so it trades on how much the cloud giants spend, and in November that trade unwound. Growth isn't the problem: revenue in the September quarter was 49.9% above a year earlier, against a peer median of 29.9%, from its SEC filings.

Cash is the weak spot. Free cash flow was negative over the last two years, at -12.6% of sales against a peer median of 13.4%, and diluted shares rose 31.5% in two years. A $2 billion buyback partly offsets that dilution, but it has to be paid for while the company is still burning cash.

## What the market is telling us
Coherent's shares moved 5% or more on 93 of the last 252 sessions, so double-digit days are common. At Wednesday's close the stock was up 6.3% this year and 54.0% below its 2 June high of $426.89.
