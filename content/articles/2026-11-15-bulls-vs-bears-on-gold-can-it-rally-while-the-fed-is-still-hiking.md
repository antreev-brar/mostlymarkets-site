Gold ended Friday, 13 November, near **$3,900 an ounce**, down more than 4% on the week in its worst week since early June, Reuters reported. On Thursday it hit a 13-month low, after October's consumer prices rose 0.4% against the 0.3% economists expected and futures traders priced in a Fed rate hike for 9 December. The metal that is supposed to protect against inflation is falling while inflation runs hot.

The argument is about who sets gold's price now. Bears say it is the Fed: every hike raises what investors give up by holding a metal that pays nothing. Bulls say it is the central banks, which bought **321 tonnes** in the third quarter, 42% more than a year earlier, the World Gold Council reported.

- **What's happening:** Gold lost more than 4% last week to about $3,900 an ounce, roughly 30% below its January record, as a hot CPI report and a 5.14% 10-year yield firmed up bets on a December Fed hike.
- **Why it matters:** It is the clearest test yet of gold as an inflation hedge: inflation is high, but the Fed's answer, higher real rates and a dollar at a 19-month high, is winning.
- **Our read:** The bears have the next four weeks; the bulls have the next two years. Even Goldman Sachs, after cutting its forecast, sees gold 6% above Friday's price by year-end, so the debate is about timing more than direction.

## The bull case

### Jeffrey Christian, managing partner, CPM Group

**The selling is close to done, and the next leg is up.** Christian sees support for gold near $3,800 and room for a rally toward **$5,000 into 2027**, IndexBox and Discovery Alert reported in recent days. His near-term call is cautious, not bearish: CPM's trade signal on 13 November told clients to "stand aside," with an expected range of $3,800 to $4,160 through 27 November, Kitco published.

### Louise Street, senior markets analyst, World Gold Council

**The buyers who matter most are buying more.** The council's third-quarter report on 3 November showed central banks adding an estimated 321 tonnes and gold funds taking in 211 tonnes, lifting their holdings to a record 4,256 tonnes, according to the council and Mining.com.au. Street called the quarter "a mixed bag" because jewellery and coin demand fell, but the council raised its expectation for full-year central-bank buying to at least match 2025's.

### Lina Thomas and Daan Struyven, commodity strategists, Goldman Sachs

**The Fed delays gold's rally; it doesn't cancel it.** In a 5 November note reported by Kitco and Quartz, they cut their end-2026 forecast to **$4,150** from $4,650 and their end-2027 forecast to $4,800 from $5,400, saying a hawkish Fed, including a December hike, will hold back worries about the dollar's value. Their support is structural: they expect central banks to buy about 60 tonnes a month in 2026 and 2027, against 17 tonnes a month before 2022, InvestingLive reported. Even their bear case, a further hike in March, has gold at $4,660 by the end of 2027.

## The bear case

### Edward Meir, analyst, Marex

**Hot inflation is bad news for gold while the Fed is the one responding.** When the CPI report came out on Tuesday, 10 November, gold fell nearly 3%. "I think it's a bit of a short-term negative for gold because the inflation numbers were running a little higher than expected," Meir said, in comments reported by CNBC and Mining.com. The mechanism is the one that broke the hedge: inflation brings hikes, and hikes lift the yield on cash and bonds that compete with gold.

### Tai Wong, independent metals trader

**$4,000 is now a ceiling, not a floor.** After Friday's producer-price report, Wong told Reuters that core producer inflation at 0.3% a month is still too high for policymakers, and that gold may struggle to get back above **$4,000** with Fed hawks "out in force," Kitco and MarketScreener reported. Traders put a 25-basis-point December hike at an 89.4% chance after the report, down from 96.2% before it, according to the same Reuters story.

## Where they agree

Both sides say the Fed, not inflation, sets gold's price for now. Goldman's base case assumes the December hike, and Wong's ceiling assumes it too. Wall Street's view tilted the same way: 10 of 13 analysts in Kitco's weekly survey expect lower prices this week. The bulls' case rests on buyers with long horizons, central banks and funds, not on the next month.

They also agree on the backdrop. The dollar hit a 19-month high last week, USAGOLD and Investing.com reported, and the 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.14% on Friday in our data, the return a gold holder gives up by not owning the bond.

## The number that decides it

The Fed's statement and projections on **9 December**. A hike is priced in; what would move gold is a hint of more. Goldman's bear scenario is a second hike in March; a signal that December is the last hike would hand the bulls their opening. Before that, the minutes of the October meeting come out at 2 p.m. ET on Wednesday, 19 November, and will show how many officials wanted to move faster.

## Our read

**The evidence favors the bears until 9 December, and the bulls after that.** Every short-term driver points one way: a dollar at a 19-month high, a 5.14% 10-year yield and hike odds near 90%. But the longest-horizon buyers are still adding, central banks by 42% more than a year ago, and the bank that cut its forecast hardest still sees gold about 6% above Friday's price by year-end. The test is the 9 December projections: a second hike signalled for 2027 would put Goldman's $4,660 bear case in play.
