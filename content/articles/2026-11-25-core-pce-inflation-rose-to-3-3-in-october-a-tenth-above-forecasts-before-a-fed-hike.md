The Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge rose **3.8%** in the 12 months to October, up from 3.6% in September, the Commerce Department said at 8:30 a.m. ET on Wednesday. That matched forecasts, but core prices, which leave out food and energy, rose 3.3% after 3.1%, a tenth of a point above the 3.2% economists expected, CNBC and Seeking Alpha reported.

## A small miss, in the wrong direction

On the month, the price index **rose 0.3%**, in line with economists polled by Reuters. The miss sits in the yearly core figure, the one Fed officials watch most closely, because it strips out swings in gasoline and food. The Hill and Morningstar put the headline rate at its highest in five months.

The direction matters more than the size. Core inflation has now risen for two straight months, which is the pattern officials described in the [minutes of their last meeting](/news/2026-11-18/fed-minutes-show-almost-all-officials-expect-more-rate-hikes-and-flag-ai-as-an-inflation-risk/), when almost all of them expected more hikes. New York Fed President John Williams said on Monday he sees inflation [ending the year at 3.5%](/news/2026-11-23/feds-williams-sees-inflation-ending-2026-at-3-5-and-says-the-fed-must-act-over-time/); October's 3.8% leaves little room for that.

Fed funds futures put the odds of a quarter-point hike on 9 December at about 98%, CoinDesk and Briefs reported, which would lift the benchmark rate from 3.75% to 4% to a range of **4% to 4.25%**. That was priced before the release, so the data changed little: FXStreet said it did not trigger a significant repricing, and CNBC said bets on further hikes in 2027 eased slightly.

Treasury yields fell rather than rose. In our data the 10-year yield was at 4.84% at 9:15 a.m. ET, down 0.06 point, and the 2-year at 4.61%. Stock futures added to losses after the release, CNBC reported, with the market still weighed down by [reports of new tariffs on 16 economies](/news/2026-11-25/us-readies-new-tariffs-on-16-economies-and-nasdaq-futures-fall-2-2/), and the VIX, Wall Street's fear gauge, was up 19.8% at 31.6.

## What's next

We read October as confirming the December hike rather than changing it: a core rate of 3.3% and rising gives no official a reason to pause, and the market had already moved there. The open question is how far it goes in 2027: JPMorgan expects [hikes at each of the next three meetings](/news/2026-11-19/jpmorgan-now-expects-fed-rate-hikes-at-each-of-the-next-three-meetings/).

The Fed announces its decision on Wednesday, 9 December, and its new projections will show how many more hikes officials expect in 2027. Markets are closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving and close at 1 p.m. on Friday.
