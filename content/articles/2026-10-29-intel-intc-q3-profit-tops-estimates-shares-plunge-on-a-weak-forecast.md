Intel ($INTC) reported third-quarter revenue of $16.51 billion, up 21% year on year and in line with the roughly $16.5 billion analysts expected, according to Reuters. Adjusted earnings of 52 cents a share beat the 40 cents Wall Street was looking for. The shares plunged 15.5% after hours to $77.76 at 4:41 p.m. ET, because Intel's forecast for the last three months of the year came in well below estimates.

```highlights

- **Revenue:** $16.51 billion vs analyst estimates of about $16.50 billion (21% year on year, in line)
- **Adjusted EPS:** $0.52 vs analyst estimates of $0.40 (a 30% beat); GAAP EPS $0.60
- **Data Center and AI revenue:** $7.32 billion, up 78% year on year
- **PC chip revenue (Client Computing and Physical AI):** $8.10 billion, down 5% year on year
- **Adjusted gross margin:** 44.1%, up from 40.0% a year earlier
- **Q4 guidance:** revenue of $15.0-16.0 billion vs $17.07 billion expected (LSEG); adjusted EPS of $0.25 vs $0.43
- **Market value:** $487 billion at Thursday's close
```

## What stood out

Servers carried the quarter. Revenue in Data Center and AI, the unit that sells Xeon processors for servers, rose 78% to $7.32 billion. "Revenue, gross margin and earnings per share exceeded guidance, including our strongest data center revenue growth in 15 years," Chief Financial Officer David Zinsner said in the results release.

PCs went the other way. The Client Computing and Physical AI Group, which makes laptop and desktop chips and still brings in about half of Intel's product revenue, shrank 5% to $8.10 billion. Reuters said analysts had expected $8.98 billion.

Profitability improved. The adjusted gross margin, the share of sales left after the cost of making the chips, was 44.1%, against 42.0% in Intel's own forecast. GAAP earnings of 60 cents a share were down from 90 cents a year earlier.

## The outlook

The forecast is what sank the stock. Intel expects fourth-quarter revenue of $15.0 billion to $16.0 billion, against $17.07 billion expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters. It sees adjusted earnings of 25 cents a share, where analysts wanted 43 cents, and an adjusted gross margin of 38.0%, down from 44.1%.

Two things are weighing on it. A shortage of memory chips has pushed up the price of PCs and hurt demand, Reuters and Bloomberg reported. "Looking ahead, we see the PC slowdown continuing at least through the first half of next year," Zinsner said in the release.

The second is a deadly explosion in October at Intel's chip factory in Kiryat Gat, Israel. Reuters and Yahoo Finance reported that it will cut about $300 million from fourth-quarter revenue. Supply is now the constraint for servers: "Our immediate priority is to unlock additional supply to meet extraordinary demand for our Xeon family," Chief Executive Lip-Bu Tan said in the release.

## Our take

The quarter itself was good: the fastest data-center growth in 15 years and margins above plan. But the stock had risen 149.5% this year going into the report, and a forecast that sits about $1.6 billion below estimates at its midpoint leaves little room for that. Next quarter, we'll watch whether Intel can make enough Xeon chips, how quickly Kiryat Gat recovers, and whether memory prices keep squeezing PC sales.
