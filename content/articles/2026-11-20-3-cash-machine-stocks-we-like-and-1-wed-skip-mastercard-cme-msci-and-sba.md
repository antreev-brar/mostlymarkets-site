On Friday the S&P 500 fell 3.7% and [entered a correction](/news/2026-11-20/stock-market-today-the-s-p-500-enters-a-correction-as-nvidia-sinks-14/), and three companies that don't need the market's money held up better than the index. CME Group **rose 0.4%**, Mastercard slipped 0.15% and MSCI lost 2.2%. A company that turns half its sales into free cash, the money left after running the business and buying equipment, can keep paying owners when funding gets dearer.

We ranked 16 large US companies on free-cash-flow margin over the 12 months to September 2026, from SEC filings, then judged each on growth, margins and debt against its industry.

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Mastercard ($MA)
**Free-cash-flow margin: 44.3% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Mastercard runs a card network, taking a small fee on each payment, and sells fraud and data services to banks.

1. **It is a cash machine that still grows fast.** Revenue rose 16.5% a year over five fiscal years, against a 9.4% median for the 46 exchanges, payment firms and asset managers in our coverage, and EPS compounded **21.0% a year**. Its free-cash-flow margin averaged 48.8% over two years, top tenth of that group.
2. **The latest quarter kept the pace.** Net revenue rose 14% to $9.8 billion and adjusted EPS was $5.31, above the $5.17 consensus cited by Newsquawk and StockStory, it reported on 29 October.
3. **The cash goes back to owners.** It bought back $3 billion of stock in the third quarter, the release shows.

**The price:** 32.7 times trailing earnings, below its median of 36.5 times since October 2024; its forward P/E of 29.4 is well above the 16.1 peer median, a gap its growth earns.

**What could go wrong:** On 19 November Mastercard said hackers took customer names and emails from outside vendors, as the Wall Street Journal and GuruFocus reported; no card data was taken ([our story](/news/2026-11-19/jpmorgan-jpm-visa-and-mastercard-say-hackers-leaked-customer-data-from-their-vendors/)). The bigger risk is spending: cross-border volume, its richest fees, grew 11% in the third quarter, and a sharp slowdown there would hit profit first.

### 2. CME Group ($CME)
**Free-cash-flow margin: 62.1% in the 12 months to September 2026.** CME runs the world's biggest futures exchange and earns a fee on every contract traded and cleared.

1. **None of its peers keeps more.** Its operating margin of **65.7%** in the past 12 months is the highest of the 26 peers with comparable data, against a 26.8% median, and it widened 10.9 points over five years.
2. **Volatility is its raw material.** Average daily volume hit a third-quarter record of 29.4 million contracts and revenue was about $1.7 billion, a record for a third quarter, CME said on 21 October, as Mondo Visione and Earnings Whispers reported.

**The price:** 25.2 times trailing earnings, just under its median of 26.0 times since November 2024; 24.4 times forward earnings is above the 16.1 peer median.

**What could go wrong:** Sales grew 6.0% a year over five years, bottom quarter of its peers: calm markets cost it volume. Chairman and Chief Executive Terrence Duffy sold about $8 million of stock, Investing.com and Benzinga reported on Friday. We first liked CME on [3 November](/news/2026-11-03/2-cash-machine-stocks-we-like-and-1-wed-skip/); a monthly volume report below a year earlier would be the first crack.

### 3. MSCI ($MSCI)
**Free-cash-flow margin: 45.9% in the 12 months to September 2026.** MSCI builds the stock indexes funds track and sells data and risk tools by subscription.

1. **Per-share growth compounds.** EPS grew **17.1% a year** over five years, ahead of the 15.7% peer median, helped by an 8.2% cut in its share count over two years.
2. **Clients stay, mostly.** Third-quarter operating revenue rose 12.4% to $892 million and adjusted EPS rose 14.3% to $5.11; the retention rate was 95.0%, MSCI said on 20 October. The weak spot is Analytics, whose revenue grew 4.8% against 18.1% in Index.

**The price:** 32.3 times trailing earnings, below its median of 36.6 times since October 2024. The stock is down 4.2% since we first liked it on 3 November.

**What could go wrong:** Part of its revenue is a fee on assets in funds tracking its indexes, so falling stock prices cut it directly. A retention rate below 95% in its fourth-quarter results would say the Analytics weakness is spreading.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### SBA Communications ($SBAC)
**Free-cash-flow margin: 36.9% in the 12 months to September 2026.** SBA owns cell towers and rents space on them to wireless carriers.

1. **The US business is shrinking.** Domestic site-leasing revenue fell 3.4% to $454.4 million in the third quarter, as Sprint and EchoStar leases ended, Earnings Whispers and Quartr reported from its 2 November results. Total revenue fell **2.5%** from a year earlier, in the bottom tenth of the 30 property companies in our coverage.
2. **The cash is thinner than it looks.** Over two years free cash flow was 27.0% of sales, below the 31.3% median for property peers, and AFFO, the per-share cash measure REITs use, fell to $3.19 from $3.30, the release shows.
3. **The debt is heavy.** Net debt was $12.5 billion, or 9.2 years of operating profit, and on 6 November it took a $2 billion loan to refinance tower bonds, Reuters and TradingView reported.

**The price:** 18.7 times trailing earnings, below its median of 24.2 times since November 2024, and 22.5% below its April high.

**What would change our mind:** domestic site-leasing revenue back above a year earlier in its fourth-quarter results, showing the carrier churn has run its course.
