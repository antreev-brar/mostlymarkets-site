On Friday, 20 November, two makers of laboratory tools sat at the top of our momentum screen: Mettler-Toledo and Revvity had each gained about half their value in six months, while the S&P 500 closed the week in a correction. Their charts look alike; their businesses don't.

Our screen ranked the companies we cover by **share price change over the six months to 20 November**, from our price files, and left out stocks we listed in the last 14 days. We then judged the leaders on five years of growth, margins, cash and returns against their peers, from SEC filings.

```picks
like: MTD, ROP, DUOL
skip: RVTY
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Mettler-Toledo ($MTD)
**Six-month return: 51.1%, to 20 November**. Mettler-Toledo makes precision balances, lab instruments and industrial inspection equipment.

1. **Earnings compound faster than sales.** Revenue grew 5.5% a year over five fiscal years, below the 8.2% median of the 65 health care stocks we cover, but earnings per share grew **11.0% a year**, above the median, because it buys back stock: diluted shares fell 5.8% in two years, in the top quarter.
2. **It turns sales into cash.** Its free cash flow margin was 21.6% over the last two years, against a peer median of 15.0%.
3. **The latest quarter raised the bar.** Sales rose 6% to $1.094 billion in the third quarter and adjusted EPS rose 13% to $12.57, its release said, and it lifted its 2026 forecast to $47.70 to $47.90 a share and guided to 10% to 12% growth in 2027, as Zacks and Finviz reported ([our earnings story](/news/2026-11-06/mettler-toledo-mtd-rises-as-axon-axon-sinks-17-thursday-evenings-earnings/)).

**The price:** 36.1 times trailing earnings, above its median of 31.9 times since November 2024 and near the top of its 23.9 to 38.8 range.

**What could go wrong:** at 33.6 times expected earnings it costs almost twice its peers' 17.8 median, so a 2027 short of its own 10% growth forecast would leave little room.

### 2. Roper Technologies ($ROP)
**Six-month return: 40.6%, to 20 November**. Roper owns a portfolio of niche software businesses, plus a few instrument makers.

1. **It grows steadily, mostly by buying.** Revenue grew **14.5% a year** over five fiscal years, and organic growth, which excludes acquisitions, was 7% in the third quarter, its release said, as Finviz and Grafa reported.
2. **Its margins are software margins.** Gross margin was 69.3% over the last two years and operating margin 28.0% over the 12 months to September 2026.
3. **It is shrinking its share count.** Diluted shares fell 8.0% in two years, and adjusted EPS rose 15% to $5.89 in the third quarter as it raised its full-year forecast to $22.35 to $22.40 a share.

**The price:** 18.1 times trailing earnings, against a median of 31.0 times since November 2024. The run is a recovery: the stock is up only 3.3% this year.

**What could go wrong:** buying growth has a cost. Return on capital is about 6.5% and net debt is 4.0 years of operating profit, and analysts flagged slowing bookings in its core enterprise software and a 4% drop in third-quarter free cash flow, Finviz reported.

### 3. Duolingo ($DUOL)
**Six-month return: 36.0%, to 20 November**. Duolingo runs the language-learning app.

1. **It grows faster than almost any consumer company we cover.** Revenue grew 31.0% a year over the last two years, in the top tenth of 66 consumer stocks, and daily active users grew **29% to 64.9 million** in the third quarter, faster than the 23% of the quarter before, its release said, as Zacks reported.
2. **It is a cash machine.** Its free cash flow margin was 35.5% over the last two years, the highest of its group, and its operating margin has widened 23 points in five years.
3. **The market already rewarded the quarter.** The stock jumped 26.0% on 4 November after the report, then gave much of it back: it closed 30.6% below its 52-week high on Friday.

**The price:** 47.8 times expected earnings, against 19.3 for its peers, but only 17.0 times free cash flow.

**What could go wrong:** paid subscribers grew only 14% to 13.1 million while users grew 29%, Finviz noted. Share count rose 4.1% in two years, in the bottom quarter.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Revvity ($RVTY)
**Six-month return: 49.8%, to 20 November**. Revvity sells life-science research tools and diagnostic tests.

1. **It hasn't grown for five years.** Revenue shrank 5.5% a year over five fiscal years, the weakest of the 18 medical device makers we cover with five years of data, and even the last two years' 4.0% a year is in the bottom quarter.
2. **It is less profitable than it was.** Its operating margin was 12.6% over the 12 months to October 2026, down 10.4 points in five years, and return on capital was about **3.2%**, against a peer median of 9.6%.
3. **The new targets are the old ones.** At its investor day on 13 November it set a long-term organic growth goal of 6% to 8%, TradingView and Quartr reported, the same range it gave at its 2024 investor day. The stock fell 7.5% between 12 and 20 November.

**The price:** 64.3 times trailing earnings, against a median of 48.6 times since November 2024, and 26.2 times expected earnings, against its peers' 17.8.

**What would change our mind:** organic growth that holds at the 7% of the [third quarter](/news/2026-11-03/revvity-rvty-shares-soar-after-a-q3-beat-and-a-higher-growth-forecast/) for a full year, well above the about 5% it expects for 2026, with the wider margin that came with it.
