On Monday, Alphabet ($GOOGL) began selling bonds in euros, its second euro sale this year after one in May, Bloomberg and Reuters, citing IFR, reported. The **seven-part** deal runs from two to 19 years, and early price talk put the longest slice at about 205 to 210 basis points over mid-swaps, a benchmark lending rate; the size will be set when it prices later today.

The timing makes it a test of the AI trade's credit. Alphabet's shares fell **12.6%** on 29 October, the worst day since Google's 2004 listing, after search revenue missed estimates and its free cash flow turned negative, at -$9.07 billion, on $51.36 billion of capital spending ([our story](/news/2026-10-29/alphabet-googl-shares-plunge-after-search-falls-short-and-cash-burn-grows/)), and investors have been pricing in more risk on the debt that pays for data centers ([our narrative on AI cash flow](/news/2026-10-31/free-cash-flow-is-now-the-scorecard-for-ai-spenders-what-the-evidence-says/)). Strong demand at tight spreads would say lenders still treat Alphabet as a top-quality borrower, unlike Oracle or CoreWeave.

The final size and price, due later today, are the numbers to watch. The shares were up 1.3% before the open, at 6:14 a.m. ET, still 30.5% below their May high.
