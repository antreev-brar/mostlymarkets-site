Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE) reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of **$14.55 billion** after the close on Monday, up 50% from a year earlier and just above the $14.4 billion analysts expected, according to the Zacks consensus. Adjusted earnings of $1.43 a share beat the $1.28 consensus and the top of HPE's own $1.20-to-$1.30 forecast. The stock slipped 2.8% to $46.70 after hours, as of 4:15 p.m. ET, having closed up 17.4% at $48.02.

## What stood out

Servers did the work. Revenue from the Cloud & AI segment, which holds HPE's servers, storage and AI systems, **rose 68.6% to $11.3 billion**, and servers alone almost doubled to $8.8 billion, the release said. The segment's operating margin roughly doubled to 18.4%, so the extra volume came with better pricing or mix rather than at any cost. *Verdict: a record quarter for revenue, and the margin says it isn't just low-profit AI boxes.*

Networking, the business HPE bought Juniper Networks to build, grew 12.3% to $3.1 billion, but its operating margin slipped to 22.1% from 23.4% a year earlier. That is the slower, higher-margin half of the story, and it lost a little ground.

The one debate is margins. Adjusted gross margin fell 2.2 percentage points from the third quarter to 38.2%, the release showed, as a bigger share of sales came from servers. HPE had said in September that a bigger mix of AI systems would pull its fourth-quarter operating margin below the third quarter's, and memory costs are the worry Citi raised before the report.

## The outlook

HPE forecast first-quarter revenue of $14.0 billion to $14.6 billion, 51% to 57% growth on a year earlier, and adjusted EPS of $1.30 to $1.40. For fiscal 2027 it guided to revenue of $59.4 billion to $63.1 billion and adjusted EPS of **$5.05 to $5.35**, against $3.75 to $3.85 it last forecast for fiscal 2026. At the midpoints, that is about 37% growth in adjusted EPS. HPE also raised its quarterly dividend to 17 cents from 14.3 cents.

## Our take

We were impressed by the server growth and the doubling in Cloud & AI margins; a 2-point drop in gross margin and a softer networking margin are the cost of that mix. The muted after-hours reaction says the beat was largely priced in by Monday's 17.4% jump, the stock's second-biggest move of the past year, which came as Nvidia's [rehiring of Jensen Huang](/news/2026-11-23/why-dell-technologies-dell-stock-is-soaring-today/) lifted AI-server stocks. HPE still trades at 10.3 times expected earnings against a 17.2 times median for the hardware makers in our data, and its 6.2% operating margin over the past 12 months, a bottom-quarter figure among them, is what this guidance has to lift.
