Four big companies reported after the close on Thursday, and on Friday the market sorted them by their forecasts. Mettler-Toledo ($MTD) and Ameren ($AEE) raised their 2026 outlooks and held up, while Axon Enterprise ($AXON) cut its sales forecast and **sank 17.2%** as of 2:15 p.m. ET, and Affirm ($AFRM) beat estimates but fell on a rise in late payments.

All four had already fallen in Thursday's regular session, when the [Nasdaq sank 5.5% into a correction](/news/2026-11-05/stock-market-today-nasdaq-sinks-5-5-into-a-correction-on-midterm-ai-fears/): Axon by 9.8% and Affirm by 7.8% before their results came out.

## Mettler-Toledo ($MTD): Asia lifts a beat and a higher forecast

The maker of laboratory and industrial scales earned an adjusted **$12.57 a share**, up 13% from $11.15 and ahead of the $12.11 Zacks consensus cited by the AP and the $12.09 S&P Global consensus cited by StockStory, the company said in its results release. Sales rose 6% to $1.09 billion, above the $1.07 billion analysts expected, StockStory and Reuters reported; Dow Jones credited gains in Asia, where local-currency sales grew 14%. Mettler raised its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast to $47.70 to $47.90, from $47.15 to $47.50, and gave a first 2027 range of $52.50 to $53.50. Shares were up 4.0% at $1,746.04 as of 2:15 p.m. ET, above their highest close in our five years of price data, after opening at $1,806.58.

## Ameren ($AEE): a beat and a bigger building plan

The Missouri and Illinois utility earned **$2.42 a share**, against the $2.29 average of six analysts surveyed by Zacks, the AP reported, and up from $2.17 a year earlier on the adjusted basis the company uses (GAAP was $2.35, flattered by a tax benefit), the release said. It raised its 2026 EPS range to $5.35 to $5.55, from $5.25 to $5.45, set 2027 at $5.70 to $5.90, and laid out $38.1 billion of capital spending for 2027 to 2031 to support 8% to 10% annual EPS growth, mostly to serve signed data-center load. Like [Evergy](/news/2026-11-06/evergy-evrg-beats-q3-estimates-and-raises-its-2026-forecast-on-heat-and-data-centres/) this week, it is a utility whose growth now hangs on data centers. Shares were up 0.2% at $113.09 as of 2:15 p.m. ET.

## Axon Enterprise ($AXON): a miss and an outlook cut

The maker of Tasers and police body cameras grew revenue **28%** to $909 million and earned an adjusted $1.72 a share, both short of estimates, and lowered its full-year revenue growth forecast because federal deployments of its Dedrone drone-detection business were delayed. It also put $1.2 billion more into drone maker Skydio. Shares were down 17.2% at $316.93 as of 2:15 p.m. ET and touched $307.00, below their 52-week closing low of $345.94 from 10 April. [Our full story on Axon](/news/2026-11-06/why-axon-enterprise-axon-stock-is-sinking-today/).

## Affirm ($AFRM): a beat, with credit costs rising

The buy-now-pay-later lender's revenue for its fiscal first quarter (the three months to September) **rose 35% to $1.26 billion**, and it earned 47 cents a share on a GAAP basis, the company said in its quarterly filing, ahead of the Zacks consensus of $1.22 billion and 35 cents; GuruFocus and Chartmill also called it a beat. Gross merchandise volume, the value of purchases it financed, rose 34% to $14.4 billion, and Affirm raised its full-year target to more than $65.25 billion, from more than $64 billion, Investing.com and Quartr reported. But delinquencies ticked up, Dow Jones reported, and a second-quarter forecast of $17.9 billion to $18.2 billion of volume read as cautious, Yahoo Finance and Investing.com said. The shares fell as much as 11% after hours on Thursday and were down 3.0% at $67.55 as of 2:15 p.m. ET.

## What they say together

Three of the four beat estimates, but the market rewarded only the two whose forecast went up. Mettler and Ameren raised their 2026 ranges and put numbers on 2027, and they are the two shares in the green. Axon's cut and Affirm's softer second-quarter volume guide did the damage, even though Affirm beat on every headline line.

The second lesson is about what investors will pay for growth after Thursday's rout. Axon, valued at about 36 times expected earnings in our quote data, lost more on a delayed contract than Affirm did on a credit worry, a sign that the most expensive growth stocks have the least room for a soft forecast. Their next reports, early next year, will show whether the federal deals close and whether Affirm's late payments level off.
