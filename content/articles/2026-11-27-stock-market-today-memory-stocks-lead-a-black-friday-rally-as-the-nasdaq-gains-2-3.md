On Friday, in a half-day session that ended at 1 p.m. ET, buyers came back from Thanksgiving to the AI stocks they had sold all month. The Nasdaq Composite **rose 2.3%** to 24,129.50, the S&P 500 gained 1.6% to 7,297.49, and the Dow added 592 points, or 1.2%, to 50,774.94. Sandisk ($SNDK) led the gainers with a 16.0% jump, Reuters and the Wall Street Journal both described the day as a rebound in battered AI shares, and the session left all three indexes with weekly gains.

## Memory and optical stocks bounced hardest

The stocks that fell furthest in November rose most. Sandisk **soared 16.0%** to $85.36, its eighth-biggest move of the past year, and Micron ($MU) gained 7.6% to $646.95, with Seagate ($STX) up 7.9% and Western Digital ($WDC) up 7.2%. Our [morning story on the storage bounce](/news/2026-11-27/sandisk-everpure-and-dell-shares-jump-what-happened/) found no company news behind it; outlets including Investing.com called it dip-buying in oversold names.

Optical networking suppliers joined in: Coherent ($COHR) closed 14.1% higher, after [an 11% gain by mid-morning](/news/2026-11-27/coherent-cohr-shares-jump-11-in-a-rebound-for-optical-ai-stocks/), and Lumentum ($LITE) added 8.0%. The size of the hole matters more than the bounce: Micron still closed 46.7% below its June high, and its volume was only 64% of its 20-day average, the mark of a thin holiday session.

## Nvidia sat out the rally

Nvidia ($NVDA) **edged up 0.8%** to $150.49, the weakest showing among the big AI names, and Reuters singled it out as a laggard. It was the stock's first full session since the Financial Times reported it had [halted server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries](/news/2026-11-26/nvidia-nvda-halts-server-shipments-to-four-southeast-asian-countries-ft-says/). Nvidia is down 30.1% over the past month in our data, even after Monday's 31.5% jump on Jensen Huang's return as chief executive.

Marvell ($MRVL) rose 7.3% and Dell ($DELL) 6.7%, while Tesla ($TSLA) gained 5.2% and Amazon ($AMZN) 4.0%. Because of their size, Amazon and Tesla added more points to the S&P 500 than Micron or Broadcom ($AVGO) in our contribution data. The reports we read tied both to the same rebound in tech, not to company news.

## Gold rose; Vertex fell

Gold futures **rose 2.9%** to $3,638.60 an ounce as of 2:01 p.m. ET, bouncing off a 15-month low and lifting miners such as Agnico Eagle ($AEM), which [jumped 7.5%](/news/2026-11-27/agnico-eagle-aem-shares-jump-as-gold-bounces-off-a-15-month-low/). Alibaba shares also [jumped after Beijing pledged market support](/news/2026-11-27/alibaba-baba-shares-jump-after-beijing-pledges-market-support/).

Vertex Pharmaceuticals ($VRTX) **fell 5.2%** to $534.57, the worst performer in the S&P 500, in its first session since the FDA [pushed back its decision on Vertex's kidney drug](/news/2026-11-25/vertex-vrtx-slides-after-hours-as-the-fda-delays-its-kidney-drug-decision-to-february/) to as late as 28 February.

## What the bounce didn't change

Bond yields rose with stocks: the 10-year Treasury yield climbed 9 basis points to 4.94%, a sign the rally came from investors leaving safe havens rather than from hopes for lower rates. Traders still put the odds of a Fed rate hike on 9 December near certainty, at about 98% to 99% in CME FedWatch data reported by Reuters and FXStreet, a day after [core PCE inflation came in at 3.3%](/news/2026-11-25/core-pce-inflation-rose-to-3-3-in-october-a-tenth-above-forecasts-before-a-fed-hike/).

The week's gains were large: the S&P 500 rose 5.6% from the 20 November close, when it [entered a correction](/news/2026-11-20/stock-market-today-the-s-p-500-enters-a-correction-as-nvidia-sinks-14/), and the Nasdaq 6.7%. Even so, Reuters and Finimize reported that both indexes remain on course for their worst November since 2008, with one session left on Monday. The next test is the November jobs report on Friday, 4 December, at 8:30 a.m. ET, the last big number before the Fed meets; our read on the shopper's side of that question is in [our Black Friday narrative](/news/2026-11-27/wall-street-is-betting-the-shopper-outlasts-a-fed-hike-black-friday-starts-the-test/).
