On Monday, investors kept selling the big AI hardware names a day before Nvidia reports, and Sandisk, Lumentum and Coherent all fell more than 8% by 10:30 a.m. ET ([our story](/news/2026-11-16/sandisk-seagate-and-western-digital-shares-slide-what-happened/)). Away from that trade sit mid-sized companies that make more profit on each sale than almost any technology giant, and get far less attention for it.

Our screen ranked companies worth $5 billion to $30 billion by operating margin, the share of sales left after all running costs, over their latest 12 months, from SEC filings in our fundamentals data, and left out stocks we listed in the last 14 days. We then judged the leaders on whether that margin is growing, turning into cash, and reaching owners, each against its own industry.

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. CF Industries ($CF)
**Operating margin: 41.6% in the 12 months to September 2026.** CF Industries makes nitrogen fertilizer, mostly ammonia and urea, from natural gas.

1. It has got much more profitable: the operating margin rose **17.4 points** over five years, the best gain among the 17 materials companies we can measure, against a median of 1.4 points.
2. Profit reaches owners: earnings per share grew 43.6% a year over five years, against a peer median of 12.4%, helped by a 15.1% cut in its share count over two years.
3. The latest quarter was strong: third-quarter net earnings rose to $2.87 a share from $2.19, on 7% more volume and prices 5% higher, the company said on 4 November, and it expects nitrogen markets to stay "constructive" into 2027.

**The price:** 9.4 times expected earnings against a peer median of 16.5 times, and its trailing P/E of 8.8 is below its two-year median of 11.3.

**What could go wrong:** natural gas. It is CF's main raw material, so a jump in US gas prices squeezes the margin, and $62 million of the third quarter's profit came from a one-off insurance recovery for its Yazoo City plant.

### 2. Texas Pacific Land ($TPL)
**Operating margin: 75.5% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Texas Pacific Land owns a large stretch of the Permian Basin in West Texas and collects royalties on the oil and gas drilled there, plus fees for water, pipelines and land use.

1. It has the highest margin of the 13 energy companies we can measure, against a median of **22.5%**, because it drills nothing: the oil companies pay the costs and it collects a share.
2. It is still growing: revenue rose 21.4% a year over five years and 34.6% in the third quarter, to a record $273.4 million, its SEC filing shows, with record produced-water royalties of $41.4 million.
3. It earns a 32.6% return on equity, in the top quarter of its peers, and it does it without diluting owners: its share count was flat over two years.

**The price:** at 29.8 times expected earnings it is the priciest stock in its group, against a median of 11.4 times. But its trailing P/E of 34.3 is near the bottom of its own two-year range of 32.0 to 88.7, and the stock has fallen 46.1% from its March high.

**What could go wrong:** royalties track oil prices and Permian drilling, so a long slump in crude would hit revenue directly; the premium to other energy stocks assumes its water and land income keeps growing faster than oil.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Copart ($CPRT)
**Operating margin: 35.4% in the 12 months to July 2026.** Copart runs online auctions for wrecked and used cars, mostly on behalf of insurers that have written them off.

1. The margin is shrinking: it fell **6.8 points** over five years, in the bottom 4% of the 89 industrial companies we cover, while their median rose 3.4 points.
2. Growth has stalled: revenue rose 2.4% in its fiscal fourth quarter to July, against a peer median of 10.3%, and 4.9% a year over two years, half its 11.6% pace over five.
3. Its core volume is falling: US insurance units sold fell 7.5% in that quarter and net income 17.4%, the company said on 10 September.

**The price:** 19.8 times expected earnings, just under the industrial median of 21.7 times, and its trailing P/E of 20.5 is well below its two-year median of 27.5. The stock is down 18.7% this year.

**What would change our mind:** US insurance volume. Copart reports its fiscal first quarter on Thursday 20 November, Investing.com and MarketBeat list; a return to growth in US insurance units would say the slump was the cycle, not lost share.
