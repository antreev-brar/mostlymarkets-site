Pfizer ($PFE) reports third-quarter results before the open on Tuesday, 3 November, with its shares at a **52-week closing high** of $30.67 after Monday's session. The stock is up 10.3% in the past month and 23.2% this year.

Last quarter, reported on 4 August, revenue rose 3% to about $15.0 billion and adjusted EPS of $0.77 beat the roughly $0.68 analysts expected, the release and Earnings Whispers show. Pfizer raised the midpoint of its 2026 revenue forecast by $500 million to $60.5 billion to $62.5 billion and kept adjusted EPS guidance at $2.80 to $3.00. The shares closed up 1.5% that day.

## What analysts expect

The Zacks consensus is adjusted EPS of **$0.72**, down from $0.87 a year earlier, on revenue of $16.13 billion, against $16.65 billion a year ago; MarketBeat has revenue nearer $15.86 billion. Much of the EPS drop is planned: Pfizer said in August that a $650 million charge for its licensing deal with Innovent Biologics would land in the third quarter, worth about 10 cents a share for the year.

## What to watch

- **The full-year EPS range.** Holding $2.80 to $3.00 after absorbing the Innovent charge would show the cost cuts are working; Pfizer announced $2.5 billion of extra savings for 2027 to 2029 in August.
- **The drugs losing patents.** Eliquis, Ibrance and Xtandi are nearing the end of their exclusivity, Zacks noted; how fast their sales fade sets the size of the hole.
- **The obesity pipeline.** Seeking Alpha's preview says analysts are looking to Pfizer's obesity programs for the growth to fill it, while TipRanks describes Wall Street as split on the stock going into the report.

## How the stock has done

Pfizer has not moved 5% or more on any of the last 252 sessions, so a big swing on Tuesday would be unusual. $1,000 invested five years ago would still be worth only $675 today, which is the gap the new-drug story has to close.
