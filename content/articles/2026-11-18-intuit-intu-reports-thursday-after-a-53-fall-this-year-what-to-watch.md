Intuit ($INTU) reports its fiscal first-quarter results after the market closes on Thursday, 19 November. It comes in as the worst of the big software stocks this year, **down 52.7%**, on fears that AI agents will do the tax and bookkeeping work that TurboTax and QuickBooks charge for.

Last quarter, on 25 August, Intuit guided fiscal 2027 revenue growth to 9% to 10%, down from 14% in fiscal 2026, its results release shows. Management said it would accept lower prices on do-it-yourself TurboTax filings to keep customers, and guided TurboTax revenue to grow only 2% to 3%, Quartz and the call transcript show. The shares opened 9.5% lower the next day in our price data and closed down 3.2%.

## What analysts expect

The Zacks consensus is **$2.47** of adjusted earnings per share, in line with MarketBeat's figure and inside Intuit's own guide of $2.44 to $2.48. Revenue is expected at about $4.30 billion, up from $3.88 billion a year earlier, the top of the company's $4.29 billion to $4.31 billion range. Intuit has beaten the Zacks EPS consensus in each of its last four quarters.

## What to watch

- **The full-year forecast.** Intuit reaffirmed revenue of $23.28 billion to $23.51 billion at its September investor day; any trim would feed the AI story the market is already selling.
- **QuickBooks.** The first quarter has almost no tax season in it, so the small-business platform is the clean read on whether customers are staying.
- **Customer counts.** With TurboTax losing customers on price, any number on how many small businesses and filers stayed is the direct test of the AI worry.

## How the stock has done

Intuit closed at $313.24 on Wednesday, down 2.35% on the day and 6.6% over the past month. It sits **53.7% below** its December 2025 high of $676.55, and on Tuesday it fell 7.2% in a software sell-off with no news of its own ([our story](/news/2026-11-17/salesforce-mongodb-and-sap-shares-slide-what-happened/)). At about 13 times forward earnings, the price already assumes AI erodes the business; the case that it won't is in [our narrative on AI and software seats](/news/2026-11-18/wall-street-is-betting-ai-will-empty-softwares-seats-3-numbers-will-decide-it/).
