Idexx Laboratories ($IDXX), which makes diagnostic tests and analysers for vets, said before the open on Monday that third-quarter earnings rose 18% to **$4.01 a share**, above the $3.71 analysts expected, according to estimates cited by GuruFocus and Nasdaq. Revenue rose 10% to $1.214 billion, against a $1.191 billion estimate. The shares jumped 6.1% to $585.00 in pre-market trading as of 7:18 a.m. ET, after falling 4.3% on Friday.

## What stood out

The number investors watch is CAG Diagnostics recurring revenue, the tests and consumables vets buy over and over for cats and dogs, and it **grew 11%** organically. Sales of VetLab consumables, the cartridges that run on Idexx's in-clinic analysers, rose 15% organically as the installed base of its premium instruments grew 11%, the release said. Reference-lab testing rose 10%; rapid assay kits, the quick snap tests, rose just 1% as testing volumes fell.

Chief Executive Mike Erickson credited new products: "Innovations like IDEXX inVue Dx with FNA, and IDEXX Cancer Dx becoming a multi-cancer panel, are helping practices detect disease earlier and work more efficiently." Idexx placed more than 1,900 inVue Dx analysers in the quarter. The quarter's EPS included 8 cents of help from an investment gain, currency moves, and tax benefits.

## The outlook

Idexx raised its 2026 organic revenue growth forecast to 9.3% to 10.0%, up 0.6 percentage points at the midpoint, and its EPS range to **$14.96 to $15.16**. It cut its full-year operating margin forecast to 32.1% to 32.3% to absorb costs from buying CoVet, an AI assistant for vet clinics, so the raise comes from sales, not margins.

## Our take

Good: recurring diagnostics grew at double digits in both the US and abroad, and free cash flow of 27.5% of sales over the past 12 months ranks in the top quarter of medical device makers in our coverage. The stock was down 18.5% this year and 28.1% below its November 2025 high going into the report, so the market had priced in slower growth than this. At 38.8 times trailing earnings against its own median of 44.7 times since mid-2024, the price is below its usual level; the test next quarter is whether instrument placements turn into the consumables growth that the margin cut is paying for.
