Citigroup ($C) booked trades at its UK securities unit using an internal risk model that regulators had not approved for those deals, understating the unit's risk-weighted assets by about **$35 billion**, Reuters reported late on Thursday, citing people familiar with the matter. Run through the standard model, the trades pushed the unit's risk-weighted assets, the measure that sets how much capital a bank must hold, sharply higher.

Citi called it a "capital modeling issue" at a European trading subsidiary that it found and fixed this summer, and said it injected capital to restore the unit's buffers. The bank said the subsidiary stayed above regulatory minimums throughout, with no effect on the holding company or its main US bank; Wells Fargo, reiterating its Overweight rating, said the error had zero impact on consolidated capital, Investing.com reported. Citi did not say how much capital it added.

The cost is less the capital than the timing. Chief Executive Jane Fraser has been trying to get regulators to lift restrictions on the bank, and as recently as the summer Citi aimed to file the paperwork this year, Reuters reported; a new risk-booking error makes that case harder. Citi shares fell 1.8% on Thursday and were down 0.5% at $114.76 before Friday's open, 20.9% below their June high. The Fed declined to comment, Reuters said.
