The S&P 500 closed in a correction on Friday 20 November, and the selling has hit good businesses along with the expensive ones. That is when a compounder, a company that grows sales and earnings year after year at high margins, can be bought nearer its usual price; it is also when a former compounder that has stopped growing starts to look cheap.

Our screen took every company in our coverage with an operating margin above 20% over the last 12 months, ranked them by revenue growth from their SEC filings, and left out stocks we listed in the last 14 days. We then judged each on five years of growth, margins and cash against its own industry, using our fundamentals data.

```picks
like: TDG, MSFT
skip: IT
```

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. TransDigm Group ($TDG)
**Revenue growth: 17.1% a year over five fiscal years to September 2026**. TransDigm makes proprietary aircraft parts, many of them sole-source, and earns most of its profit selling replacements to airlines.

1. **It compounds faster than its industry.** Sales grew 17.1% a year over five years, more than double the 8.0% median of the industrial companies we cover, and earnings per share grew 27.8% a year.
2. **Its pricing power shows in the margin.** The operating margin was 45.6% over the 12 months to September, the highest among 71 industrial peers, and it has widened 10.3 points in five years.
3. **The latest quarter kept the pace.** Fourth-quarter net sales rose 23% to $2.99 billion, the 10 November release said, though the stock fell after its fiscal 2027 earnings guide of $47.09 to $47.94 a share came in below analysts' estimates, Zacks and Finviz reported.

**The price:** 31.5 times trailing earnings, the bottom of its 31.5 to 54.7 range since November 2024, with the stock 22.7% below its January high.

**What could go wrong:** net debt of $31.6 billion is 6.6 years of operating profit, so a slump in air travel would hit a heavily borrowed business.

### 2. Microsoft ($MSFT)
**Revenue growth: 18.8% in the quarter to September 2026, against 14.6% a year over five years**. Microsoft sells software, cloud computing through Azure, and AI services to companies and consumers.

1. **Growth is speeding up at its size.** Revenue rose to $92.3 billion in the quarter to September, and Azure grew 48%, up from 42% the quarter before, Redmond Magazine and Zacks reported.
2. **It is far more profitable than its peers.** The operating margin was 47.1% over the last 12 months, against a 9.7% median for the software companies we cover, and earnings per share grew 17.4% a year over five years.
3. **Its returns hold up.** Return on capital is about 26.5%, in the top quarter of its peers, even after years of data-center spending.

**The price:** 25.3 times trailing earnings, against a median of 32.7 times since October 2024; the stock is up 0.2% this year and 12.8% below its 4 November high.

**What could go wrong:** capital spending doubled to $39.4 billion in the September quarter from a year earlier, 24/7 Wall St. and Zacks reported, and free cash flow fell to 17.3% of sales over 12 months from 21.5% over two years. If AI demand slows, that spending stops paying back.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Gartner ($IT)
**Revenue growth: down 1.3% in the quarter to September 2026, against 9.6% a year over five years**. Gartner sells research subscriptions and advice to technology and business executives.

1. **The compounding has stalled.** Revenue grew 2.5% a year over the last two years, the slowest of the IT services companies we cover, and fell in the latest quarter.
2. **The key number is barely growing.** Contract value, the annual worth of its subscriptions, rose 2.5% to $5.3 billion in the third quarter, the 3 November release said, and executives said growth excluding US federal clients would miss their earlier year-end target, Nasdaq's call transcript and Finviz reported.
3. **Buybacks are doing the work.** Earnings per share grew 26.7% a year over five years while diluted shares fell 18.0% in two; the company bought back $1.7 billion of stock in the last four quarters, the release said. That lifts per-share numbers without growing the business.

**The price:** 12.4 times trailing earnings, against a median of 18.4 times since November 2024, which already prices slower growth.

**What would change our mind:** contract value growth back above 5% a year, which would show clients are renewing despite AI tools that compete with its research. Its fourth-quarter results are the next check.
