A high gross margin, the share of each sales dollar left after the direct cost of what a company sells, says a product has pricing power. It says nothing about what is left once salaries, marketing and research are paid. On Saturday, after a week in which buyers came back to technology stocks, we screened the companies we cover on gross margin over the latest 12 months in their SEC filings, then asked which of the leaders carry that margin all the way to operating profit and cash.

Three do, and trade near or below their peers' multiples. One, ServiceNow, keeps a gross margin in the 70s but less than a tenth of its sales as operating profit.

```picks
like: APP, META, PAYX
skip: NOW
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. AppLovin ($APP)
**Gross margin: 88.8% in the 12 months to September 2026.** AppLovin sells software that places ads inside mobile games and, increasingly, for online shops.

1. **The margin reaches the bottom line.** Its operating margin was **77.8%** over the same 12 months, the highest of the 25 communication services stocks we cover, against an 18.3% median. Five years ago it was close to break-even.
2. **It is still growing fast.** Revenue rose 30.4% a year over five years, in the top quarter of its group, and 55.5% in the September quarter.
3. **The latest quarter beat its own forecast.** Third-quarter revenue rose 55% to about $2.18 billion and Chief Financial Officer Matt Stumpf credited model improvements in the core gaming business, its release, Stockanalysis and PPC Land showed. It guided to $2.365 billion to $2.395 billion for the fourth quarter and added $3.2 billion to its buyback.

**The price:** 20.3 times expected earnings against a 16.7 times group median, but 26.9 times trailing earnings against its own median of 62.3 times since late 2024, with the shares 45.6% below their December 2025 high.

**What could go wrong:** The growth still comes mainly from mobile games, by the company's own account. If the push into e-commerce ads stalls, the premium to its peers has little to rest on.

### 2. Meta ($META)
**Gross margin: 81.0% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Meta runs Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp.

1. **Profit at the top of its group.** Its operating margin of 32.9% is in the top tenth of the 16 consumer internet companies we cover, against a 13.1% median.
2. **Growth sped up.** Revenue rose 24.4% a year over two years, faster than the 18.5% over five, and **26% in the third quarter** to $64.6 billion as the average price per ad rose 14%, its release and AP showed.
3. **It earns on its capital.** Return on capital of about 18.2% is in the top quarter of its peers.

**The price:** 19.7 times expected earnings against a 17.0 times median, and 21.4 times trailing earnings against its own median of 27.0 times since late 2024.

**What could go wrong:** The AI build-out is eating the cash. The free cash flow margin fell to 11.9% over 12 months from 17.0% over two years, net debt is $72.8 billion, and Meta guided 2026 capital spending to $135 billion to $145 billion with no 2027 figure yet. Its fourth-quarter report, due in late January, is the next test.

### 3. Paychex ($PAYX)
**Gross margin: 74.4% in the 12 months to August 2026.** Paychex runs payroll, HR and benefits for small and mid-sized businesses.

1. **Cash at the top of its group.** Free cash flow was **31.3% of sales** over two years, in the top tenth of the 89 industrial companies we cover, against a 12.7% median.
2. **Strong margins and returns.** Its operating margin of 39.2% is in the top tenth, and return on capital of about 26.7% in the top quarter.
3. **The quarter held its plan.** First-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue rose 6% to $1.63 billion and adjusted earnings per share 10% to $1.34; Paychex kept its full-year outlook for revenue growth of 5% to 6%, its release and Finviz showed.

**The price:** 19.5 times expected earnings against a 20.4 times median, and 23.4 times trailing earnings against its own median of 26.4 times since late 2024.

**What could go wrong:** Growth is slowing. Revenue rose only 5.9% in the August quarter, in the bottom quarter of its group, and Paychex guided to about 4% for the second; Chief Financial Officer Bob Schrader said its core Management Solutions business came in slightly below plan.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### ServiceNow ($NOW)
**Gross margin: 73.1% in the 12 months to September 2026.** ServiceNow sells workflow software to big companies.

1. **The margin stops at the gross line.** Its operating margin of **9.7%** is only at the median of the 32 software companies we cover, a third of Paychex's, and its gross margin over two years sits just below the software median.
2. **Returns are weak.** Return on capital of about 6.5% is behind most software peers, and net debt now equals 3.4 years of operating profit.
3. **The market doubts the next leg.** Third-quarter subscription revenue rose 21% to $3.99 billion and beat its guidance, yet the shares fell after the report, Diginomica reported. UBS cut it to neutral in April, citing the risk that AI agents erode workflow software, Yahoo Finance and Tikr reported.

**The price:** 20.4 times expected earnings against a 23.8 times software median, and 66.8 times trailing earnings against its own median of 104.2 times since late 2024. Its history assumed margins that have not arrived.

**What would change our mind:** An operating margin moving toward 20% in the fourth-quarter report in late January, or subscription growth holding above 20% as the AI deals it signed start to bill.
