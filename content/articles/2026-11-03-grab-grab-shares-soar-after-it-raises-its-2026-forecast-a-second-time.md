## What happened

Shares of Southeast Asian ride-hailing, delivery and payments company Grab ($GRAB) **soared 13.1%** to $3.24 by 10:15 a.m. ET on Tuesday, after it raised its full-year forecast for the second time this year and posted record quarterly profit on its preferred measure.

Third-quarter revenue rose **27%** from a year earlier to $1.11 billion, or 30% at constant currency, the company said after Monday's close. Adjusted EBITDA, earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation with some items stripped out, jumped 73% to a record $237 million, and its financial-services arm turned a profit for the first time, Grab said, as reported by Dow Jones and MarketScreener.

The forecast is what moved the stock. Grab now expects 2026 revenue of **$4.15 billion to $4.20 billion**, up from $4.04 billion to $4.10 billion, and adjusted EBITDA of $790 million to $800 million, up from $700 million to $720 million. It also said it bought back $400 million of its shares in September and October.

## Why it matters

The quarter shows Grab growing faster while getting more profitable, even as it keeps spending to add drivers, as management described on the call. Its adjusted EBITDA margin widened to 21.3% of revenue from about 16% a year earlier, and every segment made money on that basis for the first time, according to the call transcript published by Investing.com.

It also resets the story after a setback. On 21 October Taiwan's Fair Trade Commission blocked Grab's $600 million purchase of Foodpanda's Taiwan business, pointing to Uber's ($UBER) roughly 13% stake in Grab, Fortune and Taiwan's CNA reported. Grab told Dow Jones on Tuesday that it is turning its attention back to Southeast Asia.

## What the market is telling us

Grab's shares moved 5% or more on 11 of the last 252 sessions, and if today's gain holds it will be the biggest one-day rise since 9 April 2025. Even so, the stock was down 42.7% this year at Monday's close of $2.86, and 52.9% below its 52-week closing high of $6.07, set a year ago today.

At about 19 times forward earnings, below the median for its peers in our data, the market had priced Grab as a business whose profits might stall. A second raise in one year, with buybacks on top, is evidence against that; the fourth-quarter report, which brings the 2027 outlook, is the next test.
