Meta ($META) agreed in a court filing on Wednesday not to issue its planned non-voting Class C shares until shareholder lawsuits over the plan are resolved, Bloomberg and Yahoo Finance reported. Ten suits against Chief Executive Mark Zuckerberg and the board have been consolidated in Delaware's Court of Chancery, with a trial set for **April**.

The board approved the new class in October, alongside results that showed free cash flow turning negative ([our story](/news/2026-10-22/meta-meta-shares-slide-after-free-cash-flow-turns-negative/)). Each holder was to get one Class C share per existing share, which Meta has said would help it raise money for its AI build-out. Plaintiffs, including the California State Teachers' Retirement System, argue it lets Zuckerberg sell shares without diluting the roughly 61% of votes his Class B stock controls, the Financial Times and Bloomberg reported. "We will vigorously defend against these claims," Meta said.

The pause removes one way to fund AI spending without diluting voting control, at least until spring. Meta shares closed **down 0.4% at $616.38**, 6.6% lower this year, and were 0.8% higher after hours.
