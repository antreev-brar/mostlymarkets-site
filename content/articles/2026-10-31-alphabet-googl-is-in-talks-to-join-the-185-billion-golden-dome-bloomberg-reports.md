Google, part of Alphabet ($GOOGL), is in early talks to help secure data for the Pentagon's **$185 billion** Golden Dome missile shield, Bloomberg reported after Friday's close, citing people familiar with the matter. A Pentagon official said no final decision had been made; Google declined to comment.

The talks cover Google's storage, networking and cybersecurity, to keep data flowing to the program's command-and-control software if attackers cut off landlines and cell towers, Bloomberg said. A group including Palantir ($PLTR), Anduril and SpaceX is building that software. It would be Google's first publicly known role in the program.

Gen. Michael Guetlein, who runs Golden Dome, says he must field operational capability by summer 2028. The program has run mostly on about $25 billion from a 2025 budget law, and on 11 August he told a missile-defense symposium that if lawmakers don't settle 2027 funding, "there is no Golden Dome because there is no funding."

In 2018, Google let a Pentagon AI contract, Project Maven, lapse after staff protests; in April, over fresh objections, it let the military use its Gemini models for classified work, Bloomberg reported.

Our read: this matters more for Google's defense business than for Alphabet's earnings. Google would be one of a group on one part of a program whose whole cost, spread to 2035, equals 4.7 months of Alphabet's revenue.
