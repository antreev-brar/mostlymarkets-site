Marriott International ($MAR) reports third-quarter results before the market opens on Monday, 2 November, with a call at 8 a.m. ET, the company said. It goes in near a record: the stock closed Friday at $399.30, **1.2% below** its all-year high, after rivals Hilton and Hyatt both raised their forecasts last week.

Last quarter, the war in the Middle East cut the region's revenue per available room (RevPAR, the hotel industry's key sales measure) by 43%, Reuters reported. Worldwide RevPAR still rose 3.4%, helped by 5.0% growth in the US and Canada, and Marriott raised its full-year forecast. But it guided third-quarter adjusted EPS to $2.74 to $2.82, below the $2.87 analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters, and the stock **sank 7.0%** on 3 August.

## What analysts expect

- **Adjusted EPS:** $2.87, up 16% from $2.47 a year ago, according to Zacks, whose estimate edged up 0.2% over the past 30 days. Barchart's consensus is $2.84 and AlphaStreet's is $2.85. Every figure sits above the top of Marriott's own range.
- **Revenue:** about $7.04 billion (Zacks); AlphaStreet's average of analysts is $6.92 billion. Second-quarter revenue was $7.07 billion.
- **RevPAR:** Marriott guided to worldwide growth of 3.5% to 4% for the quarter, and 3% to 3.5% for the year, up from 2% to 3%.

Marriott beat estimates in three of the last four quarters, Barchart reported.

## What to watch

- **The US number against its rivals.** Hilton's US RevPAR rose 7% in the third quarter, and Hyatt's rose 8.2%, both helped by the World Cup and the timing of holidays, according to their releases. Both raised full-year RevPAR forecasts: Hilton to 4% to 4.5%, Hyatt to 5% to 5.5%. A Marriott US figure well below theirs would point to weaker share or a softer mix of cheaper brands.
- **The Middle East.** Management said in August the region takes about one percentage point off global RevPAR. Any sign that drag is easing is the cleanest route to a beat above guidance.
- **The full-year outlook.** Marriott guided 2026 adjusted EPS to $11.64 to $11.81. With the stock near a high, a raise is closer to expected than a surprise. Hilton fell 2.1% on its results day, 27 October, despite its raise.

## How the stock has done

Marriott is **up 28.7%** this year and 12.6% over the past month, and its closing high of $404.00 came on 21 October. The shares have moved 5% or more on only three sessions in the past year, and August's report was one of them. $1,000 invested five years ago is worth $2,495 today.
