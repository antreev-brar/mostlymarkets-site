## What happened
Shares of PC and AI server maker Dell Technologies ($DELL) **slid 6.3%** to $322.90 by 10:30 a.m. ET on Monday, as AI hardware stocks extended Friday's sell-off a day before Nvidia ($NVDA) reports, CNBC and Quartz reported. Super Micro Computer ($SMCI) fell 5.8% and Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE) 4.9%; 24/7 Wall St. said a reported Justice Department probe into smuggling of AI servers to China was weighing on Super Micro for a third session.

Dell had no news of its own that explained the drop. The pressure came from two directions on Monday: worry about what Nvidia will say without Jensen Huang as chief executive, Reuters and CNBC reported, and a cut by research firm SemiAnalysis to its forecast for AI chip spending in 2028, by **28%**, Futu News and PANews reported. Fewer AI chips bought means fewer servers built around them.

## Why it matters
Dell is growing fast on AI demand: its revenue rose 57.7% year on year in the quarter to July, according to its SEC filings, against a median of 29.9% for the 31 hardware makers we track. But it earns thin margins, a 9.4% operating margin over the last 12 months against a peer median of 18.2%, so the stock trades on the volume of AI orders, and a cut to the spending forecast goes straight to the number investors care about.

Dell reports its fiscal third quarter on Tuesday 24 November, after the close, Walnut Invest and Arincen list. That report, a week after Nvidia's, will show whether the server orders it booked earlier this year are still turning into shipments.

## What the market is telling us
Dell's shares moved 5% or more on 54 of the last 252 sessions, and the last drop at least this big came on 5 November, when the stock fell 14.9%. It closed Friday up 173.7% this year, yet 41.5% below its 17 September record of $588.40. At 11.4 times forward earnings, against a peer median of 17.5, the price already assumes the AI server boom slows; Nvidia's outlook on Tuesday will say whether that assumption is too gloomy or not gloomy enough.
