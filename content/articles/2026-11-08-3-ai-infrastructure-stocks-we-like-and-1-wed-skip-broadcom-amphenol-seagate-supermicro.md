On Thursday the Nasdaq **sank 5.5%**, its worst day since April 2025, on bets that the Democrats' midterm sweep would slow the building of AI data centers, and the companies that sell into those buildings fell hardest ([our week in charts](/daily/2026-11-07/)). That sell-off made no distinction between suppliers that turn AI demand into cash and those that only turn it into sales, which is the distinction this list makes.

Our screen took the chip, networking, power and data-center stocks in our coverage, ranked them by revenue growth over the latest 12 months from their SEC filings, and then judged the fastest growers on what decides quality: growth over five years and two, operating margin, free cash flow, and the price against peers, using `analyse.py` on our fundamentals data. It left out stocks we listed in the last 14 days, which rules out Nvidia, Credo and Astera Labs.

```picks
like: AVGO, APH, STX
skip: SMCI
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Broadcom ($AVGO)
**Revenue growth: 48.7% in the 12 months to August 2026.** Broadcom designs custom AI chips and networking chips for the biggest cloud companies, and sells infrastructure software.

1. Its quarter to 2 August showed the demand is accelerating: AI chip revenue rose **221%** from a year earlier to $16.7 billion, and it guided to $21.7 billion for the current quarter, its results release said.
2. It turns sales into cash better than any of the 25 chipmakers we cover: free cash flow was 43.2% of revenue over two years, against a median of 23.7%.
3. Growth reaches the owner: earnings per share grew 49.8% a year over five fiscal years, nearly three times the chipmaker median of 17.2%, as its operating margin widened by 23 points.

**The price:** 17.0 times expected earnings, below the chipmaker median of 20.8 times, with the stock 39% below its June high.

**What would change our mind:** a cut to the $21.7 billion AI revenue guide when it next reports, or more share dilution: its share count rose 4.8% in two years, among the most in its group.

### 2. Amphenol ($APH)
**Revenue growth: 57.6% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Amphenol makes the connectors and cables that link servers inside data centers, and parts for cars, planes and phones.

1. Its third quarter beat: sales rose 65% to $10.23 billion against the $9.44 billion expected, and orders of $12.17 billion ran 19% ahead of shipments ([our recap](/news/2026-10-29/amphenol-aph-tops-q3-estimates-as-ai-datacom-sales-more-than-double/)).
2. The growth has lasted: revenue rose **21.8% a year** over five years, about four times the 5.6% median of the 31 hardware makers we cover.
3. It's getting more profitable as it grows: its operating margin of 27.7% in the latest 12 months is in the top quarter of its group, and up 6.4 points in five years.

**The price:** 23.1 times expected earnings, above the hardware median of 17.5 times, but its trailing P/E of 32.5 is near the bottom of its two-year range of 30.9 to 55.4.

**What would change our mind:** orders falling back to shipments (a book-to-bill under 1.0) in its next quarterly report.

### 3. Seagate ($STX)
**Revenue growth: 44.4% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Seagate makes the hard drives that store most of the data cloud companies keep.

1. Its profits are at a record: GAAP gross margin rose to 57.3% in the quarter to 2 October from 39.4% a year earlier, its filing shows ([our story](/news/2026-11-02/seagate-stx-and-western-digital-wdc-shares-slide-again-with-no-fresh-news/)).
2. It earns more on its capital than any hardware maker we cover, about 94%, and free cash flow reached **30.1%** of revenue in the latest 12 months, in the top tenth of its group.
3. The weak spot is the long run: revenue grew only 2.7% a year over five years, because drives are a cyclical business.

**The price:** 11.4 times expected earnings, against a hardware median of 17.5 times, after a 40.5% fall in a month.

**What would change our mind:** a drop in exabyte shipments, the total storage capacity it sells, in its next quarterly report; that would say the cycle has turned.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Supermicro ($SMCI)
**Revenue growth: 156.5% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Supermicro assembles AI servers and full racks, mostly around Nvidia's chips.

1. The growth is real: September-quarter sales nearly quadrupled to $19.9 billion and it raised its fiscal 2027 forecast to $72 billion to $82 billion ([our recap](/news/2026-11-04/supermicro-smci-shares-jump-as-sales-hit-19-9-billion-and-it-raises-its-forecast/)).
2. It doesn't turn into cash: free cash flow was **negative $6.5 billion** in the latest 12 months, minus 12% of revenue, in the bottom quarter of the 31 hardware makers we cover, because building racks ties up money in inventory.
3. Its margins are thin and owners get diluted: gross margin was 11.2% over two years against a 43.2% median, and its share count rose 17.2% in two years.

**The price:** 5.2 times expected earnings, the cheapest tenth of its group, which tells us the market doubts the profits will become cash.

**What could go wrong (for our skip):** two quarters of positive operating cash flow would show the cash problem was timing, and the multiple would have room to rise.
