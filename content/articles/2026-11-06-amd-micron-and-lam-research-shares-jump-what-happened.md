## What happened

Shares of chipmakers and the companies that build their factories jumped on Friday morning, a day after their worst sell-off in more than six years. AMD ($AMD) was **up 11.1%** at $449.35 as of 10:15 a.m. ET, Lam Research ($LRCX) 8.8% and Micron ($MU) 6.8%, as investors bought back into the stocks that fell hardest on Thursday, Benzinga and Yahoo Finance reported.

On Thursday the Philadelphia Semiconductor index fell about 12%, its worst day since March 2020, on fears that the Democrats who won Congress will slow the building of AI data centers ([our chip story](/news/2026-11-05/sandisk-micron-and-amd-shares-plunge-what-happened/)). Friday's bounce took back less than half of that: the index was up about 5% early, Yahoo Finance reported, and AMD and Micron had both fallen more than 17% on Thursday.

There was no new chip news behind it. The rally held through the October jobs report at 8:30 a.m. ET, which showed employers added 165,000 jobs against the 65,000 economists expected in FactSet's poll, with unemployment down to 4.1%, CNN and Morningstar reported; the strong number pushed Treasury yields higher, Yahoo Finance reported. Lam had a reason of its own: after Thursday's close it announced a new $25 billion share buyback authorization, its release said.

## Why it matters

The bounce reached past the chipmakers. Coherent ($COHR), which makes optical parts for AI networks, was up 11.6% and Arista Networks ($ANET) and Dell ($DELL) more than 7% each, all stocks that sank with the chips on Thursday. Nvidia ($NVDA) was up 5.7% at $191.87, still well below its October high. The question Thursday raised, whether the midterms slow AI spending, has no new answer yet; Friday's move says only that investors think a 12% one-day drop priced in a lot of it ([our narrative on the midterm AI trade](/news/2026-11-05/wall-street-is-betting-the-midterms-will-slow-the-ai-buildout-3-numbers-will-decide-it/)).

## What the market is telling us

Before Friday, Micron was up 130.0% this year but 45.9% below its 25 June closing high of $1,213.56. AMD sat 37.7% below its 6 October high and Lam 57.3% below its 30 June high. Moves this size are normal for these stocks: AMD and Lam each moved 5% or more on 56 of the last 252 sessions, and Micron on 75.

Our read: a bounce of half the drop with no new information is a relief rally, not a verdict. Nvidia reports after the close on 17 November, and what it and the cloud companies say about 2027 data-center spending will settle whether Thursday or Friday had it right.
