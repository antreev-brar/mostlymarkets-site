On Friday, 20 November, the S&P 500 closed in a correction after its worst week since April 2025, and the stocks that build AI data centers fell furthest. This short week puts two of them on the scale: Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE) reports on Monday and Dell ($DELL) on Tuesday, after their shares **lost 26.3% and 38.6%** over the past month in our data.

Then the calendar turns to the Fed. October's PCE inflation, the Fed's preferred gauge, arrives on Wednesday with GDP, jobless claims and the Beige Book, all squeezed in before markets close on Thursday for Thanksgiving. Friday is a half day, ending at 1 p.m. ET.

## Monday, 23 November

**Hewlett Packard Enterprise**, after the close. Analysts expect adjusted earnings of $1.28 a share on $14.4 billion of revenue, the Zacks consensus shows, inside HPE's own forecast. AI systems orders and the networking margin from its Juniper business are what matter ([our preview](/news/2026-11-22/hewlett-packard-enterprise-hpe-reports-earnings-tomorrow-what-to-watch/)).

**Agilent** ($A), after the close. The lab-equipment maker is expected to earn $1.72 a share, up 8.2%, Zacks says, and its stock is only 6.1% below its high ([our preview](/news/2026-11-22/what-to-expect-from-agilents-a-q4-earnings/)). Engineering firm Jacobs ($J) also reports.

## Tuesday, 24 November

**Analog Devices** ($ADI), before the open. The chipmaker guided to revenue of about $4.3 billion and adjusted earnings of $3.86 a share, give or take 15 cents, its August release said; analysts are at about $3.89, Barchart and Benzinga reported. Its industrial and car chips are the part of the chip market that hasn't been about AI, and the stock is down 10.3% in a month, less than half Dell's drop.

Medtronic ($MDT), Best Buy ($BBY) and Dollar Tree ($DLTR) also report before the open. All three are within 8% of their highs, and Best Buy's comments on holiday demand land three days before Black Friday.

**Dell**, after the close. It guided to **$49 billion** of revenue and $6.50 of adjusted earnings a share, and analysts expect about $6.59, MarketBeat and Barchart reported. It carries a $95 billion AI server backlog into a stock 52.8% below its September high ([our breakdown](/news/2026-11-22/dell-technologies-dell-is-53-below-its-high-before-tuesdays-report-is-the-business-broken/)).

Workday ($WDAY), Autodesk ($ADSK) and HP ($HPQ) report after the close too. Workday is down 18.7% this year and HP 17.2% in a month, the latter on worries that memory costs are squeezing PC makers.

## Wednesday, 25 November

**Deere** ($DE), before the open. Analysts expect about $4.07 a share, up from $3.93 a year earlier, Earnings Whispers and Hudson Labs reported. Its first outlook for fiscal 2027 will show whether the farm-equipment slump has bottomed.

**The data, at 8:30 a.m. ET.** October PCE inflation, the second estimate of third-quarter GDP, durable goods orders and weekly jobless claims, moved up a day for the holiday, all land together, Newsquawk and MarketAlleys reported. RBC Economics and the Cleveland Fed's nowcast put headline PCE inflation at about **3.8%**, up from 3.6%, and core at 3.2% to 3.3%. The Fed's Beige Book of regional business conditions follows at 2 p.m. ET.

A hotter number would make a December rate hike harder to argue against, even with stocks in a correction. Our narrative on [why Wall Street thinks the Fed won't rescue this sell-off](/news/2026-11-22/why-wall-street-thinks-the-fed-wont-rescue-this-sell-off/) has the detail.

## Thursday and Friday, 26 and 27 November

US stock and bond markets are closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving. On Friday, stocks close at 1 p.m. ET and bonds at 2 p.m. ET, Investrade's calendar shows. Black Friday gives the first read on holiday spending, with retailers already reported and Best Buy's outlook fresh.

## Our read

Dell's report matters most. The AI hardware stocks have priced a slowdown that the orders haven't yet shown: if Dell's backlog holds near $95 billion and it keeps its $192 billion revenue forecast, the sell-off has run ahead of the business; if new orders slump, the bears' case spreads from the chip-renting clouds to the whole supply chain. Wednesday's inflation data then decides whether the Fed adds a rate hike to that.
