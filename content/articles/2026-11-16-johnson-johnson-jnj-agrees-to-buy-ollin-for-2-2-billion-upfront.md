Johnson & Johnson ($JNJ) agreed on Monday to buy Ollin Biosciences for **$2.2 billion** upfront, plus about $1.1 billion more if its lead eye drug hits regulatory and sales milestones, the company said; CNBC and Reuters put the total at about $3.3 billion. Blackstone ($BX) Life Sciences will separately provide $750 million over several years to pay for the drug's Phase 3 trials, BioPharma Dive and Contract Pharma reported. The deal is expected to close by the end of 2026.

The drug, OLN324, is an injected antibody for wet age-related macular degeneration and diabetic macular edema, licensed from China's Innovent Biologics. In a small head-to-head study it dried the retina in nearly 90% of macular-edema patients at 12 weeks, against 57% on Roche's Vabysmo, Yahoo Finance and Clinical Trials Arena reported.

The terms match what the FT reported on Sunday, which we covered in [our first story](/news/2026-11-15/johnson-johnson-jnj-is-in-talks-to-buy-eye-drug-developer-ollin-for-up-to-3-4-billion/). With Blackstone carrying the trial bill, J&J's cash at risk is mostly the upfront price, small for a company worth $684 billion. Its shares were down 0.5% at $284.34 at 12:15 p.m. ET. The next test is the Phase 3 data, which will show whether the early edge over Vabysmo holds.
