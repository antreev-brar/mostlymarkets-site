Intel ($INTC) has signed its first outside customer for its most advanced manufacturing process, Chief Executive Lip-Bu Tan said on Wednesday at the TomorrowX Summit in Austin, Texas, CNBC reported. "We just signed the first customer," Tan said, without naming the client. Chief Technology Officer Pushkar Ranade later said on X that the deal covers **18A-P**, a version of the 18A process that entered risk production in June, CNBC and TokenPost reported.

It is the first marquee contract for Intel Foundry, which until now has made chips mostly for Intel's own products, and it answers the question hanging over its factory build-out, including a new campus in Arizona: whether anyone else will pay to use it. Ben Bajarin of Creative Strategies called it "a huge win for foundry" and named Nvidia and Apple among possible customers, CNBC reported. Intel gave no figures on volumes or revenue.

The shares closed **down 0.9% at $54.67**, up 48% this year but 61% below their June high, and were 2.6% higher after hours. The open questions are who the customer is and how many wafers it has committed to.
