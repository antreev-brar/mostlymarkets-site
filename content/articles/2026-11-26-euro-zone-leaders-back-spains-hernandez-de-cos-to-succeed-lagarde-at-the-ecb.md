Euro zone leaders meeting in Brussels on Thursday backed **Pablo Hernández de Cos**, the former governor of the Bank of Spain, to succeed Christine Lagarde as president of the European Central Bank, CNBC and Reuters reported. The race ended when Dutch Prime Minister Rob Jetten dropped support for the only other candidate, former Dutch central bank chief Klaas Knot, RTE reported.

Lagarde said she will step down on 7 February 2027, nearly nine months before her term ends, so Hernández de Cos would take over then.

Henry Cook of MUFG told Reuters he sees Hernández de Cos as a pragmatic centrist who will broadly keep Lagarde's approach, and he expects a quarter-point hike at the ECB's March meeting, which would be the new president's first. That would put Europe on the same path as the Federal Reserve, where markets price a quarter-point hike on 9 December after [stronger growth figures](/news/2026-11-25/us-third-quarter-gdp-growth-is-revised-up-to-4-3-from-3-4/).

Until the formal appointment in December, the choice is a political agreement, not a done deal; Lagarde runs the ECB's remaining meetings before February.
