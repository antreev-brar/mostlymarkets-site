Dell Technologies ($DELL) reports results for its fiscal third quarter, the three months to October, after the close on **Tuesday, 24 November**. It goes in after its shares **soared 20.5%** to $334.91 on Monday, its biggest gain since May, as Nvidia's reinstatement of Jensen Huang set off a rally in AI hardware ([our story](/news/2026-11-23/why-dell-technologies-dell-stock-is-soaring-today/)).

Last quarter, the three months to July, sales grew 58% to $47.0 billion, AI server sales doubled to $16.4 billion, and Dell booked $60.9 billion of AI server orders, leaving a $95 billion backlog. It raised its forecast for AI server sales this fiscal year to $74 billion from $60 billion, and the stock jumped 15.8% the next day. Then it fell 52.8% from its 17 September high by last Friday ([our breakdown](/news/2026-11-22/dell-technologies-dell-is-53-below-its-high-before-tuesdays-report-is-the-business-broken/)).

## What analysts expect

Dell guided to revenue of **$49 billion**, give or take $0.5 billion, and adjusted earnings of $6.50 a share. Analysts sit a little above that: Zacks puts consensus revenue at $50.87 billion, up 88.4% from a year earlier, and Benzinga cites $6.61 a share against $2.59 a year ago; TipRanks and MarketBeat put earnings at $6.62. Options traders are pricing a move of about 17% either way, TipRanks reported.

## The one question

Has the AI order book survived the sell-off? The stock's halving says investors fear the clouds and AI labs that fill the backlog will slow down, and that memory costs will squeeze Dell's thin margins. Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE) answered half of that on Monday: its server sales nearly doubled, but its gross margin fell 2.1 points from the quarter before, Reuters reported ([our recap](/news/2026-11-23/hewlett-packard-enterprise-hpe-beats-q4-estimates-as-server-sales-nearly-double/)).

## What to watch

- **AI orders and backlog.** A backlog holding near $95 billion would say demand held; a sharp fall in new orders against July's $60.9 billion would back the bears.
- **The $74 billion AI server goal.** A raise or a cut decides the fiscal fourth quarter's estimates.
- **Gross margin.** Memory prices are rising, and Dell keeps about a fifth of each sales dollar as gross profit, so a point lost matters.

## How the stock has done

Dell is up 166.1% this year but down 26.6% over the past month, and at $334.91 it sits 43.1% below its 52-week closing high of $588.40. It has moved 5% or more on 56 of the last 252 sessions, so a double-digit swing on Wednesday would be in character.
