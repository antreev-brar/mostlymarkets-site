Anthropic's IPO, which Reuters and The Wall Street Journal reported could raise about **$100 billion** in mid-November, will be priced after a selloff its own partners' results helped fuel, CNBC reported on Saturday. Alphabet ($GOOGL), a major supplier of its computing power, plunged 12.6% this week.

When [the prospectus went public on Monday](/news/2026-10-26/stock-market-today-anthropics-ipo-filing-lifts-chip-stocks-as-brazil-crashes/), some investors already valued the Claude chatbot's maker at about $1.5 trillion, down from roughly $2 trillion, Reuters reported.

That is about **32 times** its second-quarter revenue at an annual rate, by our arithmetic; Nvidia ($NVDA) trades at about 13 times on the same basis. IPO buyers price a newcomer against listed stocks they could buy instead, and Nvidia fell this week too.

Anthropic's prospectus commits it to spend at least $110 billion with Amazon ($AMZN) and about $114.5 billion with Google on computing through 2036 and 2033, and both are investors. The IPO tests whether stock buyers will fund a big customer of the [AI build-out they just sold off](/news/2026-10-31/bulls-vs-bears-on-the-ai-chip-selloff-a-normal-pullback-or-the-peak/).

Our read: the selloff threatens Anthropic's price more than its listing. Its first-half operating loss narrowed to $693 million, and most of its $80.7 billion net loss was a paper charge tied to its rising value, the filing shows. What changed this week was the yardstick, not the company.

Under SEC rules, its roadshow can't start before 10 November; the amended filing before it should add a price range and the third-quarter revenue the prospectus left blank.
