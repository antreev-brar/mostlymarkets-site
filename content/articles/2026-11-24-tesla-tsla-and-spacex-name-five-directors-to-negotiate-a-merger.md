Tesla ($TSLA) and SpaceX have each named independent directors to negotiate a possible merger of Elon Musk's two biggest companies, the Financial Times reported late on Monday, as Reuters and RTE reported. Three Tesla directors, Robyn Denholm, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson and Jack Hartung, and two from SpaceX, Roelof Botha and Randy Glein, are leading the talks on a combination valued at about **$3.5 trillion**, Investing.com and TipRanks reported.

Naming negotiators turns weekend speculation into a formal process. Evercore is advising Tesla, and Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs SpaceX, the reports said, and Bloomberg reported earlier this month that a deal could come as soon as December. The hardest question is control: Musk holds about 84% of SpaceX's votes but around 20% of Tesla's, so the exchange ratio decides how much of the combined company he runs, while SpaceX's US defense contracts and Tesla's reliance on China raise regulatory questions.

Tesla closed up 11.5% at $385.46 on Monday ([why it rose](/news/2026-11-23/why-tesla-tsla-stock-is-soaring-today/)) and edged up 0.7% after hours. No terms have been announced.
