## How it got here

Meta ($META) began as Facebook in 2004 and became the world's second-biggest seller of online ads by putting them in front of the people who use Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and Messenger. It nearly lost that edge once: in 2022, after Apple's privacy changes made ads harder to target and Reality Labs, its virtual-reality unit, burned billions, its revenue fell for the first time and the stock lost 74% from November 2021 to November 2022, by our price files. Its answer was AI that aims ads without Apple's data.

It worked, and Meta now spends at a scale it never has. On 21 October it reported third-quarter capital spending of **$34.91 billion**, including finance-lease payments, more than the $32.79 billion its business brought in. That left free cash flow at negative $2.12 billion, its first quarter of cash burn in more than a decade, CNBC and investingLive reported. The shares fell 7.0% the next day.

Five days later Meta started raising money by selling stock. On Friday 30 October S&P Global moved its outlook on Meta's AA- rating to negative.

**Is Meta still a great business that's going through an expensive phase, or has AI turned it into a capital-hungry one?**

## The business in one line

Advertisers pay Meta every time it shows their ads to some of the 3.64 billion people who use at least one of its apps each day, and almost nobody else pays it for anything.

Capital spending has quadrupled since the third quarter of 2024 while cash from operations rose by a third. In the latest quarter, purchases of property and equipment alone exceeded operating cash flow, before finance leases.

*Verdict: one product, ads, sold at rising prices to a growing audience, and that product now has to pay for the most expensive build-out in Meta's history.*

## The numbers that matter

### Growth: faster than its peers

Revenue grew **24.4% a year** over the past two years, in the top fifth of the 16 consumer-internet companies we track, against a median of 14.2%. Five-year growth of 18.5% a year includes 2022's decline.

### Profitability: still near the top, but a charge hit Q3

Meta kept **32.9%** of its sales as operating profit over the 12 months to September, the top tenth of its group, where the median is 13.1%. Third-quarter operating income fell 37% to $12.98 billion, after a $9.89 billion charge for its August settlement with state attorneys general, the release shows. Without it, operating income would have risen about 11%.

### Cash: the number that changed

Free cash flow was **11.9%** of sales over the past 12 months, down from 17.0% over two years and now exactly at its group's median. Alphabet's ($GOOGL) two-year figure is lower still, at 10.9%: both are paying for AI out of what used to be profit for shareholders.

### Returns: good, and about to be tested

Meta earns about **18%** a year on the capital in the business, by our approximation, in its group's top quarter. Each extra $100 billion of data centers raises the bar those returns have to clear.

### Balance sheet: roughly even, for now

Meta held $82.45 billion of cash and marketable securities against about $84 billion of debt on 30 September, its filings show. That's a balance it can't keep if it spends more than it brings in.

*Verdict: growth and margins are excellent; cash conversion, the measure Meta used to beat everyone on, is now merely average.*

## Why the stock is where it is

1. **The cash burn.** The 7.0% fall on 22 October was the worst since 30 July. Meta raised the floor of its 2026 spending plan to $135 billion to $145 billion and gave no number for 2027.
2. **New stock.** On 26 October Meta filed to raise about $95 billion with new shares, most of it gradually on the market, as we reported. It priced $25 billion of common stock at $625 a share and $12.5 billion of preferred stock that converts into common, CNBC and Yahoo Finance reported. Nvidia committed $3 billion. The stock fell 1.7% on 27 October.
3. **S&P's warning.** On 30 October S&P Global affirmed Meta's AA- rating but moved its outlook to negative, citing "significant uncertainty around its capital spending and free operating cash flow," CNBC and Investing.com reported. S&P said Meta's ability to make money from AI is "more limited than that of its peers"; Meta said it "respectfully" disagreed.

## Testing the worry

**Is the cash burn temporary?** Meta's own words say no, not soon. Chief Financial Officer Susan Li said on the call that Meta isn't "providing a specific outlook for 2027 capex at this time," and the company plans to keep investing heavily, according to the transcript. S&P expects Meta to spend more than $225 billion in 2027 and to post negative free operating cash flow of about $71 billion that year, CNBC and Investing.com reported. Wells Fargo's Ken Gawrelski expects about $250 billion, Benzinga and Yahoo Finance reported.

**Is the ad business slowing under it?** No. Meta's ad revenue grew 26% while Google search grew 12%, the widest gap in Meta's favor since 2021, CNBC reported on Sunday ([our story on that shift](/news/2026-11-01/ai-is-moving-ad-money-away-from-google-search-what-the-evidence-says/)). The fourth-quarter forecast of $73 billion to $76 billion brackets the $73.73 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC.

**Is it the industry or Meta?** The industry: Alphabet, Amazon and Oracle also spent more than their operating cash last quarter, as we [reported](/news/2026-10-31/free-cash-flow-is-now-the-scorecard-for-ai-spenders-what-the-evidence-says/). What sets Meta apart is that it has no cloud business to rent the computers to outsiders, which is S&P's point: the spending pays off only through Meta's own ads and its own AI products.

*Verdict: the demand worry fails the test; the cash worry survives, and it's lasting, not temporary.*

## The moat

- **Reach.** 3.64 billion daily users, up 3% from a year earlier. No rival in our data comes close; Pinterest and Snap are fractions of the size.
- **Pricing power.** Price per ad rose 14% in the third quarter, and Meta's 81.5% two-year gross margin is the top tenth of its group against a median of 60.1%.
- **Its own AI models.** Meta released Muse Wonder, its first frontier model, on 12 October, TechCrunch and VentureBeat reported, so it doesn't have to rent the technology that aims its ads.

Rivals: Alphabet earns a similar operating margin (33.3%) with slower growth (17.7% a year); Pinterest earns 4.2%, Snap loses money. Will Meta still be here, and stronger, in ten years? The ads business almost certainly; the open question is whether its owners will have been diluted to pay for it.

## The risks we'd watch

**1. The build-out doesn't pay.** This is the biggest, and it can be sized. If Meta sells the full $95 billion of stock at Friday's $614.99, it issues about 154 million shares, roughly **6%** of the 2.55 billion outstanding, by our count. Every dollar of future profit then splits across 6% more shares, before any return on what the money buys. Offsets: Meta's share count fell 1.0% over the past two years through buybacks, and most of the new stock is to be sold gradually, so the pace can follow the need.

**2. A downgrade.** S&P said it could cut the rating if adjusted leverage stays above 1.0 times and free operating cash flow stays negative beyond 2028, CNBC and Investing.com reported. A lower rating raises the cost of the debt Meta will also need; the price of insuring its bonds against default already hit a record after the results, Investing.com reported.

**3. Legal costs.** The $9.89 billion charge for the August settlement with state attorneys general equalled about 15% of the quarter's sales, by our count, and it came in a single quarter.

A 6% dilution is real but not ruinous for a company growing sales 26% a year; the risk becomes serious only if the spending keeps rising into 2028 with no visible return.

On earnings, Meta sits near the bottom of its two-year range and just above its peers. On cash, it costs 55.7 times what shareholders actually received over the past year, because the data centers swallowed the rest. The price assumes earnings keep growing but that investors won't see much of the cash for a while: for the cash multiple to fall to the earnings multiple, free cash flow would have to grow about 2.7-fold at today's price.

## Our verdict

**Business: Strong. Price: Below its usual price.** Meta's ads are growing at 26% with rising prices and more users, its operating margin is in the top tenth of its group, and its own AI models are improving how it aims ads. The stock trades at 21.0 times earnings against a median of 27.1 times because the cash that once went to shareholders now goes into data centers, and some of it now comes from them through new stock. What would change our mind on the business is a 2027 spending plan above $250 billion without faster ad growth to match; the next date is Meta's fourth-quarter report in late January, when it gives its 2027 outlook.

Meta has never sold more ads, and has never asked its shareholders to pay so much for the chance to keep selling them.
