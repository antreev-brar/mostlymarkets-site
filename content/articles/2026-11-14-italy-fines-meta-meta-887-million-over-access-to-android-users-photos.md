Italy's data protection authority fined Meta Platforms ($META) **805 million euros**, about $887 million, on Saturday, 14 November, saying a Facebook feature reached Android users' photos and videos without their consent, CNBC and Reuters reported. The feature suggested content from users' personal galleries for them to post, the authority said in a statement.

The fine equals about 0.47% of Meta's global revenue, the regulator said, so the money matters less than the order attached to it: Meta has 60 days to switch the feature off by default and ask users for consent separately. The authority said its investigation, opened in 2025, did not cover whether the content was used to train AI models, which Meta denied, and that it may separately review a 2026 feature that processes gallery content in the cloud, Business Recorder and the New Straits Times reported. Meta was not immediately available for comment.

The stock closed Friday at $641.69, up 2.9% on the day but 17.5% below its September high. The next dates to watch are the end of the 60-day window in mid-January and any separate review of the cloud feature.
