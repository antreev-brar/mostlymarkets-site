TSMC ($TSM) and Samsung Electronics have told Chinese customers they are suspending shipments of certain chips while they apply for licences from the US Commerce Department, the Financial Times reported on Thursday, citing people briefed on the matter; Reuters and Newsquawk carried the report. The FT tied the halt to new US curbs; a Commerce Department rule issued on 16 November takes effect on publication in the Federal Register on Thursday, Nextgov and Global Sanctions reported.

Washington's latest moves were aimed at Chinese firms renting AI chips through data centers abroad ([our story](/news/2026-11-16/us-moves-to-cut-off-chinas-remote-access-to-nvidia-nvda-chips-abroad/)); this is the first sign the curbs also reach the factories that make chips for Chinese designers. That matters for anyone counting on China demand: Alibaba said on Thursday its AI cloud sales rose 53% ([our brief](/news/2026-11-19/alibaba-baba-beats-revenue-estimates-as-ai-cloud-sales-jump-53-but-profit-misses/)) with its chip supply still in question. TSMC's US shares were **up 1.2%** at $366.89 at 6:15 a.m. ET in our data, so the market is not yet pricing in lost sales.

What isn't known yet: which chips are covered, how long licences will take, and how much revenue is held up.
