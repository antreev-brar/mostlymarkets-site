On Friday, traders sold the chipmakers they had bought on Thursday's bet that Jensen Huang's exit from Nvidia would send its customers to rivals. AMD ($AMD) **plunged 8.8%** and Broadcom ($AVGO) slid 4.8%, while the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.9% to 25,136.27 and the S&P 500 slipped 0.4% to 7,561.67. The Dow edged up 0.1% to 52,347.55, and all three indexes still ended the week higher.

## The Nvidia trade ran in reverse

Thursday's rally rested on a guess that Nvidia's ($NVDA) customers would shift orders. On Friday Benzinga described the chip rally unwinding, Investing.com put AMD's drop down to profit-taking after its run, and Yahoo Finance said the selling spread across the chip sector. It was AMD's **worst day since 5 November** in our data, when it fell 17.3%. Our [morning story](/news/2026-11-13/amd-broadcom-and-credo-shares-slide-what-happened/) traced the reversal.

Nvidia fell less, down 2.0% to $162.85, which left it worth about $3.9 trillion in our data, below $4 trillion. The week's split is still stark: from the 6 November close, AMD is up **18.9%** and Broadcom 18.2%, while Nvidia is down 12.7%. The Financial Times reported that Nvidia approached Arm's Rene Haas about the top job ([our brief](/news/2026-11-13/nvidia-nvda-approached-arms-rene-haas-to-be-its-next-ceo-ft-reports/)), and Arm ($ARM) fell 4.6%.

The companies that rent out Nvidia chips kept falling. Nebius ($NBIS) **plunged 9.9%** a day after its 16.8% drop on a trimmed outlook, as analysts flagged [delays at the data centers it rents](/news/2026-11-13/nebius-nbis-shares-slide-again-as-analysts-flag-data-center-delays/), and CoreWeave ($CRWV) slid 6.1%. Our [narrative on the trade](/news/2026-11-13/wall-street-is-betting-nvidias-loss-is-amd-and-broadcoms-gain-tuesday-will-test-it/) and the [bull and bear cases on Nvidia](/news/2026-11-13/bulls-vs-bears-on-nvidia-nvda-is-13-times-earnings-cheap-without-jensen-huang/) set out what would prove either side right.

## Wholesale inflation kept the 10-year above 5%

Producer prices rose 0.3% in October, less than the 0.4% forecast, but the annual rate climbed to 6.5%, as we [reported this morning](/news/2026-11-13/wholesale-prices-rose-less-than-expected-in-october-but-the-annual-rate-hit-6-5/). The 10-year Treasury yield rose 0.046 point to **5.149%** in our data, while the 2-year yield barely moved. CME FedWatch data still has a rate hike at the Fed's 9 December meeting fully priced, CNBC and Pluang reported.

Gold slipped 0.1% to $3,880.40 an ounce, within 1% of its 12-month low in our data, and FXStreet tied its weekly slide to the hike bets. Bitcoin **fell 4.1%** to $68,512, and Strategy ($MSTR), the biggest corporate holder, lost 2.4% after [MSCI said it will drop the stock](/news/2026-11-13/strategy-mstr-shares-slide-as-msci-drops-it-and-bitcoin-falls/).

## Earnings split the rest of the tape

Cisco ($CSCO) **slid 6.2%** even after it beat estimates and raised its AI revenue goal to $9 billion. GuruFocus and Proactive Investors both pinned the fall on margins: its adjusted gross margin fell to 65.6% from 68.1% as it sold more hardware and paid more for memory ([our recap](/news/2026-11-13/cisco-csco-beats-and-raises-as-ai-networking-sales-jump-43-but-margins-slip/)). Applied Materials ($AMAT) fell 2.7% despite [record revenue](/news/2026-11-13/applied-materials-amat-tops-q4-estimates-as-memory-chipmakers-spend-more/).

On the other side, Nu Holdings ($NU) **soared 11.8%**, its biggest gain since 5 October, a day after it reported a [record $1.1 billion quarterly profit](/news/2026-11-13/nu-holdings-nu-q3-profit-hits-a-record-1-1-billion-as-it-adds-3-7-million-customers/). Global Payments ($GPN) won back 4.2% of the 12.0% it lost on Thursday, when buyout firms [dropped their takeover talks](/news/2026-11-13/global-payments-gpn-plunges-12-as-buyout-firms-drop-takeover-talks/).

## What's next

Nvidia reports third-quarter results after the close on Tuesday, 17 November, its first report without Huang, and the first hard evidence of whether any orders moved. Our read: Friday showed how little of Thursday's swing was based on facts, since AMD gave back its entire Thursday gain without any new information about orders.
