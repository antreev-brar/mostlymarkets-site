Parker Hannifin ($PH) reported record sales of $6.0 billion for its fiscal first quarter, the three months to September, before the open on Thursday, up 18% year on year. Adjusted earnings of **$8.90 a share** beat the $8.23 Zacks consensus by 8%, and the maker of motion-control parts and aircraft systems raised its full-year forecast. The stock opened at $968.00, 6.4% above Wednesday's close, then fell with the market to close **down 2% at $892.29** as the S&P 500 lost 4.4%.

## What stood out

**Demand broadened beyond aircraft.** Organic sales, which strip out deals and currency, grew **11.5%**, and every part of the company grew by double digits on orders. Diversified Industrial International grew 11.6% organically, led by Asia at 23%, and North America grew 9.4%. "We experienced increased demand across all market verticals, resulting in double-digit organic sales growth and record backlog levels," Chief Executive Jenny Parmentier said in the release.

*Verdict: a clear step up; company sales grew only 3.9% a year over the past two years by our data, and this quarter's organic growth was three times that.*

**Margins kept climbing.** The adjusted segment margin reached a record 28.6%, with the international industrial business up 2.7 points to 27.7%. More sales through the same factories is the mechanism: Parker's costs grow slower than its volumes, so a larger share of each extra dollar falls to profit.

**Aerospace is still the engine, but slowing at the margin.** Aerospace Systems sales rose 13.8% with a 30.7% adjusted margin. Its 12-month order growth eased to 14% from 18% in the prior quarter, the release showed.

## The outlook

Parker raised its fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS range to **$36.45 to $37.25**, from $34.25 to $35.25 in August, against a Street estimate of about $35.6 cited by Benzinga and MarketBeat. Part of the lift comes from the newly completed purchase of Filtration Group, which is now in the numbers; organic growth guidance also rose, to 7.5% to 10.5% from 5.5% to 8.5%. GAAP EPS guidance fell to $29.65 to $30.45, from $30.00 to $31.00, on deal costs and amortization.

## Our take

We were impressed by double-digit organic growth across every segment and a record margin at this size. On the other hand, the deal roughly doubled long-term debt to $12.7 billion from $6.8 billion in June, and part of the guidance raise is bought rather than grown. Overall, a strong quarter that strengthens the thesis: Parker's 23.6% operating margin over the past 12 months already ranked in the top quarter of industrial peers by our data.

The reversal from a 6.4% gain at the open to a 2% loss tracked Thursday's market-wide selloff rather than anything new in the report. The stock trades at 25.1 times forward earnings against a peer median of 21.1, and sits 18.9% below its August high. The next test is fiscal second-quarter results in early February, when the first full quarter of Filtration Group shows whether the margin record holds.
