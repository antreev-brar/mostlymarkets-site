A week ago every piece of good news failed. This week one piece of news was enough. Nvidia ($NVDA) rehired Jensen Huang as chief executive before the open on Monday, its stock **soared 31.5%**, and the Nasdaq had its best day since April 2025. By Friday's 1 p.m. ET close the S&P 500 had risen 5.6% to 7,297.49 and the Nasdaq 6.7%, winning back about three-fifths of the previous week's rout.

The rally was wide but not deep. Almost every stock we track rose, yet Nvidia gave back part of Monday's jump on Tuesday and Wednesday, tariffs and a hot inflation number nearly broke the market on Wednesday, and a December rate hike is still priced as a near certainty. Both indexes remain on course for their worst November since 2008, Reuters and Finimize reported, with one session left on Monday.

Nvidia **rose 19.9%** for the week to $150.49, but it closed Friday 8.8% below Monday's $165.05, after S&P Global [put its credit rating on watch](/news/2026-11-25/sp-puts-nvidias-nvda-aa-rating-on-watch-for-a-downgrade-over-governance/) and the FT reported it had [halted server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries](/news/2026-11-26/nvidia-nvda-halts-server-shipments-to-four-southeast-asian-countries-ft-says/). It is still 37.1% below its 6 October high. Huang's return answered who runs the company, not the questions over its books ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-23/nvidias-crisis-has-moved-from-its-boss-to-its-books-what-the-evidence-says/)).
