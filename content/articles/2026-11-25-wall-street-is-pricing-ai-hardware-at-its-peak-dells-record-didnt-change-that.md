On Tuesday after the close, Dell ($DELL) reported that its revenue doubled to $54.9 billion, its AI order backlog hit a record $132.3 billion and its server margin rose. As of 7:14 a.m. ET on Wednesday the shares were **down 6.5%** before the open at $276.10, after an 11.8% fall in Tuesday's session. The story the market is telling with that price is that AI hardware profits are at or near their top, so a record quarter is worth less, not more.

The news peg is three reports in two days that beat estimates and were sold anyway. Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE) fell 16.3% on Tuesday after guiding its AI server margin down, and Analog Devices ($ADI) fell 17.4% after a flat forecast that followed 42% growth. Since Nvidia's ($NVDA) record close on 6 October, Dell has lost 48.6%, HPE 43.0%, Micron ($MU) 39.8% and Nvidia 35.1%, to Tuesday's close in our data.

The short version
- **What's happening:** AI hardware makers are growing fastest and trading cheapest: Micron and Super Micro at 3.6 times forward earnings, Dell at 10.3 and Nvidia at 10.8, against a 19.0 chipmaker median.
- **Why it matters:** A multiple that low says investors expect today's profits to shrink. If that is wrong, the cheapest stocks have the most room to recover; if it is right, the dearer ones still have further to fall.

## The story in one sentence

> Today's margins are "likely to mark the peak of the current cycle." Pzena Investment Management, a value fund, in its review of memory makers' margins in May 2026

Pzena wrote that about memory. This month the market has applied it to the whole AI hardware chain, from memory to servers to the analog chips that power data centers. The logic runs like this. If the biggest buyers' spending growth slows in 2027 and 2028 just as new factories and server lines come online, prices and margins fall from records. The companies earning the most today would then lose the most, so investors pay little for this year's profit.

## The evidence for it

### 1. Record quarters are being sold, not bought

HPE reported revenue up 50% on Monday and fell 16.3% on Tuesday. Analog Devices reported its biggest quarter ever on Tuesday morning and **plunged 17.4%**, its biggest move of the past year in our data, because its guide implied almost no growth next quarter ([our recap](/news/2026-11-24/analog-devices-adi-beats-q4-estimates-but-a-flat-q1-forecast-sends-shares-down-8/)). Dell fell with them before its own report, then beat on every line and kept falling. Mizuho cut its Dell price target to $500 from $650, citing lower multiples across the group rather than the quarter, Investing.com reported.

### 2. The fastest growers carry the lowest multiples

Micron's revenue rose **379%** in its latest quarter and Super Micro's ($SMCI) 298%, yet both trade at 3.6 times forward earnings, by our data from their filings. Paying less than four years of next year's profit for a company only makes sense if that profit is expected to fall. The same pattern runs through servers and storage: HPE is at 8.5 times, Western Digital ($WDC) 9.5 and Dell 10.3, against a 16.5 median for hardware makers.

### 3. The margin guide has turned at a server maker

HPE's Chief Financial Officer Marie Myers said the operating margin of its Cloud & AI segment, home of its servers, will **fall to about 14%** in fiscal 2027 from 18.4% in the fourth quarter, Seeking Alpha and GuruFocus reported. She tied it to a bigger share of lower-margin AI systems and to server price increases that are expected to slow ([our story](/news/2026-11-24/hpe-hpe-slides-11-5-before-the-open-as-it-sees-ai-server-margins-falling-to-14/)). Analog Devices also guided its adjusted operating margin to about 50.5% from 52.3%, its release showed.

### 4. The forecasts for later years are being cut

Research firm SemiAnalysis cut its forecast for global AI accelerator spending in 2028 by **28%**, and its 2030 figure to $923 billion from $1.531 trillion, OfficeChai, Phemex and Magica reported on 16 and 17 November. In flash memory, TrendForce now expects contract prices to fall in 2027 as Chinese cloud companies cut orders, it said on 19 November. TF International's Ming-Chi Kuo said on 20 November that Google cut planned 2027 shipments of its Broadcom-designed TPU chips by about 20%; Google has announced no change ([our story](/news/2026-11-20/broadcom-avgo-shares-sink-after-a-report-that-google-cut-its-2027-tpu-orders/)).

## The case against it

The companies doing the selling and the buying both say demand is still rising. "We ended the quarter with a record $132.3 billion AI backlog after booking $60.2 billion of orders, each rigorously vetted for customer quality and access to capital, land and power," Dell Chief Operating Officer Jeff Clarke said in Tuesday's release. Alphabet's ($GOOGL) Chief Financial Officer Anat Ashkenazi said on its 28 October call that she expects capital spending "to increase significantly in 2027", as we reported at the time.

1. **Orders are still outrunning shipments.** Dell booked $60.2 billion of AI orders in the quarter against **$22.0 billion** of AI server revenue, so its backlog grew by about $37 billion in three months ([our recap](/news/2026-11-24/dell-technologies-dell-beats-q3-estimates-as-revenue-doubles-and-its-ai-backlog-hits-132-billion/)). A peak in orders would show up here first, and it hasn't.
2. **HPE's squeeze is not the industry's.** Dell's server-and-storage unit earned a 17.0% operating margin, up from 12.4% a year earlier, even as low-margin AI racks grew to 40% of its sales. Nvidia guided to $130 billion of fourth-quarter revenue on 17 November, above the LSEG consensus.
3. **The price already assumes the fall.** Nvidia's trailing P/E of 19.6 is less than half its 45.9 median since August 2024, and Dell's is 17.1 against 20.2. If earnings merely hold through 2027, these stocks are priced for a drop that doesn't come.

The first is the strongest, because it rests on signed orders rather than forecasts. What it can't answer is the price those orders carry in 2028, and that is where SemiAnalysis made its biggest cuts.

Wednesday's leg of the selling has a second cause the peak story doesn't cover. Reuters reported overnight that the administration is preparing tariffs on 16 economies, Taiwan and South Korea among them ([our brief](/news/2026-11-25/us-readies-new-tariffs-on-16-economies-and-nasdaq-futures-fall-2-2/)), which would raise the cost of the chips and memory inside every AI server.

## What would change minds

- **Tuesday, 1 December, after the close:** Marvell ($MRVL) and Credo ($CRDO) report, on the Nasdaq calendar. Both sell the connections inside AI clusters; a 2027 outlook that still rises would undercut the peak call for networking.
- **Wednesday, 9 December, after the close:** Broadcom ($AVGO) reports. Its AI revenue outlook for 2027 will confirm or rule out Kuo's TPU cut, the one claim that touches next year's orders directly.
- **Thursday, 17 December:** Micron reports. It guided to an adjusted gross margin of about 86.25%; a figure below that would be the first margin crack from a maker rather than a forecaster.

## Our read

**Partly supported.** The peak is well documented where prices reset every quarter: flash memory forecasts have turned and HPE has guided its server margin down by about four points. The weakest link is demand for 2027, where Dell's backlog grew by about $37 billion in a quarter and Alphabet says spending will rise significantly, so the evidence points to a peak in margins at some makers, not in orders. At 10 times forward earnings, Dell and Nvidia are priced for a fall no buyer has announced. The number we'll watch is Broadcom's 2027 AI revenue outlook on 9 December, the first big supplier to speak after this week's sell-off.
