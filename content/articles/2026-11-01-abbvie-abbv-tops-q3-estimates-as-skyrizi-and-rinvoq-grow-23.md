AbbVie ($ABBV) reported third-quarter revenue of $17.40 billion on Friday morning, up 10.3% year on year and a little above the $17.32 billion analysts expected, according to MarketBeat's consensus. Adjusted earnings of **$3.82 a share** beat the $3.74 average estimate listed by Nasdaq and Earnings Whispers, and the company raised its full-year profit forecast. The shares closed up 1.4% at $294.89, a record close, on a day the Nasdaq fell 2.9%.

## What stood out

**Two drugs now carry the company.** Skyrizi and Rinvoq, AbbVie's newer treatments for psoriasis, Crohn's disease and arthritis, brought in **$8.48 billion** between them, almost half of all revenue, the release showed. They are the replacements for Humira, the arthritis drug that was once the world's best seller and has been losing sales to cheaper copies since 2023.

*Verdict: the handoff from Humira is done; growth is back in double digits on two drugs, not one.*

**Neuroscience is the second engine.** Sales rose 19.4% to $3.39 billion, led by the antipsychotic Vraylar at $1.07 billion and Botox for migraines at $1.08 billion. AbbVie now expects about $13 billion of neuroscience sales this year, the company said.

**Aesthetics is the weak spot.** Revenue from Botox Cosmetic and dermal fillers slipped 1.4%, the release showed, so a business AbbVie paid $63 billion for in its 2020 purchase of Allergan is still not growing.

## The outlook

AbbVie raised its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast to **$13.95 to $13.99**, from $13.76 to $13.96, and lifted its revenue outlook by about $200 million to $67.8 billion. It now sees Skyrizi at $21.8 billion and Rinvoq at $10.3 billion for the year. The profit range includes a $0.69-a-share hit from deals for drugs still in development booked through September, and excludes any further such charges, which the company says it can't forecast.

The dividend goes up 5.2% to $1.82 a quarter, payable on 16 February 2027.

## Our take

We were impressed that both newer drugs grew more than 20% at their size, and that neuroscience is now adding almost as much growth as Rinvoq. On the other hand, the revenue beat was only 0.5%, aesthetics shrank again, and AbbVie carried net debt of $64.3 billion at the end of June, 3.8 years of operating profit, by our data. Overall, a solid quarter that confirms the thesis: revenue grew 8.2% a year over the past two years, above the drugmaker median of 6.4%.

The price already reflects much of that. The stock is up 29.1% this year and trades at 19.6 times forward earnings, against a peer median of 18.1. The next test is the 2027 forecast in February, when investors will see how much Skyrizi and Rinvoq can add on top of a $32 billion base. Our [preview](/news/2026-10-29/abbvie-abbv-reports-earnings-tomorrow-what-to-watch/) set out what we were watching, and fellow drugmaker Regeneron ($REGN) also reported on Friday.
