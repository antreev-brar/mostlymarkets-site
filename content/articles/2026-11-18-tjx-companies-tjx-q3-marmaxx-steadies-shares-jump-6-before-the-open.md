TJX Companies ($TJX), the owner of T.J. Maxx, Marshalls and HomeGoods, reported fiscal third-quarter sales of **$15.95 billion** on Wednesday, ahead of the $15.84 billion analysts expected, according to IBES data from LSEG cited by Reuters. Adjusted EPS of $1.34, excluding tariff refunds, matched the consensus, Newsquawk and Investing.com reported. The shares **jumped 6.3%** to $138.00 before the open, as of 9:15 a.m. ET, in our data.

## What stood out

The number investors came for was Marmaxx, the T.J. Maxx and Marshalls division that brings in most of the company's sales. Its comparable sales, those at stores open a year or more, **rose 2%**, up from 1% in the second quarter, and the company said it expects further improvement in the fourth quarter, Reuters reported. That is still a third of last year's 6%, but it moves in the direction Chief Executive Ernie Herrman promised in August, when he called the slowdown "self-inflicted," Reuters and TIKR reported.

*Verdict: a start on the repair, not a finished one.*

The rest of the business carried the beat. HomeGoods comparable sales rose 5%, the release showed, and the pretax profit margin, profit before tax as a share of sales, reached **13.0%**. Half a point of that came from refunds of tariffs paid earlier, so the underlying margin was 12.5%, still below last year's 12.7%. "I am very pleased with our consolidated third quarter results," Herrman said in the release.

*Verdict: solid, with the refunds flattering the headline profit.*

## The outlook

TJX now expects full-year comparable sales to **rise 4%**, the top of its earlier 3% to 4% range, and adjusted EPS of $5.17 to $5.20, up from $5.15 to $5.20, the release said. It still plans to buy back about $3.0 billion of stock this year.

The holiday quarter is softer on paper. TJX guided to adjusted EPS of $1.42 to $1.45 on comparable sales up 2% to 3%, below the roughly $1.48 consensus cited by TheFly and MarketBeat, and said the forecast now assumes higher fuel costs.

## Our take

We were impressed that sales beat for a company already growing on top of a 5% comparable gain a year ago, and that Marmaxx turned up. On the other hand, the earnings beat was refund-padded, and the fourth-quarter range sits below estimates, though TJX's forecasts proved conservative in each of the last two quarters, when comparable sales beat its 2% to 3% plan. The market chose the Marmaxx number: the stock had fallen 13.9% from the day before its August report into Tuesday's close, so it priced in a broken division, as we wrote in our [preview](/news/2026-11-17/what-to-expect-from-tjx-companies-tjx-q3-earnings/).

The long-run picture doesn't change: revenue has grown 13.4% a year over five fiscal years, just above the 12.8% median of 66 consumer peers in our data, and return on equity of 57% ranks in their top quarter. TJX reports its holiday quarter in late February; Marmaxx back at 3% would close the debate.
