## What happened
Shares of chip designer AMD ($AMD) **fell 9.1%**, networking-chip maker Credo ($CRDO) 9.5% and power-chip maker Monolithic Power ($MPWR) 8.7% by 10:15 a.m. ET on Tuesday, as chip stocks gave back part of Monday's rally on Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia's chief executive. Chip-equipment makers fell with them: Teradyne ($TER) lost 8.8%, KLA ($KLAC) 7.8% and Applied Materials ($AMAT) 6.5%.

There was no company news behind the drop. Seeking Alpha, Yahoo Finance and Investing.com all described a retreat after Monday's record-breaking rally, with Nvidia ($NVDA) itself down 5.9% at $155.38 by 10:15 a.m. ET. CNBC reported that the investigations into Nvidia are not over, and Reuters said investors were weighing the risks that remain around Huang's return ([our bulls-vs-bears on the reinstatement](/news/2026-11-23/bulls-vs-bears-on-nvidia-nvda-after-huangs-return-is-the-crisis-over/)).

## Why it matters
The size of Tuesday's fall tracks the size of Monday's jump for most of the group: Credo rose **22.6%** on Monday and KLA 15.0%, so both still sit well above Friday's close. AMD is the exception. It rose only 6.8% on Monday, so Tuesday's drop puts it about 3% below where it ended last week, the weakest showing of the group across the two days.

No outlet has given an AMD-specific reason. It comes after a month in which, as we wrote on 20 November, the market stopped [treating AMD as the winner of Nvidia's crisis](/news/2026-11-20/wall-street-bet-amd-would-win-nvidias-china-crisis-now-it-is-pricing-china-risk-across-the-chip-chain/) and began pricing China risk across the whole chip chain.

## What the market is telling us
AMD's shares moved 5% or more on 63 of the last 252 sessions, so swings like this are common for it. The stock closed Monday up 111.7% this year but 30.2% below its 6 October closing high of $649.42. Credo is a wilder ride still, with 108 moves of 5% or more in a year, and it sits 59.5% below its June high. We read Tuesday as the market pricing back some of Monday's relief, not new information about chip demand; the next real test is how the open inquiries into Nvidia are resolved.
