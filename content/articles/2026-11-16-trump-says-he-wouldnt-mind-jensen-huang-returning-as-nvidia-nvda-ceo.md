President Donald Trump said on Monday he "would not mind" if Jensen Huang returned as chief executive of Nvidia ($NVDA), speaking to reporters at the White House before leaving for Asia, Reuters, Bloomberg and NBC News reported. He also said he did not think the scandal that led to Huang's resignation on 12 November put US national security at risk, CNBC reported, and defended Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick.

The remark cuts against his own Treasury secretary: Scott Bessent said on Sunday that Nvidia's board should "go outside the company" for its next chief ([our story](/news/2026-11-15/bessent-says-nvidia-nvda-should-hire-an-outside-ceo-after-huang/)), and the Financial Times reported the same day that Huang has discussed a return ([our story](/news/2026-11-15/jensen-huang-has-discussed-a-return-as-nvidia-nvda-ceo-ft-reports/)). The choice still rests with Nvidia's board, which has an investigation under way. The stock was **down 1.9%** at $159.75 at 10:30 a.m. ET, below Friday's one-year low close.

What's next: Nvidia reports its third quarter after the close on Tuesday 17 November, when interim Chief Executive Colette Kress will face the first public questions on the search.
