On Friday, 13 November, the 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.14%, which sets the bar for any stock bought for its dividend. A yield above 5% only beats a Treasury if the company can keep paying it, and the test is cash: whether the free cash flow a business throws off, after its capital spending, covers the dividend with room left over.

Our screen ranked the companies in our coverage by dividend yield, then checked each payout against free cash flow over the 12 months to September 2026, using SEC filings in our fundamentals data and the dividend implied by each stock's current yield. We left out companies with no free cash flow figure or losses, and stocks we listed in the last 14 days.

```picks
like: MO, CMCSA
skip: UPS
```

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. Altria ($MO)
**Dividend yield: 5.65%, as of 13 November 2026.** Altria sells Marlboro cigarettes in the US, plus oral nicotine pouches and other smoke-free products.

1. The cash is steady: free cash flow has stayed between **$8.1 billion and $9.1 billion** every year since 2020, and was $9.1 billion in the 12 months to September 2026. At its current yield the dividend costs about $7.4 billion a year, so cash covers it 1.2 times.
2. It keeps more of each dollar than anyone in its group: its free cash flow margin of 38.6% over the last 12 months is the highest of the 35 consumer-staples companies we compare it with, against a median of 10.1%.
3. Its quarter beat and it raised guidance: on 29 October Altria lifted its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast to $5.66 to $5.74 from $5.61 to $5.72, its release said, and its cigarette volume fell 4.0%, less than the industry's estimated 5.0%, Investing.com reported.

**The price:** 13.5 times expected earnings against a peer median of 17.4 times, but its trailing P/E of 16.2 is above its two-year median of 12.2 after a **36.4% rise this year** to a 52-week high.

**What could go wrong:** volume. Revenue has shrunk 2.3% a year over five years, so the dividend depends on price rises outrunning the fall in packs sold; a year in which cigarette volumes fall faster than prices rise would show up first in that free cash flow figure.

### 2. Comcast ($CMCSA)
**Dividend yield: 5.25%, as of 13 November 2026.** Comcast sells home broadband and mobile service under the Xfinity name and owns NBCUniversal and Sky.

1. The dividend is the smallest claim on its cash in this list: free cash flow was **$19.7 billion** in the 12 months to September 2026, and at its current yield the dividend costs about $4.7 billion, a quarter of it.
2. The rest goes to buybacks: its diluted share count fell 8.2% in two years, in the top quarter of the 25 communication-services companies we compare it with, where the median fell 2.7%.
3. The quarter was mixed: reported revenue fell 6.1% to $29.3 billion after the spin-off of its cable networks into Versant in January, but rose 0.5% on a like-for-like basis, its release said, while it lost 144,000 broadband customers and its adjusted free cash flow fell 14.8% to $4.2 billion, Investing.com and TradingView reported.

**The price:** 7.4 times expected earnings, against a peer median of 16.1 times, and 4.5 times free cash flow. The stock is 22.5% below its February high.

**What could go wrong:** broadband. It is losing customers to fiber and fixed-wireless rivals while carrying net debt of $79.8 billion, 4.4 years of operating profit, so a faster slide in subscribers would squeeze the cash that pays for both the dividend and the buybacks.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### United Parcel Service ($UPS)
**Dividend yield: 5.84%, as of 13 November 2026.** UPS delivers parcels in the US and around the world.

1. The dividend takes almost all of the cash: at the **$1.64 a quarter** declared on 4 November, it costs about $5.6 billion a year on 852 million shares, against free cash flow of $6.4 billion in the 12 months to September 2026, 88% of it. In fiscal 2025 free cash flow was $4.8 billion, less than that bill.
2. It keeps little of each sale: its free cash flow margin of 5.9% over two years is in the bottom quarter of the 89 industrial companies we compare it with, against a median of 12.7%, and its operating margin of 7.1% sits in the bottom quarter too.
3. Growth has stalled: revenue grew 0.9% a year over five years, in the bottom tenth of the group, even after a strong third quarter in which revenue rose 6.7% to $22.9 billion and UPS raised its 2026 adjusted free cash flow target to about $6.1 billion, its release and CNBC said.

**The price:** 14.5 times expected earnings against a peer median of 21.7 times, but its trailing P/E of 21.7 is above its two-year median of 16.4.

**What would change our mind:** cash. UPS's own target of about $6.1 billion this year is barely above the dividend; free cash flow that holds above $7 billion through a full year would give the payout real cover.
