On Monday, cloud provider TensorWave said it signed a six-year agreement to supply OpenAI with **457 megawatts** of dedicated computing capacity at several US data centers, running AMD's ($AMD) Instinct MI455X chips, according to its release on Business Wire, also reported by Reuters and Pulse 2. The capacity starts coming online in the first half of 2027 and should be fully deployed by early 2028; TensorWave called it its largest deployment and is the first neocloud, a company that rents out AI chips, publicly named as a supplier under the AMD and OpenAI pact.

That pact, signed in October 2025, covers 6 gigawatts of AMD chips for OpenAI over several generations, and AMD granted OpenAI a warrant for up to 160 million AMD shares that vests as milestones are met, AMD's filing shows. Monday's deal names who will build part of it, on the day research firm SemiAnalysis cut its 2028 AI chip spending forecast by 28% ([our debate](/news/2026-11-16/bulls-vs-bears-on-ai-spending-semianalysis-cut-28-morgan-stanley-says-the-cycle-is-intact/)). AMD was **down 5.4%** at $505.42 at 10:30 a.m. ET with the rest of AI hardware.

What isn't known: the contract's dollar value, which TensorWave did not disclose, and how the capacity is financed.
