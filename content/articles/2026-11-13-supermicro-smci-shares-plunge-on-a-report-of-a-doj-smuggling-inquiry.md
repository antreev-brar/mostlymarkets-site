## What happened
Shares of AI-server maker Supermicro ($SMCI) **plunged 11.3%** on Thursday after Bloomberg reported that federal prosecutors in Manhattan are examining whether entities linked to Alice Huang helped smuggle Supermicro products into China, a report Reuters and Benzinga carried. Huang is the former Megaspeed executive at the center of the week's reports on Nvidia's ($NVDA) co-founder, who resigned as Nvidia's chief executive the same day.

Earlier on Thursday, Semafor reported three separate Justice Department inquiries, into Nvidia, Megaspeed and Supermicro, Yahoo Finance and Benzinga reported. Supermicro said it had been responsive to inquiries and had fully cooperated with US and Taiwanese officials, according to Reuters. No charges have been reported, and Bloomberg noted that such inquiries can end without any.

The shares closed at $26.99, down 11.3% from the previous close, on 2.6 times their usual volume in our data.

## Why it matters
Supermicro sells servers built around Nvidia's chips, and US rules bar the most advanced of them from China. A smuggling case would put its export compliance, and its standing with US customers and Nvidia, under scrutiny again. The stock had only just recovered: it [jumped after its 3 November report](/news/2026-11-04/supermicro-smci-shares-jump-as-sales-hit-19-9-billion-and-it-raises-its-forecast/), when quarterly sales reached $19.9 billion and it raised its forecast.

It also ties Supermicro to the same story that cost Nvidia its CEO, which is why the stock fell more than twice as far as Nvidia did on Thursday.

## What the market is telling us
Supermicro's shares moved 5% or more on 72 of the last 252 sessions. The last move at least this big in the same direction was on 10 June 2026, when the stock fell 28.0%. The stock is down 7.8% this year, and at $26.99 it sits 46.2% below its 52-week closing high of $50.17 from 2 June 2026. $1,000 invested in Supermicro five years ago would be worth $6,178 today.

The market is pricing a legal risk it can't yet size: nothing is known about the inquiry's scope or timing. Before Friday's open the stock was up 1.0% at $27.25, as of 7:15 a.m. ET.
