This week, Anthropic plans to price its shares on Wednesday and start trading on Nasdaq on Thursday, and Amazon ($AMZN) owns a stake worth about **$244 billion to $272 billion** at the price range, according to a Reuters tally and FourWeekMBA's count from the filing. That is about 9% of Amazon's $2.88 trillion market value, for about $18 billion invested since 2023.

## How it got here

Amazon started as an online bookshop in 1994, became the store for almost everything, and then, in 2006, began renting out its spare computing power as Amazon Web Services. For most of the past decade the pattern held: the store brought in customers on thin margins, and AWS and advertising made the money.

The AI boom changed what Amazon spends on. Capital spending was $77.26 billion in the third quarter alone, more than double a year earlier, and Amazon still expects about $220 billion for 2026, its 29 October release said. On the same day it missed earnings estimates, and the stock fell to $240.35 by the next close before recovering 10.9% to $266.66 on Friday.

At the same time, its earnings have been swelled by paper gains on Anthropic: a $53.4 billion pre-tax gain in the second quarter alone, Amazon's release and Beancount's analysis of it showed. **Is Amazon still a great business when nearly every dollar it makes goes into data centers, or has it become a bet on one AI customer?**

## The business in one line

Shoppers and third-party sellers pay it for goods, delivery and Prime memberships every day; advertisers pay it for space on its pages; and companies pay AWS monthly, by usage, for computing.

## The numbers that matter

### Growth
Revenue grew **13.2% a year** over five years and 13.4% a year over the last two, both at or below the median of the 16 consumer internet companies we cover, from SEC filings. Amazon is too big to grow like MercadoLibre, but AWS's 39% is speeding it up from within.

### Profitability
The operating margin was **13.1%** over the 12 months to September, up 5.2 points in five years. North America's margin was about 7.7% in the third quarter, up from about 4.5% a year earlier, by our arithmetic from the release: the store is no longer a break-even business.

### Cash
Free cash flow was **negative $46.9 billion** over the latest 12 months, or minus 5.9% of revenue, the worst among its peers, whose median is plus 11.9%. Operating cash flow of $168.3 billion was healthy; capital spending was simply larger.

### Returns
Return on capital was about **11.2%**, a little below the 12.1% peer median, because the new data centers are on the books before they earn.

### Balance sheet
Amazon owed **$125 billion** more than it held in cash at the end of September, about 1.2 years of operating profit. That's manageable for a company of its size, but it is borrowing to build.

*Verdict: the profit engine has never been stronger; the cash engine has stopped for now.*

## Why the stock is where it is

1. **The spending bill.** Capital spending more than doubled to $77.26 billion in the third quarter, and free cash flow went negative, as our [Q3 recap](/news/2026-10-29/amazon-amzn-q3-aws-speeds-up-but-ads-and-profit-miss-estimates/) showed. The stock fell 4.2% on 29 October and 1.4% more the next day.
2. **A soft profit outlook.** Fourth-quarter operating income guidance of $28.5 billion to $33.5 billion sat below the roughly $33 billion analysts expected.
3. **A rebound on AWS.** Phillip Securities upgraded the stock to buy on 2 November, citing AWS, 24/7 Wall St. and the Motley Fool reported, and the shares have recovered most of the post-results drop since.
4. **The Anthropic windfall.** Since 23 October, the last close before Anthropic's public filing, Amazon has risen about 8% while Nvidia fell, as our [Anthropic narrative](/news/2026-11-15/wall-street-is-betting-anthropics-ipo-can-rescue-the-ai-trade-3-numbers-will-decide-it/) showed.

## Testing the worry

**The spending is temporary or lasting?** Partly temporary. Amazon's own forecast is AWS growth of "over 40% both in 2027 and in 2028," Chief Executive Andy Jassy said in the release, which is the demand the buildings are for. But Amazon hasn't given a 2027 spending number, and Microsoft and Alphabet are raising theirs too ([our Microsoft breakdown](/news/2026-11-08/microsoft-msft-sells-more-than-ever-but-keeps-less-cash-is-the-business-still-strong/)), so this is the industry's cost of staying in the race, not an Amazon mistake.

**Contained or spreading?** Contained to AWS. North America's operating income rose from $4.8 billion to $8.7 billion in a year, so the store is funding more of the build, not less.

**Is the earnings number real?** Not all of it. Over the last 12 months net income was $134.8 billion while operating income was $104.4 billion, our data shows; the gap is mostly Anthropic gains, which are paper profits until shares are sold. Once Anthropic is listed, Amazon will mark the stake to its market price every quarter, so its earnings will swing with Anthropic's stock.

**The bull case, with arithmetic.** If AWS grows 40% a year as Amazon projects, its $184 billion annual run rate becomes about $360 billion by late 2028. At its third-quarter margin of about 39% ($17.8 billion of profit on $46.0 billion of sales), that is roughly $140 billion of yearly operating profit from AWS alone, more than the whole company earns now.

*Verdict: the worry that survives is timing. The demand is documented; the cash payback is not.*

## The moat

- **Scale in the cloud.** AWS ran at $184 billion of yearly revenue in the third quarter, Jassy said. Azure grew faster, at 48%, and Google Cloud at 103% from a smaller base ([our Alphabet breakdown](/news/2026-11-07/alphabet-googl-had-its-worst-day-since-2004-is-the-business-broken/)), so AWS is losing share while gaining dollars.
- **The delivery network.** North America's margin nearly doubled in a year on 6% growth, which shows its warehouses and vans now earn more on each parcel.
- **Advertising on its own shelves.** Ad sales were $19.64 billion in the third quarter. Shoppers search with intent to buy, which is why brands pay, though growth slowed to 11% on Prime Day timing.

Will Amazon be here, and stronger, in 10 years? Its store and cloud say yes; whether it's stronger depends on what the AI buildings earn.

## The risks we'd watch

1. **The spending doesn't pay back on time.** The size of it, on our own assumption of a six-year life for the gear and buildings: $220 billion ÷ 6 ≈ **$37 billion a year** of new depreciation, about a third of the last 12 months' $104.4 billion operating profit. That is a rough worst case, because AWS revenue growth offsets it, and some of the spending is on buildings that last longer.
2. **Anthropic concentration.** Anthropic's filing shows about $895 billion of computing commitments across its suppliers, and growth slowed to 47% quarter on quarter in the third quarter from 144%, as our [story on the filing](/news/2026-11-02/anthropics-ipo-filing-shows-17-billion-of-q3-sales-and-a-swing-to-an-operating-loss/) showed. A weak debut would hit both Amazon's stake and the believability of AWS's biggest customer.
3. **Debt.** Net debt is $125 billion; another year of negative free cash flow adds to it.

In proportion: even the worst case on depreciation leaves Amazon profitable, and the stake is worth more than 13 times the $18 billion invested. These are risks to the price, not to survival.

The trailing P/E flatters it, because it counts the Anthropic gains. On operating profit, which leaves them out, Amazon trades at **27.6 times**, its cheapest of the last three years by our arithmetic from SEC filings and our prices (using today's share count, which rose 1.8% in two years). Take out the stake at the middle of the IPO range and the rest of Amazon trades at about 25 times operating profit.

The forward P/E of 29 is the expensive reading, half again its peers' median, because forecasts of next year's earnings leave out gains like the Anthropic marks. That price assumes AWS keeps growing near 40% and spending stops rising in 2027.

## Our verdict

**Business: Solid. Price: Below its usual price.** Amazon earns more from operations than ever, its store finally pays, and AWS's 39% growth backs up the spending. But free cash flow is negative $46.9 billion, net debt is rising, and part of its reported profit is a paper gain on one customer. On operating profit the stock is cheaper than in each of the last two years, at 27.6 times. What would change our mind is a 2027 spending plan that rises again without AWS growth above 40%. The first test is Anthropic's debut on Thursday 19 November; the bigger one is the spending guide with its fourth-quarter results early next year.

Amazon has never been more profitable, and it has never kept less of what it makes.
