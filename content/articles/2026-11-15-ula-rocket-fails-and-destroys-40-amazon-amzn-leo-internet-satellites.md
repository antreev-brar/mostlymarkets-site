A Vulcan Centaur rocket from United Launch Alliance, the joint venture of Boeing ($BA) and Lockheed Martin ($LMT), failed about 10 minutes after lifting off from Cape Canaveral at 12:40 p.m. ET on Sunday, destroying the **40 Amazon Leo internet satellites** it carried, Reuters, Space.com and Spaceflight Now reported. It was ULA's first total mission failure since the company was formed in 2006, and the cause isn't known yet.

The launch was Vulcan's return to flight after nine months and the first of 38 Vulcan flights Amazon ($AMZN) bought for Leo, its planned satellite internet service and rival to SpaceX's Starlink, Spaceflight Now reported. Amazon now has to wait for ULA's failure investigation before it can fly on Vulcan again, which puts the rest of those flights on hold just as Amazon is spending about $220 billion this year on data centers ([our breakdown](/news/2026-11-15/amazon-amzn-spends-all-its-cash-on-ai-is-the-business-still-strong/)).

What's next: how long the investigation will take isn't known yet; the first trading reaction for all three stocks comes at Monday's open.
