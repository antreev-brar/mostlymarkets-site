This week's three biggest retail reports all beat on the numbers, and the stocks still went three different ways. Target ($TGT) raised its forecast on Wednesday and has since climbed to within 2% of its 52-week high; TJX Companies ($TJX) jumped on the same day as its Marmaxx stores recovered; and Ross Stores ($ROST) sank 9.0% after hours on Thursday on a holiday profit guide just below estimates.

Our screen: companies worth $10 billion or more that reported in the last seven days and that we recapped. We judged each on its quarter, then on five years of growth, operating margins, cash and return on capital from SEC filings, the metrics that separate good retailers from the rest, against 66 consumer-discretionary peers (32 staples retailers for Target), and then on the price.

```picks
like: ROST, TJX
skip: TGT
```

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. Ross Stores ($ROST)
**Return on capital of 76.7%, the top tenth of 27 consumer peers, from filings to August 2026.** Ross runs the Ross Dress for Less and dd's Discounts off-price chains.

The third quarter was clean: earnings of $1.91 a share beat the $1.82 Zacks consensus, comparable-store sales rose 8% on more customer traffic, and the operating margin widened 0.7 point to 12.3%, with no tariff refunds this time ([our recap](/news/2026-11-19/ross-stores-rost-tops-q3-estimates-but-its-holiday-profit-guide-sends-shares-down-9/)). The fall came from the fourth-quarter guide of $2.16 to $2.25 a share against about $2.26 expected.

1. **Each dollar tied up in the business earns more than three times what peers make:** return on capital of 76.7%, against a 22.0% median.
2. **Growth is speeding up:** sales rose 13.3% on a year earlier in the latest quarter in our data, the top quarter of peers, after 7.8% a year over two years.
3. **It pays for itself:** free cash flow was 11.4% of sales over the past 12 months, up from 9.7% over two years, with $3.3 billion of net cash and the share count down 3.6% in two years.

**The price:** 25.0 times forward earnings, above the 20.0 times peer median, while its trailing P/E of 26.6 is close to its 25.0 median since September 2024. The premium is in line with returns that rank in the top tenth.

**What would change our mind:** holiday comparable sales below the 4% bottom of its range when it reports the fourth quarter.

### 2. TJX Companies ($TJX)
**Revenue growth of 13.4% a year over five fiscal years to January 2026, above the 12.8% peer median.** TJX owns the T.J. Maxx, Marshalls and HomeGoods off-price chains.

Sales of $15.95 billion beat the $15.84 billion IBES estimate from LSEG, comparable sales rose 4% against a 2% to 3% plan, and Marmaxx, the T.J. Maxx and Marshalls division that brings in most of its sales, improved to 2% from 1% ([our recap](/news/2026-11-18/tjx-companies-tjx-q3-marmaxx-steadies-shares-jump-6-before-the-open/)). Its holiday profit guide also trailed estimates, but the market forgave it.

1. **It earns well on its owners' money:** return on equity of 57.0%, the top quarter of peers, against a 27.1% median.
2. **The cash is turning up:** free cash flow was 9.4% of sales over the past 12 months, up from 8.2% over two years and above the 9.2% median, and it plans about $3.0 billion of buybacks this year.
3. **No debt to worry about:** $3.1 billion of net cash at 1 August 2026.

**The price:** 26.5 times forward earnings, above the 20.0 times peer median, but its trailing P/E of 25.8 is below its 29.9 median since August 2024. It was 9.2% down this year at Thursday's close, so the market prices less than its usual premium for a business that has not changed much.

**What would change our mind:** Marmaxx comparable sales stuck at 2% or lower in the holiday quarter, reported in late February.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Target ($TGT)
**Free cash flow of 3.5% of sales over two years to August 2026, the bottom quarter of 33 staples retailers.** Target runs big-box stores selling groceries, household goods, clothing and home furnishings.

The quarter was good: sales rose 7.1% to $27.07 billion against $26.56 billion expected in an LSEG survey cited by CNBC, comparable traffic rose 5.0%, and the full-year forecast went up, though $0.50 of its $2.65 adjusted EPS came from tariff refunds ([our recap](/news/2026-11-18/target-tgt-beats-q3-estimates-as-traffic-jumps-5-and-raises-its-forecast/)).

1. **One quarter doesn't undo two flat years:** revenue grew 0.2% a year over two years and 2.3% a year over five, both behind most staples retailers.
2. **Thin margins:** the operating margin was 5.6% over the past 12 months, against a 13.6% peer median, and the gross margin ranks in the bottom quarter.
3. **Cash is the weak spot:** the 3.5% free cash flow margin compares with a 10.1% peer median, alongside $9.9 billion of net debt.

**The price:** its trailing P/E of 17.4 is well above its 14.0 median since August 2024, and the stock was up 71.3% this year at Thursday's close, 1.4% below its 52-week high. The price already assumes the turnaround keeps going.

**What could go wrong (for our skip):** a holiday quarter that keeps comparable sales near 5% without refunds would show the turnaround is lasting, and the long-run numbers would start to catch up.
