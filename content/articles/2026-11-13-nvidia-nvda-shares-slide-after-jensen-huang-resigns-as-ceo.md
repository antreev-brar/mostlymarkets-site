## What happened
Shares of AI-chip maker Nvidia ($NVDA) **slid 4.3%** on Thursday after the company said co-founder Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and director with immediate effect. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO and stay CFO while the board searches inside and outside the company, Nvidia said in an SEC filing.

Huang, who had run Nvidia for 33 years, left after a week of reports on his relationships: first with a former executive of Megaspeed, a Singapore customer US intelligence reportedly suspects of spying for China, then with Nvidia employees, NBC News and CBS News reported. The board, which had opened an independent probe on Sunday, thanked him for his "immeasurable contributions" and named Harvey Jones its first-ever chair.

The stock fell as much as 14.4% in the first minutes, to $148.80, then recovered. The shares closed at $166.19, down 4.3% from the previous close, on 4.5 times their usual volume, in our data.

## Why it matters
The question has shifted from Huang's conduct to whether customers stay. Nvidia said operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap would continue without interruption. Traders bought rivals anyway: AMD ($AMD) rose 5.9% and Broadcom ($AVGO) 5.5%, which mlq.ai and Proactive Investors tied to hopes they win share.

Analysts cut targets fast. Benzinga counted nine banks lowering them on Thursday, including Bernstein's Stacy Rasgon, who downgraded the stock to market perform with a **$200 target**, a call TipRanks also lists. Kress delivers the third-quarter report on Tuesday 17 November after the close, the first test of whether orders held up.

## What the market is telling us
Nvidia's shares moved 5% or more on 9 of the last 252 sessions. The last move at least this big in the same direction was on 10 November 2026, when the stock fell 5.5%. The stock is down 10.9% this year, and at $166.19 it sits 30.5% below its 52-week closing high of $239.24 from 6 October 2026. $1,000 invested in Nvidia five years ago would be worth $5,469 today.

The intraday recovery says investors see a governance shock, not a broken business, and Tuesday's report is where that view gets tested. Before Friday's open the stock was up 2.8% at $170.85.
