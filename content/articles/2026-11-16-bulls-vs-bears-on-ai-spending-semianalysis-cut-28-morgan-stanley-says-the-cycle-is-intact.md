On Monday, chip research firm SemiAnalysis cut its forecast for what the world will spend on AI chips in 2028 by **28%**, Futu News, PANews and Investing.com reported. A day earlier, Morgan Stanley's strategists had told clients to own as many stocks as their mandates allow, because the AI spending cycle is intact. Both can't be right about the same order book, and Nvidia ($NVDA), the biggest seller into it, reports on Tuesday.

The number at the center is the slope of AI chip spending after 2027. By 10:30 a.m. ET, Micron ($MU) had fallen 7.1%, Broadcom ($AVGO) 5.1% and Nvidia 1.9%, and the Nasdaq was down 1.8% at 10 a.m., with Reuters pointing to the worry over Nvidia before its report.

## The bull case

### Michael Wilson, chief US equity strategist, Morgan Stanley
**The AI spending cycle is intact, so stocks are worth owning to the hilt.** In a note on 15 November, Wilson's team moved global stocks to "max overweight" and set an S&P 500 target of 8,700 for the end of 2027, about 15% above Friday's close, Bloomberg, MarketWatch and MLQ reported. The target rests on 19 times forward earnings of $458 a share, MLQ and BigGo Finance reported. The risks they named themselves are inflation that forces the Fed to tighten further and an AI spending cycle that ends sooner than they expect.

### Venu Krishna, US equity strategist, Barclays
**Stay long AI, but know that the index now hangs on it.** On Monday, Krishna trimmed Barclays' end-2027 S&P 500 target to 8,700 from 8,800 while raising his 2027 earnings estimate to $434 a share from $414, Investing.com, Reuters and TokenPost reported. He expects chips and tech hardware to supply at least half of the index's earnings growth in both 2027 and 2028, and called that concentration a potentially systemic risk. Barclays stayed long AI and quality growth stocks and upgraded health care as a hedge, Investing.com reported.

## The bear case

### SemiAnalysis, chip research firm
**AI chip spending will grow far more slowly after 2027 than it expected.** The firm cut its forecast for global AI accelerator spending by 28% for 2028, 41% for 2029 and 40% for 2030, to $923 billion from $1.531 trillion, citing tighter capital markets and slower expansion at the frontier AI labs, Futu News, PANews and KuCoin reported. It also cut its forecast for the total silicon wafer area of AI chips shipped from 2026 to 2030 by 17%, and named Nvidia the most exposed supplier.

### Frank Lee, analyst, HSBC
**Nvidia has its own problems on top of the cycle's.** Lee cut Nvidia to hold and his price target to $170 from $365, MLQ, Traders Union and TIKR reported. He expects pressure on the stock until a permanent chief executive is named and proves they can execute. He also estimates that tighter US export rules could cut Nvidia's fiscal 2028 earnings per share by 13%, because about 11% of its revenue over the past six quarters came from markets outside the US and Taiwan that could be targeted.

### Kristalina Georgieva, managing director, IMF
**The money paying for the build-out is fragile.** Georgieva told CNBC on Monday that the world economy is "standing on one leg", leaning on AI. The IMF puts AI deals in which money invested comes back to the investor as orders at a little over $1 trillion, about two-thirds funded by debt, CNBC and MLQ reported. That ties directly to SemiAnalysis's reason for its cut: if lenders tighten, the labs and neoclouds that borrow to buy chips buy fewer of them ([our narrative on the money loop](/news/2026-11-16/wall-street-is-betting-ais-1-trillion-money-loop-breaks-at-the-neoclouds-nvidias-report-will-test-it/)).

## Where they agree
SemiAnalysis's biggest cuts are to 2029 and 2030, while the strategists' targets run only to the end of 2027, so the two sides are partly arguing about different years. Both sides also name the same weak point: financing. Wilson lists a shorter spending cycle and tighter Fed policy as his risks, Krishna calls the hardware concentration systemic, and SemiAnalysis and Georgieva both point at debt. They disagree on timing: whether the slowdown after 2027 is already the story, or a problem for 2028.

## The number that decides it
Nvidia's forecast for its fourth quarter, due after the close on Tuesday 17 November with a 5 p.m. ET call, is the first hard number since the cut. Analysts polled by LSEG expect about $109 billion of revenue for the third quarter, Invezz and BigGo Finance reported; the guide for the next quarter, and what interim Chief Executive Colette Kress says about 2027 orders, will tell us whether customers are still placing orders at last quarter's pace. Dell's results on 24 November are the second read, from the server side.

## Our read
The bears have the fresher evidence, and Krishna's own arithmetic makes it count: if hardware supplies half the index's earnings growth, a 28% cut to 2028 chip spending is a cut to the bulls' targets too, not just to Nvidia's. But SemiAnalysis's cut is a model of 2028 to 2030, not an order cancelled by a buyer. **Too early to tell, with the burden now on the bulls**: a fourth-quarter guide from Nvidia that shows customers still adding orders would answer the forecast for now; a guide flat with the third quarter would make the cut look like the start of a trend, not an outlier.
