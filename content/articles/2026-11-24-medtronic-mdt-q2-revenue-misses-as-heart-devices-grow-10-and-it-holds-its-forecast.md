Medtronic ($MDT) reported fiscal second-quarter revenue of **$9.36 billion** before the open on Tuesday, up 4.5% from a year earlier and 6.5% on an organic basis, short of the $9.48 billion average analyst estimate reported by Reuters. Adjusted earnings of $1.34 a share edged past the $1.33 expected, and the company kept its full-year forecast unchanged. The shares fell as much as **4.3%** in early pre-market trading, to $96.43 at 6:44 a.m. ET, though thin pre-market prints had them close to Monday's $100.72 close by 7:00 a.m. ET in our data.

## What stood out

The headline miss is mostly arithmetic. Medtronic completed the separation of MiniMed, its diabetes business, on 15 October through an exchange offer, so the quarter carried only part of that unit's sales: the "other" line that holds it fell to $629 million from $792 million a year earlier, the release showed. Organic growth, which strips it out, was **6.5%**, faster than the 5.5% of a year ago. *Verdict: the core business grew, but the reported number fell short of where analysts had it.*

Cardiac ablation, the tools that treat irregular heartbeats with pulsed-field energy, is the engine. Sales **grew 58%**, 80% in the US, and lifted the cardiovascular unit, Medtronic's largest, to 10.4% organic growth. The release put the US ablation share gain at about 7 points, so the growth is coming from rivals such as Boston Scientific ($BSX), not only from a growing market.

Neuroscience and medical surgical grew a slower 3.2% and 4.4% organically. Those two make up more than half of sales, which is why a 58% ablation line still adds up to mid-single-digit growth overall.

## The outlook

Medtronic held its fiscal 2027 forecast of **7.25% to 7.75%** organic revenue growth and $5.94 to $6.00 of adjusted EPS, the range it raised on 1 September. The LSEG consensus of $5.97, cited by Reuters, sits inside it. From here the forecast has to be met without any contribution from diabetes.

## Our take

We were impressed by ablation: a 58% growth rate at this size is the clearest evidence in years that Medtronic can win in a fast market. On the other hand, revenue missed, and TD Cowen had said before the report that a guidance raise was likely, so a hold reads as caution. One quarter doesn't change the long-run picture: over five years Medtronic's revenue grew 3.8% a year, in the bottom quarter of the 19 device makers in our data, and at 16.8 times expected earnings the stock is priced close to its peers' 17.8 times. The third-quarter report in February, the first full quarter without diabetes, is the next test; [our preview](/news/2026-11-23/what-to-expect-from-medtronics-mdt-q2-earnings/) set out the bar.
