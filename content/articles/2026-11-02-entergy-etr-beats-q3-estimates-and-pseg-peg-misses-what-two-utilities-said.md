Two big power utilities reported before the open on Monday, and they split. Entergy ($ETR), which serves Louisiana, Arkansas, Mississippi and Texas, beat estimates as data centers and metals plants used more of its power. New Jersey's PSEG ($PEG) **missed on profit and revenue**, and its shares fell 2.3% to $72.59 by 10:15 a.m. ET, while Entergy slipped 0.4%.

## Entergy: industrial demand did the work

Entergy earned an adjusted **$1.98 a share** in the third quarter, up from $1.53 a year earlier and ahead of the $1.71 LSEG consensus, the company's release and LSEG data cited by Rallies showed. Net income rose to $940 million from $694 million. Weather-adjusted retail sales, which strip out the effect of a hot or mild summer, grew 4.5%, and industrial sales rose 4.9%, led by data center and primary-metals customers, the company said.

It narrowed its 2026 adjusted earnings forecast to $4.35 to $4.45 a share, and raised its quarterly dividend 6% to $0.68, StockTitan and GuruFocus reported from the release. The new range's midpoint, $4.40, sits just below the $4.41 some analysts expected, IndexBox reported. Our [preview from Sunday](/news/2026-11-01/what-to-expect-from-entergys-etr-q3-earnings/) set out why its data-center contracts matter.

## PSEG: a miss, and the forecast held

PSEG's non-GAAP operating earnings, its preferred measure, came to **$1.15 a share**, up from $1.13 a year earlier but short of the $1.21 consensus of five analysts in Nasdaq's earnings calendar. Revenue of $3.02 billion fell short of the $3.25 billion analysts expected, GuruFocus reported. On a GAAP basis, net income fell to $376 million, or $0.76 a share, from $622 million, or $1.24, the company said.

PSEG kept its 2026 forecast of $4.28 to $4.40 a share in operating earnings, and repeated its goal of growing that measure 6% to 8% a year through 2030, according to its release.

## What they say together

The two reports show where utility growth is coming from. Entergy's customers include the industrial and data-center builders of the Gulf Coast, and its stock was up 19.1% this year through Friday; PSEG's growth plan rests on its regulated New Jersey grid, and its stock was down 7.5%. Investors are paying for power demand they can see in the sales numbers, and Entergy can show it. PSEG's test is whether it can keep its forecast after a quarter that fell short.
