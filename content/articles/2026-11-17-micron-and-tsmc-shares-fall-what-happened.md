## What happened
Shares of memory-chip maker Micron ($MU) **fell 3.6%** and Taiwan Semiconductor ($TSM), the contract chipmaker known as TSMC, fell 3.7% by 12:15 p.m. ET on Tuesday, the second day of losses for both before Nvidia ($NVDA) reports after the close. Micron was hit by a broker's cut to its flash-memory price forecast, 24/7 Wall St. and GuruFocus reported; TSMC by a new US rule on chips for Chinese customers, Reuters and TipRanks reported.

In a note on Tuesday, GF Securities said it now expects prices for NAND, the flash memory used in phones and data-center storage, to stay flat in the first quarter of 2027 and fall in each of the three quarters after, according to 24/7 Wall St. and Seeking Alpha. It had expected a **6% rise** in the first quarter. The broker said supply could grow 28% in 2027 and named Sandisk ($SNDK) the most exposed, while it still favors Micron for its DRAM and high-bandwidth memory.

TSMC's Taiwan-listed shares fell in Asian trading after the Commerce Department's rule on Monday, which tightens checks on foundries that make advanced chips for Chinese designers, Reuters and MarketScreener reported. "Samsung Foundry therefore might potentially face additional customer due diligence and licensing requirement," CLSA analyst Sanjeev Rana told Reuters.

## Why it matters
The two causes hit different parts of the AI chip trade. A NAND glut would cut into the flash part of Micron's sales, the part with the least AI protection, even if its DRAM and AI memory stay tight. For TSMC, the rule adds paperwork and the risk of lost orders from Chinese chip designers, on top of the same rule's limits on Chinese companies renting Nvidia chips abroad ([our brief](/news/2026-11-16/us-moves-to-cut-off-chinas-remote-access-to-nvidia-nvda-chips-abroad/)).

Stocks slipped across the market before Nvidia's results, Reuters reported, and both fell further than the index. Nvidia itself was down 1.4% at 12:15 p.m. ET. Its report tonight is the first since Jensen Huang resigned ([our preview](/news/2026-11-16/what-to-expect-from-nvidias-nvda-q3-earnings/)), and it is the next read on AI chip demand for both suppliers.

## What the market is telling us
Micron's shares moved 5% or more on 77 of the last 252 sessions, so a 3.6% day is ordinary for it; the last bigger drop was 5 November, when it fell 17.5%. It closed Monday up 151.9% this year but 40.8% below its 25 June high of $1,213.56. TSMC moves 5% far less often, on 20 of the last 252 sessions, and closed Monday up 22.9% this year and 23.1% below its 5 October high. We read Micron's drop as the bigger warning: a forecast cut for prices goes to its earnings, while TSMC's rule touches only its orders from Chinese chip designers.
