## How it got here

JPMorgan Chase is the product of mergers that ran from Chase Manhattan and J.P. Morgan in 2000 to Bank One, which brought Jamie Dimon in 2004, and the rescues of Bear Stearns and Washington Mutual in 2008 and First Republic in 2023. Each deal added deposits, branches or clients, and left it the largest bank in the country by assets.

The past five years were its best run. Earnings per share grew **17.7% a year** over five years, against 11.7% for the median of the 15 banks in our data, and $1,000 in the stock five years ago was worth $2,075 on Friday. Higher interest rates fattened what it earns on deposits, and this year the SpaceX listing and an equity-trading boom helped it to a record $21.2 billion second-quarter profit, including a $4.6 billion gain on Visa shares, the bank said.

The third quarter changed the tone. On 13 October JPMorgan reported a rare miss on earnings estimates, booked a $658 million loss on bonds, and said it had bought back less stock to spend more on its own businesses. Since then a Senate probe threat and two senior departures have kept the stock below its August high. **Is this still the best bank in America, and is it worth paying more for than the rest?**

## The business in one line

Households pay it through card interest and fees and the spread on their deposits every month; companies and investors pay it fees for every deal, trade and payment it handles.

Inside the investment bank, the shape of the quarter was equities **up 49%** to $5.0 billion while fixed-income trading fell 4% to $5.4 billion, Investing.com and BigGo reported. Investment-banking fees rose 14% from a year earlier, and JPMorgan ranked first in the world with a 9.2% share of fees so far this year, the bank said.

## The numbers that matter

We judge JPMorgan against the 15 banks in our coverage, from Bank of America to US Bancorp.

### Growth

Revenue grew **8.7% a year** over five fiscal years, to $182.4 billion in 2025, twice the 4.3% median of its peers in our data. In the 12 months to September 2026 it reached $203.2 billion, though that includes the Visa gain.

### Profitability

The bank earned a **20% return on tangible equity** in the third quarter, or 21% without the bond loss, against the 17% it targets through a cycle, the bank's slides showed, as reported by Investing.com and Yahoo Finance. Net income was $15.3 billion, up 6% from a year earlier.

### Returns

Return on equity was **17.7%** over the past 12 months in our data, the highest of the 13 banks we can rank and well above the 10.9% median. Bank of America ($BAC) sits at exactly that median; Citigroup ($C) is at 6.7%.

### Shareholder payouts

Diluted shares fell **6.4%** over two years in our data, against 5.4% for the peer median, as buybacks retired stock. That slowed sharply in the third quarter: "In line with our hierarchy, we reduced share repurchases this quarter," Dimon said, a choice management tied to better returns from lending and investing inside the bank rather than to capital rules, according to Yahoo Finance and Beinsure.

### Balance sheet

Book value was **$372 billion** on 30 September in our data. The standardized CET1 ratio, the core capital cushion regulators watch, fell to 13.9% from 14.2% in June as the bank lent more and bond losses ate into equity, Investing.com and Yahoo Finance reported. That is still well above what it must hold, which is why management feels able to deploy it.

## Why the stock is where it is

From the angle of a worried owner, four things explain the 6.4% gap to the August high:

1. **A rare miss, on bond losses.** Third-quarter earnings of $5.57 a share fell short of the $5.90 analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC; even without the $658 million securities loss, earnings of $5.75 were below forecasts, Investing.com and Quartz reported. The stock closed down 0.9% at $331.43 that day.
2. **Costs rising faster than income.** Expenses rose 12% to $27.3 billion in the quarter, and finance chief Jeremy Barnum said analysts' estimate of about $114 billion for 2027 was close to the bank's own early read, MarketBeat and Yahoo Finance reported.
3. **Washington.** On 5 November, after the midterm elections, CNBC and Reuters reported that Democrats planned to investigate JPMorgan's relationship with Jeffrey Epstein once they control the Senate. The stock **fell 4.2%** that day, its biggest one-day drop since 23 February in our data.
4. **The bench.** Marianne Lake, who ran the consumer bank, said in June she would retire, and on Friday card chief Allison Beer told the bank she was leaving for an outside job, Bloomberg and Banking Dive reported.

## Testing the worry

**The miss is temporary and contained.** The bond loss came as bond prices fell, with the 10-year Treasury yield near 5.1% on Monday, and it cost $0.18 a share once. Underneath it, credit was calm: the card charge-off rate was guided to about 3.2% for the year, below the 3.4% the bank had pencilled in earlier, Yahoo Finance and GuruFocus reported, and total credit costs of $2.7 billion were less than a fifth of the quarter's profit.

**The cost worry is lasting, and it is the one that survives.** Management's central case for 2027 net interest income outside trading is about $107 billion, against about $106.2 billion guided for 2026, MarketBeat and Beinsure reported. If expenses reach about $114 billion next year against the roughly $105 billion the bank guided for 2026 last December, that is about $9 billion more cost against under $1 billion more core interest income, by our arithmetic, so fees and trading have to cover the rest.

**Washington is contained for now, but open-ended.** Senator Ron Wyden's August report alleged that JPMorgan was slow to report more than $1 billion of Epstein-linked transfers; JPMorgan said the report rested on false claims, Al Jazeera and Axios reported. A probe brings hearings and headlines; no fine or charge has been proposed.

**The departures are contained.** Co-presidents Doug Petno and Troy Rohrbaugh were named in June, and Dimon, 70, plans to stay at least three more years, a person familiar with the matter told reporters, InvestmentNews and Bloomberg Law reported.

## The moat

- **Deposits at scale.** JPMorgan ranked first in US retail deposits for the sixth straight year, Dimon said in the results release, and has set a long-term goal of 15% of all US deposits. Cheap, sticky deposits fund its lending. Bank of America, the nearest rival, earns a 10.9% return on equity in our data.
- **The top investment bank.** First in global investment-banking fees with a 9.2% share this year, the bank said. Citigroup, a rival in corporate banking and payments, earns 6.7% on equity.
- **The biggest card franchise.** A 23.6% share of US card spending and 17.7% of card balances, Banking Dive reported from the bank's figures. Wells Fargo ($WFC) earns 13.1% on equity.
- **A tech budget nobody can match.** About $19.8 billion on technology in 2026, AOL and Finviz reported, against about $14 billion at Bank of America.

## The risks we'd watch

1. **Costs outrunning revenue.** Each 1% overshoot on a $114 billion expense base is about $1.1 billion of pre-tax profit a year, by our arithmetic, about 7% of one quarter's profit.
2. **The trading boom fading.** Equities revenue of $5.0 billion was up 49%, so a year earlier it was about $3.4 billion. A return to that level would take about $1.6 billion a quarter off revenue, about 3% of the total, by our arithmetic.
3. **A tougher Washington.** Hearings on Epstein from January, and capital rules: JPMorgan has estimated that the draft rules would raise its required capital by about 4%, roughly $20 billion, while rivals' fell, Bloomberg Government and Reuters reported, which would leave less for buybacks.

The price assumes JPMorgan stays the best earner in the group. A 27% premium to its peers' forward multiple pays for a return on equity about 60% above theirs; it doesn't assume the gap widens.

## Our verdict

**Business: Strong. Price: About its usual price.** JPMorgan earns more on its capital than any big US bank in our data, grows faster than its peers, and still beat its own return target in a quarter it called a miss. What has changed is the cost bill: the bank is choosing to spend on itself rather than buy back stock, and next year's guidance leaves core interest income nearly flat. What would change our mind is a 2027 expense guide above $114 billion without a matching lift in fees; the bank's fourth-quarter report in January is where we'll see it. The tension is plain: the best bank in America is asking shareholders to trust it with more of their money just as its costs start to grow faster than its income.
