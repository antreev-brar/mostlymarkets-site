The Justice Department will review DoorDash's ($DASH) roughly **$17 billion** purchase of Instacart, Reuters reported on Monday evening, citing two people familiar with the matter; Bloomberg reported the same. The DOJ declined to comment on the deal itself, and Reuters said the timing of the review and the agency's concerns were not known.

DoorDash agreed on 29 October to pay $65 a share in cash and stock, a 26% premium, and it owes Instacart a **$1.25 billion** fee if the deal fails antitrust review, the companies' announcement and Reuters showed. The two companies held merger talks once before, in 2021, which stalled over price and regulatory concerns, Reuters noted ([our story on the deal](/news/2026-10-30/doordash-dash-tops-q3-estimates-as-orders-grow-28/)).

DoorDash slipped 1.3% after hours to $167.50 as of 7:08 p.m. ET, and is down 25.0% this year. The companies expect to close in late 2027 or the first half of 2028; a "second request" for more documents would be the first sign the review will run long.
