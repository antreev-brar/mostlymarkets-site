BHP Group ($BHP) told investors on Sunday evening, New York time, that it aims to roughly double copper output from its South Australian mines, to about **500,000 tonnes a year by 2035** and up to 650,000 tonnes by 2040, Reuters and Australian Mining reported. The plan rests on a bigger block cave, an underground mining method, at Olympic Dam and on expanding the site's smelter, which alone could cost up to $4.1 billion, according to Proactive and Market Index.

BHP said demand growth will need new mine supply, Reuters reported, and the plan makes copper the centre of its growth just as iron ore slid to a four-year low in China last week ([our story](/news/2026-11-16/bhp-freeport-and-rio-tinto-slide-as-iron-ore-hits-a-four-year-low-in-china/)). The US-listed shares **fell 13.4%** over the month to Friday and sit 31.9% below their 25 August high in our data.

Nothing is committed yet: the smelter's investment decision is due in the second half of 2027, according to Market Index and Argus, so the near-term test is how the US-listed shares take the plan when Wall Street opens on Monday.
