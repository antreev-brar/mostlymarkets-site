US employers added **165,000 jobs** in October, the Labor Department said on Friday at 8:30 a.m. ET, more than double the 65,000 economists expected in FactSet's poll, and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% from 4.2%. Futures traders raised the odds of a quarter-point Fed rate hike at the 8-9 December meeting to about 80%, Reuters reported, from about two-thirds on Thursday.

## What the report showed

Revisions made the picture stronger: September's gain was raised to 60,000 from 29,000, and August and September together by 84,000, CNN and Morningstar reported. The fall in unemployment came as more people joined the labor force, so it was not people giving up looking.

Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% in the month and 3.1% from a year earlier, both above forecasts, Morningstar reported. That is still below the 3.7% rise in consumer prices in the year to September, so paychecks are losing ground, but the monthly pace is the faster pay growth the Fed worries could feed inflation.

One soft spot: the average spell of unemployment rose to 27.1 weeks, the longest since September 2021, Morningstar reported. People who lose jobs are finding it harder to get new ones even as employers add workers overall.

## What economists said

"A strong rebuttal to fears that the economy is heading into a meaningful slowdown," said Seema Shah, chief global strategist at Principal Asset Management, adding that a December hike "remains firmly on the table," Yahoo Finance and Mortgage Professional America reported. Capital Economics' Ariane Curtis wrote that falling stocks are tightening financial conditions, but doubted "those moves have been enough so far to rule out a December rate hike," Pound Sterling Live reported. Lee Hardman of MUFG argued the opposite, that tighter financial conditions could matter more to the Fed than the jobs data.

## What markets did

The 10-year Treasury yield rose to **4.87%** at 10:15 a.m. ET in our data, from 4.84% on Thursday, and the dollar index rose 0.6%. Stocks rose anyway: the S&P 500 was up 1.1% and the Nasdaq Composite 1.8% at 10:15 a.m. ET, led by the chipmakers that fell hardest on Thursday ([our chip story](/news/2026-11-06/amd-micron-and-lam-research-shares-jump-what-happened/)).

## What's next

October's consumer price index comes out on Tuesday, 10 November, and producer prices on Friday, 13 November, the Bureau of Labor Statistics schedule shows. With oil falling from about $103 a barrel at the end of October to near $91, the CPI's energy line is the one that could still talk the Fed out of a December hike ([our narrative on the hike](/news/2026-11-06/why-wall-street-thinks-the-fed-will-hike-into-a-stock-sell-off/)).
