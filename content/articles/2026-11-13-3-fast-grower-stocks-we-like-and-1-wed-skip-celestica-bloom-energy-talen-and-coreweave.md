On Friday the AI trade had another bad day: Celestica fell 5.4%, Bloom Energy 4.9% and CoreWeave 6.1%, the day after Nvidia's chief executive resigned ([today's market wrap](/news/2026-11-13/stock-market-today-chipmakers-give-back-their-nvidia-windfall-as-yields-climb/)). Three of our four picks are 45% to 62% below their 52-week highs, which makes this a good week to ask which of them are growing into real profit and which are growing on borrowed money.

Our screen ranked our coverage on revenue growth in the 12 months to September 2026, from SEC filings, leaving out stocks we listed in the last 14 days, then judged the leaders on margins, cash, debt and price against peers. We passed on the two fastest, Joby Aviation and AST SpaceMobile, because each had sales under $150 million and losses more than four times that.

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Celestica ($CLS)
**Revenue growth: 61.6% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Celestica builds networking switches and server racks to order for cloud companies.

1. Its outlook says the growth isn't over: third-quarter revenue rose **83%** to $5.83 billion, and in late October it set a 2027 target of about $40 billion, nearly double its raised $21.0 billion for 2026, with $21.50 of adjusted earnings per share, 24/7 Wall St. and Kalkine reported.
2. It earns well on what it ties up: return on capital was about 38.7%, in the top quarter of the 31 hardware makers we compare it with, against a median of 16.5%, and it holds $3.1 billion more cash than debt.
3. The weak spot is margin: an operating margin of 8.3% sits behind most peers, though it targets 9.0% on an adjusted basis in 2027.

**The price:** 16.0 times expected earnings against a peer median of 18.0, and 11.2 times its own 2027 earnings target; its trailing P/E of 22.8 is about half its median of 42.5 since March 2025.

**What could go wrong:** cash. Free cash flow was 3.0% of revenue in the latest 12 months, in the bottom quarter of its group; if it stays that thin through 2027, the growth is consuming the profit.

### 2. Bloom Energy ($BE)
**Revenue growth: 110.1% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Bloom makes fuel cells that run on natural gas and power data centers on site.

1. Growth reached the bottom line: third-quarter revenue was a record **$1.24 billion**, up 138% from a year earlier, and operating income rose to $225 million from $8 million, its release said, as Oilprice.com reported.
2. It is getting more profitable, not just bigger: its operating margin of 14.9% is 13.8 points higher than five years ago, in the top quarter of the 89 industrial companies we compare it with.
3. Orders keep coming: on 5 November it signed NTT DATA for 900 megawatts, its largest international deal ([our story](/news/2026-11-05/why-bloom-energy-be-stock-is-sinking-today/)).

**The price:** 47.9 times expected earnings, more than twice the 21.7 times of its peers.

**What could go wrong:** dilution and cash. Its diluted share count rose 42.3% in two years, and free cash flow was minus $181 million in the third quarter.

### 3. Talen Energy ($TLN)
**Revenue growth: 90.2% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Talen owns nuclear and gas power plants in the mid-Atlantic and sells their output and capacity.

1. Much of the growth was bought, and it is paying off: third-quarter revenue rose to **$1.33 billion** from $812 million, helped by the Freedom and Guernsey gas plants it bought in November 2025, its release said. This week it sold 750 megawatts of Guernsey's capacity for seven years from 2029, worth about $100 million a year, its filing showed, as Benzinga reported.
2. The cash goes back to owners: free cash flow was 9.8% of revenue over two years, in the top tenth of the 33 utilities we compare it with, where the median is negative, and its diluted share count fell 9.8% in two years. A $1.5 billion buyback is under way ([our story](/news/2026-11-03/talen-energy-tln-shares-soar-on-a-1-5-billion-buyback-and-its-2027-outlook/)).
3. Its accounting profit looks weaker than its cash: third-quarter net income fell to $138 million after $190 million of unrealized losses on its power hedges, its release showed.

**The price:** 13.7 times expected earnings against a utility median of 19.2, the cheapest of our three.

**What could go wrong:** debt. Net debt of $9.2 billion is about 4.4 times this year's guided adjusted EBITDA of $2.1 billion; if power prices fall short of the 2027 forecast, that leverage cuts the other way.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### CoreWeave ($CRWV)
**Revenue growth: 129.6% in the 12 months to September 2026.** CoreWeave rents out Nvidia chips by the hour to AI labs and big tech companies.

1. The demand is real: third-quarter revenue rose **168%** to about $3.66 billion, its contracted backlog reached about $132.5 billion, and it raised its 2026 revenue forecast to $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion, its release said ([our story](/news/2026-11-13/why-coreweave-crwv-stock-fell-after-nvidias-ceo-resigned/)).
2. The cash isn't: free cash flow was minus $20.4 billion in the latest 12 months, about two dollars spent for every dollar of revenue, the worst in its group.
3. Debt carries it: net debt was $43.4 billion at the end of September, more than four times its yearly sales.

**The price:** there is no P/E because there are no profits; the price assumes the backlog turns into profit before the debt comes due.

**What would change our mind:** Nvidia. If Nvidia's report on Tuesday 17 November shows its customers' orders held up through the change at the top, CoreWeave could recover quickly, as its 24.7% jump on 11 November showed.
