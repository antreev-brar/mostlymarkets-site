## What happened

Shares of database and cloud company Oracle ($ORCL) **slid 5.6%** to $123.36 as of 10:15 a.m. ET on Monday after it filed to raise $20 billion: $10 billion of new bonds and $10 billion of new stock, to pay for its AI data-center build-out, Reuters and CNBC reported. The bonds come in six parts maturing between 2029 and 2046; their interest rates, and the number of shares to be sold, hadn't been set.

The timing is what stung. At its investor day on Wednesday 28 October, Oracle said it would raise no more than $20 billion of new money from bond and stock markets from fiscal 2027 through fiscal 2030, split roughly evenly between equity and debt, not counting refinancing, InvestingLive and StockTitan reported. The stock **jumped 10.7%** that day. Five days later, Oracle has filed for the full $20 billion in one go.

## Why it matters

The cap was the answer to investors' biggest worry, which is how Oracle pays for a build-out that has pushed its free cash flow deep below zero. Over the 12 months to August, free cash flow was **minus 40% of revenue**, against plus 29% for the median software company in our coverage, according to its SEC filings. Oracle said last week that its capital spending peaks this fiscal year and next, Reuters reported.

Selling $10 billion of stock also adds to dilution that is already running ahead of peers: Oracle's diluted share count rose 5.2% over the last two years, against 0.3% for the median software company. Not everyone turned bearish. Stephens upgraded the stock to Overweight on Monday morning, citing its cloud-infrastructure outlook, TradingView and Investing.com reported.

## What the market is telling us

Oracle's shares moved 5% or more on 38 of the last 252 sessions. The last drop at least this big came on 14 October 2026, when the stock fell 8.8%. Through Friday it was down 33.0% this year and 50.2% below its 52-week closing high of $262.61 from 31 October 2025.

At $123.36 the stock is 5.5% below its close the day before the investor day, so the whole jump the cap bought is gone. We read Monday's drop as investors pricing the dilution now rather than doubting the plan; the next tests are the interest rate the bonds price at this week, and whether free cash flow improves when Oracle next reports results.
