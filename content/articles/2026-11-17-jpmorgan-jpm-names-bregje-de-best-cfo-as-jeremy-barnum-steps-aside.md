JPMorgan Chase ($JPM) said on Tuesday that Bregje de Best, 43, finance chief of its Commercial & Investment Bank, will succeed Jeremy Barnum as chief financial officer after the bank reports its first-quarter 2027 results, according to its release and Reuters. Barnum, CFO for six years, becomes a vice chairman and stays until early 2028; he will lead the January and April earnings calls and February's investor day alongside de Best.

The CFO is the bank's voice on the numbers investors trade on: net interest income guidance, expenses and buybacks. That matters now, because JPMorgan is spending more and buying back less, as we set out in [our breakdown on Monday](/news/2026-11-16/jpmorgan-jpm-is-spending-more-and-buying-back-less-is-the-business-still-strong/). De Best has spent 20 years at the bank, so this reads as planned succession, not a change of course.

The stock was **down 1.0%** at $334.90 at 10:30 a.m. ET, a little more than Bank of America, Citigroup and Wells Fargo, which were down 0.3% to 0.7%. The next test is the fourth-quarter report in January, where the bank usually sets out its interest-income and expense targets for the year.
