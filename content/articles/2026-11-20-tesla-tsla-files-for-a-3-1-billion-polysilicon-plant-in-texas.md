Tesla ($TSLA) has applied for Texas tax incentives for a **$3.1 billion** plant near Corpus Christi, in Nueces County, that would make solar-grade polysilicon, the raw material for solar cells, Reuters and PV Tech reported on Friday. The plant would start operating in 2030 and employ 515 people by 2032, according to the application under the state's Jobs, Energy, Technology and Innovation program.

Polysilicon is mostly made in China, so the plant would give Tesla its first domestic source. It adds to the roughly $10.1 billion factory near Houston that Tesla disclosed earlier this year to make solar ingots, wafers and cells, Fox Business and PV Tech reported. Together the two plans come to about **$13.2 billion**, more than the $9.9 billion of net cash Tesla held at the end of September, according to its filings.

The filing says Tesla would not make the investment in Texas without the tax break, pv magazine and PV Tech reported, and the company has not taken a final investment decision. The shares closed **7.6%** lower at $345.82 on Friday in a broad tech selloff.
