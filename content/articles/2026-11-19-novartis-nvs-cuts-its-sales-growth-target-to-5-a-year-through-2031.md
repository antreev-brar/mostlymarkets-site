Novartis ($NVS) said on Thursday it expects sales to grow **5% a year** at constant currencies through 2031, below the 5% to 6% range it set last year for 2025 to 2030, Reuters and CNBC reported from its investor event in London. It raised the peak-sales forecasts for Kesimpta, Scemblix and Leqvio to a combined $18 billion from $14 billion, and said its core operating margin should return above 40% by 2029.

The cut matters more than the upgrades because the target runs into Kisqali, its breast-cancer drug, facing US generic competition in the second half of 2031, CNBC and Investing.com reported. The plan assumes no large acquisitions, Reuters reported, after trial setbacks this year that included a $2.2 billion third-quarter writedown and the [pelacarsen failure](/news/2026-11-07/novartis-nvs-shelves-pelacarsen-after-it-cut-lp-a-78-but-not-heart-attacks/). Its US shares were **down 2.2%** at $151.86 at 6:15 a.m. ET in our data, against a 12.6% gain this year to Wednesday.

What's next: fourth-quarter results will show whether Kesimpta and Scemblix are on course for the new peaks, since Chief Executive Vas Narasimhan said most growth after 2031 should come from medicines Novartis develops itself.
