A high gross margin, the share of each sale left after the direct cost of making it, says a company can charge far more than its products cost. What it does with the rest decides whether owners see any of it, and this month's sell-off has priced two of these businesses as if they were ordinary. F5 ($FFIV) is **33.1% below** its 9 October high after a soft forecast, and Tapestry ($TPR) fell 4.4% on Tuesday, leaving it 22.1% below its August high.

Our screen took the stocks we cover with the highest gross margins over the 12 months to their latest quarter, from our analysis of SEC filings, and left out anything we've listed in the past 14 days. We ran all of them through our quality check: growth, operating margin, free cash flow, returns and share count against each company's peers, then the price.

```picks
like: TPR, FFIV
skip: DDOG
```

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. Tapestry ($TPR)

**Gross margin: 78.3% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Tapestry owns the Coach and Kate Spade handbag and accessory brands.

1. **Coach is accelerating.** On 5 November Tapestry said Coach sales rose 16% in its first fiscal quarter, lifting total revenue 11%, and it raised its full-year revenue outlook to $8.5 billion to $8.6 billion from $8.4 billion to $8.5 billion, the release showed. The stock jumped 7.1% that day, [as we reported](/news/2026-11-06/tapestry-tpr-jumps-iron-mountain-irm-and-ralph-lauren-rl-sink-thursdays-earnings/).
2. **It keeps the margin.** Operating margin was **24.5%** over the past 12 months, against 13.8% for the median consumer discretionary stock we cover, and free cash flow was 23.0% of sales, in the top tenth.
3. **The cash goes back to owners.** Diluted shares fell 13.3% in two years, about three times the sector median's 4.4%, and Tapestry plans to return $2 billion this fiscal year, including $1.7 billion of buybacks, the release and Zacks reported.

Revenue grew 6.9% a year over five fiscal years, behind its sector, but the last two years ran faster at 10.9%. The weak spot is Kate Spade, whose sales fell 10% in the quarter, FashionUnited and Zacks reported.

**The price:** 15.8 times expected earnings, against a sector median of 19.9 times, and a trailing P/E of 16.5 near the bottom of its two-year range.

**What could go wrong:** Coach is now about seven-eighths of revenue, so a fashion cycle turning against it would hit the whole company; its next report, early next year, is the test of whether holiday demand held.

### 2. F5 ($FFIV)

**Gross margin: 81.9% in the 12 months to September 2026.** F5 sells software and hardware that keep big websites and apps running and secure.

1. **It compounds earnings.** Earnings per share grew **18.6% a year** over five fiscal years, against 14.0% for the median tech hardware maker we cover, helped by a 2.4% cut in share count over two years.
2. **The cash is real.** Free cash flow was 27.3% of revenue over two years, in the top tenth of its peers, whose median is 13.4%.
3. **The fall came from the forecast, not the quarter.** Fourth-quarter revenue rose 13% to $917 million on 27 October, but F5 guided to 8% to 9% growth in fiscal 2027 and a lower adjusted gross margin as memory costs rise, the release showed. The stock plunged 20.4% the next day, [as we reported](/news/2026-10-28/f5-ffiv-shares-plunge-after-a-soft-forecast-and-a-piper-sandler-downgrade/).

**The price:** 18.6 times expected earnings, about its peers' 17.6 times, and a trailing P/E of 25.6, below its two-year median of 27.4. The board added $1.5 billion to its buyback authorization, the release showed.

**What could go wrong:** the hardware refresh fades. Systems revenue grew 29% last quarter, and management expects slower growth in the second half of fiscal 2027, and the guided adjusted gross margin of 80.5% to 82.5% is already down from 84% in the fourth quarter; the low end would mean memory costs are eating more of each sale.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Datadog ($DDOG)

**Gross margin: 79.6% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Datadog sells software that monitors companies' cloud systems and apps.

1. **Little of the margin reaches profit.** Operating margin was **2.3%** over the past 12 months under standard accounting, against a software median of 9.7%, and it has improved by just 1 point in five years. Datadog's own adjusted measure, which leaves out stock pay, was 28% last quarter, 24/7 Wall St. and the release showed.
2. **Owners are diluted.** Diluted shares rose 4.9% in two years, against a median of 0.3%.
3. **Growth is set to slow.** Third-quarter revenue rose 35% to $1.20 billion, but its fourth-quarter forecast of $1.19 billion to $1.20 billion implies about 25% growth, the Motley Fool reported. The stock fell 14.4% on 4 November.

Its strength is growth: revenue rose 41.5% a year over five years, in the top tenth of software companies.

**The price:** 59.5 times expected earnings, more than twice the software median of 25.0 times, even after a 43.0% fall from its 9 October high.

**What would change our mind:** an operating margin under standard accounting above 10% with share count flat, which would show the gross margin reaching owners; its fourth-quarter report early next year is the next look.
