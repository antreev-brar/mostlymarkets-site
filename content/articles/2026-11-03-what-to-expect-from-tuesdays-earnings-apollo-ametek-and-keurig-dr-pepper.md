Three companies worth between $50 billion and $71 billion report before the open on Tuesday, 3 November, Election Day: private-markets firm Apollo Global Management ($APO), industrial instrument maker Ametek ($AME) and Keurig Dr Pepper ($KDP). Each tests a different part of the market: private credit, factory spending and a consumer-staples company in the middle of a breakup.

## Apollo Global Management ($APO), before the open

Analysts expect adjusted net income of **$2.21 a share**, according to Zacks, with MarketBeat at $2.22, up from $1.90 a year earlier; the Zacks figure was revised down 1.6% over the past 30 days, Yahoo Finance reported. Last quarter, on 4 August, Apollo earned $2.11 against the $2.16 expected, MarketBeat data show, yet the stock rose 2.9% that day. The shares are down 17.5% this year and 21.8% below their January high. The call is at 8:30 a.m. ET.

## Ametek ($AME), before the open

Ametek guided to adjusted EPS of $2.08 to $2.10, and the Zacks consensus sits just above that at **$2.12**, up 12.7% from a year earlier; MarketBeat has $2.11. Zacks expects sales of $2.16 billion, up 14.3%. Ametek beat the Zacks estimate in each of the last four quarters, and last quarter's record $2.09 a share, on sales up 15%, sent the stock up 4.2% on 4 August. The order trend, the earliest read on its customers' factory and aerospace spending, will move the shares more than the beat.

## Keurig Dr Pepper ($KDP), before the open

The Zacks consensus is **$0.63 a share**, up 16.7% from $0.54 a year earlier, on revenue of $7.36 billion; MarketBeat has $0.62 on $7.33 billion. Revenue is up sharply because Keurig Dr Pepper bought coffee company JDE Peet's on 1 April, and this is its first report since naming the leaders for its planned split into two companies early next year, Investing.com reported. RBC Capital Markets expects results in line and the annual guidance reaffirmed, MarketScreener reported.

## The one with the most riding on it

Keurig Dr Pepper. The stock **rose 21.2%** in the past month to a 52-week closing high of $36.82 on Monday, so a report that merely meets estimates, as RBC expects, leaves little room. Apollo has the opposite set-up: down 17.5% this year after a miss last quarter, it needs a clean quarter more than a big beat. Ametek's guide-and-beat pattern is the most predictable of the three.
