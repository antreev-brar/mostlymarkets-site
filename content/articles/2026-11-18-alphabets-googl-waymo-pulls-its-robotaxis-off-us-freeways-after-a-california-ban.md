Waymo, Alphabet's ($GOOGL) self-driving unit, has stopped giving robotaxi rides on freeways in every US market where it offered them, after California's Department of Motor Vehicles barred its driverless cars from state freeways and called them an "imminent hazard to public safety," NBC News and CBC reported. The DMV issued the order on 11 November and disclosed it this week.

The regulator cited two incidents: three Waymo cars driving into a construction zone, and one on Interstate 280 in San Francisco trying to get around California Highway Patrol cars running a traffic break, the reports said. It is Waymo's second freeway pullback this year: it pulled out in May after seven cars entered a Bay Area construction zone, and returned in July after a software update. City-street rides, more than half a million a week by Waymo's count, carry on.

The ban lasts until Waymo shows the DMV "effective corrective actions," and no date has been set. Waymo said it is still testing on freeways with trained specialists behind the wheel. Alphabet shares were up 1.4% before the open, as of 9:15 a.m. ET.
