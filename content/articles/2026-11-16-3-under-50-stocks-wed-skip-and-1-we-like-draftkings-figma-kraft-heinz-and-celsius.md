Two of the four stocks here reported results in the first week of November and lost a sixth or more of their value in a day: DraftKings ($DKNG) fell 21.3% on 6 November and Figma ($FIG) 16.7% on 5 November.

Our screen took stocks under $50 a share with positive free cash flow and ranked them by revenue growth over the 12 months to September 2026, from SEC filings in our fundamentals data, leaving out stocks we listed in the last 14 days. We then judged the names on profit, cash and returns, each against its own industry.

```picks
skip: DKNG, FIG, KHC
like: CELH
```

## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. DraftKings ($DKNG)
**Revenue growth: 17.4% in the 12 months to September 2026.** DraftKings runs online sports betting and casino games in the US states that allow them.

1. **The growth doesn't reach profit.** Revenue rose 58% a year over five years, among the fastest of the 66 consumer stocks we cover, yet the operating margin over the last 12 months was **minus 6.3%**, in the bottom 5% of the group.
2. **The profit plan just got pushed back a year.** On 5 November it cut its 2026 adjusted EBITDA forecast to $175 million to $275 million, from $700 million to $900 million, to pay for Predictions, its answer to prediction markets such as Kalshi, according to its release and Bloomberg ([our story](/news/2026-11-06/draftkings-dkng-sinks-27-as-predictions-spending-guts-its-profit-outlook/)).
3. **The balance sheet carries the bill.** It had net debt of $1.1 billion at the end of September and book value of just $180 million, and the diluted share count rose 2.2% over two years while most consumer peers shrank theirs.

**The price:** with no earnings there is no P/E; it trades at 27.7 times free cash flow.

**What would change our mind:** Predictions reaching the $650 million to $750 million of 2027 revenue the company guides to, with spending on it falling.

### 2. Figma ($FIG)
**Revenue growth: 44.4% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Figma sells web-based design software that product teams use to draw and test apps and websites.

1. **Fast growth, heavy losses.** Third-quarter revenue rose 43% to $391.4 million, but the operating margin over 12 months was **minus 40.7%** on a GAAP basis, which counts stock-based pay as a cost: in the bottom tenth of the software companies we cover.
2. **The growth is slowing.** Its fourth-quarter guidance of $402 million to $404 million implies about 33% growth, down from 43%, and the shares fell on 5 November as investors worried that handing customers more AI credits without raising prices will cost it margin, according to 247 Wall St. and Tech Insider.
3. **The cash is thinner than it looks.** Free cash flow was 16.0% of revenue over 12 months, against a software median of 28.8%.

**The price:** 67.4 times expected earnings against a software median of 26.7 times, so earnings would have to grow about 2.5-fold just to reach the median multiple.

**What would change our mind:** a GAAP operating profit, or AI features it can charge for that hold growth above 30% into 2027.

### 3. Kraft Heinz ($KHC)
**Revenue growth: minus 1.5% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Kraft Heinz makes packaged foods: Heinz ketchup, Kraft macaroni and cheese, Oscar Mayer meats and Philadelphia cream cheese.

1. **It has been shrinking for years.** Revenue fell 1.0% a year over five years and 2.6% a year over the last two, near the bottom of the 32 staples companies we cover. Third-quarter organic sales fell 1.1%, the company said on 4 November.
2. **Profit went backwards.** The operating margin over 12 months was **minus 13.4%**, the worst in its group, and 26.8 points lower than five years ago.
3. **The fix is still a year away.** In its 12 November plan, Kraft Heinz said it expects top- and bottom-line growth to return only in the second half of 2027 and will give formal 2027 guidance with its fourth-quarter results.

**The price:** 13.5 times expected earnings against a staples median of 18.1 times, and 8.5 times free cash flow.

**What would change our mind:** two quarters of rising North American volumes, the region where organic sales fell 3.2% in the third quarter.

## 1 stock we like

### Celsius Holdings ($CELH)
**Revenue growth: 49.1% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Celsius sells energy drinks under the Celsius, Alani Nu and Rockstar Energy brands, distributed largely through PepsiCo.

1. **It compounds, on every horizon.** Revenue grew 81% a year over five years and 52% a year over two, the fastest of the consumer staples companies we cover; earnings per share grew 47% a year over five years, against a group median of 1%.
2. **The money earns its keep.** Return on capital was **26.1%**, in the top eighth of staples, against a median of 14.2%.
3. **The latest quarter beat.** Third-quarter revenue rose 16.8% to $847.2 million, the company said on 5 November, and adjusted earnings of 41 cents a share beat the 34-cent Zacks consensus. Alani Nu grew about 24% to $410.1 million and is now its biggest brand.

**The price:** 22.0 times expected earnings, against a staples median of 18.1 times. Its trailing P/E is 36.4 times, against a median of 124.7 times since November 2024, and the stock is 42.8% below its 20 January high.

**What could go wrong:** the namesake Celsius brand shrank about 6% to $356.8 million in the quarter, and gross margin fell to 46.8% from 51.3% on aluminum, fuel and a heavier mix of direct-store deliveries, according to the release and MarketBeat's call summary. Management now says the return to low-50s margins will take longer, so another drop in either number would weaken the case.
