On Tuesday 17 November, Nike closed **down 6.3%** at $35.85 on the day of its first investor day in nine years, its biggest one-day fall since April. The price of one share says nothing about what a business is worth, so we looked for the under-$50 stocks whose numbers back their price.

The screen: stocks trading under $50 a share with positive free cash flow over the last 12 months, ranked by revenue growth over those 12 months, from SEC filings. We left out stocks from our lists of the last 14 days, then judged each on growth, margins, cash and valuation against its peers, long run first.

```picks
skip: TOST, NKE, GME
like: LVS
```

## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. Toast ($TOST)

**Revenue growth: 22.1% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Toast sells restaurants their tills, software and card processing, earning most of its money on payment fees.

1. **The growth is real, but little of it is kept.** Its operating margin was **7.2%** over the last 12 months, against a median of 26.8% for the 46 payments, exchange and asset-management companies we compare it with.
2. **The fee model is in court.** On 29 October the Federal Trade Commission and a group of states sued Toast, alleging hidden fees behind its $0-a-month plan and hard-to-cancel subscriptions, the FTC said and Restaurant Dive and the Boston Globe reported.
3. **A good quarter didn't lift the stock.** On 3 November Toast reported revenue of $1.98 billion, 21% more locations at about 189,000, and a higher 2026 adjusted EBITDA forecast of $840 million to $850 million, its release said, but the stock fell after the report, Benzinga and TIKR reported. On 11 November Bernstein cut it to market perform with a $38 target, down from $45, TradingView and GuruFocus reported.

**The price:** 31.9 times expected earnings, against a peer median of 17.0, and 32.6 times free cash flow.

**What would change our mind:** a formal 2027 forecast in February that shows the operating margin climbing toward the peer median, or a settlement with the FTC that leaves its fees intact.

### 2. Nike ($NKE)

**Revenue growth: minus 1.2% in the 12 months to August 2026.** Nike is the world's largest sportswear brand.

1. **Five years of almost no growth.** Revenue grew 0.8% a year over five fiscal years, in the bottom tenth of the 66 consumer companies we compare it with.
2. **Earnings have gone backwards.** Earnings per share fell **10.0% a year** over five years, against 13.6% growth for the median peer.
3. **Its own plan says sales fall again.** At Tuesday's investor day Nike reaffirmed that fiscal 2027 revenue will fall by a high-single-digit percentage, with an adjusted operating margin of about 6% and adjusted EPS of $1.15 to $1.35, its release said and Investing.com reported ([our story](/news/2026-11-17/nike-nke-targets-5-billion-of-added-sales-and-a-12-to-14-margin-by-2031/)).

**The price:** 29.2 times expected earnings, against a peer median of 20.0, and 25.8 times free cash flow.

**What would change our mind:** quarterly reports that track the plan, with sales declines narrowing in fiscal 2028 as Nike forecasts, would mean the 2031 targets carry the price.

### 3. GameStop ($GME)

**Revenue growth: minus 7.7% in the 12 months to July 2026.** GameStop sells video games, consoles and, increasingly, trading cards and other collectibles.

1. **Sales keep shrinking.** Revenue fell 6.5% a year over five years and 11.7% a year over two, last among our 66 consumer companies. Second-quarter sales fell to **$790.2 million** from $972.2 million, its release said.
2. **Profit leans on investments.** Second-quarter net income of $298.7 million included about $238 million of gains on its eBay derivative and stake, against a loss of about $75 million on digital assets, its preliminary release said and Barchart and Pulse 2.0 reported.
3. **Shareholders were diluted.** The diluted share count rose 53.0% in two years, in the bottom 1% of its group, where the median fell 4.4%.

**The price:** 26.7 times expected earnings, against a peer median of 20.0, after a 26.3% rise this year to Tuesday's close.

**What would change our mind:** total sales growing again as collectibles, now 45.1% of sales, outgrow the decline in games, and adjusted EBITDA above its $650 million forecast for the year to January 2027.

## 1 stock we like

### Las Vegas Sands ($LVS)

**Revenue growth: 10.9% in the 12 months to September 2026.** It owns casino resorts in Macao and Marina Bay Sands in Singapore.

1. **It keeps a large share of each sale.** Its operating margin was **20.9%** over the last 12 months, in the top quarter of our 66 consumer companies, whose median is 13.8%, and its two-year free cash flow margin of 14.3% beats their 9.0%.
2. **It hands the cash back.** The diluted share count fell 12.4% in two years. In the third quarter it bought back $950 million of stock and raised its quarterly dividend 20% to $0.36 from early 2027, Sigma and Gaming.net reported.
3. **It is winning share in Macao.** Its share of Macao mass-market gaming revenue reached 26.1% in the third quarter, its highest since late 2023, GGRAsia and Sigma reported.

**The price:** 13.9 times expected earnings, against a peer median of 20.0, and 10.3 times free cash flow. Its trailing P/E of 16.1 is near the bottom of its 13.9 to 31.4 range since October 2024, after a 39.6% fall this year to Tuesday's close.

**What could go wrong:** the third quarter was weak. Net income fell 29.7% to $352 million and adjusted property EBITDA dropped to $1.12 billion from $1.34 billion as high-end gamblers spent less in both markets, Sigma and G3 Newswire reported. With net debt of $12.6 billion, about 4.4 years of operating profit, another quarter like that in its fourth-quarter report would test the buyback.
