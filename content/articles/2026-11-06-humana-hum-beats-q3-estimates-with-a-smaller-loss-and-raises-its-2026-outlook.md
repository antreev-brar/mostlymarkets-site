Humana ($HUM) reported third-quarter revenue of $41.16 billion before the open on Friday, **up 26% year on year** and above the roughly $40.9 billion analysts expected, according to S&P Global data cited by StockStory. Its adjusted loss of 70 cents a share was smaller than the $1.00 loss in LSEG's consensus, Reuters reported, and it raised its 2026 profit floor. The shares **rose 4.0% to $428.12** as of 2:15 p.m. ET, after touching $444 earlier in the session.

## What stood out

**Growth came at a price.** Humana added about **1.2 million Medicare Advantage members** this year, a 23% jump, Quartz and Benzinga reported from the release. The benefit ratio, the share of premiums paid out in claims, rose 2.6 points from a year earlier, which Forbes reported Humana put down partly to new members running at higher costs than its existing ones.

*Verdict: the growth is real, and costly in its first year, as new members usually are.*

**Costs still came in under plan.** A benefit ratio of **93.7%** against guidance above 94% is the reason the loss was smaller than feared. The adjusted operating cost ratio also fell to 11.2% from 11.8%, the release showed. The Insurance segment still lost $299 million on an adjusted basis in the quarter, against a $270 million profit a year earlier.

*Verdict: better than feared, but the core insurance business is still in the red for the quarter.*

**The care-delivery arm keeps adding patients.** CenterWell Senior Primary Care, Humana's own chain of clinics for older patients, grew by 146,800 patients, or **30%**, this year. Humana also swapped its clinic-funding partnerships with Welsh, Carson, Anderson & Stowe for a new joint venture with BlackRock, which reversed a $1.2 billion liability and explains why GAAP profit guidance more than doubled while the adjusted figure barely moved.

> "We are also increasingly driving financial and operating performance toward being 'ahead' of our plan." — Chief Executive Jim Rechtin, in the results release

## The outlook

Humana raised its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast to **at least $9.50**, from at least $9.00, against the roughly $9.15 analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters. That still means a steep fall from $17.14 in 2025: the release blames a drop in its Star Ratings, the federal quality scores that set Medicare bonus payments for 2026.

The bigger news for the stock is 2027. On the call, Rechtin said early sign-ups in the Medicare enrollment window, which runs to 7 December, were ahead of plan and that Humana now expects membership to grow in 2027, Fierce Healthcare and Seeking Alpha reported. That comes on top of October's ratings, which put 95% of its members in plans rated four stars or higher for 2027, up from about a fifth for 2026; the stock **jumped 11.6%** on 9 October when they came out.

## Our take

We were impressed by the cost control: the quarter beat on the line that has sunk Humana before, and it beat on revenue too. On the other hand, this is still a loss-making quarter in a year when adjusted earnings will fall by almost half, and the faster it grows, the more expensive new members it has to bring down to a profit. Overall, a solid quarter that strengthens the recovery case without proving it.

The move today owed more to the 2027 membership comment than to the 30-cent beat. Over five fiscal years, our analysis of SEC filings shows revenue up 10.9% a year, just ahead of the 10.3% peer median, but EPS down 17.2% a year, near the bottom of the group, and $1,000 put into the stock five years ago is worth about $908. At **34.2 times** expected earnings, double the peer median of 17.0, the price assumes 2026 is the trough and that profits rebuild quickly once the Star Ratings bonus returns in 2028. The next test is Humana's investor update on 10 December, where it will measure progress against the targets it set at its 2025 Investor Day.
