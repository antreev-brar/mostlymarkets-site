On Thursday the market's money kept chasing a handful of AI chip stocks, with AMD up 30% in a week after Nvidia's CEO quit ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-13/wall-street-is-betting-nvidias-loss-is-amd-and-broadcoms-gain-tuesday-will-test-it/)). At the other end of the market, some companies with growing sales trade at 8 times expected earnings or less, and the question is which of them are cheap for a reason.

Our screen took the companies in our coverage whose revenue grew over the latest 12 months and ranked them by forward P/E, the share price divided by the next year's expected earnings per share. We then judged the cheapest on growth over five years and two, cash, returns on capital and buybacks against their peers, using SEC filings in our fundamentals data, and left out stocks we listed in the last 14 days. We passed on Valero, the cheapest at 5.5 times, because its third-quarter refining margin of $39.24 a barrel was triple a year earlier's, and earnings built on that are unlikely to last.

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. EOG Resources ($EOG)
**Forward P/E: 8.2 times, as of 12 November 2026.** EOG drills for oil and natural gas, mostly in US shale fields.

1. Its quarter set records: oil-equivalent output rose 10% from a year earlier to about 1.43 million barrels a day in the third quarter, adjusted earnings rose to **$4.87 a share** from $2.71, and it raised its regular dividend 8%, its release said, as Oilprice.com also reported ([our recap](/news/2026-11-06/eog-resources-eog-tops-q3-estimates-on-record-output-and-raises-its-dividend-8/)).
2. It turns sales into profit better than most: its operating margin was 35.8% in the 12 months to September 2026, in the top quarter of the 25 energy companies we compare it with, against a median of 22.5%, and five years ago that margin was 33 points lower.
3. Cash goes back to owners: it spent $1 billion on buybacks in the quarter and still has $10.6 billion of authorization left, its release said, and its diluted share count fell 8.1% in two years, against a peer median rise of 0.3%.

**The price:** 8.2 times expected earnings against a peer median of 11.4 times; its trailing P/E of 10.1 is below its two-year median of 11.1.

**What would change our mind:** oil. Revenue rose 46% in the quarter while output rose 10%, so most of the gain came from prices, and with WTI crude at $88.56 at 7:06 a.m. ET on Friday a fall back toward last year's levels would take earnings down with it.

### 2. Synchrony Financial ($SYF)
**Forward P/E: 7.5 times, as of 12 November 2026.** Synchrony issues store-branded credit cards for retailers and lends for big purchases at the till.

1. Its borrowers are paying better: net charge-offs, the loans it writes off, fell to **4.79%** of loans in the third quarter from 5.16% a year earlier, its quarterly filing showed, as 24/7 Wall St. also reported.
2. It shrinks fast: the diluted share count fell 18.7% in two years, the most among the 46 payments, card and asset-management companies we compare it with, and quarterly earnings per share rose to $2.68 in the third quarter from $2.59 in the second.
3. Returns hold up: return on equity was 20.1% in the latest 12 months, above the group median of 18.0%.

**The price:** 7.5 times expected earnings against a group median of 17.4 times, and its trailing P/E is about its usual level, 7.5 against a two-year median of 8.0. The stock is down 14.1% this year.

**What would change our mind:** credit. Revenue barely grew over two years, so earnings rest on losses staying low; a rise in the charge-off figures it publishes every month would undo the case.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Global Payments ($GPN)
**Forward P/E: 6.0 times, as of 12 November 2026.** Global Payments processes card payments for merchants, and this year it completed its purchase of Worldpay.

1. The buyout floor is gone: the stock **plunged 12.0%** on Thursday, its worst day since April 2025, after Reuters reported that buyout firms CD&R and GTCR had dropped talks to take it private, partly because financing a deal of that size was difficult, Investing.com and TipRanks reported.
2. The growth is bought, not earned: revenue rose 63.7% in the third quarter because of Worldpay, but over five years it grew 0.7% a year, the bottom of the 46 companies in its group, and the diluted share count rose 10% in a year as it issued stock for the deal.
3. Cash went the wrong way: free cash flow was minus 1.5% of revenue in the latest 12 months against a group median of 24.5%, and it carries $22.6 billion of debt against $5.6 billion of cash.

**The price:** 6.0 times expected earnings and 1.0 times book value, among the cheapest in its group, but a low multiple on earnings that don't turn into cash is a warning, not a bargain.

**What could go wrong:** a buyer. The people Reuters cited said talks could resume or another bidder could emerge, Investing.com reported; a new approach would lift the stock straight back.
