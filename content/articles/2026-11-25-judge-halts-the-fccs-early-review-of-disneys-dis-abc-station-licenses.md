On Wednesday, U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan in Washington granted Walt Disney ($DIS) and its ABC network a preliminary injunction that **halts the Federal Communications Commission's early review of the licenses of eight ABC-owned TV stations** while their lawsuit proceeds, CNN and CBS News reported. She found ABC was likely to show the review was retaliation for its programming, and turned down a broader order that would have barred any FCC pressure on the network, Deadline and TheWrap reported.

The FCC under Chairman Brendan Carr ordered the review in April, years before the licenses came up for renewal, and Disney sued in August, calling it a First Amendment case. The agency says it is examining ABC's compliance with anti-discrimination rules. ABC's request did not cover the FCC's separate inquiries into Disney's diversity practices and the talk show The View.

The ruling removes, for now, a direct threat to stations Disney owns outright, and comes as investors were already warming to the stock after [its fiscal fourth quarter](/news/2026-11-17/disney-dis-tops-q4-estimates-and-raises-its-dividend-33/). Disney **rose 2.5%** on Wednesday to close at a 52-week high, though our data can't say whether the ruling landed before the close.
