A $35 billion error in how Citigroup ($C) counted risky assets at its UK trading unit has cost at least two managing directors who oversaw the bank's regulatory reports their posts, The Wall Street Journal reported after Friday's shortened session, a story Reuters relayed. The understatement, first reported by Reuters on 20 November, came to roughly **20% of the unit's reported risk-weighted assets**, the Journal said, and Citi put about $3 billion of capital into the subsidiary to restore its buffers.

The head of risk at the UK unit, Manjira Sen-Gosain, left in early November, according to the Financial Conduct Authority's register, City AM reported. Citi has said the unit stayed above regulatory minimums throughout and that the parent company's capital was unaffected, as we noted in [our first story on the error](/news/2026-11-20/citigroup-c-underestimated-risky-assets-by-35-billion-at-a-uk-trading-unit/).

The money is small for a bank worth about $191 billion; the record is the cost. Chief Executive Jane Fraser is trying to get regulators to lift the 2020 consent orders over Citi's risk controls, and a fresh reporting failure, now with people moved out over it, makes that case harder. Citi shares closed up 2.0% at $115.59 before the report, 20.6% below their June high. Whether regulators open a new review isn't known; Citi's fourth-quarter results in January are the first chance for analysts to ask.
