## What happened

Shares of research and advisory firm Gartner ($IT) **plunged 18.1%** to $192.68 by 10:15 a.m. ET on Tuesday, after its third-quarter results showed contract value, the yearly subscription revenue it has under contract, growing just 2.5%. The fall came even though profit beat estimates by a wide margin and the company raised its full-year outlook.

Adjusted earnings per share came to **$3.59**, up 30.1% from a year earlier and well above the $2.84 analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters and Investing.com. Revenue was $1.5 billion, down 1.3% from a year earlier, roughly in line with the $1.49 billion estimate. Gartner said in its release that it raised its full-year forecasts for revenue, adjusted EBITDA, adjusted EPS and free cash flow.

The number investors watch most went the other way. Contract value rose to **$5.3 billion**, up 2.5% on a currency-neutral basis and 1.1% from the second quarter, the release said; in the research unit that sells to technology buyers it grew only 2.1%. On the call, analysts pressed management on slowing growth outside the US federal government, the hit from the Middle East conflict and whether Gartner has enough salespeople, according to call transcripts published by Investing.com and Benzinga.

## Why it matters

Contract value is Gartner's leading indicator: what it signs this year becomes next year's research revenue, which is most of the business. Chief Executive Gene Hall said in the release that contract value growth had accelerated again, the third quarter in a row it has picked up, but from a low base. Reported revenue still fell, so the 30% jump in adjusted EPS came from margins and a smaller share count, not from more customers paying more: diluted shares are down 15% over two years, the fastest shrink among its peers in our data.

The stock had also run up into the print. It rose **27.3%** in the month to Monday's close, so the bar was higher than the estimates suggest. Investors have sold Gartner hard on report days before: it fell 27.6% on 5 August 2025 and 20.9% on 3 February this year.

## What the market is telling us

Gartner's shares moved 5% or more on 30 of the last 252 sessions. Today's drop, if it holds to the close, would be its worst day since 3 February 2026. Before today the stock was down 6.8% this year, and $1,000 invested in Gartner five years ago would have been worth $693 at Monday's close.

At about 12 times forward earnings, against a median of 17 times for its peers in our data, the shares already priced in slow growth. Today's reaction says investors want proof that contract value can climb back toward mid-single digits before they pay more. The next read comes with fourth-quarter results early next year, alongside the 2027 outlook that sent the stock down 20.9% last time.
