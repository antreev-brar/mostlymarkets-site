AI infrastructure stocks have swung hard this month: down with Nvidia's leadership crisis, up 15% or more for many on Monday when Jensen Huang returned, and down again on Tuesday morning. When a whole group moves together, the useful question is which businesses turn their AI sales into cash, and which are spending it faster than it comes in.

We screened chip, networking, power and data-center companies in our coverage for revenue growth over the 12 months to their latest quarter, from SEC filings, leaving out stocks on our lists in the past two weeks. Then we read each one's cash flow, margins and share count against its peers using our fundamentals data.

```picks
like: AVGO, LRCX
skip: COHR
```

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. Broadcom ($AVGO)
**Revenue growth: 48.7% in the 12 months to August 2026.** Broadcom designs custom AI chips for cloud giants, including Google's TPUs, along with networking chips and infrastructure software.

1. **The growth turns into cash.** Free cash flow was 44.2% of revenue over the past 12 months, in the top tenth of the 25 chipmakers we cover, against a 22.2% median.
2. **It compounds per share.** Earnings per share grew 49.8% a year over five fiscal years, against a 17.2% peer median, while the operating margin rose 23.1 points to 48.0%.
3. **The quarter was its biggest.** Revenue reached **$29.6 billion** in the quarter to 2 August, up 85.5% on a year earlier, its 10-Q shows.

**The price:** 17.5 times expected earnings, a little below the 18.6 times chipmaker median, and 40.0 times trailing earnings against its own median of 88.8 times since 2024.

**What could go wrong:** analyst Ming-Chi Kuo said on 20 November that Google cut planned 2027 TPU orders by about 20%, which sent the stock down 12.2% that day ([our story](/news/2026-11-20/broadcom-avgo-shares-sink-after-a-report-that-google-cut-its-2027-tpu-orders/)). If Broadcom's next AI revenue outlook confirms it, the growth rate behind the cheap multiple shrinks.

### 2. Lam Research ($LRCX)
**Revenue growth: 33.4% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Lam makes the etch and deposition machines that chipmakers use to build memory and logic chips.

1. **Returns at the top of its group.** Return on capital of about 74.7% ranks in the top tenth of the chipmakers we cover, against a 17.8% median.
2. **A record quarter.** Revenue rose to **$8.22 billion** in the quarter to 27 September, a record and above the $8.13 billion consensus compiled by Investing.com, the results release showed.
3. **It shrinks its share count.** Diluted shares fell 3.7% over two years, in the top quarter of its peers, and it holds $3.7 billion of net cash.

**The price:** 19.1 times expected earnings, close to the chipmaker median of 18.6 times, and 31.3 times trailing earnings against its own median of 34.0 times since 2024.

**What could go wrong:** China made up 35% of its September-quarter revenue, up from 26% in June, the release showed, so any tightening of US export rules hits it directly.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Coherent ($COHR)
**Revenue growth: 30.8% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Coherent makes lasers and the optical transceivers that move data between AI servers.

1. **It is burning cash to grow.** Free cash flow was minus 23.2% of revenue over the past 12 months, in the bottom tenth of the 31 hardware makers we cover. In the September quarter it was about **minus $868 million**, as capital spending rose to $776 million from $104 million a year earlier, the results and SimpleTechTrend's call summary showed.
2. **Shareholders have been diluted.** Diluted shares rose 31.5% over two years, against a slight fall for the median peer.
3. **Returns are thin.** Return on equity of 8.5% is in the bottom quarter of its group, against a 20.0% median.

**The price:** 16.7 times expected earnings, about the 16.3 times hardware median, which looks cheap only until cash flow is counted.

**What would change our mind:** the spending is for a $20 billion five-year supply deal that ramps in early 2028, Seeking Alpha and SimpleTechTrend reported, and the company announced a $2 billion buyback last week, GuruFocus and TradingView reported. Positive free cash flow in a quarter before that ramp would show the capacity is paying for itself sooner.
