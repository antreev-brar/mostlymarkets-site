Walt Disney ($DIS) reports its fiscal fourth quarter, the 14 weeks to late September, before the open on Tuesday, 17 November, with the stock **0.8% below** its 52-week closing high. It is the first report since Bloomberg reported that managers had been asked to find up to 20% in annual savings ([our story](/news/2026-11-03/disney-dis-plans-thousands-of-job-cuts-with-a-20-savings-target/)), and the first chance for Chief Executive Josh D'Amaro to put numbers on fiscal 2027.

Last quarter, adjusted EPS rose to $2.06 from $1.61, above the $1.86 analysts expected, while revenue of $25.25 billion, up 7%, came in a little short of forecasts, Investing.com and Yahoo Finance reported. Segment operating income rose 21% to $5.6 billion, its release showed, led by the parks. The stock **rose 3.7%** that day in our data.

## What analysts expect

The Zacks consensus is **$1.67 a share** on an adjusted basis, up 50.8% from $1.11 a year earlier, on revenue of $25.26 billion, up 12.4%, Zacks and Breaking The News reported; Barchart's figure is $1.68. Much of that growth is the calendar: fiscal 2026 has 53 weeks and the extra one falls in this quarter, so it runs 14 weeks against 13, EBC and TheStreet noted. Analysts expect Experiences, the parks and cruise unit, to top **$10 billion** of quarterly revenue for the first time, about 14% more than a year ago, BlogMickey and Pomegra reported.

The debate this report settles: **how much of the cost cutting reaches profit?** Bloomberg reported D'Amaro plans to put part of the savings back into programming, parks and cruise ships, so the size of the plan matters less than how much of it shows up in the fiscal 2027 forecast.

## What to watch

- **Fiscal 2027 guidance.** Disney has said it expects double-digit adjusted EPS growth excluding the extra week, Investing.com and Yahoo Finance reported; a number below that would undo the stock's run.
- **The cuts.** A savings figure, a charge, and where the money goes.
- **Parks attendance.** Experiences crossing $10 billion on 14 weeks is the expectation; slower international visits, flagged in the job-cut reports, are the risk.

## How the stock has done

Disney closed at $118.70 on Monday, up 7.3% over the past month and 4.3% this year, while the S&P 500 fell for a third straight day. It trades at 15.7 times forward earnings, level with the median of its media peers in our data. $1,000 invested five years ago would be worth $747 today.
