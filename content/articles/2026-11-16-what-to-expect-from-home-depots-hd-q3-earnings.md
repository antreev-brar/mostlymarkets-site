Home Depot ($HD) reports its fiscal third quarter, the three months to early November, before the open on Tuesday, 17 November, with a call at 9 a.m. ET, MarketBeat and TipRanks reported. October retail sales come out an hour earlier, at 8:30 a.m. The stock **rose 18.6%** between 13 October and 13 November in our data, even as existing-home sales fell to their slowest pace since 2010 ([our story](/news/2026-11-13/home-sales-fell-to-3-75-million-in-october-the-slowest-pace-since-2010/)).

Last quarter, comparable sales, those at stores open a year or more, **rose 1.7%**, ahead of the 0.9% StreetAccount's analysts expected, and adjusted EPS of $4.92 beat the $4.73 consensus, CNBC and Yahoo Finance reported. Customer transactions fell 1% while the average ticket rose, its release showed. It kept its fiscal 2026 forecast of comparable sales flat to up 2%, and Chief Financial Officer Richard McPhail blamed "frozen housing market conditions." The stock slipped 0.1% that day.

## What analysts expect

The consensus is **$3.84 a share** on an adjusted basis, up from $3.74 a year earlier, on revenue of about $42.8 billion, MarketBeat and Benzinga reported; Nasdaq's data shows $3.86. Revenue was $41.4 billion a year earlier, in Home Depot's filings, so analysts expect growth of about 3.5%, inside the company's 2.5% to 4.5% full-year range.

The debate this report settles: **was the October rally a bet on a housing turn that hasn't come?** With the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.13% and home sales at a 16-year low, a rise in comparable sales has to come from bigger tickets and professional contractors, not more shoppers.

## What to watch

- **Comparable sales.** A figure near last quarter's 1.7% keeps the full-year range intact; a negative one would put its low end, flat, at risk.
- **Transactions.** Another fall in customer visits would say the growth is price, not demand.
- **The full-year forecast.** Management reaffirmed it in August; any cut after an 18.6% month would test the stock, and Lowe's ($LOW) reports on Wednesday into the same question.

## How the stock has done

Home Depot closed at $347.51 on Monday, up 12.2% over the past month and 1.0% this year, 11.1% below its closing high of $391.05 from 13 February. It trades at 22.7 times forward earnings, above the 19.9 times median of its retail peers in our data, near its own two-year median trailing multiple. $1,000 invested five years ago would be worth $886 today.
