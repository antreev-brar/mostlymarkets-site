Home Depot ($HD) reported fiscal third-quarter sales of **$43.4 billion** on Tuesday, up 4.9% from a year earlier and above the $42.8 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC. Adjusted earnings per share of $3.86 beat the $3.84 consensus. The company also said Chief Financial Officer Richard McPhail will succeed Ted Decker as chief executive on 23 November, and the stock was down 0.7% at $345.00 in premarket trading at 6:30 a.m. ET.

## What stood out

**Comparable sales**, sales at stores open a year or more, rose 2.2%, the best of the year after 0.6% in the first quarter and 1.7% in the second, and ahead of StreetAccount's 1.8%, CNBC reported. The mix is what it has been all year: shoppers came in less often, with comparable transactions **down 0.5%**, but spent 3.0% more each visit. That is growth from price and bigger projects, not from more customers, which fits a housing market where existing-home sales are running at their slowest since 2010.

*Verdict: better than feared, but still a ticket story, not a traffic story.*

**Margins** went the other way. Gross margin fell to 32.8% from 33.4%, by our arithmetic from the release, as cost of sales rose 6.0% against 4.9% sales growth, and operating income rose only 2.4%. The cash was stronger: operating cash flow for the nine months reached **$15.5 billion**, up from $13.0 billion, as the company tied up far less money in working capital.

*Verdict: sales beat, profit only kept pace.*

**The new chief** is the bigger news for holders. McPhail takes over from Decker, who took medical leave in August and will become chair, Reuters reported. Ann-Marie Campbell becomes president and chief operating officer, and Jordan Broggi becomes finance chief. "Results in the third quarter exceeded our expectations," McPhail said in the release, signing as incoming chief executive.

## The outlook

Home Depot replaced its ranges with single points. It now expects fiscal 2026 sales **to grow about 4.5%**, the top of its old 2.5% to 4.5% range, and comparable sales to rise about 1.4%, in the upper half of the old flat-to-2% range, according to the release and its August guidance. Adjusted EPS growth of about 2% sits at the midpoint of the old flat-to-4% range, and the adjusted operating margin of about 12.8% is at the bottom of the old 12.8% to 13.0% range. In short: more sales than planned, at slightly thinner margins.

## Our take

We were impressed by comparable sales: a 2.2% rise with mortgage rates where they are is the best evidence yet that professional contractors and bigger tickets can carry the business through a frozen housing market. On the other hand, the gross margin slipped, the full-year margin forecast went to the low end, and earnings per share will grow about 2% this year. Overall it is a solid quarter that does not change the long-run picture in our data: sales grew 4.5% a year over five years, a third of the median pace of our consumer discretionary peers.

The muted premarket reaction follows an **18.6% run** in the month to 13 November ([our preview](/news/2026-11-16/what-to-expect-from-home-depots-hd-q3-earnings/)); at 22.7 times forward earnings, above its retail peers' 19.9 times median, a small beat was already in the price.
