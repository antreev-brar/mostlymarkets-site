Lowe's ($LOW) said before the open on Wednesday that third-quarter sales rose 9.3% to $22.76 billion, with comparable-store sales **up 1.8%** against the 0.4% analysts expected, according to its release and LSEG data cited by Reuters. Adjusted earnings of $3.10 a share topped the $2.88 consensus, but about $250 million of refunds of tariffs charged under the IEEPA emergency-powers law added **$0.34 a share**, the company said.

Without the refunds, earnings would have been about $2.76, below forecasts. Lowe's now expects fiscal-year sales of about **$92.5 billion**, up from $92.0 billion, with comparable sales up about 0.5% instead of flat, but cut its operating-margin outlook to about 11.1% from 11.2%. That mix, more shoppers at thinner margins, is the same one that sent Home Depot ($HD) lower on Tuesday after its own beat ([our recap](/news/2026-11-17/home-depot-hd-tops-q3-estimates-and-names-cfo-richard-mcphail-ceo/)), and it tests the bet that investors can hide in the American shopper ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-17/wall-street-is-hiding-in-the-shopper-this-weeks-retailers-will-test-it/)).

On the call with Chief Executive Marvin Ellison this morning, the question is how much of the margin cut comes from the acquisitions that lifted sales ([our preview](/news/2026-11-17/what-to-expect-from-lowes-low-q3-earnings/)).
