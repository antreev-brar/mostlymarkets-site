Visa ($V) and Mastercard ($MA) asked US District Judge Brian Cogan in Brooklyn on Monday to give final approval to their $38 billion settlement with merchants over card swipe fees, Reuters and Seeking Alpha reported. Cogan did not rule at the hearing. The deal would cut swipe fees by 0.1 percentage point for five years, cap the rate on standard consumer cards at 1.25% for eight years, and for the first time let merchants refuse some cards, according to Reuters and Yahoo Finance.

The fees are the networks' core business, and this case has hung over both since 2005; a judge rejected an earlier $30 billion version in June 2024 as too small, before giving this one preliminary approval in June 2026. More than 1,000 businesses have objected, including Walmart, and Cogan warned that rejecting the deal could mean a "protracted process" if the case went to trial.

Visa was **down 0.7%** and Mastercard 1.3% at 10:30 a.m. ET, both within 3% of their 4 November highs. The next step is Cogan's ruling on final approval.
