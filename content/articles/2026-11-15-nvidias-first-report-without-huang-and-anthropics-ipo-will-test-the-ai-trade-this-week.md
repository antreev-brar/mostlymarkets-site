The argument this week is whether the AI trade can survive the loss of its best-known salesman. Nvidia ($NVDA) reports its third quarter after the close on Tuesday, its first report since Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive on Thursday, 12 November, with the stock at its lowest close in a year after **six straight weekly losses**. Traders spent last week betting that its customers would move orders to AMD ($AMD) and Broadcom ($AVGO); Tuesday's numbers are the first hard evidence either way.

A day later, Anthropic plans to price the biggest AI share sale yet, at up to about $1.4 trillion, and the bond market adds its own test: the Fed's October minutes land on Wednesday with the 10-year Treasury yield above 5%. The weekend brought no relief from crypto, where bitcoin fell below $65,000 on Saturday for the first time since August.

## The week in seven takes

### Nvidia reports at the end of six straight weekly losses

Nvidia has **fallen for six weeks** in a row in our data, to $162.85 on Friday, its longest weekly losing streak since 2012, CNBC and MarketWatch reported. The shares are 31.9% below their 6 October record close. Interim Chief Executive Colette Kress, also the finance chief, will present the results ([our bulls and bears](/news/2026-11-13/bulls-vs-bears-on-nvidia-nvda-is-13-times-earnings-cheap-without-jensen-huang/)).

### The bar is $109 billion, and the guidance after it


Analysts polled by LSEG expect revenue of about **$109 billion** for the quarter that ended on 25 October, a little above Nvidia's own forecast, Invezz and BigGo Finance reported. The figure that matters more is the fourth-quarter forecast: a cut would suggest customers are pausing, which is the bet behind AMD's and Broadcom's 18% gains last week ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-13/wall-street-is-betting-nvidias-loss-is-amd-and-broadcoms-gain-tuesday-will-test-it/)). Washington stays on the calendar after Tuesday: three House committees want Huang for a closed-door interview on 1 December, and the Senate Banking Committee asked Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to appear on 10 December, ABC News and Decrypt reported.

On Saturday, Altimeter Capital's Brad Gerstner and Atreides Management's Gavin Baker urged Nvidia's board to run a "slow, measured, and deliberate" search for a new chief and to weigh "the potential return of Jensen Huang," CNBC and Seeking Alpha reported ([our brief](/news/2026-11-14/two-nvidia-nvda-investors-ask-its-board-to-keep-jensen-huangs-return-on-the-table/)). They stopped short of asking for him back now. Expect the question on Tuesday's call.

Anthropic plans to price its shares on Wednesday and start trading on Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker ANT, CNBC reported, and the range values it at about **$1.3 trillion** at the midpoint, CBC and NBC News reported. That is below the roughly $1.5 trillion talked about before the AI sell-off ([our bulls and bears](/news/2026-11-07/bulls-vs-bears-on-anthropics-ipo-is-1-5-trillion-a-fair-price/)). Anthropic's computing bill is revenue for Amazon ($AMZN), Alphabet ($GOOGL) and the chipmakers, so demand for its shares is a read on the whole AI spending chain.

Lowe's ($LOW) **soared 20.7%** and Home Depot ($HD) 18.6% between 13 October and Friday, in our data, even as existing-home sales fell to their slowest pace since 2010 ([our story](/news/2026-11-13/home-sales-fell-to-3-75-million-in-october-the-slowest-pace-since-2010/)). Home Depot reports on Tuesday, Lowe's, Target ($TGT) and TJX ($TJX) on Wednesday and Walmart ($WMT) on Thursday, TipRanks and StockTitan reported. Walmart goes in 4.6% below its high at 46 times earnings ([our breakdown](/news/2026-11-14/walmart-wmt-reports-thursday-at-46-times-earnings-is-the-business-worth-it/)).

## What's next

All times ET.

- **Monday, 16 November:** Baidu ($BIDU) and Tyson Foods ($TSN) report before the open. Fed Chair Warsh speaks at noon.
- **Tuesday, 17 November:** October retail sales at 8:30 a.m. Home Depot and Disney ($DIS) report before the open; Nvidia after the close, with a call at 5 p.m.
- **Wednesday, 18 November:** Lowe's, Target and TJX before the open. Fed minutes at 2 p.m. Anthropic is expected to price its IPO; Palo Alto Networks ($PANW) reports after the close.
- **Thursday, 19 November:** Walmart and Alibaba before the open. Anthropic's first day of trading on Nasdaq. Trump and Xi meet in Shenzhen. Intuit ($INTU) reports after the close.

We think Nvidia's fourth-quarter forecast matters more than anything else this week: it guided to $108 billion for the third quarter, and a forecast that keeps growing would undercut last week's 18% rally in AMD and Broadcom, which rests on its customers walking away.
