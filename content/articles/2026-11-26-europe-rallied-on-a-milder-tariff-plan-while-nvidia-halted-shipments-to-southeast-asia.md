Wall Street is shut for Thanksgiving, but the rest of the world spent Thursday trading on what happened in New York on Wednesday afternoon. The Nasdaq had been down 4.6% at its low on a new US tariff proposal, hot core inflation and a third day of AI selling, then [closed down just 0.6%](/news/2026-11-25/stock-market-today-the-dow-erases-a-1100-point-drop-as-tariffs-and-ai-fears-fade-by-the-close/). Overnight, investors read the small print of the tariff plan and found it milder than the headlines: the new duties would not stack on top of most existing ones, and nothing can take effect before hearings in late January.

So Europe bought. The STOXX 600 **rose about 1.7%** in morning trading, Reuters and Investing.com reported, and Asia rose too, with Japan's Nikkei higher. US stock futures point to gains for Friday's half session, the Wall Street Journal and MarketWatch reported. The one fresh shock is Nvidia's: the Financial Times reported it has stopped server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries.

Tech and bank shares led Europe's rebound, Investing.com reported, a day after the same groups fell on AI-spending and inflation worries. CNBC described investors on both continents as fishing for bargains. Asia's markets had already risen overnight, following Wall Street's late recovery.

### The tariff plan is a proposal with a January deadline

```stat
[{"value": "14", "label": "Economies facing proposed duties", "note": "Of the 16 the trade office investigated"},
 {"value": "5 January", "label": "Written comments due", "note": "Public hearings start on 25 January"}]
```

The trade office's [Section 301 proposal](/news/2026-11-25/us-proposes-tariffs-on-14-trading-partners-with-20-on-china-and-a-15-cap-for-taiwan/) would not stack on the forced-labor tariffs already charged on 60 economies, and outside China it would not stack on existing Section 232 duties, law firms Kelley Drye and Wiley wrote in client alerts. Reuters reported the new rates would stay within caps set in the administration's trade deals, 20% for China. That is why Wednesday's morning panic looked overdone by the afternoon: the bill is smaller than feared, and it is weeks away.

The S&P 500 **slipped 0.25%** to 7,182.78 and the Dow rose 0.33% to 50,182.95. Nvidia ($NVDA) fell 3.9% to $149.30 after S&P Global [put its credit rating on watch](/news/2026-11-25/sp-puts-nvidias-nvda-aa-rating-on-watch-for-a-downgrade-over-governance/), while Meta ($META) rose 3.4%. Chip-equipment and memory stocks lost again, led by [Lam Research](/news/2026-11-25/lam-research-applied-materials-and-asml-shares-slide-what-happened/).

Nvidia told partners including Dell ($DELL) and Supermicro ($SMCI) to stop shipping its servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, the FT reported, according to Reuters and Yahoo Finance ([our brief](/news/2026-11-26/nvidia-nvda-halts-server-shipments-to-four-southeast-asian-countries-ft-says/)). It follows a Commerce Department rule requiring export licenses for those countries, aimed at Chinese buyers using data centers abroad. Friday's half session is the first chance for the stock, and its server partners, to trade on it.

Strategist Binky Chadha argued that the Nasdaq 100's roughly 12% fall since mid-October is a tech volatility shock, and that such shocks have historically bottomed within six to eight weeks, MarketWatch and Bloomberg reported. His case rests on earnings growth and a broadening recovery, with the focus shifting from AI spending to AI revenue, Investinglive reported. Six to eight weeks from mid-October puts the bottom he expects between now and mid-December.

Spot gold fell as much as 3% on Wednesday after [core PCE inflation came in at 3.3%](/news/2026-11-25/core-pce-inflation-rose-to-3-3-in-october-a-tenth-above-forecasts-before-a-fed-hike/), Reuters and Investing.com reported, and it remains about 37% below its record from earlier this year. Higher rates raise the cost of holding a metal that pays no interest. CME FedWatch puts the odds of a quarter-point hike on 9 December at about 96%.

Brent rose 3.5% on Wednesday after Yemen's Houthis said they attacked Saudi Arabia's largest refinery, CNBC and Yahoo Finance reported. A 1.1 million-barrel rise in US crude stocks capped the gain, and traders are now looking to the OPEC+ meeting on Sunday, 29 November, Anadolu Agency reported. Among the big stocks we track, Wednesday's best was HP ($HPQ), which [jumped 11.5%](/news/2026-11-24/hp-hpq-beats-q4-estimates-on-tariff-refunds-as-pc-units-fall-29/) after its quarter, its biggest gain since 22 May.

## What's next

All times ET.

- **Sunday, 29 November:** OPEC+ meets.
- **Monday, 30 November:** Rubrik ($RBRK) reports after the close.
- **Tuesday, 1 December:** the ISM manufacturing index and October job openings. Marvell ($MRVL), NetApp ($NTAP), MongoDB ($MDB) and Credo ($CRDO) report after the close. Jensen Huang's House interview is due.
- **Wednesday, 2 December:** Salesforce ($CRM) and Snowflake ($SNOW) report after the close.
- **Friday, 4 December:** the November jobs report at 8:30 a.m., the last big number before the Fed decides on 9 December.

We think Friday's half session tests the shipping halt more than the tariffs: Europe has already priced the milder tariff plan, but the 38% Nvidia has lost from its October high came on governance and China-access news, and this is more of the same.
