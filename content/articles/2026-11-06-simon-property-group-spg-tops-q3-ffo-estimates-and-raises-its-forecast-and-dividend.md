Simon Property Group ($SPG) reported third-quarter real estate funds from operations of **$3.37 a share** after the close on Thursday, up 4.7% from a year earlier and above the $3.32 Zacks consensus. FFO, the cash-earnings measure property trusts use in place of EPS, came to $3.09 on the broader definition, which includes a $0.27 non-cash mark-to-market loss, and on that basis MarketScreener counted a miss against FactSet's $3.31. Simon raised its 2026 forecast and its dividend, and the shares were **up 0.9% at $228.60** as of 12:15 p.m. ET Friday.

## What stood out

**Shoppers are spending more at Simon's malls.** Sales by its tenants rose **14.8%** to $852 a square foot over the past 12 months, and shopper traffic growth sped up for a third straight quarter, the release said. "Leasing momentum continued, rents and occupancy increased," Chief Executive Eli Simon said in the release.

*Verdict: strong; tenant sales that high give Simon room to keep raising rents at renewal.*

**Rents are doing the work.** Base minimum rent rose 6.1% to $62.75 a square foot, and net operating income from US properties grew 6.6%, while occupancy edged up to 96.5%. With the malls nearly full, growth now has to come from higher rents rather than filling space.

## The outlook

Simon raised its 2026 real estate FFO range to **$13.30 to $13.40** a share, from $13.20 to $13.30, above the $13.24 Zacks consensus, Yahoo Finance and MarketBeat reported. It raised its quarterly dividend to $2.30 and bought back 1.14 million shares in the quarter at an average of $207.98.

## Our take

We were impressed by tenant sales and rent growth, which show the best malls are still pulling in shoppers and retailers. On the other hand, the beat was small, the headline FFO fell on mark-to-market losses tied to its Klépierre bonds, and occupancy has little room left to rise. Overall, a solid quarter that confirms the story without changing it.

The stock had risen 14.7% in the month before the report and is up 22.4% this year, 4.3% below its July closing high, so investors had already priced in a good quarter, and the after-hours reaction was muted, Chartmill noted. The next test is fourth-quarter results early next year, with the 2027 forecast.
