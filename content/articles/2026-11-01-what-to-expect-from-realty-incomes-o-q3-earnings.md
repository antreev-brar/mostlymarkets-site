Realty Income ($O), the landlord to about 15,600 shops, warehouses and other buildings, reports third-quarter results after the market closes on Monday, 2 November, with a call at 5 p.m. ET, the company said on 1 October. It goes in with the stock **15.7% below** its March high, because the question for a borrower-landlord this autumn is the cost of money.

Last quarter was steady. Adjusted funds from operations (AFFO), the cash-profit measure REITs use, was **$1.09 a share**, up 3.8% and in line with the Zacks consensus, and revenue rose 9.7% to $1.55 billion. Realty Income lifted its 2026 AFFO forecast to $4.44 to $4.45 a share, from $4.41 to $4.44, and its investment target to $10 billion from $9.5 billion. The stock slipped 0.5% the next day, on 6 August.

## What analysts expect

- **AFFO:** $1.11 a share, up from $1.08 a year ago, according to Zacks via Yahoo Finance; Zacks says the estimate rose a cent over the past two months. Benzinga's figure is also $1.11.
- **Full year:** Wall Street's $4.45 a share sits at the top of the company's range, both outlets show.
- **Revenue:** providers disagree. Zacks expects $1.58 billion, others far less, so it is a weak guide.

## What to watch

- **The spread.** Realty Income aims to buy buildings at yields about 1.5 percentage points above its cost of capital. Last quarter it bought at a 7.3% initial cash yield. With the 10-year Treasury yield at **4.95%** on Sunday evening, any drop in that buying yield, or a cut to the $10 billion target, would tell investors the spread is narrowing.
- **How it pays for growth.** Public share sales covered only 18% of its investment this year, against a 47% average over three years, as it leans on private funds and partners, GuruFocus and ts2.tech reported. Its diluted share count is up 7.3% in two years, more than most real estate stocks in our data. Wells Fargo cut its price target to $59 from $64 on 19 October, and Barclays to $61 from $65, both keeping neutral ratings.
- **Occupancy.** It was 98.8% at the end of June, down from 98.9% in March. A further slip would point to tenant stress among its retailers.

## How the stock has done

Realty Income closed Friday at $56.93, **up 1.0%** this year and 6.4% over the past month, but 15.7% below its 52-week closing high of $67.56 from 2 March. $1,000 invested five years ago is worth $812 today, before dividends.

Those dividends are the point for most holders. On 8 September it raised its monthly payout to 27.15 cents, its 136th increase, an annual rate of $3.258, or a yield of about 5.7% at Friday's price. That is less than a percentage point above the 10-year Treasury, which is the comparison its shareholders are making.
