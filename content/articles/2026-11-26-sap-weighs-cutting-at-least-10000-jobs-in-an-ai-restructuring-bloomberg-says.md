SAP ($SAP), Europe's most valuable tech company, is drafting a plan to cut at least **10,000 jobs** to reshape its business for artificial intelligence, Bloomberg reported on Thursday, citing people familiar with the matter; Reuters carried the report and said SAP declined to comment. The cuts would fall mainly on product, engineering and sales, and the final number could change after talks with labor representatives, the people said.

SAP employs more than 110,000 people, so the plan would touch roughly one job in eleven. It ran a restructuring of about the same size in 2024, which cost about €3 billion ($3.3 billion) and relied largely on voluntary departures, Bloomberg and The Next Web noted. It comes after a month in which software stocks, SAP among them, fell sharply ([our story on the 17 November sell-off](/news/2026-11-17/salesforce-mongodb-and-sap-shares-slide-what-happened/)).

SAP's US shares closed at $209.37 on Wednesday, down 13.8% this year, and first trade on the report in Friday's shortened session. The cost of the plan and whether SAP confirms it are not known yet.
