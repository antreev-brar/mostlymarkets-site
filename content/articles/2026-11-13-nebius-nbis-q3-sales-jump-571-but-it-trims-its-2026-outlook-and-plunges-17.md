Nebius ($NBIS), which rents out Nvidia-powered computing for AI, reported third-quarter revenue of **$980.8 million** before the open on Thursday, up 571% year on year and above the roughly $907.5 million analysts expected, TheStreet and TradingKey reported. But it said 2026 revenue would land at the low end of its $3 billion to $3.4 billion range, Benzinga and Briefs reported, and the stock **plunged 16.8%** to close at $128.64, its worst day since 1 July.

## What stood out
The quarter was bigger than expected, but the timetable slipped. Nebius said delays at data centers built by other companies would push part of this year's revenue into 2027, TheStreet and Briefs reported, including part of the capacity contracted for Microsoft ($MSFT) at a New Jersey site, the shareholder letter and ShiftDelete said. More than half of the Microsoft capacity is now due by the end of 2026 and the rest in the second quarter of 2027.

Spending is going the other way. Nebius raised its 2026 capital spending plan to about **$27 billion** and set 2027 at $40 billion to $45 billion, SiliconANGLE, Blockonomi and ShiftDelete reported. It said customer prepayments on the contracts signed in the quarter covered more than all of the related spending, which limits how much it has to borrow up front. That matters because CoreWeave ($CRWV), its bigger rival, funds much of its build-out with debt, and lenders have grown choosier about AI borrowers this month.

## The outlook
Beyond the trimmed 2026 sales range, Nebius kept its target of 5 gigawatts of contracted power by the end of 2026 and said it now has more than 4, its letter shows. It no longer guides to annualized revenue run rate for year-end, and instead expects more than $7 billion by February 2027.

## Our take
Demand isn't the problem: revenue grew almost sevenfold and customers are prepaying for capacity. The problem is that Nebius depends on other companies' buildings to deliver it, and on the day Nvidia's CEO resigned, the market had no patience for a delay. With the stock up 53.7% this year but 55.1% below its June high, the next test is whether the Microsoft capacity arrives on the new schedule, which its next report will show. Before Friday's open the shares were down another 2.0%, at $126.10.
