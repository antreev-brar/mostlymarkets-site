## How it got here

Altria is what was left of Philip Morris after it spun off its international cigarette business, Philip Morris International ($PM), in 2008. Since then it has sold Marlboro and a few other brands in the US only, a market where fewer adults smoke every year.

Its attempts to find the next product have cost it dearly. It paid $12.8 billion for a 35% stake in vape maker Juul in December 2018, took a $4.5 billion charge on it within a year, and exited in 2023 when the stake was valued at $250 million, Fortune and Axios reported. It bought vape maker NJOY for about $2.75 billion that year, and a trade-court ruling in Juul's favor has since kept NJOY's main device off the US market, Bloomberg Law and Yahoo Finance reported.

On Thursday, Altria said it is considering an offer for Britain's Imperial Brands, likely solely in cash, and is talking with a third party about buying part of Imperial's business, according to its statement under UK takeover rules reported by CNBC and Investing.com. Imperial was worth **£26.77 billion ($35.07 billion)** at its high on Thursday, Reuters calculated. **Can a business this good at shrinking keep its returns if it buys more cigarettes abroad?**

## The business in one line

Adult smokers in the US pay Altria for cigarettes, cigars and oral nicotine, mostly in packs bought several times a week, and Altria raises the price of those packs most years.

Cigarettes are the business; everything else is a rounding error so far.

## The numbers that matter

For a consumer staples company, operating margin, return on capital and free cash flow decide quality, and debt counts against. Altria tops its group on the first three. All figures are from our analysis of SEC filings, ranked against the 35 consumer staples stocks we cover.

### Growth
Revenue including excise taxes fell **2.3% a year** over five fiscal years, in the bottom tenth of its group against a 4.4% median rise, and 1.1% a year over the last two. *Verdict: shrinking, slowly.*

### Profitability
The operating margin was **47.0%** over the 12 months to September 2026, the highest in its group against a 13.6% median, and it rose 0.9 points over five years while the median peer's fell 2.7. Philip Morris International, the closest peer, earned 38.6%. *Verdict: price increases have outrun lost volume.*

### Cash
Free cash flow was **38.6% of revenue** over the past 12 months, about $9.1 billion, the highest in its group against a 10.6% median. The dividend, raised in August to $1.11 a quarter, the company said, costs about $7.4 billion a year at today's share count, or about four-fifths of that cash. *Verdict: plenty of cash, most of it already promised.*

### Per share
Diluted shares fell 2.0% over two years. Adjusted earnings per share rose 4.4% to $5.42 in 2025, and Altria now expects $5.66 to $5.74 for 2026, its release showed. *Verdict: low single-digit compounding, helped by buybacks.*

### Balance sheet
Net debt was **$21.4 billion** at the end of September, 1.9 years of operating profit, and shareholders' equity is negative after years of buybacks. *Verdict: manageable today, with limited room for a cash deal.*

## Why the stock is where it is

The shares are up 32.3% this year and close to their high, but the worried investor has four points:

1. **Volume keeps falling.** Altria's domestic cigarette volume fell 10% in 2025, the full-year call showed, and 2.9% in the third quarter, or about 4% adjusted for trade inventory, against an estimated 5% fall for the industry, Altria reported.
2. **Smokers are trading down.** Marlboro's share of retail cigarette sales fell a full point to 39.5% in the third quarter, while discount brands rose 2.0 points to 34.1% of the market as money got tighter, according to the release and Zacks.
3. **The next product hasn't worked.** NJOY ACE is still off the market, and on! held 8.3% of the oral tobacco category in the third quarter, up 0.1 point on a year earlier but down 0.3 points from the second quarter, the release showed.
4. **Now a cash deal abroad.** A cash bid near Imperial's market value would more than double Altria's net debt before counting Imperial's own profit and debt.

## Testing the worry

**Volume: lasting, but contained.** Cigarette volumes have fallen for decades, and Altria has grown operating profit anyway. Smokeable operating income rose 3.9% in the third quarter on higher prices, the release and an earnings-call summary showed. The decline also eased this year: Altria estimated about 20 million adult vapers in the US, down a million on a year earlier, as enforcement against illicit disposable vapes pushed some users back to cigarettes, the call showed. That's a tailwind that may not repeat.

**Trading down: the one that survives.** The pricing engine depends on Marlboro holding its premium. Marlboro kept 60% of the premium segment, the release showed, but the premium segment itself is shrinking as discount brands gain. If smokers keep moving to cheaper packs, each price rise buys less profit.

**New products: lasting, and spreading to the deal.** Juul and NJOY were Altria's two big attempts to buy a future outside cigarettes, and both have so far lost money or stalled. Imperial would be a third large purchase, this time of more cigarettes, not fewer.

**The deal: unknown until 24 December.** Altria's statement left room for a partner to take part of Imperial, which would cut the bill. RBC Capital Markets analyst James Edwardes Jones suggested a joint bid with Japan Tobacco would sidestep competition objections, LSE.co.uk reported; Bloomberg reported Japan Tobacco was studying its own deal, as Reuters and CNBC noted.

## The moat

1. **Marlboro's pricing power.** Altria grew its smokeable margin 1.8 points to 63.4% in 2025 while selling 10% fewer cigarettes, the full-year release and call showed. Few consumer brands can raise prices that far into falling demand.
2. **Regulation that keeps rivals out.** New tobacco products need FDA authorization, and the import ban on NJOY ACE shows how patents and rules can shut a product out. The same wall protects Marlboro from new cigarette brands.
3. **Scale in distribution.** Philip Morris USA held 45.7% of retail cigarette sales in the third quarter, up 0.3 points, the release showed.

Rivals: Philip Morris International, which owns the rival Zyn pouch brand, grew revenue 7.7% a year over two years with a 38.6% operating margin, on our data.

## The risks we'd watch

1. **Debt.** Each $10 billion borrowed at an illustrative 6% would cost about $600 million a year in interest before tax, roughly 5% of Altria's $11 billion of 12-month operating profit.
2. **The dividend promise.** Altria targets mid-single-digit dividend growth through 2028, the company said in August. With four-fifths of free cash flow already paid out, a large cash deal leaves little room for that and buybacks.
3. **Faster decline.** If enforcement against illicit vapes fades and volumes fall back to 2025's 10% pace, price increases alone would have to carry profit growth.

## Our verdict

**Business: Solid. Price: Above its usual price.** Altria earns the highest operating margin and free cash flow margin in its group, and it has kept growing adjusted earnings per share, from $5.19 in 2024 to $5.42 in 2025 and a forecast $5.66 to $5.74 this year, while volumes fell. It falls short of Strong because its sales are shrinking and its record with large purchases outside Marlboro is poor. The shares trade at 15.8 times trailing earnings, above their 12.3 times median since late 2024, after a 32.3% rise this year. What would change our mind is the Imperial bid itself: a firm offer by 24 December funded mostly with debt would make the balance sheet the main risk, while a walk-away or a deal shared with a partner would leave the cash machine intact. A company built to manage decline now has to decide whether to buy more of it.
