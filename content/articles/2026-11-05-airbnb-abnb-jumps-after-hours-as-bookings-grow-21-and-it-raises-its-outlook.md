Airbnb ($ABNB) reported third-quarter revenue of $4.87 billion after the close on Thursday, up 19% from a year earlier and ahead of the $4.75 billion in Benzinga's consensus. Diluted earnings rose to $3.16 a share from $2.21, against the $2.84 Benzinga's consensus expected, and the company raised its full-year outlook. The stock, which had **fallen 3.5%** in the regular session to $170.13, was up 10.2% at $187.50 in after-hours trading at 5:14 p.m. ET.

## What stood out

Bookings sped up. Gross booking value, the total guests spent on the platform, rose **21%** to $27.7 billion, from 16% growth in the second quarter, according to the release, and nights and seats booked rose 16%. Airbnb said revenue growth came mainly from strong double-digit growth in nights stayed and a moderate rise in average daily rates, the price per night.

Profit rose faster than sales. Adjusted EBITDA, a measure of operating profit, rose 22% to $2.5 billion, a 52% margin against 50% a year earlier. The company credited AI for part of that: about 40% of customer-support issues were resolved without a human agent in the quarter, up from a third in the second quarter, and support cost per booking fell more than 17%.

The newer businesses are small but feed the core one. Hotels are still a single-digit share of nights booked, Airbnb said, but about 35% of guests whose first Airbnb booking is a hotel come back to book a home.

## The outlook

Airbnb expects fourth-quarter revenue of **$3.22 billion to $3.28 billion**, up 16% to 18%, including about two points of help from currencies, above the $3.16 billion Benzinga's consensus expected. For the full year it now expects revenue growth in the high teens and an adjusted EBITDA margin of about 36%, both raised. It also announced a new $12 billion buyback, after cutting its diluted share count by about 10% since 2022.

## Our take

A rare consumer quarter where growth sped up and margins widened at once. The weak spot is the fourth-quarter margin, which Airbnb expects to be down slightly, partly from the timing of marketing spending. Before the report, the stock was up 25.4% this year and 10.7% below its August high, so the after-hours jump would take it back near that peak. The next test is whether bookings growth holds near 20% next quarter, against a fourth quarter that grew 16% a year ago.
