Stocks that combine fat margins with growth are rare, and November's selloff has marked down several of them. On Friday, as buyers returned to beaten-down stocks, Hubbell ($HUBB) still closed 22.2% below its April high and S&P Global ($SPGI) 14.0% below its January high, while Airbnb ($ABNB) **rose 4.1%** and sits within 6% of its August peak. Their third-quarter reports, the last of them from Airbnb on 5 November, show why we like all three, and why eBay ($EBAY), the screen's other fast grower, is a skip.

Our screen took the companies in our coverage with an operating margin above 20% and ranked them by revenue growth in the 12 months to September 2026, from SEC filings and Friday's closes. We set aside the leader, Lumentum, because [Marvell agreed to buy it in stock](/news/2026-11-02/marvell-mrvl-agrees-to-buy-lumentum-lite-for-115-billion-in-stock/) on 2 November, so its price now follows Marvell's. We judged the rest on five-year growth, margins, cash, returns and debt against their own industries, then on the price.

```picks
like: ABNB, HUBB, SPGI
skip: EBAY
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Airbnb ($ABNB)

**Revenue growth: 16.7% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Airbnb runs an online marketplace for short-term home rentals and experiences, and takes a fee on each booking.

1. **It has compounded for five years and is speeding up.** Revenue grew **29.4% a year** over five fiscal years, in the top quarter of 66 consumer stocks we cover, against a median of 12.8%. Third-quarter revenue rose 19% to $4.9 billion as nights and seats booked grew 16% to 155 million, the results release said, its fastest growth in more than three years, Crypto Briefing reported.
2. **Growth now arrives as profit.** Operating margin was 22.5% over the last 12 months, in the top quarter against a 13.8% median, up from a loss in 2020. Return on capital was 56.1%, also in the top quarter.
3. **The cash goes back to owners.** Airbnb holds $4.9 billion more cash than debt, cut its diluted share count by 7.0% in two years, and approved a new $12 billion buyback with its results, the release and Quantisnow reported. It guided to fourth-quarter revenue growth of 16% to 18%.

**The price:** 33.4 times trailing earnings, on its own two-year median of 33.3, and 29.1 times forward earnings against a 20.0 median for its consumer peers. That premium is paid for the growth.

**What could go wrong:** the timing of cash. Its Reserve Now, Pay Later option added about 3 points to nights growth in the quarter but collects guests' money later, Quantisnow reported. If revenue growth fell back toward the 6.6% two-year median of its peers, the premium would have little support.

### 2. Hubbell ($HUBB)

**Revenue growth: 16.0% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Hubbell makes electrical products for buildings and data centers, and grid equipment for power utilities.

1. **Profit per share has compounded fastest.** Earnings per share grew **20.8% a year** over five fiscal years, in the top quarter of 89 industrial stocks we cover, against an 11.3% median. Operating margin widened by 7.3 points over the same span, to 20.2%.
2. **Data centers are pulling demand forward.** Third-quarter net sales rose 24.6% to $1.87 billion, with 11.3% organic growth and the rest mostly from its NSI Industries purchase, the release said. Organic growth in its electrical segment was 22%, which the company credited to data center markets, Investing.com reported.
3. **The outlook went up.** Hubbell raised its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast to $20.40 to $20.60, from $20.25 to $20.55, and its organic growth forecast to 10% to 11%, the release said.

**The price:** 25.4 times trailing earnings, below its two-year median of 28.2, and 19.4 times forward earnings against a 20.4 industrial median.

**What could go wrong:** debt and the utility side. Net debt is $4.8 billion, 3.6 years of operating profit, after the NSI deal, and the company expects only 6% to 7% organic growth from its utility segment this year. A pause in data center building would hit the segment that is doing the work.

### 3. S&P Global ($SPGI)

**Revenue growth: 18.2% in the 12 months to September 2026.** S&P Global rates corporate and government debt, runs the S&P 500 and other indexes, and sells financial data.

1. **It turns sales into cash like few others.** Free cash flow was **33.8% of revenue** over two years, in the top quarter of 46 payments, asset-management and exchange companies we cover, against a 22.3% median. Return on capital was 20.2%, nearly double the median.
2. **The long run is solid and the year was raised.** Revenue grew 15.6% a year over five fiscal years, against a 9.4% median. Third-quarter revenue rose 7% to $3.68 billion and adjusted EPS 9% to $4.58, and the company raised its 2026 revenue growth outlook to 7.4% to 8.4%, from 5.9% to 7.9%, the release and Minichart reported.
3. **It is pruning.** With its results it started a strategic review of Capital IQ and related data businesses, which could end in a separation, Investing.com and Minichart reported.

**The price:** 27.4 times trailing earnings, against its own two-year median of 36.7 and near the bottom of its 23.5 to 47.1 range. That is cheap for its own history, though 23.8 times forward earnings is above its peers' 16.2 median.

**What could go wrong:** per-share growth. EPS grew 8.7% a year over five years, in the bottom quarter of its peers, and operating margin fell 6.4 points over a span that includes its 2022 merger with IHS Markit. If quarterly growth stayed near 7%, the low multiple would be deserved.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### eBay ($EBAY)

**Revenue growth: 15.7% in the 12 months to September 2026.** eBay runs an online marketplace where people and small businesses sell new and used goods.

1. **The fast year sits on a slow five.** Revenue grew **4.5% a year** over five fiscal years, the bottom quarter of 16 consumer internet companies we cover, against a 17.2% median. Operating margin fell 9.1 points over the same span, to 20.3%.
2. **Some of the growth is bought.** Third-quarter revenue rose 14% to $3.2 billion, but the Depop purchase added about 2 points, and growth excluding acquisitions slowed to about 11% from 14% in the second quarter, Digital Commerce 360 and Value Added Resource reported. International sales volume grew just 4%.
3. **What it does well is real but priced.** Return on capital was 19.9% and free cash flow 19.5% of revenue over the last 12 months, both above its peers, and buybacks cut the share count 8.3% in two years.

**The price:** 24.6 times trailing earnings against its own two-year median of 20.0, after a 36.9% rise in a year. The multiple prices the recent pace, not the five-year record.

**What would change our mind:** growth excluding acquisitions holding above 10% in its fourth-quarter report, due early next year, with margins steady.
