On Wednesday the biggest health insurers fell for a second day on a federal audit rule, and AES got the last approval it needed to be taken private at $15 a share. Both stories touch this list: one says cheap stocks can get cheaper, the other that a low multiple can stop meaning anything.

Our screen took the stocks in our coverage whose revenue grew over the 12 months to September 2026 and ranked them by forward price-to-earnings ratio, lowest first, leaving out stocks we listed in the last 14 days. We then judged each on its returns, growth and share count against peers over five years, using SEC filings, and on its latest results.

```picks
like: ALL, UHS, CI
skip: AES
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Allstate ($ALL)
**Forward P/E: 8.9 times, against 13.3 for insurers in our data, as of 18 November 2026.** Allstate sells car and home insurance in the US.

1. **It earns more on its capital than almost any peer.** Return on equity was 35.9% over the 12 months to September 2026, ranking in the top tenth of 23 insurers, where the median is 17.9%.
2. **It made money through a bad catastrophe quarter.** Third-quarter catastrophe losses roughly tripled to $1.65 billion, yet adjusted net income came to $9.76 a share, the company said in its 4 November results release.
3. **Per-share earnings compound.** Earnings per share grew 17.1% a year over five years, above the peer median of 16.3%, and the share count fell 4.7% over two years.

**The price:** 6.0 times trailing earnings, below its own two-year median of 6.9 times.

**What would change our mind:** a hurricane or wildfire season that pushes catastrophe losses past a quarter's underwriting income, which was $2.0 billion in the third quarter.

### 2. Universal Health Services ($UHS)
**Forward P/E: 8.4 times, about half the 16.1 times for health care services peers, as of 18 November 2026.** Universal runs acute-care and behavioral-health hospitals.

1. **Its margins lead its group.** Operating margin was 10.8% over the 12 months to September 2026, against a peer median of 3.6%, and return on equity was 19.2%, in the top tenth.
2. **It shrinks its share count.** Diluted shares fell 13.2% over two years, and it bought back $242 million of stock in the third quarter, according to its results release. Earnings per share grew 16.0% a year over five years, against a peer median of 3.0%.
3. **The worry is already in the price.** Third-quarter earnings fell to $5.22 a share from $5.86 as new hospitals ramped up slower than planned, and management has warned that Medicaid payments will fall in 2027. The stock is down 14.7% this year and 23.8% below its high.

**The price:** 7.8 times trailing earnings, against its own two-year median of 9.8 times.

**What would change our mind:** 2027 guidance, due with fourth-quarter results, that shows the Medicaid cut taking more than a year's earnings growth.

### 3. Cigna ($CI)
**Forward P/E: 9.1 times, against 16.1 for health care services peers, as of 18 November 2026.** Cigna runs Express Scripts, a pharmacy-benefit manager, and a commercial health insurer.

1. **It grows faster than its group.** Revenue grew 11.4% a year over five years, in the top quarter of 17 peers, and return on capital of 11.3% beats the 8.4% median.
2. **It is raising its numbers.** On 5 November it raised its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast to at least $30.60, and in September it set a target of 10% to 14% a year in adjusted EPS growth through 2030.
3. **The market is treating it apart from the insurers.** In Wednesday's sell-off over the audit rule it was down 2.6% at midday while UnitedHealth was down 8.4% ([our story](/news/2026-11-18/unitedhealth-humana-and-centene-shares-slide-what-happened/)).

**The price:** about 9.3 times its own 2026 forecast, and 12.6 times trailing earnings against a two-year median of 13.1 times.

**What would change our mind:** the weak spot is GAAP earnings per share, which fell 0.7% a year over five years; a 2027 forecast below the 10% growth it has promised would make the target look loose.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### AES ($AES)
**Forward P/E: 7.3 times, against 19.0 for utilities, as of 18 November 2026.** AES generates and sells power in the US and abroad.

1. **The price is set by a buyout.** A consortium led by BlackRock's Global Infrastructure Partners and EQT agreed in March to buy AES for $15.00 a share in cash, an enterprise value of about $33.4 billion including debt.
2. **The deal is about to close.** AES said on 17 November that the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission had issued the last approval needed, with closing expected on 2 December, after which the shares will be delisted.
3. **There is nothing left to collect.** The stock closed at $15.02 on Wednesday, two cents above the offer, so the low multiple describes earnings the public will no longer own.

**The price:** above the $15.00 cash offer.

**What could go wrong:** for us, only if the deal fell through before 2 December, which would leave a business whose free cash flow was negative 14.5% of sales over two years.
