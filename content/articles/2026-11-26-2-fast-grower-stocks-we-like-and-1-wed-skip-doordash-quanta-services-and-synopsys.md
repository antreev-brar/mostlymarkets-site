On Wednesday, the last session before Thanksgiving, all three stocks on this list sat between 24% and 30% below their 52-week highs, though each grew revenue 37% or more in the 12 months to its latest quarter. After a month in which fast-growing stocks fell together, the useful question is how each one grew: on its own, or by buying another company.

We started from the fastest revenue growers in our coverage over the 12 months to their latest quarter, from SEC filings, leaving out stocks on our lists in the past two weeks. Then we checked whether the growth was organic, whether it lasted five years, and whether it reached cash flow, each against the company's peers in our fundamentals data.

```picks
like: DASH, PWR
skip: SNPS
```

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. DoorDash ($DASH)
**Revenue growth: 35.0% in the 12 months to September 2026.** DoorDash runs food and grocery delivery apps in the US and, since buying Deliveroo, across Europe.

1. **The growth has lasted.** Revenue grew **36.6% a year** over five fiscal years, almost three times the 12.8% median of the 66 consumer stocks we cover, and 29.6% a year over the last two.
2. **Most of it is its own.** Without Deliveroo, orders still grew 17% and gross order value 23% in the September quarter, the results release showed ([our recap](/news/2026-10-30/doordash-dash-tops-q3-estimates-as-orders-grow-28/)).
3. **It turns growth into cash.** Free cash flow was 16.8% of revenue over two years, in the top quarter of its peers against a 9.4% median, and the operating margin rose 20.4 points over five years to 5.3%.

**The price:** 38.2 times expected earnings, nearly double the 20.1 times peer median, but 27.0 times free cash flow for a company growing sales about 30% a year.

**What could go wrong:** the roughly $17 billion Instacart deal is under Justice Department review, Reuters and Bloomberg reported on 16 November, and DoorDash owes a $1.25 billion fee if it fails. On 23 November it lined up new loans to help pay for the deal, an 8-K filing, Dow Jones and Seeking Alpha showed, and the stock rose 5.7% that day. More debt and a long review would weigh on the cash flow that makes the case.

### 2. Quanta Services ($PWR)
**Revenue growth: 37.1% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Quanta builds and maintains power lines, substations, and other grid equipment for utilities and data centers.

1. **It compounds per share.** Earnings per share grew **17.2% a year** over five fiscal years, against an 11.3% median for the 89 industrial stocks we cover, while revenue grew 20.5% a year against 8.0%.
2. **The order book is a record.** Backlog, work signed but not yet done, reached $63.7 billion at the end of September, about $10 billion more than three months earlier, the results release and call showed ([our recap](/news/2026-10-30/quanta-services-pwr-tops-q3-estimates-as-backlog-hits-a-record-64-billion/)).
3. **The margins are a contractor's.** The operating margin of 6.8% over the past 12 months is in the bottom quarter of industrials, and free cash flow was 6.8% of revenue over two years. That's normal for labor-heavy construction, and the margin hasn't slipped over five years.

**The price:** 27.1 times expected earnings against a 20.1 times industrial median, but 51.2 times trailing earnings, below its own median of 64.6 times since late 2024.

**What could go wrong:** the stock is 28.0% below its 6 May high and fell 4.1% on Wednesday alone. Net debt is 1.8 years of operating profit, so a pause in utility and data-center orders would show up in a backlog that, for now, is still rising.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Synopsys ($SNPS)
**Revenue growth: 46.3% in the 12 months to July 2026.** Synopsys sells the design software and chip blueprints that chipmakers use, and since July 2025 Ansys's engineering simulation software.

1. **The growth was mostly bought.** Revenue rose to $2.48 billion from $1.74 billion in the quarter to July, but its core chip-design software (EDA) grew **8.5%**, the results release and Zacks showed. Over five fiscal years revenue grew 13.9% a year, behind the 15.9% median of the 32 software companies we cover.
2. **Shareholders paid for it.** Diluted shares rose 23.2% over two years, against almost no change for the median software peer, and the operating margin fell 3.9 points over five years while the median peer's rose 6.3.
3. **Returns and debt haven't caught up.** Return on capital is about 1.7%, against a 9.9% peer median, and net debt of $6.4 billion equals 8.0 years of operating profit.

**The price:** 22.8 times expected earnings, about the 23.5 times software median, but 71.7 times trailing earnings against its own median of 52.7 times since 2024.

**What would change our mind:** Synopsys reports its fiscal fourth quarter on Tuesday, 8 December, after the close, the company said. EDA growth back in double digits and a 2027 outlook showing margins rising as Ansys is folded in would show the deal earning its cost.
