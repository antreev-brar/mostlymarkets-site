Over the weekend, Iranian and Qatari officials held a third straight day of talks aimed at ending the war with Iran, and President Donald Trump signaled he wants to give diplomacy more time, Arab News and Barchart reported. By 4:50 a.m. ET on Monday, US crude had **fallen 4.3%** to $99.60 a barrel, in our data.

Stock futures pointed higher after Friday's chip rout, Reuters and The Wall Street Journal reported, and by 6:14 a.m. ET AMD ($AMD) was up 1.7% and CoreWeave ($CRWV) 3.4% before the open. Tonight brings the first two tests of the week: Apple ($AAPL) and Palantir ($PLTR) report after the close, a day before the midterms and AMD.

## This week in seven takes

### US crude is back below $100 on Iran talks

Brent and US crude each gained nearly 15% in October, Arab News reported, so this morning's drop gives back only part of the month. A deal would not quickly restore tanker traffic, analysts said, according to Arab News, and The Wall Street Journal reported last week that the US has told Gulf allies to prepare for a short, intense campaign against Iran after Tuesday's vote ([our story on oil and the Fed](/news/2026-10-31/the-story-wall-street-is-telling-about-100-oil-and-the-fed/)).

### Goldman raised its oil forecasts on the same weekend


Goldman Sachs now builds a 1 million barrel-a-day supply disruption into its 2027 base case and says a risk premium of about $27 a barrel "now explains most of October's crude price rally," CNBC and Investing.com reported. That premium is what a lasting Iran deal would remove, and what a failed one would add to.

After Friday's close, S&P Global Ratings cut Meta's ($META) outlook to negative from stable, keeping its AA- rating, CNBC and Yahoo Finance reported, citing heavy AI spending and a September quarter in which it spent more cash than it made. S&P said Meta's ability to make money from AI is more limited than its peers'; a Meta spokesperson told Reuters the company "respectfully" disagreed ([our breakdown of Meta](/news/2026-11-01/meta-meta-now-spends-more-than-it-makes-in-cash-is-the-business-still-strong/)).
