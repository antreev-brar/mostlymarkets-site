Johnson & Johnson ($JNJ) said on Saturday that Icotyde, its once-daily psoriasis pill, met the main goal of a late-stage trial in psoriatic arthritis: **58%** of patients on the 200 mg dose reached ACR20, a 20% improvement in joint symptoms, at week 16, against 41% on placebo. The ICONIC-PsA 1 data, in patients never treated with a biologic drug, were presented as a late-breaking abstract at the ACR Convergence meeting and also reported by Reuters.

The result matters because arthritis is the next market for a drug J&J is building its immunology franchise around. The FDA approved Icotyde for plaque psoriasis on 18 March, and a pill that also treats the joint disease many psoriasis patients develop would compete with injections. Response rates kept rising to week 24, when 69% reached ACR20, with no new safety signals, J&J said. Placebo patients switched to the drug at week 16, so that later figure has no placebo comparison.

Icotyde is not approved for psoriatic arthritis. The second trial, ICONIC-PsA 2, which includes patients who have already tried biologics, has not reported yet. The shares closed Friday at $276.22, near their 52-week high.
