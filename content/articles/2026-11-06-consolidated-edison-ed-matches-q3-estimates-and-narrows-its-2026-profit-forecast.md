Consolidated Edison ($ED), the utility that carries power, gas and steam to New York City and Westchester, reported third-quarter adjusted earnings of **$2.07 a share** after the close on Thursday, 5 November, up from $1.90 a year earlier and level with the $2.07 consensus compiled by Zacks; FactSet's consensus was $2.03. Revenue rose 7.6% to $4.91 billion, above the $4.77 billion Zacks consensus, by our analysis of the quarterly filing. The stock slipped 0.3% after hours and was **down 1.4%** at $119.72 as of 2:15 p.m. ET on Friday, after closing Thursday at a 52-week high of $121.44.

## What stood out

A utility's rate base is the value of the poles, wires and pipes regulators let it earn a return on, so it is the engine of its profit. CECONY's larger electric rate base added **17 cents a share** to the quarter, the release showed, all of the year's gain on its own. Revenue barely matters to the bottom line here: under New York's decoupling rules, sales volumes and fuel costs pass through to customers.

*Verdict: the growth came from the right place, the regulated investment.*

The drag was the shares Con Edison sells to pay for that investment. New stock **cost 5 cents a share** in the quarter and 14 cents over nine months. Over two years its diluted share count rose 7.0%, against 3.4% for the median of the 33 utilities we track, by our analysis of SEC filings.

*Verdict: the cost of a big building program, paid by today's shareholders.*

Chairman and Chief Executive Tim Cawley pointed to what that money buys. "We're at the center of one of the nation's largest electrification efforts," he said in the release, adding that Con Edison plans about $797 million of climate-resilience spending from 2026 to 2030. Chief Financial Officer Kirk Andrews said it has reached agreement with its regulator and other parties on a new investment plan for its steam business, which awaits approval.

## The outlook

Con Edison narrowed its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast to **$6.05 to $6.15**, from $6.00 to $6.20, keeping the midpoint at $6.10. That sits on the roughly $6.10 analysts expected for the year, according to Zacks and Earnings Whispers. Nine-month adjusted EPS of $5.08 leaves about $1 a share for the fourth quarter, against 83 cents (GAAP) a year earlier.

## Our take

We liked the quality of the quarter: rate-base growth did the work, and the tighter range removes the low end. On the other hand, there was no beat to speak of against Zacks, and dilution keeps eating part of each year's gain. Friday's dip was not specific to Con Edison: 11 of the 15 big utilities in our price files were lower by 2:15 p.m. ET, with a median fall of about 1%, after [October's strong jobs report](/news/2026-11-06/employers-added-165-000-jobs-in-october-keeping-a-december-hike-in-play/) kept a December rate hike in play.

The run explains the muted reaction. The stock was up **22.3% this year** at Thursday's close and 16.1% in the past month, by our price files, so an in-line quarter had little left to add. At 20.4 times trailing earnings it trades above its two-year median of 18.4, though its forward P/E of 18.9 is level with peers. Over five years, EPS grew 11.5% a year, a top-quarter pace among utilities, while return on equity of 8.7% sits in the bottom quarter.

This quarter does not change that picture.
