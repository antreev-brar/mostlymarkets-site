On Monday, BP ($BP) said it had completed the sale of 65% of Castrol, its motor-oil brand, to the investment firm Stonepeak, bringing in about **$6 billion** after tax, which it will use to reduce its debts, BP said in a release also reported by Investing.com and Rigzone. The deal, agreed in December 2025, valued Castrol at $10.1 billion including debt.

BP keeps 35% and can sell it after a two-year lock-up, but expects no earnings or dividend from the stake in the short to medium term. The cash lands on a balance sheet BP has already cut hard: net debt fell to $13.9 billion at the end of September from $22.2 billion at the end of 2025, its third-quarter filing shows, so the proceeds could take it to around $8 billion before other cash flows ([our story on BP's quarter](/news/2026-11-01/bp-bp-tops-q3-estimates-with-its-highest-profit-in-18-years/)).

That raises the question BP's shares have been waiting on: whether Chief Executive Meg O'Neill turns the windfall into buybacks, which BP has held back while rivals pay out. She lays out BP's strategy and financial targets in New York on 15 December. The American shares were down 0.9% before the open, at 6:11 a.m. ET.
