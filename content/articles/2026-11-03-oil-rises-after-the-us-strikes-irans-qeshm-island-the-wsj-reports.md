US forces struck Iran's Qeshm Island on Monday night, The Wall Street Journal reported, citing American officials, and oil gave back part of Monday's drop. By 4:50 a.m. ET on Tuesday, US crude was **up 1.2%** at $97.65 a barrel and Brent up 1.3% at $106.08, in our data. CNBC and Gulf Business carried the report; US Central Command had not confirmed it, and Iranian state media reported several explosions on the island.

Qeshm sits at the mouth of the Strait of Hormuz, the route for a large share of the world's seaborne oil. Officials described the strikes as defensive, meant to stop Iran attacking ships in the strait, and part of a series of limited strikes in recent weeks, CNBC and The National reported. It cuts against Monday's hopes for diplomacy, which sent [crude down 7.9% and airline stocks up](/news/2026-11-02/stock-market-today-oil-sinks-8-but-a-tech-reversal-drags-the-nasdaq-down-1-1/); one analyst told CNBC the report put some risk premium back into the price.

What isn't known is whether Washington confirms the strikes or Tehran responds, as Americans vote in the midterms ([the story Wall Street is telling about $100 oil](/news/2026-10-31/the-story-wall-street-is-telling-about-100-oil-and-the-fed/)).
