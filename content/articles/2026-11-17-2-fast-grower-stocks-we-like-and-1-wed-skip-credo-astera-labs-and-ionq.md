On Tuesday, with Nvidia ($NVDA) due to report after the close, the fastest-growing AI chip suppliers sit far below their summer highs: in our data Credo ($CRDO) and Astera Labs ($ALAB) **fell 28.9% and 29.2%** in the month to Monday's close, after an AI hardware sell-off on 5 November. That leaves a question a long-term owner can answer with the companies' own numbers: is the growth real, and does it turn into cash?

Our screen ranked the companies in our coverage by revenue growth over their latest 12 months, from SEC filings in our fundamentals data, and left out stocks we listed in the last 14 days. We then judged the leaders on whether that growth makes money and cash, against their own industries, long run first.

```picks
like: CRDO, ALAB
skip: IONQ
```

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. Credo Technology ($CRDO)
**Revenue growth: 165.1% in the 12 months to August 2026.** Credo makes the chips and active electrical cables that link servers and switches inside AI data centers.

1. The growth has lasted: revenue rose **86.8% a year** over five fiscal years, the fastest of the 22 chipmakers we can measure, against a median of 14.2%.
2. It makes money doing it: the operating margin was 31.7% over the last 12 months, above the chipmakers' 29.6% median, and it turned 27.6% of sales into free cash flow, against a median of 22.2%.
3. What changed lately: the stock fell 17.7% on 5 November in the AI hardware sell-off and closed Monday 60.1% below its June high, in our data, as AI hardware stocks sold off together ([our story](/news/2026-11-13/amd-broadcom-and-credo-shares-slide-what-happened/)).

**The price:** 16.5 times expected earnings, against a chipmaker median of 21.7 times, which puts it in the cheapest quarter of its peers despite growing faster than all of them.

**What could go wrong:** the latest quarter's margin. Operating income fell to $120.7 million in the quarter to August from $155.8 million in the quarter before, its filings show, even as revenue rose, so its next report, due in early December, has to show that the squeeze was temporary. Its share count also rose 17.7% over two years, against a flat median.

### 2. Astera Labs ($ALAB)
**Revenue growth: 115.2% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Astera Labs makes connectivity chips, including its Scorpio switches, that move data between AI processors and memory.

1. The latest quarter sped up: third-quarter revenue rose **154% year on year** to $584.9 million, and the company guided fourth-quarter revenue to $730 million to $750 million, up about 27% at the midpoint, its release showed and Zacks reported.
2. It has pricing power: its gross margin averaged 74.4% over two years, in the top tenth of chipmakers, against a median of 55.6%.
3. It is cash generative: free cash flow was 25.2% of sales over two years, above the 23.7% median, and operating income more than tripled in a year to $179.1 million in the third quarter.

**The price:** 32.5 times expected earnings, against a chipmaker median of 21.7 times, so it is not cheap. The stock has fallen 58.1% from its June high, so the premium is far smaller than it was in the summer.

**What could go wrong:** the price assumes the AI build-out keeps growing. Its growth rides on AI server spending, so a pause by the big buyers would hit revenue fast, and its share count rose 17.4% over two years.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### IonQ ($IONQ)
**Revenue growth: 376.5% in the 12 months to September 2026.** IonQ builds quantum computers and sells access to them, and it now owns a chip foundry after buying SkyWater.

1. It loses far more than it sells: the third-quarter operating loss was **$433.1 million** on revenue of $173.8 million, its release showed, and the operating margin over 12 months was -333.9%, in the bottom tenth of hardware makers.
2. Much of the growth is bought: $83.5 million of the quarter's revenue came from the SkyWater foundry, Quantum Computing Report and the release showed, so the quantum business itself was about half the total.
3. Owners are being diluted: the diluted share count rose 87.1% over two years, the worst of 31 hardware makers we cover, and IonQ raised about $2 billion in an October share sale.

**The price:** the trailing P/E of 39.4 is misleading; IonQ's $564 million third-quarter net income came from a non-cash gain on its warrants, not from operations, the release showed. The stock is down 41.0% this year.

**What would change our mind:** a path to cash. IonQ raised its 2026 revenue forecast to $505 million to $515 million; a quarter in which free cash flow loss shrinks while revenue grows without new acquisitions would make the case.
