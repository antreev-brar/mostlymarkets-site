Investors ran for cover this week: as the S&P 500 fell into a correction on Friday, food, health care and bonds rose, and General Mills ($GIS) gained 3.7% while the Nasdaq sank 5.3%, as we reported in [Friday's market wrap](/news/2026-11-20/stock-market-today-the-s-p-500-enters-a-correction-as-nvidia-sinks-14/). Whether a 5%-plus yield is shelter depends on the cash behind it.

Our screen took the highest-yielding stocks we cover that we haven't listed in the past 14 days and checked whether free cash flow in the 12 months to September 2026 (August for General Mills) covers the dividend, then ran each through our usual quality and price checks against its industry, using SEC filings. Cover is the free cash flow yield, from our data, divided by the dividend yield.

## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. General Mills ($GIS)
**Dividend yield: 6.8%, as of Friday's close.** The maker of Cheerios, Pillsbury and Blue Buffalo pet food.

1. **Weak: sales are shrinking.** Revenue fell 3.9% a year over the past two years, in the bottom 5% of the consumer staples stocks we cover, and was down 2.8% in the quarter to August.
2. **Weak: the payout eats almost all the cash.** Free cash flow covers the dividend only 1.2 times, and the operating margin over the past 12 months was **negative 1.1%**, against a 13.6% median for its peers.
3. **The warning is now public.** Piper Sandler downgraded the stock on 12 November, saying a 30% to 50% dividend cut may be needed to bring debt, above four times EBITDA, back to a comfortable level by 2029, GuruFocus and MarketScreener reported. The company has said it intends to keep the $0.61 quarterly payout.

**The price:** 11.6 times expected earnings, against a 17.6 median for staples, a discount earned by shrinking sales.
**What would change our mind:** volume growth and a dividend held with room to spare in its 17 December results.

### 2. Ford ($F)
**Dividend yield: 5.4%.** The Detroit carmaker, with a lending arm, Ford Credit.

1. **Weak: thin margins.** Its gross margin was 10.9% over two years, against a 39.9% median for the 66 consumer discretionary stocks we cover, and the operating margin over the past 12 months was **negative 3.8%** on a GAAP basis.
2. **The electric-car drag isn't over.** Model e, its EV unit, lost $1.1 billion in the third quarter and Ford expects about $4 billion of losses for 2026, with break-even targeted for 2029, the company and Electrive reported.
3. **Cash is thin relative to sales.** Free cash flow was 2.8% of revenue over the past 12 months, the bottom quarter of its group; the dividend is covered twice, but on a small base.

**The price:** 6.5 times expected earnings, a third of the sector median, which prices a weak business rather than a bargain.
**What would change our mind:** a smaller EV loss guide for 2027 with its fourth-quarter results early in 2027.

### 3. Alexandria Real Estate Equities ($ARE)
**Dividend yield: 7.2%.** A landlord to life-science labs in Boston, San Francisco and San Diego.

1. **Weak: rents are falling.** Revenue dropped 10.7% in the third quarter from a year earlier, the worst of the 30 real estate stocks we cover, and same-property net operating income fell 6.7% on a GAAP basis, the company said.
2. **Losses from writedowns.** It booked a net loss of $3.74 a share in the quarter on impairments of buildings held for sale, according to its results and GuruFocus.
3. **More pain is scheduled.** Management flagged pressure in the first half of 2027 from 1.4 million square feet of expiring leases, Tradingkey and GuruFocus reported. Funds from operations, a REIT's cash-earnings measure, cover the $0.72 quarterly dividend about 2.2 times on the $6.40 2026 guide, so the payout is safe for now; the business behind it isn't growing.

**The price:** half its book value (0.5 times), the market's price for a shrinking landlord.
**What would change our mind:** occupancy back above 90% and same-property income growing again.

## 1 stock we like

### T. Rowe Price ($TROW)
**Dividend yield: 5.2%.** The Baltimore fund manager, with $1.86 trillion under management at the end of September.

1. **Solid: highly profitable.** Its operating margin was 32.0% over the past 12 months, above the median for the 46 payments, exchange and asset-management stocks we cover, and its return on equity was 19.2%.
2. **The cash covers the dividend 1.4 times,** and the payout has risen every year for 40 years: the 2026 rate of $1.30 a quarter, declared again on 4 November, was a 2.4% increase, the company said.
3. **The flows are the weak spot.** Clients pulled a net $28.3 billion in the third quarter, mostly from stock funds, though its ETFs reached $33.8 billion and management expects outflows to ease in the fourth quarter, Investing.com and Tradingkey reported. Five-year revenue growth of 3.3% a year is in the bottom tenth of its group.

**The price:** 10.1 times expected earnings, against a 16.1 median for its peers, and below its own two-year median trailing P/E of 11.3.
**What could go wrong:** fees fall with the stock market, so a deeper sell-off cuts its revenue directly; a year of outflows above $100 billion would make the dividend harder to keep raising.
