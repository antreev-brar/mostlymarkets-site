Pfizer ($PFE) said before the open on Tuesday that it earned **an adjusted 87 cents a share** in the third quarter, well ahead of the 73 cents analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters. Revenue was $16.58 billion, flat on a year earlier and above LSEG's $15.87 billion estimate, and Pfizer raised its 2026 forecasts for sales and profit. The shares were **unchanged at $30.67** in pre-market trading as of 9:30 a.m. ET, at the stock's highest close in a year.

## What stood out

The headline revenue figure hides two businesses moving in opposite directions. Sales of products other than its Covid vaccine and pill **grew 7%** at constant currency, while Covid sales kept falling, leaving the total flat, the release said. Dow Jones reported that Eliquis, the blood thinner Pfizer sells with Bristol-Myers Squibb, kept growing, alongside the cancer drugs Padcev and Lorbrena.

*Verdict: the post-Covid business is growing, just not fast enough yet to show in the total.*

The products Pfizer launched or bought in recent years, led by Padcev, the migraine drug Nurtec and Lorbrena, brought in **$3.8 billion**, up 32%. They are the drugs meant to replace the sales Pfizer loses as Eliquis, Ibrance and Xtandi lose patent protection over the next few years.

Profit held up despite a cost Pfizer had flagged: adjusted EPS matched last year's 87 cents even after a $650 million charge for its licensing deal with Innovent Biologics, worth about 10 cents a share, landed in this quarter.

## The outlook

Pfizer raised its 2026 revenue forecast by $1.5 billion at both ends, to $62.0 billion to $64.0 billion, and its adjusted EPS range to $2.95 to $3.05 from $2.80 to $3.00, still including the Innovent charge. The release said the guidance assumes no share buybacks this year. Pfizer also named Cecile Guegan, its interim finance chief since the summer, as chief financial officer from 1 November.

## Our take

We were impressed by the size of the beat: 14 cents of EPS above LSEG's figure, with a charge inside it. On the other hand, revenue was flat, and over five years Pfizer's EPS has shrunk 3.6% a year, the bottom tenth of its drugmaker peers, by its SEC filings. Overall, it's a solid quarter for a company still filling a patent hole.

The muted pre-market reaction makes sense: the stock was **up 23.2% this year** to Monday and already at its 52-week closing high, and at 11.1 times forward earnings it is about 40% cheaper than its peers' median of 18.4, by our data. The test is the 2027 guidance, which Pfizer usually gives in December; a range that grows on 2026 would show the new drugs are outrunning the patent losses.
