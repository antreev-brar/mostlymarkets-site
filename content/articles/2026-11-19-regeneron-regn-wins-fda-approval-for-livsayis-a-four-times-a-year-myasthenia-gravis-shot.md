The US Food and Drug Administration approved Regeneron's ($REGN) Livsayis on Wednesday, 18 November, for adults with generalized myasthenia gravis who test positive for anti-acetylcholine receptor antibodies, BioSpace and Pharmaceutical Technology reported. Generalized myasthenia gravis is an autoimmune disease that weakens muscles. Livsayis is injected under the skin once every three months, which Benzinga and RTTNews described as the only four-times-a-year treatment approved for the disease.

The approval rests on the Phase 3 NIMBLE trial, in which patients on Livsayis improved by 4.5 points on a standard daily-function scale after 24 weeks, against 2.2 points on placebo. It is Regeneron's first approved drug of its RNA-silencing kind, developed with Alnylam, the outlets reported.

The stock barely moved: it fell 1.3% on Wednesday and was down 0.9% at $937.20 at 10:30 a.m. ET on Thursday, so the market had largely priced the approval. What isn't known yet is the price, and how quickly doctors switch patients from the more frequent infusions and shots already on the market; Regeneron's next quarterly report, early next year, is the first chance to see sales.
