Williams ($WMB), which runs the Transco gas pipeline from Texas to New York, said after the close on Monday that it earned an adjusted **$0.54 a share** in the third quarter, in line with the $0.54 consensus Newsquawk and Yahoo Finance cited; Rallies put the I/B/E/S estimate a cent higher. Adjusted EBITDA, its main profit measure, rose 6% from a year earlier to $2.04 billion, against about $2.03 billion expected, Newsquawk reported. The shares fell 4.3% to $71.19 in the regular session before the release, their biggest drop since 15 October.

## What stood out

This was the first quarter to include Momentum Midstream, the Haynesville gas-gathering business Williams closed on 3 September, and higher service revenue from new power generation, the acquisition, new projects and storage drove the growth, Rallies reported from the release. GAAP net income of $699 million included an $83 million gain on selling a Permian gathering system.

Williams also announced a 9.4 billion cubic feet expansion of its Clay Basin gas storage, and said the Plato North part of Socrates, its on-site power project for data centers in Ohio, is on track to start up by mid-November, according to its release.

## The outlook

Williams kept its 2026 adjusted EBITDA forecast at $8.3 billion to $8.5 billion and said it is tracking to the upper half, the release said. It expects growth capital spending of $7.3 billion to $7.9 billion this year, Rallies reported. Its call is at 9:30 a.m. ET on Tuesday.

## Our take

An in-line quarter from a pipeline company that rarely surprises: its shares haven't moved 5% in a session all year. The price is the question, not the quarter: Williams trades at 29.7 times forward earnings, against 12.2 for energy peers in our coverage, because investors are paying for its data-center power business. Tuesday's call, and any new power customers named on it, will matter more than these numbers. Our [preview](/news/2026-11-01/what-to-expect-from-williams-wmb-q3-earnings/) set out the bar.
