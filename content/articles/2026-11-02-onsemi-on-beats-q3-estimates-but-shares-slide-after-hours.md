Onsemi ($ON) said after the close on Monday that its third-quarter revenue **grew 11% to $1.72 billion** and adjusted earnings per share rose to $0.90, ahead of the $0.87 analysts expected, Investing.com reported; Zacks had the consensus at $0.88. The power-chip maker said revenue, gross margin and EPS all beat the midpoint of its own forecast. The shares, which had fallen 2.2% to $70.11 in the regular session, **slid 6.3%** to $65.70 after hours as of 4:30 p.m. ET; we found no clear cause for the drop in two outlets.

## What stood out

Profit grew four times faster than sales. The adjusted gross margin **rose 3.2 points** from a year ago, to 41.2%, as onsemi closes and consolidates factories, which it calls "fab right", and adjusted operating margin reached 23.2%. Chief Financial Officer Thad Trent pointed in the release to "earnings per share growing more than 40% year-over-year on 11% revenue growth."

AI is the growth line. Sales of power chips for AI data centers grew 175% from a year earlier, the release said, without giving the dollar figure. The Power Solutions group, which holds that business, grew 21%, while the automotive-heavy Analog and Mixed-Signal group grew 2%. Onsemi also agreed to buy Ferric, a maker of power-delivery chips for AI processors, and raised its 2030 AI data-center revenue target by $500 million to more than $3 billion.

## The outlook

The fourth-quarter revenue forecast's midpoint, $1.765 billion, would be 15% above a year earlier, and adjusted EPS of $0.95 to $1.07 would top this quarter's $0.90. Onsemi also cut the price of its planned Synaptics purchase in October, to $123 a share in cash, about $5.7 billion, from roughly $7 billion in stock, which it says will add to adjusted EPS straight away, Morningstar and Capital.com reported. Its call starts at 5 p.m. ET.

## Our take

The quarter was better on every line, and the margin recovery is real. But onsemi is still a weaker business than most chipmakers: over the last two years its gross margin averaged 37.8% against a 55.6% median for chipmakers in our coverage, and revenue is only now back to its late-2024 level, according to its SEC filings. At $70.11 before the report the stock was 47.6% below its June high, part of a chip selloff that has little to do with onsemi's own numbers; the call's detail on AI data-center sales in dollars is the next thing that could move it.
