This week's reports split the big companies we recapped into two kinds: those whose quarter changed the argument about them, and those whose quarter confirmed it. Nvidia ($NVDA) guided to $130 billion on Tuesday, Disney ($DIS) lifted its dividend by a third that morning, and Tyson Foods ($TSN) guided to falling sales a day earlier.

Our screen: companies worth $10 billion or more that reported in the last seven days and that we recapped. We judged each on the quarter, then on five years of growth, margins, cash and returns from their SEC filings, against their industry peers, and then on the price. Two we like and one we'd skip.

```picks
like: NVDA, DIS
skip: TSN
```

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. Nvidia ($NVDA)
**Third-quarter revenue up 103% to $115.8 billion, reported 17 November.** Nvidia designs the graphics processors and networking gear that train and run most AI models.

The quarter beat the $109.1 billion LSEG consensus by 6%, and the fourth-quarter guide of $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, came in about $5 billion above forecasts, the company and Reuters said ([our recap](/news/2026-11-17/nvidia-nvda-tops-q3-estimates-and-guides-to-130-billion-in-its-first-report-without-huang/)).

1. **Revenue grew 66.9% a year over five fiscal years**, nearly five times the median chipmaker's 14.2%, and the last two years ran faster still, at 77.4% a year.
2. **The growth is profitable:** the operating margin was 65.2% over the 12 months to July 2026, the highest tenth of the 25 chipmakers we cover and up 33.2 points over five years.
3. **The profits are cash:** free cash flow was 42.5% of revenue over two years, against a chipmaker median of 23.7%.

**The price:** 12.7 times forward earnings, against a chipmaker median of 21.8 times, and its trailing P/E of 19.7 is at the bottom of its range since August 2024. The market is pricing a business it no longer trusts to keep growing.

**What would change our mind:** a fourth-quarter result in February below the bottom of the $126.1 billion to $133.9 billion guide, or a CEO pick from outside the company that signals a change of strategy.

### 2. Walt Disney ($DIS)
**Fiscal fourth-quarter adjusted EPS of $1.77, up from $1.11, reported 17 November.** Disney runs theme parks and cruise ships, film studios, ESPN and the Disney+ and Hulu streaming services.

The quarter beat the Zacks consensus of $1.67, revenue rose 16% to $25.9 billion, streaming operating income doubled to $824 million, and the company raised its dividend 33% to $2.00 a share for fiscal 2027 ([our recap](/news/2026-11-17/disney-dis-tops-q4-estimates-and-raises-its-dividend-33/)).

1. **The operating margin reached 19.3% over the 12 months to October 2026**, better than most of the 25 media and communication stocks we cover, and 7.8 points higher than five years ago.
2. **Buybacks are shrinking the share count:** diluted shares fell 5.4% over two years, about twice the peer median's 2.7%, so each owner's slice of the profit grows faster than the profit.
3. **The guide is for more:** about 8% growth in segment operating income and double-digit adjusted EPS growth in fiscal 2027, excluding an extra week, the company said.

**The price:** 15.7 times forward earnings against a peer median of 16.1 times, and a trailing P/E of 20.8, right at its own median since November 2024. About its usual price for a business whose streaming arm has just turned into a profit engine.

**What could go wrong:** revenue grew only 5.8% a year over two years, behind most peers, and net debt of $37.8 billion, 1.9 years of operating profit, limits how far buybacks can go if parks spending slows.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Tyson Foods ($TSN)
**Fiscal fourth-quarter adjusted EPS down 47% to $0.61, reported 16 November.** Tyson processes and sells beef, chicken and pork, and makes packaged foods such as Jimmy Dean and Hillshire Farm.

It missed the $0.65 Zacks consensus, its beef unit lost $171 million on an adjusted basis, and it guided to a 3.5% to 5.5% drop in fiscal 2027 sales as cattle stay scarce ([our recap](/news/2026-11-16/tyson-foods-tsn-misses-q4-profit-estimates-as-beef-losses-deepen/)). The stock still rose 8.7% that day, by our data.

1. **Weak margins:** the operating margin was 1.9% over the 12 months to October 2026, in the bottom quarter of the 35 consumer staples stocks we cover, and 7.4 points lower than five years ago.
2. **Earnings have shrunk:** EPS fell 34.3% a year over five fiscal years, against a staples median of a 1.1% gain.
3. **Little cash, more debt:** free cash flow was 2.6% of revenue over two years, a quarter of the median, and net debt is 6.3 years of operating profit.

**The price:** a trailing P/E of 49.7, above its 31.5 median since November 2024, because profits fell faster than the stock. Its forward P/E of 19.9 is above the staples median of 18.1 for a business in the bottom quarter on every return measure.

**What would change our mind:** a beef unit back to break-even in a quarterly report, which would show the cattle cycle turning.
