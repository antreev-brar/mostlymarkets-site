## What happened

Shares of power producer Talen Energy ($TLN) **soared 8.3%** to $360.55 by 10:30 a.m. ET on Tuesday, after it gave its first forecast for 2027, pointing to profit growth of about a quarter, and announced a $1.5 billion accelerated share buyback.

Talen expects 2027 adjusted EBITDA, earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, of **$2.5 billion to $2.85 billion**, and adjusted free cash flow of $1.5 billion to $1.75 billion, the company said in its results release. At the midpoints that is growth of about 26% and 27% on this year's guidance, which it reaffirmed with an adjusted EBITDA midpoint of about $2.1 billion.

It will also buy back $1.5 billion of stock through an accelerated repurchase, finished by the end of the first quarter of 2027, and raised its total buyback authorization to $3.0 billion through 2028, according to the release and the call transcript published by Investing.com. Third-quarter adjusted EBITDA was $669 million and adjusted free cash flow $433 million.

## Why it matters

The buyback is large for a company of Talen's size: $1.5 billion is about **9%** of its $17 billion market value at this morning's price. Talen has already cut its diluted share count by 23% over two years, the most among its peers in our data, so the new program extends a record of returning its power-plant cash to shareholders.

The 2027 forecast is what makes it credible. Talen sells power from its nuclear and gas plants, a business investors have re-rated on demand from data centers, and a 26% rise in profit gives investors a number for how much the AI power trade is worth to it next year.

## What the market is telling us

Talen's shares moved 5% or more on 35 of the last 252 sessions; if today's gain holds it will be the biggest one-day rise since 6 October, when the stock jumped 12.4%. Before today it was down 11.2% this year and 24.0% below its 52-week closing high of $438.12 from 22 June.

At about 14 times forward earnings, against a peer median of nearly 19, the market had been pricing Talen's growth with some doubt. A shrinking share count and a 2027 forecast put a floor under the per-share numbers; the risk is the power prices behind them, which the next capacity auction in the mid-Atlantic grid will test.
