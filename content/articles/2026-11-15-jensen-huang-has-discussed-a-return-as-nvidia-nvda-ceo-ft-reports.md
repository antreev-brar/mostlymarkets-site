On Sunday, the Financial Times reported that Jensen Huang has discussed returning as chief executive of Nvidia ($NVDA) with people close to him, three days after he resigned, Reuters and the Mac Observer reported. One ally told the FT they were putting together a "war room" of Silicon Valley executives to push for his return and to seek White House support.

The decision belongs to Nvidia's 10 remaining directors, all independent since Huang left the board, with Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress serving as interim CEO. It pulls against Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who on Sunday urged the board to hire an outsider ([our story](/news/2026-11-15/bessent-says-nvidia-nvda-should-hire-an-outside-ceo-after-huang/)), and follows Saturday's letter from two investors asking it to keep Huang's return on the table ([our story](/news/2026-11-14/two-nvidia-nvda-investors-ask-its-board-to-keep-jensen-huangs-return-on-the-table/)). The stock closed Friday at $162.85, **31.9% below** its 6 October record.

What's next: Nvidia reports its third quarter after the close on Tuesday, 17 November, when Kress and the board will face their first public questions on the search.
