## What happened

Shares of Zebra Technologies ($ZBRA), which makes barcode scanners, label printers and rugged handheld computers for shops and warehouses, **sank 10.2%** to $324.16 by 10:30 a.m. ET on Tuesday. Its third-quarter results beat estimates, but its fourth-quarter profit forecast came in below what analysts expected and its gross margin shrank.

Sales rose **22.3%** from a year earlier to $1.61 billion and adjusted EPS was $4.95, against estimates of $1.57 billion and $4.89, the company's release and Investing.com showed. Zebra raised its full-year sales growth forecast to about 17%.

The problem was the next quarter. Zebra guided to fourth-quarter adjusted EPS of **$5.00 to $5.40**, the middle of which sits below the roughly $5.21 analysts had pencilled in, Investing.com and GuruFocus reported. Its gross margin, the share of sales left after the cost of making its products, fell to 45.7% from 48.0% a year earlier.

## Why it matters

Zebra's margins are being squeezed by the price of memory chips, which go into every handheld computer and tablet it sells. On its second-quarter call in July, management put the gross cost of higher memory prices at about $120 million and said it was raising its own prices to recover about $90 million of it, GuruFocus and Investing.com reported. The 2.3-point drop in gross margin shows prices have not yet caught up.

That makes Zebra the latest device maker to pay for the memory boom, a day after [Apple said](/news/2026-11-02/apple-aapl-beats-q4-estimates-but-memory-costs-squeeze-its-margin-forecast/) memory costs would squeeze its margin. We set out the evidence in [our story on memory becoming the device makers' bill](/news/2026-11-03/memorys-boom-has-become-the-device-makers-bill-what-the-evidence-says/).

## What the market is telling us

Zebra's shares moved 5% or more on 12 of the last 252 sessions, and if today's drop holds it will be the worst day since 28 October 2025, when the stock fell 11.7%. Before today it was up 48.7% this year and 7.6% below its 52-week closing high of $391.00 from 21 October.

At about 15.5 times forward earnings, below the 19 times median for its peers in our data, the market isn't paying for the sales growth because it can't yet see how much of it reaches profit. The fourth-quarter margin, reported early next year, is the number that decides it.
