Autodesk ($ADSK) said after the close on Tuesday that fiscal third-quarter revenue **rose 17% to $2.16 billion**, above the $2.13 billion analysts expected, and adjusted EPS of $3.20 beat the $3.07 consensus, according to MarketBeat and 24/7 Wall St. It raised the midpoint of its full-year revenue and billings outlook. The shares still fell 5.0% after hours to $243.00 as of 4:15 p.m. ET, after closing up 0.1% at $255.70.

## What stood out

Construction did the work. Revenue from architecture, engineering and construction software, Autodesk's biggest segment, **rose 19% to $1.10 billion**, and the company credited construction and emerging-market demand, the release said. That was the company's sixth straight quarter above the consensus for adjusted EPS, by 24/7 Wall St.'s count.

Two things flatter the headline. Currency moves added about three points: growth at constant currency was 14%, not 17%. And billings, the invoices that turn into revenue over the following year, grew 12%, slower than revenue, and investors read billings as a guide to revenue over the coming year.

## The outlook

Autodesk now expects full-year revenue of $8.36 billion to $8.375 billion and billings of $8.6 billion to $8.65 billion, raising both midpoints, according to Stocktitan and Securities.io. Part of that comes from buying MaintainX, a maintenance-software maker, which the release said will add about $60 million of revenue in the second half. For the fourth quarter it guided to revenue of $2.22 billion to $2.235 billion and adjusted EPS of $3.21 to $3.26.

## Our take

The quarter was solid, but no outlet we checked had confirmed a reason for the after-hours drop by the time we published. The shape of the report points to the gap between 17% revenue growth and 12% billings growth, the number software investors read as the next year's revenue. By our data Autodesk keeps 91 cents of each sales dollar after direct costs, the highest among the software peers in our data, and trades at 19.7 times forward earnings against a 23.7 median, so the bar is a modest one. Billings in the fourth quarter, the year's biggest renewal season, will settle it.

## What the market is telling us

Autodesk's shares have moved 5% or more on 14 of the last 252 sessions. The stock is down 13.6% this year, and at $255.70 it sits 17.6% below its 52-week closing high of $310.25 from 2 December 2025.
