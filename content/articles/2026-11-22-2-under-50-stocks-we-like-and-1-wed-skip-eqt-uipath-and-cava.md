The S&P 500 fell 8.6% last week and closed Friday, 20 November, in a correction, and the selling pulled plenty of cheaper stocks down with the AI names. A low share price says nothing about value on its own, so the useful question is which sub-$50 stocks are good businesses at a fair price and which are just smaller numbers.

Our screen took every stock in our coverage priced under $50 a share with positive free cash flow, ranked them by revenue growth over the latest 12 months from their SEC filings, and left out stocks we listed in the last 14 days. We then judged the leaders on margins, cash and five-year growth against their own industries, using our fundamentals data.

```picks
like: EQT, PATH
skip: CAVA
```

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. EQT ($EQT)
**Revenue growth: 18.6% in the 12 months to September 2026**. EQT is one of the largest US natural gas producers, selling gas from the Marcellus and Utica shale to utilities, power plants and exporters.

1. **It turns sales into cash like few energy companies.** Free cash flow was **40.7% of revenue** over the last 12 months, the highest of the 15 energy stocks we can measure, against an 11.9% median.
2. **It has grown through the cycle.** Revenue rose 23.1% a year over five years, against a 14.1% median for energy peers, and its operating margin of 41.8% is in the top tenth.
3. **It is getting bigger again.** On 26 October EQT agreed to buy Range Resources in an all-stock deal worth about $12.8 billion, Bloomberg and the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette reported, leaving Range holders with about 27% of the combined company. Third-quarter free cash flow fell to $283 million from $484 million a year earlier as it curtailed some output, the company's release said.

**The price:** 11.6 times trailing earnings, against a median of 20.2 times since October 2024, and 8.0 times free cash flow; the stock is 28.0% below its March high.

**What could go wrong:** share count already rose 12.4% in two years from earlier deals, and the Range purchase adds more stock, so falling gas prices would spread thinner profits over more shares.

### 2. UiPath ($PATH)
**Revenue growth: 21.5% a year over five fiscal years to January 2026**. UiPath sells software that automates repetitive office work, now including AI "agents" that handle whole workflows.

1. **It has become profitable without losing its margins.** The operating margin rose **21.7 points** over five years to 8.9%, in the top quarter of the 28 software companies we can measure, on a gross margin of 82.7%.
2. **It is shrinking its share count.** Diluted shares fell 7.9% in two years, in the top quarter of its peers, so each share owns more of a business that turns 21.1% of revenue into free cash.
3. **Growth has slowed, and the market has priced it.** Revenue grew 11.6% a year over the last two years, behind most software peers, and Cleveland Research flagged downside risk to its annual recurring revenue on 18 November, Seeking Alpha and GuruFocus reported. The stock is 43.2% below its December 2025 high.

**The price:** 12.7 times forward earnings, about half the 23.7 times median for software peers, and 15.7 times free cash flow.

**What could go wrong:** if AI models from bigger platforms do the same automation more cheaply, growth could fall further. It reports third-quarter results after the close on Thursday, 3 December, the company said.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Cava Group ($CAVA)
**Revenue growth: 27.0% in the 12 months to October 2026**. Cava runs about 500 Mediterranean fast-casual restaurants in the US.

1. **The growth is real, the profit is thin.** Revenue grew 25.5% a year over two years, in the top tenth of consumer stocks, but the operating margin was **4.9%** over 12 months, in the bottom quarter.
2. **Little of it becomes cash.** Free cash flow was 2.8% of revenue over 12 months, against a 9.2% median for its peers, because new restaurants soak up the money.
3. **The latest quarter slipped.** Same-restaurant sales rose 3.1% in the third quarter against about 3.6% expected, and earnings of 11 cents a share missed by a cent, Finviz and Nation's Restaurant News reported; management blamed about 1.5 points on a Cyclospora scare tied to lettuce.

**The price:** 77.8 times forward earnings, four times the 19.5 times median for consumer peers, even after a 49.9% fall from its April high.

**What would change our mind:** an operating margin heading toward 10% while restaurant openings stay near 20% a year, which would show the new stores can pay for themselves. Its fourth-quarter report early next year is the next check.
