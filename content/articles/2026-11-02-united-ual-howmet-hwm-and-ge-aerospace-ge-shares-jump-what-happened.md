## What happened

Shares of United Airlines ($UAL) **jumped 6.1%** to $110.61 as of 10:15 a.m. ET on Monday, aircraft-parts maker Howmet Aerospace ($HWM) rose 6.1%, and jet-engine maker GE Aerospace ($GE) gained 4.2%, as the price of oil fell hard. US crude was down 6.8% at $97.01 a barrel at 10:05 a.m. ET, after Iranian and Qatari officials held a third straight day of talks on ending the war with Iran, CNBC, the Wall Street Journal and Arab News reported.

None of the three companies put out news of its own. The move split along one line, oil: Delta ($DAL) rose 3.7%, Southwest ($LUV) 3.9%, and cruise operator Royal Caribbean ($RCL) 4.6%, while Exxon Mobil ($XOM) fell 1.9% and ConocoPhillips ($COP) 2.4%, by 10:15 a.m.

## Why it matters

Jet fuel is one of an airline's two biggest costs, alongside labor, so a lower oil price feeds almost straight into profit. Airline stocks have traded as the mirror image of crude all year: they rose 3% to 7% in a day on 24 June, when oil fell back to its pre-war level, Reuters reported via Investing.com and the Globe and Mail. GE Aerospace and Howmet are paid by how much the world's planes fly, through spare parts and engine servicing, so cheaper fares and more flights reach them too.

The catch is that a ceasefire hasn't been agreed, and the same headline risk runs both ways. Goldman Sachs raised its oil forecasts over the weekend, saying supply disruption risks remain high, as our [morning Daily](/daily/2026-11-02/) reported, and our [narrative on $100 oil](/news/2026-10-31/the-story-wall-street-is-telling-about-100-oil-and-the-fed/) sets out what the war has done to prices.

## What the market is telling us

United's shares moved 5% or more on 24 of the last 252 sessions, so swings this size are common for it. Through Friday the stock was down 6.8% this year and 23.4% below its 52-week closing high of $136.11 from 26 June, about when oil last fell back toward pre-war levels.

We read Monday's jump as a bet on diplomacy rather than on the airlines' own numbers. If US crude holds below $100 through the week, the fuel-cost relief starts to show up in fourth-quarter estimates; if the talks stall, these gains are the first to go.
