Walmart ($WMT) reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of **$188.8 billion** on Thursday morning, up 5.2% year on year and above the $187.27 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC. Adjusted earnings came to 65 cents a share, a penny above the LSEG consensus and above the top of the 62 to 64 cents Walmart had guided in August. The shares still **fell 2.0% to $124.80** in premarket trading as of 9:14 a.m. ET.

## What stood out

The US business did the heavy lifting. Walmart U.S. net sales reached **$125 billion**, up 3.6%, and comparable sales excluding fuel rose 2.9%, a step up from the 2.6% that [knocked the stock 9.2% in August](/news/2026-11-18/what-to-expect-from-walmarts-wmt-q3-earnings/), CNBC reported. Global e-commerce sales grew 17%, with digital sales and advertising again the fastest-growing lines, according to CNBC and FashionUnited.

"We delivered strong results, growing our core business and gaining market share," Chief Executive John Furner said in the release. The gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings came from a net loss on equity investments, which Walmart strips out of its adjusted figure.

## The outlook

This was the third time this year Walmart raised its full-year forecast, the Associated Press reported. It now expects adjusted earnings of **$2.84 to $2.89** a share and adjusted operating income growth of 8.0% to 8.6%, up from 7.0% to 8.5%.

The sales guide is where the premarket selling found its reason. Net sales growth of 4.9% to 5.2% beats Walmart's own old range but sits below the 5.5% analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC and Quartz. Chief Financial Officer John David Rainey also warned on the call that surging diesel and transportation costs are starting to feed into the cost of goods, Investing.com and ESM Magazine reported.

## Our take

A clean quarter: revenue, earnings and comparable sales all met or beat, and e-commerce kept compounding. The problem is the price. At 42.5 times forward earnings against a consumer-staples median of 18 times, and up 14.3% this year, the stock needed the sales outlook to beat the Street as well, and it didn't. Target ($TGT) [beat on a 5% jump in traffic](/news/2026-11-18/target-tgt-beats-q3-estimates-as-traffic-jumps-5-and-raises-its-forecast/) a day earlier, so the US shopper isn't the worry. Watch the holiday-quarter comparable sales and the gross margin in February for how much fuel costs bite.
