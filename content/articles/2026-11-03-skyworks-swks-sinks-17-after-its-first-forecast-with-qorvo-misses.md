Skyworks Solutions ($SWKS) forecast sales of **$1.80 billion to $1.90 billion** for its December quarter after the close on Tuesday, its first outlook since combining with rival Qorvo, short of the $1.99 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters and CNBC. The shares were down 17.4% at $70.58 by 7:59 p.m. ET, after closing up 0.3% at $85.40.

## What stood out

The quarter itself wasn't the problem. Revenue of **$1.05 billion** and adjusted EPS of $1.28 were roughly what Wall Street expected, Reuters and Chartmill reported, though sales were still about 5% below a year earlier. Skyworks makes the radio-frequency chips that let phones and other devices connect to networks.

## The outlook

The forecast is the first that includes Qorvo, and it shows the combined company starting smaller than analysts had modeled. The $1.85 billion midpoint is made of about $1.07 billion from legacy Skyworks and about $780 million from legacy Qorvo, the company said, as reported by Investing.com and StockTitan. Reuters tied the shortfall to a sluggish smartphone market, the same market where dearer memory chips are squeezing phone makers this year ([our story on memory's bill](/news/2026-11-03/memorys-boom-has-become-the-device-makers-bill-what-the-evidence-says/)).

Skyworks is targeting **$500 million or more** of annual cost savings from the deal, but expects only about $150 million of them in fiscal 2027, according to the release and Chartmill.

## How the stock got here

Before the report, Skyworks closed at $85.40, 6.6% below its 52-week closing high of $91.45 from 23 September, and up 34.7% this year in our data. The longer record is weaker: $1,000 invested five years ago would be worth $492 today. An after-hours drop of 17.4% would, if it holds on Wednesday, cut this year's gain to about 11%.

## Our take

The merger was meant to give two phone-chip suppliers scale, and the first combined forecast says the market they share is still shrinking: a 7% miss on revenue and about 17% on EPS against LSEG estimates. The stock had risen 34.7% this year before Tuesday's report, so the savings now have to arrive faster than the $150 million planned for fiscal 2027. The next test is the March-quarter outlook, due with the December results.
