Stocks fell for a third straight session on Monday as Nvidia ($NVDA) led AI stocks lower a day before its first results since Jensen Huang quit as chief executive, CNBC and the AP reported. The Nasdaq Composite **fell 2.5%** to 24,509.76, the S&P 500 lost 1.9% to 7,416.59 and the Dow dropped 739 points, or 1.4%. Nvidia closed down 4.1% at $156.14, its lowest close in a year.

## Nvidia's week starts at a one-year low

Nvidia has now lost **34.7% from its 6 October high close**, and 27.1% in the past month alone, by our price data. On Tuesday after the close, interim Chief Executive Colette Kress takes Wall Street's questions for the first time, AP reported. The pressure on Monday came from three sides: a Commerce Department rule to stop Chinese firms renting advanced chips in overseas data centers ([our story](/news/2026-11-16/us-moves-to-cut-off-chinas-remote-access-to-nvidia-nvda-chips-abroad/)), the unsettled hunt for a new chief, which President Donald Trump stirred by saying he [would not mind Huang's return](/news/2026-11-16/trump-says-he-wouldnt-mind-jensen-huang-returning-as-nvidia-nvda-ceo/), and a lower forecast for AI chip spending.

That forecast did the most damage below Nvidia. Research firm SemiAnalysis cut its estimate of 2028 spending on AI accelerators by 28%, and the companies that supply the chips' memory, storage, tools and cables fell hardest: Micron ($MU) **dropped 7.6%**, Sandisk ($SNDK) 8.6% and Taiwan Semiconductor ($TSM) 6.3%. We covered the [storage makers](/news/2026-11-16/sandisk-seagate-and-western-digital-shares-slide-what-happened/), the [chip-equipment makers](/news/2026-11-16/teradyne-lam-research-and-asml-shares-slide-what-happened/), the [optical networking stocks](/news/2026-11-16/lumentum-coherent-and-arista-shares-slide-what-happened/) and [Dell](/news/2026-11-16/why-dell-technologies-dell-stock-is-falling-today/), which closed down 7.0%.

The fear gauge moved with them: the VIX, which measures the options market's expected swings in the S&P 500, **rose 18.7%** to 22.91. For the argument over whether this is a pause in AI spending or the start of a cut, see our [bulls-vs-bears piece](/news/2026-11-16/bulls-vs-bears-on-ai-spending-semianalysis-cut-28-morgan-stanley-says-the-cycle-is-intact/) and today's [big story](/news/2026-11-16/wall-street-is-betting-ais-1-trillion-money-loop-breaks-at-the-neoclouds-nvidias-report-will-test-it/).

## Anthropic's listing is the week's second test

Anthropic plans to price its offering on Wednesday and start trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker ANT, the company and Yahoo Finance said. It is selling 825 million shares at **$70 to $78** each, which at the top of the range would raise about $64 billion and value it at about $1.4 trillion, CNN reported. Orders have reached close to seven times the shares on offer, Bloomberg reported on Sunday, as cited by Yahoo Finance.

That matters for the stocks that fell on Monday: a $64 billion sale draws money that fund managers must find somewhere, and a weak debut would mark down every private AI valuation behind it. Nvidia reports the day before it prices.

## What rose: meat, medicine and utilities

The gainers were businesses with little to do with AI. Tyson Foods ($TSN) **jumped 8.7%**, its biggest gain since February 2022, even though its adjusted earnings missed estimates, as investors looked past the quarter to its fiscal 2027 outlook, Investing.com and Finwire reported ([our recap](/news/2026-11-16/tyson-foods-tsn-misses-q4-profit-estimates-as-beef-losses-deepen/)). Health insurers Centene ($CNC) and Elevance Health ($ELV) rose 4.9% and 3.9%, and utilities PG&E ($PCG) and Edison International ($EIX) about 3%; we found no single news cause for those gains.

Oil fell 3.5% to $85.46 a barrel for WTI as Middle East exports recovered, CNBC reported, and the WSJ pointed to expectations of a global surplus. Cheaper oil helped the 10-year Treasury yield **ease 0.02 percentage point** to 5.13%, Reuters reported. Gold fell to its lowest in more than a year as the dollar rose, Reuters and the WSJ reported.
