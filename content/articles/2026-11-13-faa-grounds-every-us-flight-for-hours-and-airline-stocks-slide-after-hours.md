The Federal Aviation Administration halted every departure from US airports on Friday afternoon and lifted the nationwide ground stop at **8:15 p.m. ET**, hours later, Fox Business, Axios and Flightradar24 reported. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed a communications problem affecting all of the Air Route Traffic Control Centers, the facilities that guide planes between airports, according to Fox News and NBC News.

Airline stocks took it in after-hours trading: Delta Air Lines ($DAL) **fell 2.5%** to $84.14 and United Airlines ($UAL) 1.9% to $107.20 by 7:59 p.m. ET, while Southwest ($LUV) slipped 1.3%. A lost Friday evening means cancelled fares, rebooked passengers and crews out of position heading into a weekend, and Duffy said delays and cancellations would continue. Microsoft said there was no indication the stop was related to any of its services, Reuters and the Deccan Herald reported, a day after its own [Outlook outage](/news/2026-11-13/microsoft-msft-slips-2-before-the-bell-as-its-outlook-outage-enters-a-second-day/).

The stop lands two weeks before the Thanksgiving travel rush, one of the busiest travel weeks of the year, so a repeat then would cost the airlines more than Friday did. What isn't known yet is what set off the failure and how many flights were cancelled; the weekend's schedules and Monday's open will show the cost.
