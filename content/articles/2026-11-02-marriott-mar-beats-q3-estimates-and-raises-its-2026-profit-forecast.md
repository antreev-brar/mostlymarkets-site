Marriott International ($MAR) said before the open on Monday that third-quarter adjusted earnings rose to **$3.02 a share** from $2.47 a year ago, beating the $2.84 consensus cited by MarketBeat and clearing the top of its own $2.74-$2.82 forecast. Revenue rose 8% to $7.01 billion. The shares rose 2.7% to $410.12 in pre-market trading as of 7:13 a.m. ET, above their record close of $404.00 from 21 October.

## What stood out

The beat came from the fee business, which is what Marriott really sells: it owns few hotels and collects fees from the owners who fly its brands. Gross fee revenues **rose 14%**, the release said, as franchise fees grew 15% on higher co-branded credit card fees, higher RevPAR (revenue per available room, the industry's key sales measure) and more rooms. Incentive fees, paid when managed hotels beat profit hurdles, rose 22%, with "all regions" up.

The US carried the quarter. RevPAR in the US and Canada **rose 7.4%**, roughly in line with Hilton's 7% and below Hyatt's 8.2%, according to their releases, so Marriott kept pace with the World Cup lift its rivals reported. Abroad, RevPAR in Asia-Pacific excluding China rose over 9%, and Chief Executive Anthony Capuano said that while the Middle East conflict "continued to impact results in that region, trends improved meaningfully from the second quarter."

Net rooms grew 4.6% from a year earlier, and the development pipeline hit a record of more than 634,000 rooms. Marriott bought back $1.3 billion of stock in the quarter, but total debt rose to $16.8 billion from $16.2 billion at the end of 2025.

## The outlook

Marriott now expects 2026 adjusted EPS of **$12.07 to $12.14**, up from $11.64 to $11.81 in August, and fourth-quarter adjusted EPS of $3.14 to $3.21. It sees fourth-quarter RevPAR growth of 4.5% to 5%, a slowdown from the third quarter's 6.5% as the World Cup comparison falls away, and net rooms growth at the low end of its 4.5% to 5% range. It plans to return at least $4.8 billion to shareholders this year. The call starts at 8 a.m. ET.

## Our take

A clean quarter: the US held up against its strongest rivals, the Middle East drag is shrinking, and the forecast rose by more than the third-quarter beat alone. The catch is the price. At 33.5 times forward earnings against a median of 19.5 times for consumer stocks in our coverage, and 41.4 times trailing earnings against its own median of 30.9 times since mid-2024, the stock already assumed a raise. The number to watch is fourth-quarter US RevPAR, the first quarter without the World Cup; Marriott's 4.5% to 5% forecast for worldwide growth sets the bar.
