Alibaba () shareholders filed a proposed class action in Manhattan federal court on Thursday accusing the company and Chief Executive Eddie Wu of misleading investors about its ties to Megaspeed, a Singapore firm suspected of helping Chinese buyers reach restricted Nvidia () chips, Reuters and Benzinga reported. The suit says Alibaba's 2024, 2025 and 2026 annual reports understated the risk that export-control violations could hurt its business, and it seeks unspecified damages.

The complaint ties the claim to a **14% fall** in Alibaba's shares over four days last week, after reports that Alibaba had become Megaspeed's dominant customer. It adds a legal front to a stock already down 43.2% this year at Thursday's close of $83.31, and to the export-control squeeze that [halted some TSMC and Samsung shipments](/news/2026-11-19/tsmc-tsm-and-samsung-halt-some-chip-shipments-to-chinese-customers-under-new-us-curbs/) to Chinese customers this week. Nvidia faces separate shareholder suits over the same scandal.

The claims are allegations, and no response from Alibaba was reported. The shares were up 0.8% at $84.00 at 6:15 a.m. ET.
