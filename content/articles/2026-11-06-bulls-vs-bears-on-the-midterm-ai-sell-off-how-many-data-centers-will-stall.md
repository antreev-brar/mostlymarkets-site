Two days after Democrats won Congress and the Texas governor's race, Wall Street is split on one question: how much of America's planned AI data-center building will the new state rules actually stop? On Friday, Bernstein told clients the hit to credible capacity is just 2% to 4% and the sell-off is overblown, Benzinga and Investing.com reported. UBS analysts put about 30% of the capacity due to be added in 2028 at risk.

The difference is worth a lot of money. Chip and power stocks bounced on Friday, but since Election Day's close on Tuesday, cooling-equipment maker Vertiv ($VRT) is still **down 22.5%** and Micron ($MU) 19.3% in our prices.

- **What's happening:** Estimates of how much data-center building the midterms put at risk range from 2% to 4% of capacity (Bernstein) to 30% of 2028 additions (UBS), and AI suppliers such as Vertiv and Micron have lost about a fifth of their value on the gap.
- **Why it matters:** Chipmakers, power-equipment makers and memory makers are priced on 2027 and 2028 orders that need these buildings to exist.

## The bull case

### Madison Rezaei, analyst, Bernstein

**The capacity really at risk is small, and most of it still gets built.** In a note on 6 November, reported by Benzinga and Investing.com, Rezaei's team estimated that the new state-level risk cuts credible US data-center capacity by 2% to 4% nationally, even counting tougher politics in Texas, Georgia and Ohio. Their reasoning: developers may have to pay more toward the grid and to local communities, but "a lot of these GWs still get built." Bernstein also pointed out that Texas permits were already delayed under the outgoing governor, so some of the slowdown was known before Tuesday.

### Mark Haefele, chief investment officer, UBS Global Wealth Management

**This was a positioning reset, not the end of the AI spending cycle.** In UBS's daily note on 6 November, also reported by Morningstar, Haefele said the chip index's roughly 12% fall on Thursday looked like investors cutting crowded bets rather than a change in the businesses. He set out what would change his mind: evidence that AI revenue growth is slowing, that cloud capacity is going unused, or that cheaper AI is not drawing enough new use.

## The bear case

### UBS data-center analysts

**A pause in a few big states would hit the 2028 pipeline hard.** UBS's analysts estimated that construction pauses could put about 30% of the capacity expected to be added in 2028 at risk, with Texas alone accounting for 18 gigawatts, Finimize and the Northeast Times reported. That is the same bank as Haefele, looking at a different question: not whether demand exists, but whether the buildings can be finished on time.

### Citi Research

**Local opposition was growing fast before the vote, and the vote confirmed it.** Citi's tracker of US data-center bans and pauses went from near zero in mid-2025 to more than 500 by October 2026, and Citi called the trend "exponential," Benzinga and KuCoin News reported on Thursday. Voters then sided against data centers on 40 of the 41 local ballot measures on Tuesday, The Hill and The Next Web reported. Citi still expects the centers to be built, but said where, how and how fast may change.

## Where they agree

Nobody quoted here expects the buildings to be cancelled outright; the argument is about delay and cost. Goldman Sachs, whose economists estimate that states likely to tighten rules hold about 45% of planned US capacity, also expects those pauses to be short-lived, Investinglive and CoinCentral reported. The three big numbers measure different things: Bernstein's is lost capacity, UBS's is 2028 additions at risk of delay, and Goldman's is the pipeline sitting in exposed states.

## The number that decides it

The test is whether spending planned for 2027 slips into 2028 or disappears. The first hard evidence will come from the companies building the centers: Nvidia reports on 17 November, and any change in its data-center outlook, or in the 2027 capital-spending plans of Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon, will show whether orders are moving or vanishing. In Texas, the governor-elect takes office in January, and her first rules will show whether a pause lasts months or years.

## Our read

The evidence favors the bulls on whether the buildings get built and the bears on when. Bernstein's 2% to 4% and Goldman's "short-lived" both assume Texas writes rules within a year, and that is the weakest link: a pause that runs into 2028 turns UBS's 30% into real lost orders for that year. With Vertiv down 22.5% and Micron 19.3% in three sessions, the stocks are pricing something closer to the bear case, so Nvidia's outlook on 17 November is the number we'll watch.
