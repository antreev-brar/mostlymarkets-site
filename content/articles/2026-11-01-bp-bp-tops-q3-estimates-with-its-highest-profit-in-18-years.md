BP ($BP) reported third-quarter underlying replacement cost profit, its headline measure, of **$8.46 billion** on Friday, up from $2.2 billion a year earlier and above the $7.33 billion analysts expected in a company-compiled poll, RTÉ and Yahoo Finance reported. It was BP's highest quarterly profit in 18 years, Rigzone and The National reported. The company's US-listed shares edged up 0.1% to $50.39 at the close.

## What stood out

**Refining and trading made the quarter.** BP's results statement credited higher refining margins and a stronger gas marketing and trading result, and customers and products, the division that runs its refineries and fuel stations, earned $6.9 billion. Coverage tied the windfall to the war in the Middle East, which has pushed up oil prices and the price of turning crude into fuel.

*Verdict: a war-driven quarter, like [Exxon's](/news/2026-11-01/exxon-mobil-xom-tops-q3-estimates-as-refining-profit-more-than-doubles/) and [Chevron's](/news/2026-10-30/chevron-cvx-tops-q3-estimates-as-refining-profit-jumps/), but BP is spending it differently.*

**The cash went to debt, not buybacks.** Net debt fell by more than $8 billion to $13.9 billion, which BP said puts it a year ahead of its target. Reuters reported BP will use the windfall to settle its remaining Deepwater Horizon liabilities, from the 2010 Gulf of Mexico spill, early.

**Green assets took the hit.** Reported profit of $4.7 billion was far below the underlying figure because of about $4 billion of impairments, mainly on Lightsource bp, its solar arm, and offshore wind, according to the call coverage.

## The outlook

BP gave no profit forecast, and no buyback. It suspended repurchases in February, and Chief Executive Meg O'Neill said they are still not the priority for now, Reuters and AFP reported, even as Shell and TotalEnergies raised theirs that week. O'Neill described the results as strong, helped by higher prices and margins, and she will set out her longer-term strategy in December.

## Our take

We were impressed by the cash: an $8 billion cut in net debt in one quarter repairs a balance sheet that had $21.4 billion of net debt at the end of 2025. On the other hand, the profit rests on war-tight fuel markets, and the shares barely moved because BP is the one big oil company not handing the windfall back through buybacks. Overall, a strong quarter that buys O'Neill room rather than rewarding shareholders now.

The ADRs trade at 6.9 times forward earnings, about half the 12.8 times median of our energy group, a discount that leaves room for a rerating if the cash keeps coming. The December strategy update is the next test: a buyback restart there would close part of that gap.
