Vivmark Residential ($VMRK), the apartment landlord formed when AvalonBay and Equity Residential merged on 17 August, said after the close on Wednesday that core funds from operations, the cash-earnings measure real estate trusts are judged on, came to **$1.03 a share** in the third quarter. That beat the 97-cent average of five analysts surveyed by Zacks Investment Research, AP reported, and compares with $0.98 a year earlier. Revenue was $1.19 billion, and the stock rose 2.4% on Thursday to close at $65.25, on a day the S&P 500 fell 4.4%.

## What stood out

**Core FFO beat by six cents.** It strips out one-offs such as merger costs. Even so, management said on the call that it absorbed about $8 million of extra interest expense from merger accounting. A 6.2% beat is large for a landlord whose rents reset once a year.
*Verdict: a clean beat on the measure that counts.*

**The GAAP loss is the merger, not the buildings.** Vivmark posted a net loss of **$1.27 a share**, AP and the release said, of which $0.92 was merger costs. That is why Investing.com scored it a miss against a 38-cent GAAP forecast. The accounting also blurs the comparisons. Equity Residential was the legal buyer and AvalonBay the accounting buyer, so every figure before 17 August is AvalonBay's alone. The $1.19 billion of revenue therefore can't be read against last year, and revenue forecasts from $796 million to $1.33 billion show analysts' models had not caught up.
*Verdict: noise. The per-share cash measure is the only clean read this quarter.*

**The debate: will the merger pay?** The question over this deal is whether two big landlords can save enough to justify combining. Vivmark lifted its net run-rate synergy target, the yearly savings once the integration is done, **to $135 million** from $125 million. Gross savings rose to $185 million from $175 million, it said on Thursday's call. Total merger costs are now expected at about $725 million, $25 million below the first estimate, even though that estimate left out integration costs. Chief Executive Benjamin Schall said results in the first quarter as Vivmark were "ahead of plan". On the call, he said 2026 would carry "a penny or two" of negative synergies, with savings ramping in 2027.
*Verdict: the first scorecard says the savings case is getting stronger, but most of it is still to be earned.*

## The outlook

Vivmark guided fourth-quarter core FFO to **$1.01 to $1.05 a share**, a midpoint level with the third quarter. It raised its 2026 same-store revenue growth forecast to 2.2% from the 2.0% it gave in early September. Same-store revenue is rent from buildings owned in both years. On the call, management put the growth already built into 2027 from existing leases at about 1%, with physical occupancy near 96% for the merged portfolio.

## Our take

We were impressed by the beat and by a synergy target that rose while the cost estimate fell. On the other hand, same-store growth of 2.2% is slow, and only about 1% of next year's growth is already locked in. So 2027's growth leans on the savings. Before the merger, Equity Residential's revenue grew 3.8% a year over five fiscal years, our analysis of its SEC filings shows. That is less than half the 9.0% median for the 30 real estate stocks we cover.

The market liked it. The stock rose **2.4% on Thursday** on 1.9 times its 20-day average volume, the morning after the release, while the Nasdaq [sank 5.5% into a correction](/news/2026-11-05/stock-market-today-nasdaq-sinks-5-5-into-a-correction-on-midterm-ai-fears/). No outlet we found tied the gain to one line of the report. As of 2:15 p.m. ET Friday it was down 0.2% at $65.12, up 10.1% over the past month and 7.0% below its 52-week closing high of $70.15 from 7 July.
