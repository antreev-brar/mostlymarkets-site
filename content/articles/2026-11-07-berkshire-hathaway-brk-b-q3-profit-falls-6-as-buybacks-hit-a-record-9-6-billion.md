Berkshire Hathaway ($BRK.B) said on Saturday morning that its operating earnings fell **5.8%** in the third quarter to $12.71 billion, from $13.49 billion a year earlier, as profit from insurance underwriting dropped, CNBC and Bloomberg reported. Revenue rose 11% to $105.65 billion, Reuters reported. The bigger news was the buyback: Berkshire spent **$9.6 billion** on its own stock, its most in any quarter, more than double the $4.5 billion of the second quarter. The shares don't trade until Monday; they closed Friday at $570.89, 1.4% below their record close.

No widely cited consensus estimate for Berkshire's operating earnings appeared in the coverage; few analysts follow the company, so we judge the quarter against last year.

## What stood out

### Insurance carried the decline
Underwriting profit, what Berkshire's insurers keep after paying claims and costs, fell to about **$1.35 billion** after tax from about $2.37 billion a year earlier, according to 24/7 Wall St. and CNBC. A year ago it had more than tripled on light catastrophe losses, so the comparison was hard. Investment income at the insurers also fell, Reuters and Bloomberg reported, so both halves of the insurance business earned less.

*Verdict: weak, and the one part of the business that shrank.*

### The railroad and the factories grew
BNSF, Berkshire's railroad, earned **$1.73 billion** after tax, up 19.3%, on higher volumes and better efficiency, according to 24/7 Wall St. and a Futu News summary of the release. CNBC reported that the energy and manufacturing businesses also earned more than a year ago.

*Verdict: solid. The operating businesses outside insurance are growing.*

### The one debate: is the stock still cheap enough to buy?
Under Warren Buffett, Berkshire bought back no stock at all in the third quarter of 2025, Barron's reported. Chief Executive Greg Abel, who took over in January, wrote in this year's letter that Berkshire buys shares when they trade below its estimate of intrinsic value, "conservatively determined." The $9.6 billion beat the roughly $9.3 billion record from the third quarter of 2020, Benzinga reported at the time, and Berkshire kept going: it bought back the equivalent of about 2,554 Class A shares from 1 October to 28 October, Reuters reported, worth about $2 billion.

That makes buybacks Abel's clearest statement yet. Our data puts the stock at about 1.6 times book value as of June, and Berkshire was still buying in October with the shares near a record.

## The outlook

Berkshire gives no forecast. The cash pile still grew by about $12 billion in the quarter despite the buybacks, so the bigger question, a large acquisition, is still open. Its next filing of stock holdings, the 13F, is due on 16 November.

## Our take

A weaker insurance quarter against a very strong one last year, with the railroad, utilities and factories growing, is a sound quarter for a conglomerate. The buyback is what changes the story: a management that buys at about 1.6 times book is telling shareholders it thinks the stock is worth more, and the stock is up **11.7%** in the past month and 13.6% this year. What to watch next quarter is whether insurance underwriting steadies and whether the buyback pace holds into the fourth quarter.
