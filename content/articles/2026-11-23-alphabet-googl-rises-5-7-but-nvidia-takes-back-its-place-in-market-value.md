## What happened
Shares of Google parent Alphabet ($GOOGL) **rose 5.7%** to $280.43 by 10:15 a.m. ET on Monday, as tech stocks rebounded after Nvidia ($NVDA) reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, Bloomberg and CNN reported ([our story](/news/2026-11-23/nvidia-nvda-shares-soar-29-before-the-open-after-it-reinstates-jensen-huang/)). Alphabet had no news of its own.

The twist is the ranking. On Friday, as Nvidia fell 14%, Alphabet passed it in market value for the first time in more than two years, MarketWatch and IBTimes reported, making Alphabet the third most valuable US company behind Apple ($AAPL) and Microsoft ($MSFT). Monday's gains reverse that: on our quotes Nvidia is worth about $3.84 trillion, ahead of both Microsoft and Alphabet.

## Why it matters
Alphabet was the safe place in last week's AI sell-off, a company that sells AI through search, cloud and its own chips rather than one that depends on others' spending, and CNBC called it a tech bright spot on Friday. On a day when investors buy back the hardest-hit AI names, that safety counts for less, which is why it lagged Nvidia's 26.8% gain.

## What the market is telling us
Alphabet's shares moved 5% or more on only 6 of the last 252 sessions, so Monday is a big day for it even as it trails the market's leaders. It closed Friday down 15.2% this year, at $265.29, 34.1% below its 13 May closing high of $402.62. We read the ranking swap as a measure of mood, not of the businesses: the gap between the three is small enough that one more week like the last two would reorder it again.
