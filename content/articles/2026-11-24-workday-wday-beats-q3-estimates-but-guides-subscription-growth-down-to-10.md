Workday ($WDAY) said after the close on Tuesday that adjusted EPS for its fiscal third quarter **rose to $2.82**, beating the $2.74 analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters and TheFly. Revenue rose 11.2% to $2.705 billion, just above the $2.69 billion consensus, and it raised its full-year subscription forecast. But it guided to subscription growth of 10% next quarter, down from 12.8%, and the shares fell 4.9% after hours to $170.51 as of 4:15 p.m. ET.

## What stood out

Profit grew far faster than sales. Workday's adjusted operating margin **rose to 31.4%** from 28.5% a year earlier, and it spent $750 million buying back about 3.8 million shares. That is why EPS rose 22% while revenue grew 11%.

The AI products are the bright spot. "Our AI products drove nearly $150 million in new ACV, more than three times what we delivered a year ago," Chief Executive Aneel Bhusri said in the release, using the industry term for new annual contract value. More than 2,200 core customers have bought at least one AI product, up nearly 25% from the previous quarter.

## The outlook

The forecast for the fourth quarter is where the story turns. Subscription revenue of $2.600 billion would be **10% growth**, down from 12.8% in the quarter just reported, and total subscription backlog rose only 6.1% from a year earlier. Benzinga and Investing.com both reported the stock sliding after the release despite the beat. The 12-month backlog, which grew 12.3%, argues the slowdown may be gentler than the fourth-quarter guide suggests.

## Our take

This is a company trading growth for profit: margins up three points, buybacks large, and growth heading toward 10%. The market has priced much of that in already: by our data Workday trades at 14.9 times forward earnings, against 23.7 times for software peers. What to watch next quarter is whether $150 million of AI contract value grows fast enough to lift subscription growth back above 10%.
