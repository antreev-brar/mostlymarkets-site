On Tuesday, Vertex Pharmaceuticals **rose 4.4%** to $562.42 as of 2:15 p.m. ET, within 1.1% of its 52-week high, while Salesforce rose 4.1% and still sits about 28% below its own high. Both keep more than three-quarters of each sales dollar after direct costs. The market prices that margin very differently, and that is where this list splits.

We screened our coverage on gross margin over the latest 12 months reported in SEC filings, leaving out stocks from our lists of the past 14 days, then judged the leaders on operating margin, cash, per-share growth and forward P/E against their industry peers (`analyse.py`).

## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. Vertex Pharmaceuticals ($VRTX)
**Gross margin: 85.5% in the 12 months to September 2026**, in the top tenth of the 18 drugmakers we cover. Vertex sells the cystic fibrosis drugs Trikafta and Alyftrek and is building a pain business around Journavx.

1. **It is a strong business.** Operating margin was 33.2% over the past 12 months, the best of its peers with a full year of data, and revenue grew 14.1% a year over five years against a peer median of 8.1%.
2. **Per-share earnings lag the sales.** EPS grew 8.3% a year over five years, below the 13.4% peer median, and operating margin fell 11.3 points over the period, partly on charges for the companies it bought. Expenses tied to the Crinetics acquisition cut third-quarter GAAP net income to $654 million from $1.1 billion, the release and Zacks showed.
3. **The core franchise is shifting, not growing fast.** Third-quarter revenue rose 12% to $3.44 billion as Alyftrek sales reached $705.8 million and Trikafta sales fell about 8%, Zacks reported.

**The price:** 29.9 times forward earnings against a drugmaker median of 16.9, and 44.5 times free cash flow, with the stock at its high.

**What would change our mind:** the FDA's decision on povetacicept for the kidney disease IgAN, due 30 November, and the early-2027 data that follow.

### 2. Edwards Lifesciences ($EW)
**Gross margin: 78.0% in the 12 months to September 2026**, the highest of the 18 medical-device makers we cover. Edwards makes heart valves fitted by catheter (TAVR) and valve-repair devices.

1. **The margin hasn't widened.** Operating margin was 21.6% over the past 12 months, above the device median, but it rose just 0.4 points over five years, in the bottom quarter of its peers.
2. **Per-share compounding is weak.** EPS grew 7.1% a year over five years, in the bottom quarter, against a 9.5% median.
3. **Its biggest product is slowing to market pace.** Third-quarter TAVR sales rose 8% to $1.24 billion, and management said future growth would come mainly from more procedures rather than share gains, MedTech Dive and Finviz reported, though it raised its 2026 forecast for the third time.

**The price:** 32.6 times forward earnings against a device median of 18.6, within 2.0% of its 52-week high.

**What would change our mind:** TAVR growth back above 10% for a full quarter, or an operating margin that climbs for a full year.

### 3. Estée Lauder ($EL)
**Gross margin: 76.0% in the 12 months to September 2026**, the highest of the 31 consumer-staples companies we cover. Estée Lauder sells Estée Lauder, Clinique and MAC cosmetics and fragrances.

1. **Little of that margin survives.** Operating margin was 6.7% over the past 12 months, against a staples median of 13.6%, and it fell 11.0 points over five years, in the bottom quarter.
2. **Per-share earnings have shrunk for five years.** EPS fell 42.3% a year over five fiscal years, the worst of its group, and diluted shares rose 1.8% in two years.
3. **The turnaround is real but early.** First-quarter organic sales rose 6% and the adjusted operating margin widened to 12.1% from 7.3%, helped by restocking at Chinese airport duty-free shops ([our recap](/news/2026-11-02/estee-lauder-el-beats-q1-estimates-and-raises-its-outlook-but-shares-turn-lower/)).

**The price:** 30.2 times forward earnings against a staples median of 17.9, which already assumes the recovery runs on.

**What would change our mind:** a GAAP operating margin back above the 13.6% staples median for a full year.

## 1 stock we like

### Salesforce ($CRM)
**Free cash flow margin: 34.5% in the 12 months to July 2026**, in the top quarter of the software companies we cover, from a gross margin of 77.3%. Salesforce sells customer-relationship software and Agentforce AI agents.

1. **The margin reaches cash.** Operating margin rose 17.9 points over five years against a software median of 6.3, and free cash flow of $15.2 billion in the past 12 months is above its 33.1% two-year average.
2. **It returns that cash.** Diluted shares fell 15.6% in two years, the biggest cut among the software companies we cover.
3. **Growth is slow but steady.** Second-quarter revenue rose 11% to about $11.3 billion, including $456 million from Informatica, which it bought, and the company raised its fiscal 2027 forecast to $46.1 billion to $46.4 billion, the 26 August release showed.

**The price:** 13.2 times forward earnings against a software median of 23.7, and a trailing P/E of 16.9 against its own median of 35.5 since August 2024. The stock is down 30.0% this year to Monday's close.

**What could go wrong:** seats and debt. Revenue grew 9.8% a year over two years, in the bottom quarter of software, and investors fear AI agents will cut the number of paid users ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-18/wall-street-is-betting-ai-will-empty-softwares-seats-3-numbers-will-decide-it/)); the Informatica deal left net debt of $31.0 billion, 3.5 years of operating profit. Its third-quarter report on Wednesday 2 December, against its own forecast of $11.42 billion to $11.5 billion, is the first test.
