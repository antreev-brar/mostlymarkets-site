US shoppers spent **$9.7 billion** online on Black Friday through 6:30 p.m. ET, up 12.8% from the same point a year ago, Adobe Analytics said late on Friday, as Reuters and Stocktwits reported. Adobe now expects the full day to reach $13.0 billion to $13.4 billion, a record, against the $12.9 billion it forecast in September and the $11.8 billion shoppers spent last year.

The pace matters because it beats the plan in a season squeezed by inflation and a likely Fed rate hike on 9 December. Adobe's September forecast called for 9.2% growth on Black Friday; the new range implies roughly 10% to 14%. Our [Black Friday narrative](/news/2026-11-27/wall-street-is-betting-the-shopper-outlasts-a-fed-hike-black-friday-starts-the-test/) set out why retailers' stocks hang on the shopper outlasting higher rates, and online dollars count price as well as volume, so a record total can still hide fewer items bought.

Adobe's final Black Friday tally should come over the weekend, and Cyber Monday on 30 November is expected to be bigger still, at $15.1 billion. Adobe sees the five days from Thanksgiving to Cyber Monday reaching $47.5 billion, up 7.4%.
