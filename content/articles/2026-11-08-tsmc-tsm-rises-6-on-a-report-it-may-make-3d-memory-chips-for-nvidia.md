Taiwan Semiconductor () **rose 6.3%** to $375.44 on Friday, its biggest gain since July, after The Information reported that the world's largest contract chipmaker has begun drawing up plans to make vertically stacked 3D DRAM, a kind of memory chip, at the request of Nvidia ($NVDA), its biggest customer, Investing.com and TipRanks reported. Neither company commented on the report.

It matters because memory has become the scarce part of an AI server, and Nvidia buys it from a handful of memory makers, among them Micron ($MU), which rose 5.1% on Friday regardless. A second kind of supplier would give Nvidia leverage on price, but TSMC did not mention memory on its 15 October earnings call, and any new supply would take years to arrive, Yahoo Finance and TIKR reported. The stock is still 18.0% below where it was a month ago, in our data ([our memory narrative](/news/2026-11-03/memorys-boom-has-become-the-device-makers-bill-what-the-evidence-says/)).

TSMC reports its October sales on Tuesday, 10 November, and Nvidia reports its quarter on 17 November, when analysts can ask about the plan.
