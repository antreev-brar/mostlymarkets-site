Investors spent Monday chasing the biggest AI names back up after Nvidia [rehired Jensen Huang](/news/2026-11-23/stock-market-today-nvidia-adds-952-billion-as-jensen-huang-returns-and-the-nasdaq-jumps-6-5/). This list looks the other way: at companies worth $5 billion to $30 billion that turn an unusually large share of their sales into operating profit, the kind of business that rarely makes the front page.

Our screen ranked those companies by operating margin over the 12 months to their latest quarter (September or early October 2026), from SEC filings in our data, and left out any stock we listed in the past 14 days. We then judged each against its industry peers on growth, cash flow and price. Three of the four below keep more than 40 cents of every sales dollar as operating profit; the fourth is losing money.

```picks
like: VRSN, CPAY, GEN
skip: MOS
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Verisign ($VRSN)
**Operating margin: 67.6% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Verisign runs the registries for .com and .net web addresses and collects a fee each time a domain is registered or renewed.

1. Its margin has held between 65% and 68% in every fiscal year since 2020, the best of the internet-infrastructure companies in our data, against a 15.6% median.
2. It turns 59.1% of revenue into free cash flow, almost four times the peer median, and has used it to cut its share count by 7.1% in two years.
3. Growth is the weak spot: revenue rose 5.5% a year over five years, below the 8.0% peer median, and 5.7% in the latest quarter.

**The price:** 33.8 times trailing earnings, above its 30.6 times median since October 2024, and 1.3% below its 52-week high. We like the business more than the entry point.
**What could go wrong:** a quarter in which renewals slip and revenue growth falls below 4%.

### 2. Corpay ($CPAY)
**Operating margin: 43.9% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Corpay processes payments for businesses: fuel and toll cards for vehicle fleets, corporate bill pay and cross-border transfers.

1. Revenue grew 13.6% a year over five years and 17.9% in the latest quarter, both above the 9.4% and 12.4% medians for payment companies in our data.
2. The margin has stayed between 40% and 45% for six fiscal years, and return on equity of 37.7% is in the top quarter of its peers.
3. On 19 November it agreed to put $1.1 billion into a TPG-led take-private of bill-payment company BILL, about a $6 billion deal that it expects to add to 2027 earnings, TradingView and Seeking Alpha reported.

**The price:** 14.3 times expected earnings, below the 15.7 times peer median, though 24.0 times trailing earnings is a little above its 22.7 times median since November 2024.
**What could go wrong:** if the BILL stake drags on its margin, or fuel-card volumes fall with oil prices.

### 3. Gen Digital ($GEN)
**Operating margin: 41.8% in the 12 months to 2 October 2026.** Gen Digital sells consumer security and identity-protection subscriptions under the Norton, Avast and LifeLock names.

1. The margin rose 7.3 points over five years, and 41.8% is in the top tenth of the 31 systems-software companies in our data, against a 9.7% median.
2. Free cash flow was 33.7% of revenue over the past 12 months, above its 31.6% two-year average.
3. What changed: revenue growth slowed to 12.2% in the latest quarter from 16.5% a year over two years, in the bottom quarter of its peers.

**The price:** 9.6 times expected earnings against a 24.0 times peer median, and 15.1 times trailing earnings against its own 26.9 times median since October 2024. That is the cheapest of our three by a wide margin.
**What could go wrong:** growth slowing below 10%, which would make its $7.7 billion of net debt, 3.5 years of operating profit, harder to carry.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Mosaic ($MOS)
**Operating margin: -5.1% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Mosaic mines and sells phosphate and potash fertilizer to farmers.

1. Revenue has fallen from $19.1 billion in fiscal 2022 to $12.1 billion in 2025, and dropped 10.2% in the latest quarter, the worst of 24 materials stocks in our data.
2. Free cash flow was negative 6.8% of revenue in the past 12 months, in the bottom tenth of its peers.
3. What changed: Freedom Broker cut it to sell on 11 November, citing sulfur costs, a raw material for phosphate, squeezing profits, Seeking Alpha and TradingView reported.

**The price:** 0.6 times book value looks cheap, but 49.6 times expected earnings is more than three times the 14.6 times materials median, so the market is already paying for a recovery.
**What would change our mind:** a jump in crop prices or a fall in sulfur costs would lift its margins fast; it earned 25.0% in fiscal 2022.
