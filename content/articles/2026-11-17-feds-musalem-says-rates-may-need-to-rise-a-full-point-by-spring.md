St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said on Tuesday that the Federal Reserve's benchmark rate, now 3.75% to 4.00%, is **about a percentage point too low**, and that the economy will likely need several more increases before spring 2027, Reuters and Seeking Alpha reported. At a quarter point a time, that means as many as four hikes in the coming months, CNBC reported. He wants the real policy rate, the rate after inflation, at 2% to 2.5% by next spring, and said a path with no more hikes is unlikely but still plausible.

Musalem does not vote on rates this year, CNBC noted, but his number goes well beyond the single quarter-point hike at the 8-9 December meeting that futures priced at about 87% odds on Friday, as we [reported after October's wholesale prices](/news/2026-11-13/wholesale-prices-rose-less-than-expected-in-october-but-the-annual-rate-hit-6-5/). Four would take the top of the range to 5%, close to the 5.12% the 10-year Treasury yield closed at on Monday. On the same morning, [October retail sales](/news/2026-11-17/retail-sales-slowed-to-0-2-in-october-but-the-core-gauge-beat-forecasts/) showed core spending beating forecasts.

The Fed's minutes from its October meeting, due this week, will show how many colleagues share his view.
