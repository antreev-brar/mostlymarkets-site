The third-quarter season is mostly over, and the stocks that rose on their reports were the ones where growth sped up. Eaton ($ETN) and Airbnb ($ABNB) both did that last week; Marriott ($MAR) beat and raised too, but its price had already moved ahead of it. Eaton is also a test of the week's AI sell-off: it fell with the data-center suppliers despite a record quarter.

Our screen took the companies worth $10 billion or more that reported in the past seven days and that we recapped, then judged each on our data from SEC filings: growth over five and two years, margins, cash flow and valuation against peers and its own history, in the 12 months to September 2026 unless stated.

```picks
like: ETN, ABNB
skip: MAR
```

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. Eaton ($ETN)
**Sales growth last quarter: 28%, to a record $8.95 billion (quarter to September 2026)**. Eaton makes the switchgear, transformers and power-distribution gear that data centers, utilities and buildings need. On 3 November it beat estimates, sped organic growth up to 19% from 14% and raised its 2026 forecast for the second time ([our recap](/news/2026-11-03/eaton-etn-beats-q3-estimates-and-raises-its-2026-forecast-again/)).

1. **Growth is speeding up, not peaking.** Revenue grew 9.0% a year over five fiscal years, a little ahead of the 8.0% median for the 89 industrials in our coverage, and 14.0% a year over the past two, in the top quarter.
2. **Profit has compounded faster than sales.** Earnings per share grew 24.5% a year over five years, in the top quarter of industrials and more than twice the 11.3% median.
3. **The orders cover next year.** Its electrical backlog was up 46% from a year earlier at the end of September, the company said, with record orders in all three segments.

**The price:** 34.2 times trailing earnings, below its own median of 36.5 times since late 2024, and 22.5 times forward earnings against 21.0 for peers. The stock is 25.7% below its August high after the AI-supplier sell-off.

**What could go wrong:** Eaton's free cash flow margin, 12.1% over two years, is just below the industrial median, and if the big cloud companies slow their 2027 data-center plans early next year, the backlog would stop growing first.

### 2. Airbnb ($ABNB)
**Gross booking value growth: 21%, to $27.7 billion (quarter to September 2026)**. Airbnb runs the marketplace for short-term home rentals and, lately, hotels and experiences. On 5 November bookings sped up from 16% growth in the second quarter, revenue beat estimates and it raised its outlook and announced a $12 billion buyback ([our recap](/news/2026-11-05/airbnb-abnb-jumps-after-hours-as-bookings-grow-21-and-it-raises-its-outlook/)). The stock **soared 11.7%** on Friday.

1. **It has grown far faster than its sector.** Revenue grew 29.4% a year over five years and 13.4% a year over the past two, both in the top quarter of consumer stocks in our coverage, against medians of 12.8% and 6.6%.
2. **It keeps almost everything it sells.** Its gross margin was 83.1% over two years, the highest among 41 consumer peers, and its operating margin was 22.5% in the latest 12 months, against a 13.8% median.
3. **It shrinks its share count.** Diluted shares fell 7.0% over two years, with $4.9 billion of net cash at the end of September.

**The price:** 35.4 times trailing earnings, a little above its own median of 33.3 times since late 2024, and 33.6 times forward earnings, well above the 19.7 times peer median. We think the growth gap pays for most of that premium.

**What could go wrong:** the stock is 0.3% below its record close after rising 40.0% this year, so a slowdown in bookings growth back toward the 16% of the second quarter would hit a price that leaves little room.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Marriott International ($MAR)
**Trailing P/E: 41.6 times (6 November 2026)**. Marriott owns few hotels and earns fees from the owners who fly its brands. On 2 November it beat estimates, with fees up 14%, and raised its 2026 earnings forecast to $12.07 to $12.14 a share ([our recap](/news/2026-11-02/marriott-mar-beats-q3-estimates-and-raises-its-2026-profit-forecast/)).

1. **Recent growth trails its peers.** Revenue grew 5.2% a year over the past two years, below the 6.6% median for consumer stocks and well below Airbnb's 13.4% and Hilton's 7.4%.
2. **The boost is fading.** Marriott guided fourth-quarter growth in revenue per available room to 4.5% to 5%, down from 6.5% in the third quarter as the World Cup comparison falls away, and net rooms growth to the low end of its range.
3. **The business is good, not exceptional.** Its free cash flow margin was 10.1% over two years, near the 9.0% sector median, and its operating margin of 15.8% sits just above the 13.8% median.

**The price:** 41.6 times trailing earnings, against its own median of 31.9 times since late 2024 and near the top of its 25.4x to 42.4x range; 34.4 times forward earnings, against 19.7 for peers and 14.6 for Booking Holdings.

**What would change our mind:** a fall back toward its own median multiple, or US revenue per available room holding above 6% in the first quarter without the World Cup.
