## 3 stocks we like

### 1. MetLife ($MET)
**Forward P/E: 9.2 as of 2:15 p.m. ET Wednesday.** A life insurer selling group benefits, retirement products and life cover worldwide.

1. **It earns well on its capital.** Return on equity was **23.1%** in the 12 months to September, above the 17.9% median of the 23 insurers we cover.
2. **It shrinks its share count.** Diluted shares fell 9.1% over two years, top quarter among insurers.
3. **The quarter was strong, with help.** Adjusted earnings per share rose 17% to $2.78, but variable investment income of $720 million ran far above its $400 million quarterly norm, Zacks and the release showed, so the beat flatters the run rate.

**The price:** 12.9 times trailing earnings against its own two-year median of 15.0, and 9.2 times forward against 13.5 for insurers.
**What could go wrong:** revenue grew only 2.6% a year over five fiscal years, bottom quarter among insurers, so the case rests on buybacks and returns. If investment income falls back to its $400 million norm, about $320 million a quarter of pre-tax income goes with it.

### 2. Huntington Bancshares ($HBAN)
**Forward P/E: 8.9 as of 2:15 p.m. ET Wednesday.** A Columbus, Ohio, regional bank that has bought Cadence Bank.

1. **It has grown faster than its peers for years.** Revenue rose **11.1% a year** over five fiscal years, the fastest of the 15 banks we cover against a 4.3% median, and earnings per share rose 15.0% a year, above the 11.7% median.
2. **The deal is paying in interest income.** Third-quarter net interest income, was $2.07 billion, up 37% from a year earlier, and its margin widened to 3.25%, the release and TradingKey's call summary showed. Management expects the full benefit of the Cadence cost cuts from the fourth quarter.
3. **The weak spot is returns.** Return on equity was 7.7%, bottom quarter among banks, because diluted shares rose 38.6% over two years to pay for the deals.

**The price:** 0.9 times book value and 8.9 times forward earnings against a 10.1 bank median.
**What could go wrong:** if the cost savings don't lift return on equity toward the 10.9% bank median in 2026, the extra shares were simply dilution.

### 3. Phillips 66 ($PSX)
**Forward P/E: 6.5 as of 2:15 p.m. ET Wednesday.** A refiner that also runs pipelines and chemicals.

1. **Its returns are among the best in energy.** Return on equity was **36.6%** in the 12 months to September, top quarter among the 22 energy stocks we can measure, against a 17.5% median.
2. **It is using the windfall well.** Third-quarter adjusted earnings were a record $5.6 billion, or $14.08 a share, the release and Oilprice.com reported. Net debt fell to $12.7 billion, by our data.
3. **The windfall is fading.** Diesel refining margins hit records in September and have eased since, RBN Energy and InvestingLive reported, and the stock is 20.3% below its 14 October closing high.

**The price:** 7.6 times trailing earnings against its own two-year median of 19.6. If earnings halved, it would be about 15, still below that median.
**What could go wrong:** refining profit is the spread between crude and fuel prices, and it can collapse faster than forecasts are cut.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Strategy ($MSTR)
**Forward P/E: 6.2 as of 2:15 p.m. ET Wednesday, the cheapest on our screen.** A small software company turned bitcoin holder, funded by selling shares and preferred stock.

1. **Its profit is the bitcoin price.** Third-quarter net income was $19.5 billion, almost all of it a $20.9 billion paper gain on bitcoin; without it, the business lost money, Kalkine and Bitcoin Magazine reported from its 10-Q. Its forward P/E is a bitcoin forecast.
2. **Owners keep getting diluted.** Diluted shares rose **117%** over two years, the most among the 32 software companies we cover.
3. **Index funds must sell.** MSCI deletes Strategy from its USA index after the close on 30 November, MSCI's review and CoinMarketCap reported ([our 13 November story](/news/2026-11-13/strategy-mstr-shares-slide-as-msci-drops-it-and-bitcoin-falls/)). On Tuesday the stock closed at $77.55, its lowest close in a year and 60.4% below its May high.

**The price:** 0.6 times book value, but that book is mostly bitcoin valued at 30 September.
**What would change our mind:** paying its preferred dividends, about $420 million in the third quarter, without selling new common shares. The fourth-quarter report in early February will show how it was funded.
