Last week the Nasdaq Composite fell 3.4%, its fourth weekly loss in a row, on two fears: that Democrats' midterm sweep will slow the building of AI data centers, and that a strong jobs report on Friday makes a Federal Reserve rate hike in December likely ([our weekend edition](/daily/2026-11-07/)). This week brings the first hard evidence on both, and much of it comes from companies whose stocks have already been cut deeply.

Several of the week's reporters lost a fifth or more of their value between 6 October and Friday's close, in our data, among them CoreWeave ($CRWV), Nebius ($NBIS), Applied Materials ($AMAT) and Cisco ($CSCO). A low bar helps a stock on decent news; it also means the market already expects bad news, so these reports test whether the selling went too far.

## Monday, 9 November: power, private markets and GTA

Constellation Energy ($CEG), the biggest US nuclear operator, and NRG Energy ($NRG) report before the open, after falling 6.7% and 13.5% on the day after the election, when the Texas governor's race raised the risk of a pause on new data-center permits ([our Constellation preview](/news/2026-11-08/what-to-expect-from-constellation-energys-ceg-q3-earnings/)). KKR ($KKR) also reports before the open, with Zacks expecting $1.58 a share of adjusted net income ([our KKR preview](/news/2026-11-08/what-to-expect-from-kkrs-kkr-q3-earnings/)).

After the close come Occidental Petroleum ($OXY), which fell 11.9% last week as oil slid toward $90 ([our preview](/news/2026-11-08/what-to-expect-from-occidental-petroleums-oxy-q3-earnings/)), and Take-Two Interactive ($TTWO), ten days before Grand Theft Auto VI launches ([our preview](/news/2026-11-08/what-to-expect-from-take-two-interactives-ttwo-q2-earnings/)). Fed officials begin a week of speeches the same day, Investing.com and Stocktwits reported.

## Tuesday, 10 November: the two biggest tests

At 8:30 a.m. ET the Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes October's consumer price index. Economists in Bloomberg's survey expect prices to have risen **0.3%** in the month, after 0.6% in September, and core prices 0.2%, Business Standard and Morningstar reported. There will be no 12-month rate, because October 2025 prices were never collected during that year's government shutdown, StockTitan and Bloomberg reported. Futures put the odds of a quarter-point hike on 9 December near 78% after Friday's jobs data, Reuters reported ([our debate on the hike](/news/2026-11-08/the-debate-over-a-december-rate-hike-daly-says-tighten-tom-lee-says-the-fed-may-skip/)).

After the close, CoreWeave, the AI cloud renter that has lost half its value in a month, reports its third quarter. Its backlog of signed contracts was $104 billion at the end of June, its release showed. Microsoft ($MSFT), its biggest customer, is reviewing which outside providers it rents from, CNBC reported ([our narrative on the neoclouds](/news/2026-11-07/wall-street-is-betting-the-neoclouds-are-ais-weak-link-coreweaves-report-will-test-it/), [our Microsoft breakdown](/news/2026-11-08/microsoft-msft-sells-more-than-ever-but-keeps-less-cash-is-the-business-still-strong/)). TransDigm ($TDG) and Sea ($SE) report before the open.

## Wednesday, 11 November: Veterans Day and Nvidia's deadline

US stock exchanges are open on Veterans Day. It is also the date by which House committee chairs asked Nvidia ($NVDA) Chief Executive Jensen Huang to accept an 18 November briefing or offer another, after a Financial Times report on a US government memo about him ([our brief](/news/2026-11-07/ft-says-a-us-memo-ties-nvidia-nvda-ceo-to-a-suspected-chinese-spy-days-before-a-house-briefing/)).

## Thursday, 12 November: chip gear and networking

Applied Materials, which makes the machines that build chips, and Cisco, the networking company, report after the close. Applied has **fallen 37.3%** in a month and Cisco 20.4%; Applied's orders are the first read on whether chipmakers plan to spend less on new factories next year, and Cisco's on whether AI networking demand survived the sell-off. Nebius, down 48.2% in a month, reports before the open, and weekly jobless claims come out at 8:30 a.m. ET.

## Friday, 13 November: wholesale prices

October's producer price index, the prices businesses charge each other, comes out at 8:30 a.m. ET, the Bureau of Labor Statistics schedule shows. After Tuesday's CPI it is the last inflation reading of the week, and its energy line will show how much of oil's slide from about $103 a barrel at the end of October had reached businesses.

## What we'll be watching

The order matters. A cool CPI on Tuesday morning would ease the rate worry before CoreWeave reports that evening, and a strong CoreWeave backlog would ease the AI worry before Applied and Cisco on Thursday. Two bad readings on the same Tuesday would leave the Nasdaq, already 11.3% below its 52-week high, with both of its fears confirmed before Nvidia reports on 17 November.
