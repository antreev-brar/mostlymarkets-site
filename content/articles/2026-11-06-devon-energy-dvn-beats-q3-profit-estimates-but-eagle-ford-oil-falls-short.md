Devon Energy ($DVN) reported third-quarter core earnings of **$1.42 a share** after the close on Thursday, above the $1.24 Zacks consensus; StockStory, using S&P Global's estimate of $1.31, also counted a beat. Oil output of 553,000 barrels a day came in light in the Eagle Ford, the Texas field it is selling, and revenue missed estimates. The shares fell 5.7% before Friday's open but recovered to **$42.61, down 0.6%**, as of 12:15 p.m. ET.

## What stood out

**The Eagle Ford was the soft spot.** Oil volumes there fell short because of the timing of well completions, offset by solid results elsewhere led by the Permian, the release said, and Investing.com tied the early slide to that shortfall. Devon agreed on 8 October to sell its Eagle Ford assets for $4.2 billion in cash, with closing expected early in 2027.

*Verdict: a miss in the business it is leaving, which matters less than the headline suggests.*

**The merger is paying faster than promised.** Devon now expects to exit 2026 at an annual savings rate of about **$350 million**, 75% above its August plan, with about $700 million of savings in 2027 on the way to at least $1 billion by the end of next year. Unhedged gas prices also improved to 66% of the Henry Hub benchmark as new Permian pipelines opened, above its guidance.

*Verdict: strong, and the part of the story that should outlast one quarter's oil volumes.*

## The outlook

Devon kept its 2026 guidance and gave a first look at 2027: oil output of about **525,000 barrels a day**, 1% to 3% growth on a pro forma basis, for about $5.3 billion of capital. It plans to lift buybacks above $1 billion in the fourth quarter and accelerate them in early 2027 with the Eagle Ford money, and to repay another $1.5 billion of debt to reach its $9 billion target.

## Our take

We were impressed by the cash: $2.0 billion of free cash flow in a quarter, with buybacks rising and debt falling. On the other hand, oil came in light and the revenue line missed, and 1% to 3% oil growth next year makes Devon a cash-return story, not a growth one. Overall, a solid quarter, and the market's recovery from the early drop suggests investors read it the same way.

At 7.7 times forward earnings against an energy median of 11.6 by our data, and with a two-year free cash flow margin in the top tenth of its peers, the price assumes little. The stock is 19% below its 30 October high as US crude has fallen sharply over the past week. The next test is the Eagle Ford sale closing and the size of the first buyback after it.
