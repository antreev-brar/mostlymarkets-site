The AI trade lost its cushion on Friday. A day after Anthropic's record IPO, the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.3% and turned negative for 2026, and Nvidia ($NVDA) **plunged 14.0%** after Reuters reported the SEC is examining its accounting. Over the weekend the argument on Wall Street moved from "is it a bubble?" to a sharper question: is the bubble bursting, or is the air coming out slowly enough to leave the buildout standing?

The number both sides point to is how far the most leveraged AI names have already fallen. CoreWeave ($CRWV), which rents Nvidia chips to AI labs, closed Friday **71.4% below** its May high by our data, and Nebius ($NBIS) 71.6% below its June high, while Nvidia itself is 47.5% off its 6 October peak.

## The bull case

### Mark Hackett, chief market strategist, Nationwide
**This is a washout, not a bubble bursting.** Hackett told Reuters on Friday that the sell-off was "not a bubble bursting" and that the AI trade still has room to run, likening the move to the panic after the April 2025 "Liberation Day" tariffs, Reuters and Yahoo Finance reported. He argued in comments reported by Spectrum News on Saturday that the AI-led washout could prove constructive for the wider market, because it resets prices without a break in the economy.

### Ed Yardeni, president, Yardeni Research
**The bubble is deflating without bursting, and the bond market is doing the work.** Yardeni has argued this month that "the bond market is going to regulate the availability of capital to the AI trade," pointing to growing scrutiny of how CoreWeave and Nebius fund themselves, Benzinga and BeInCrypto reported. His case is that a bubble everyone is watching tends to let air out early, which lowers the odds of a burst that drags the economy down; he expects chip stocks to rebound, Stocktwits reported.

## The bear case

### Gary Gensler, former SEC chair, MIT
**AI is already a bubble, and its burst will cause a recession before the 2028 election.** In a CNN interview published on Monday, 16 November, Gensler said "the only question is if it's a soft recession or something more dramatic," CNN and Inc. reported. He compared the boom with the railroads, 1920s electricity and the 1990s internet, all real technologies whose financing ran ahead of their profits, Tom's Guide and Anadolu reported.

### Michael Burry, investor known for "The Big Short"
**The risk is how the buildout is paid for, not who runs Nvidia.** Burry has argued in posts this month that Nvidia's investments in its own customers leave its sales exposed if their funding slows, Yahoo Finance and Benzinga reported. On Friday he disclosed that he had sold half of his put options on Nvidia and on a semiconductor ETF, taking profit, but kept longer-dated Nvidia puts expiring in February 2027, Crypto Briefing and BigGo Finance reported: a bet that the decline has further to run.

### Steve Sosnick, chief strategist, Interactive Brokers
**The dip-buyers have turned into sellers.** Sosnick told CNN on Friday there was "a real exodus out of tech stocks," with buying the dip giving way to selling into rallies, Yahoo Finance and WPSD reported. He said investors were already questioning the cost and payoff of AI spending and were coming off euphoric levels, a combination he called "a lot for the market to bear."

## Where they agree

All five put money, not demand, at the center. Yardeni's bond market, Gensler's financing bust and Burry's customer-funding loop all describe the same pressure point: the chip-renting clouds and data-center builders that pay for AI with borrowed money. Nobody on either side disputes that the chips are selling; Nvidia's third-quarter revenue beat analysts' estimate by $6.7 billion on Tuesday, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC ([our recap](/news/2026-11-17/nvidia-nvda-tops-q3-estimates-and-guides-to-130-billion-in-its-first-report-without-huang/)).

## The number that decides it

The test is whether the leveraged buyers keep ordering. If they can still borrow, Nvidia's $130 billion forecast holds and the bulls' "deflation" is right; if they can't, orders get pushed out and the bears' burst begins at the suppliers. Dell ($DELL), which sells AI servers to these clouds, reports after the close on Tuesday, 24 November, and Micron ($MU) on 17 December.

## Our read

The evidence today favors "deflating" on demand and "bursting" on funding. Nvidia's $130 billion forecast and a 14% drop on an accounting report, not on orders, say the selling is about price and trust, not a collapse in chip sales. But the bears' strongest point is already showing up where they said it would: CoreWeave and Nebius are down more than 70% from their highs, a sign that the market is closing the funding door Yardeni describes. We'd call it too early to tell, and Dell's backlog on Tuesday is the first number that will; for the chain from Nvidia to its renters, see [our narrative on AI's money loop](/news/2026-11-16/wall-street-is-betting-ais-1-trillion-money-loop-breaks-at-the-neoclouds-nvidias-report-will-test-it/) and [why the Fed won't rescue this sell-off](/news/2026-11-22/why-wall-street-thinks-the-fed-wont-rescue-this-sell-off/).
