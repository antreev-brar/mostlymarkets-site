Southern Co. ($SO) reported third-quarter adjusted earnings of **$1.94 a share** before the open on Thursday, up from $1.60 a year earlier and 13% above the $1.71 Zacks consensus. Revenue rose 13% to $8.84 billion, and the Atlanta-based utility raised its 2026 forecast to $4.70 a share. The stock closed **up 0.2% at $93.34**, holding steady as the S&P 500 fell 4.4%.

## What stood out

**Customers are using more power.** Retail electricity sales rose **3.5%** after adjusting for weather, the company's quarterly filing and its call showed, with data-centre demand at Georgia Power leading the commercial side. A utility earns a regulated return on the grid it builds, so rising demand is what justifies the next round of spending and the earnings that come with it.

*Verdict: demand growth of this size is rare for a regulated utility, and it is what the long-term plan rests on.*

**Weather helped too.** A hot quarter added about $218 million to retail revenue, the filing showed, so part of the beat won't repeat. GAAP earnings were lower than adjusted, at $1.77 a share, after items the company excludes from its adjusted figure.

## The outlook

Southern raised its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast to **$4.70**, from a range of $4.50 to $4.60 it had said it would reach near or at the top of, AP and Benzinga reported; Benzinga put the Street estimate at $4.59. It did not change its long-term growth targets but said it could raise them at its update early next year. Its $81 billion five-year spending plan, plus about $4 billion of projects already identified, could approach $100 billion, management said on the call, Seeking Alpha and Motley Fool's transcript showed.

## Our take

We were impressed by a 13% beat and a forecast raise in a business where beats are usually measured in cents. On the other hand, weather flattered the quarter, and a capital plan heading toward $100 billion means more borrowing on top of net debt of $72.8 billion, 10 years of operating profit by our data. Overall, a strong quarter that supports the thesis that the Southeast's data-centre boom is turning into utility earnings.

The stock barely moved because much of that was expected: it rose 9.3% in the month before the report and trades at 20.0 times forward earnings, above [Duke Energy's](/news/2026-11-05/duke-energy-duk-beats-q3-estimates-and-aims-for-the-top-of-its-2026-range/) 19.2. The next test is the long-term update early next year, and whether the growth targets go up.
