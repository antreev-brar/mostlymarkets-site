Global Payments ($GPN) **plunged 12.0%** to $88.19 on Thursday after Reuters reported that buyout firms CD&R and GTCR had dropped their pursuit of the payments processor, Investing.com and TipRanks reported. The talks never got past an early stage, partly because financing a deal that size was difficult with borrowing costs high, the people Reuters cited said.

The fall erased the takeover premium: reports of a roughly $40 billion buyout, with GTCR as the company's biggest shareholder, had lifted the stock in late October, and it closed at a 52-week high of $101.59 on Tuesday. It was its worst day since April 2025, on 2.8 times its usual volume in our data.

Without a bid, the stock trades on its own numbers, which are weak: free cash flow was negative over the latest 12 months and debt stands at $22.6 billion, its filings show, which is why we put it on our [value list as the one we'd skip](/news/2026-11-13/2-value-stocks-we-like-and-1-wed-skip-eog-synchrony-and-global-payments/). The people Reuters cited said talks could resume or another buyer could emerge.
