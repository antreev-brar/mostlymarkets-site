Zoetis ($ZTS) reported third-quarter revenue of $2.39 billion before the open on Thursday, down 1% from a year earlier but above the $2.30 billion Zacks consensus, and adjusted earnings of $1.75 a share against the $1.51 analysts expected. It raised its 2026 earnings forecast too. But US sales of medicines for pets fell 8%, and on the call management said it is planning for 2027 on the assumption that competition and soft demand continue; the shares **fell 7.95% to $82.11**, their second-biggest drop of the past year.

## What stood out

**Pet owners are cutting back.** Companion animal medicines are most of Zoetis' business, and in the US that line fell **8%**, which the release put down to "macro-driven price sensitivity and competitive pressure in dermatology, as well as generic competition". "That cost pressure is making pet owners more value conscious and deliberate when making trade-offs to care for their pets," Chief Executive Kristin Peck said, according to Reuters. She said US vet visits fell 3% in the first nine months.

*Verdict: the weakness is in the core franchise, not a side business, and it has run all year.*

**Livestock and buybacks carried the beat.** Livestock revenue grew in both segments. Adjusted net income fell 5%, but adjusted EPS rose 3% because there are fewer shares. Zoetis has cut its share count 9.2% over two years by our data, the most among its health-care peers.

## The outlook

Zoetis raised its 2026 adjusted EPS range to **$6.25 to $6.35** and narrowed revenue to $9.22 billion to $9.32 billion, which is shrinking on an organic basis. It gave no numbers for 2027. On the call, management declined to comment on Wall Street's models and said its 2027 budget assumes no improvement in the economy or in competition, according to the transcript published by StockAnalysis and 24/7 Wall St's summary.

## Our take

We were impressed that Zoetis still beat on earnings with its biggest market shrinking. On the other hand, the beat came from livestock and buybacks, while the question investors own the stock for, whether its pet dermatology and pain franchises can grow again against cheaper rivals, got a cautious answer. Overall, a quarter that confirms the slowdown rather than ending it, and that is what the stock priced.

Zoetis is down **34.7% this year** and 37% below its February high, and it trades at 12.9 times forward earnings against 18.2 for its peer median by our data, so a lot of bad news is already in the price. The next test is fourth-quarter results early next year, when it gives its first 2027 forecast.
