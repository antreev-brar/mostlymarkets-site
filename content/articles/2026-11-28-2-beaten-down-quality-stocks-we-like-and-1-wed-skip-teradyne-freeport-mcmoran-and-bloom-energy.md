Friday's half-day rally, with the Nasdaq up 2.3%, lifted the stocks November's sell-off hit hardest, but most remain far below their summer highs. Some are good businesses marked down with the speculative ones: chip-testing equipment maker Teradyne ($TER) closed Friday 53.9% below its 30 June high, and copper miner Freeport-McMoRan ($FCX) 36.5% below its 25 August high, in our data.

Our screen took profitable companies in our coverage and ranked them by how far each stock closed on Friday, 27 November, below its 52-week closing high, leaving out our picks of the past 14 days. We then judged the leaders on SEC-filing numbers from `analyse.py`, long run first and against each sector, then the price against its own history.

```picks
like: TER, FCX
skip: BE
```

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. Teradyne ($TER)

**Below its 52-week high: 53.9%, at Friday's close.** Teradyne makes the machines that test chips before they ship, plus industrial robots.

1. **AI chips have turned a flat business into a fast one.** Revenue grew just 0.4% a year over five fiscal years, in the bottom quarter of the 25 chipmakers we track, but **35.2% a year** over the last two, above their 25.6% median. In the quarter to September it rose 70% to $1.31 billion, above the top of its own forecast, the results release on 27 October showed, with chip testing alone bringing in $1.07 billion.
2. **It earns more on its capital than almost any peer.** Return on capital of about 39% is more than double the 17.8% median, in the top quarter, and the share count fell 4.1% over two years as it bought back stock. The weakness is the long run: the operating margin, 32.1% over the past 12 months, is 9.4 points below five years ago.
3. **The forecast holds up.** Teradyne guided fourth-quarter revenue of $1.25 billion to $1.35 billion, about 20% above a year earlier at the midpoint, and put full-year 2026 revenue at about $5.2 billion, more than 60% growth, the release and Investing.com reported. The sell-off since has been about AI spending forecasts, not Teradyne's orders.

**The price:** 20.9 times expected earnings, a little above the 18.9 times chipmaker median; on a trailing basis it trades at 25.6 times, against its own two-year median of 46.8 times.

**What would change our mind:** a fourth-quarter result or 2027 outlook, due in late January, that shows chipmakers cutting test spending, which would mean the AI demand behind the last two years is fading.

### 2. Freeport-McMoRan ($FCX)

**Below its 52-week high: 36.5%, at Friday's close.** Freeport mines copper, gold and molybdenum in the Americas and at Grasberg in Indonesia, one of the world's biggest copper mines.

1. **It is far more profitable than other miners.** The operating margin was 27.6% in the 12 months to September 2026, against an 11.2% median for its metals peers and up 8.0 points over five years. Earnings per share grew **30.0% a year** over five fiscal years, in the top quarter, and return on capital of about 22% is in the top tenth.
2. **Next year's volumes are already climbing back.** Grasberg is still recovering from a mud rush in 2025, but Freeport expects copper sales to rise more than 20% in 2027 and about another 10% in 2028, Quartr and Investing.com reported from its third-quarter call. It beat third-quarter estimates with adjusted EPS of $0.88 against $0.80, though it trimmed its 2026 and 2027 copper sales forecasts by about 2% to 2.5%, Canadian Mining Journal reported.
3. **Debt is low for a miner.** Net debt was $5.1 billion at 30 September, about 0.7 years of operating profit. The stock's slide since August tracked copper's fall to a near five-month low on a firmer dollar and rate-hike worries, as [we reported on 18 November](/news/2026-11-18/agnico-eagle-freeport-mcmoran-and-newmont-shares-slide-what-happened/), not anything at the mines.

**The price:** 12.4 times expected earnings, against 16.2 times for its peers; on a trailing basis 21.3 times, against its own two-year median of 33.1 times.

**What would change our mind:** another delay to Grasberg's ramp, which Freeport aims to have near full capacity by the end of 2027, or a further cut to the 2027 sales forecast with fourth-quarter results in January.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Bloom Energy ($BE)

**Below its 52-week high: 52.9%, at Friday's close.** Bloom makes fuel-cell systems that generate power on site, much of it for data centers.

1. **The growth is real.** Revenue rose 138% to $1.24 billion in the quarter to September, a record, and Bloom raised its 2026 revenue forecast to $4.4 billion to $4.6 billion, its release on 22 October and Crypto Briefing showed. Over two years revenue grew **74.2% a year**, the fastest of the 89 industrial companies we track.
2. **Shareholders have paid for it.** The diluted share count rose 42.3% in two years, in the bottom 2% of its peers, so each share owns far less of the growth. Gross margin of 32.0% over two years sits in the bottom quarter, against a 39.0% median.
3. **The cash lags the sales.** Operations used $27 million in the third quarter and free cash flow was about minus $181 million, the release and Yahoo Finance reported. Over the past 12 months free cash flow was 11.4% of sales, below the 13.2% median.

**The price:** 33.4 times expected earnings, against 20.4 times for industrial peers, and 110 times free cash flow.

**What would change our mind:** steady positive free cash flow and an end to the dilution; the fourth-quarter report early next year is the next check.
