President Trump said in a New York Post interview published late on Tuesday that it is "probably not too late" for the US government to take an equity stake in Nvidia ($NVDA), pointing to its **10% stake in Intel** ($INTC) as the precedent, Investing.com and Yahoo News reported. He said he had considered the idea but had not raised it with Nvidia's board. He also said a pardon for Chief Executive Jensen Huang is "always an option, I guess, but I don't think it'll be necessary," CNBC and CNN reported, and that Huang should "just tell the truth" to Congress rather than plead the Fifth.

A government stake would add a new shareholder with its own aims to a company whose board [rehired Huang](/news/2026-11-23/nvidia-nvda-shares-soar-29-before-the-open-after-it-reinstates-jensen-huang/) on Monday after Trump pushed for his return, sharpening the question critics raised on Tuesday about the board's independence. Nvidia fell 5.9% on Tuesday and was **down 4.6% at $148.21** before the open as of 6:16 a.m. ET, in our data, though the broader chip selloff on [tariff reports](/news/2026-11-25/us-readies-new-tariffs-on-16-economies-and-nasdaq-futures-fall-2-2/) makes the stake comment hard to separate.

Huang's House interviews start on 1 December. Whether the stake idea becomes a formal approach is not known; our [narrative](/news/2026-11-23/nvidias-crisis-has-moved-from-its-boss-to-its-books-what-the-evidence-says/) tracks what is still open.
