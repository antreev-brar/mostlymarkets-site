Friday's sell-off in American chipmakers reached Asia overnight and came back. Taiwan and South Korea led the region lower as investors cut their AI holdings a day before Nvidia ($NVDA) reports, Reuters and Yahoo Finance reported, and by 6:15 a.m. ET Taiwan Semiconductor's ($TSM) US shares were **down 4.3%** in premarket trading, with Nvidia off 2.2% and S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 futures lower.

The weekend did not settle who runs Nvidia. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent wants an outsider, the Financial Times reported that Jensen Huang has discussed coming back, and the calls from the tech industry for his return grew louder, CNBC reported. Against that, Morgan Stanley's strategists told clients on Sunday to own as many stocks as their mandates allow, and the head of the IMF warned that the world economy now leans on AI.

Samsung, SK Hynix and TSMC all fell in Seoul and Taipei on Monday as Friday's US chip sell-off spread, Investing.com, Yahoo Finance and MarketScreener reported, with weak Chinese and Japanese data adding to the pressure. In our data, AMD ($AMD) **fell 4.1%** before the open after Friday's 8.7% drop, and Broadcom ($AVGO) 3.0%: the two stocks traders had bought last week as Nvidia's possible winners ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-13/wall-street-is-betting-nvidias-loss-is-amd-and-broadcoms-gain-tuesday-will-test-it/)).

Three things happened over the weekend: Bessent said Nvidia should "go outside the company" for its next chief ([our brief](/news/2026-11-15/bessent-says-nvidia-nvda-should-hire-an-outside-ceo-after-huang/)), the FT reported Huang has discussed a return ([our brief](/news/2026-11-15/jensen-huang-has-discussed-a-return-as-nvidia-nvda-ceo-ft-reports/)), and Elon Musk said Nvidia would lose "critical" deals without him ([our brief](/news/2026-11-15/musk-says-nvidia-nvda-will-lose-critical-deals-without-jensen-huang/)). Nvidia closed Friday **31.9% below** its 6 October record. Interim Chief Executive Colette Kress will face the question on Tuesday's 5 p.m. call.

Strategists led by Michael Wilson moved global stocks to **"max overweight"**, saying the AI spending cycle is intact, Bloomberg and MarketWatch reported, and set an S&P 500 target of 8,700 for the end of 2027, InvestingLive and MLQ reported. They kept the US at overweight and raised Europe and Japan to overweight. The risks they named are inflation that pushes the Fed to tighten further and an AI spending cycle that ends sooner than they expect, which are the two things this week tests.

### The IMF put a number on AI's money loop

IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva told CNBC on Monday, at the ASEAN summit in the Philippines, that the global economy is "standing on one leg", leaning on AI. Deals in which money invested in an AI company comes back to the investor as orders now total **a little over $1 trillion**, about two-thirds funded by debt, CNBC and NewsBytes reported, citing IMF estimates. She called the recent market swings "orderly", MLQ reported, which is the reassurance; the debt share is the warning, because Nvidia's own investments in its customers are part of that loop.
