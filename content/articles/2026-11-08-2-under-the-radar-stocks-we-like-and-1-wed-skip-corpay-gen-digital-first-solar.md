On Thursday the Nasdaq sank 5.5%, its worst day since April 2025, and in the past week the money has stayed fixed on a few dozen AI names ([our week in charts](/daily/2026-11-07/)). Meanwhile two mid-sized companies that most investors never think about reported quarters that beat forecasts and raised their outlooks, and both turn about 40 cents of every sales dollar into operating profit.

Our screen took companies in our coverage worth $5 billion to $30 billion, ranked them by operating margin over the latest 12 months from their SEC filings, and then judged the leaders on what decides quality: growth over five years and two, free cash flow, returns on capital, and the price against peers and their own history, using our fundamentals data. It left out stocks we listed in the last 14 days.

```picks
like: CPAY, GEN

```

## 2 stocks we like

### 1. Corpay ($CPAY)
**Operating margin: 43.9% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Corpay runs payment networks for businesses: fuel and fleet cards, cross-border payments for companies, and hotel booking for airline crews.

1. Its latest quarter sped up: revenue rose **18%** from a year earlier to $1.38 billion in the quarter to September, 11% on an organic basis, and it raised its 2026 forecast to $5.34 billion to $5.36 billion, its results release said. The stock rose 6.8% on 5 November, the day after.
2. The growth has lasted: revenue grew 13.6% a year over five fiscal years, against a median of 9.6% for the 46 payments, exchange and asset-management companies we compare it with, and its operating margin is in the top quarter of that group.
3. Profit reaches shareholders: free cash flow was 25.9% of revenue over two years, and buybacks cut the share count 6.4% in two years, about twice the group's median.

**The price:** 15.5 times expected earnings, below the group median of 17.9 times, though its trailing P/E of 25.0 is above its two-year median of 22.7.

**What would change our mind:** organic growth falling back toward single digits when it reports again in February; management said the third quarter included about one point of one-off gains that may not repeat, MarketBeat's summary of the call reported.

### 2. Gen Digital ($GEN)
**Operating margin: 41.8% in the 12 months to 2 October 2026.** Gen Digital sells Norton and Avast security software and LifeLock identity protection to households, mostly as subscriptions.

1. It beat and raised in late October: revenue rose 12% to $1.37 billion in its fiscal second quarter, and it lifted its full-year revenue forecast to $5.465 billion to $5.515 billion from $5.375 billion to $5.475 billion, its release said. The stock **jumped 15.8%** on 29 October in our data.
2. Its margin is in the top tenth of the 32 software companies we cover, against a median of 9.7%, and free cash flow reached 33.7% of revenue in the latest 12 months, above its two-year average of 31.6%.
3. Revenue grew 14.4% a year over five years, close to the software median of 15.9%, while buybacks cut the share count 3.1% in two years.

**The price:** 9.9 times expected earnings, against a software median of 26.7 times, and its trailing P/E of 15.0 is near the bottom of its two-year range of 12.1 to 33.2.

**What would change our mind:** debt. Net debt of $7.7 billion equals 3.5 years of operating profit, so a slowdown in subscriptions would bite; and its cost of revenue grew about twice as fast as revenue last quarter, Earnings Whispers noted.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### First Solar ($FSLR)
**Operating margin: 37.7% in the 12 months to September 2026.** First Solar makes thin-film solar panels in the US and abroad for utility-scale solar farms.

1. Sales are shrinking: revenue fell **23%** from a year earlier to $1.23 billion in the third quarter, and it cut its 2026 sales forecast to $4.6 billion to $4.9 billion from $4.9 billion to $5.2 billion, its release said, as PV Tech and Mercom also reported.
2. The margin doesn't arrive as cash: free cash flow was 4.6% of revenue in the latest 12 months, the lowest of the 25 chipmakers we group it with, against a median of 22.2%.
3. The stock already rallied on politics: it rose 4.6% on 4 November as Democrats won Congress, William Blair upgraded it the next day, and it has gained 12.5% in a month, though it remains 36.8% below its June high.

**The price:** 11.6 times trailing earnings, below its two-year median of 14.9, but 92.9 times free cash flow.

**What could go wrong:** the backlog. First Solar has 44 gigawatts of contracted sales worth about $13.5 billion, PV Tech and Mercom reported, and if that converts into cash faster, or a Democratic Congress strengthens solar support, the low earnings multiple would look cheap.
