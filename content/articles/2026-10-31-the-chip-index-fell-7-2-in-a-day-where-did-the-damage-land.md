On Friday, 30 October, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index **fell 7.2%**, NBC News and Reuters reported, the day after Intel ($INTC) issued a weak forecast and warned that a shortage of memory chips is choking demand for PCs ([our market wrap](/news/2026-10-30/stock-market-today-intels-worst-day-since-1974-sinks-the-nasdaq-2-9/)). The selling reached almost every kind of chip company: of the chip and chip-equipment stocks we cover, only Monolithic Power Systems ($MPWR) rose.

The index was on track for a monthly loss of more than 15%, Reuters reported. Here is where Friday's damage landed, group by group, using our closing prices.

## Intel and its processor rivals fell hardest

Intel **plunged 28.6%**, its worst day since 1974, Benzinga and CNN reported. Its rivals in processors, Qualcomm ($QCOM), AMD ($AMD) and Arm ($ARM), each fell about 10% to 11%.

Even without Intel, those three fell 10.7% on average, more than any other group. None of them had reported anything new ([our story](/news/2026-10-30/amd-arm-and-qualcomm-shares-slide-what-happened/)).

The groups: memory and storage is Micron, Sandisk, Western Digital and Seagate; AI and networking is Nvidia, Broadcom, Marvell, Astera Labs and Credo; servers and PCs is Dell, HP, Hewlett Packard Enterprise and Supermicro; equipment is Applied Materials, Lam Research, KLA, ASML and Teradyne; analog is Analog Devices, NXP, Microchip and ON Semiconductor.

## Memory and storage fell despite strong results

Sandisk ($SNDK) **plunged 13.5%**, the day after it beat estimates ([our story](/news/2026-10-30/sandisk-sndk-tops-fiscal-q1-estimates-as-data-center-sales-jump-61/)).

The hard-drive makers Seagate ($STX) and Western Digital ($WDC), and Micron ($MU), fell between 5% and 8%. In Seoul, SK hynix had already fallen more than 7% before New York opened ([our story on Seoul's week](/news/2026-10-31/koreas-kospi-jumped-6-6-then-fell-3-5-how-seouls-week-set-up-the-chip-rout/)).

## AI chips fell less, except the network makers

Nvidia ($NVDA) **fell 4.2%** and Broadcom ($AVGO) 5.5%.

The makers of chips that move data inside AI data centers fell harder. Marvell Technology ($MRVL) and Astera Labs ($ALAB) fell about 10% to 11%, and Credo Technology ($CRDO) 8.7%.

## Chipmaking machines and TSMC fell about 6% to 7%

The companies that sell chipmaking equipment, Applied Materials ($AMAT), Teradyne ($TER), ASML ($ASML), Lam Research ($LRCX) and KLA ($KLAC), **fell 5.7% to 7.5%**.

Taiwan Semiconductor's shares fell nearly 2% in Taipei, where trading ended before New York's selloff, Reuters reported. Its US-listed shares ($TSM) then fell 6.0%.

## Server and PC makers took Intel's PC warning

Dell ($DELL) **plunged 10.8%** and HP ($HPQ) 9.6%, as Intel's warning about the PC market hit the companies that build PCs ([our Dell story](/news/2026-10-30/why-dell-technologies-dell-stock-is-sinking-today/)).

Server makers Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE) and Supermicro ($SMCI) fell less, by 6.4% and 4.3%.

## Analog chips held up best

Analog Devices ($ADI), NXP Semiconductors ($NXPI), Microchip Technology ($MCHP) and ON Semiconductor ($ON) **fell 2.4% to 4.7%**. Monolithic Power Systems rose 1.1%.

## Nvidia did the most damage to the S&P 500

Percentage moves understate what the biggest companies did to the market.

By our estimate from Nasdaq closes and CNBC market values, Nvidia's drop took **0.31 point** off the S&P 500, more than Intel's 0.20 point.

Together, Nvidia, Intel, AMD and Broadcom accounted for more than 40% of the index's 1.82% fall, and the chip stocks in the index for more than half of it.

In dollars, Nvidia lost about $217 billion of market value on Friday and Intel about $139 billion, by our estimate. Benzinga and Tom's Hardware put the day's loss across chip stocks at about $1 trillion.

## Our read

We think Friday's damage followed two lines: exposure to PCs, and how far a stock had run. Intel, AMD, Marvell and Arm had all more than doubled this year before Friday, and each fell about 10% or more.

The rule wasn't perfect: Micron, up 227% this year before Friday, fell 5.3%. But the analog chipmakers, which had risen far less, fell least. That looks to us like investors cutting their biggest winners on a scare, not a verdict on AI demand: Nvidia, whose chips that demand pays for, fell 4.2%.
