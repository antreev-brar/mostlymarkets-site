On Friday, 30 October, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index **sank 7.2%**, NBC News and Reuters reported, the day after Intel ($INTC) issued a weak forecast and warned that a shortage of memory chips is choking demand for PCs ([our market wrap](/news/2026-10-30/stock-market-today-intels-worst-day-since-1974-sinks-the-nasdaq-2-9/)). Of the chip and chip-equipment stocks we track, only Monolithic Power Systems ($MPWR) rose.

## Intel and its processor rivals fell hardest

Intel **plunged 28.6%**, its worst day since 1974, CNN reported. Its three rivals in processors, Qualcomm ($QCOM), AMD ($AMD) and Arm ($ARM), fell 10.7% on average, more than any other group.

The groups: memory and storage is Micron, Sandisk, Western Digital and Seagate; AI and networking is Nvidia, Broadcom, Marvell, Astera Labs and Credo; servers and PCs is Dell, HP, Hewlett Packard Enterprise and Supermicro; equipment is Applied Materials, Lam Research, KLA, ASML and Teradyne; analog is Texas Instruments, Analog Devices, NXP, Microchip and ON Semiconductor.

None of the three rivals had reported anything new ([our story](/news/2026-10-30/amd-arm-and-qualcomm-shares-slide-what-happened/)). The link is memory: suppliers are steering production toward the high-bandwidth chips that AI servers use, which has pushed up the cost of PCs and weighed on demand, Reuters reported. Intel sees "the PC slowdown continuing at least through the first half of next year," Chief Financial Officer David Zinsner said in the results release.

Qualcomm had flagged the same pressure. Chief Executive Cristiano Amon said in the results release that its revenue for the June quarter reached the high end of its forecast "despite a challenging memory and supply environment." Its revenue from phone chips fell 20% from a year earlier that quarter, though the release didn't say why.

## Memory and storage fell despite strong results

Sandisk ($SNDK) **plunged 13.5%** the day after it beat estimates and gave a revenue forecast implying that its growth from the previous quarter would roughly halve, to about 12% ([our story](/news/2026-10-30/sandisk-sndk-tops-fiscal-q1-estimates-as-data-center-sales-jump-61/)).

Micron ($MU) and the hard-drive makers Seagate ($STX) and Western Digital ($WDC) fell 5% to 8%. In Seoul, SK hynix had already fallen more than 7% before New York opened ([Seoul's week](/news/2026-10-31/koreas-kospi-jumped-6-6-then-fell-3-5-how-seouls-week-set-up-the-chip-rout/)).

Micron and Sandisk trade at about five times their expected earnings for the next 12 months, on CNBC data, against 17 for Nvidia. We read that price as already assuming today's profits are the peak ([why memory stocks have fallen](/news/2026-10-31/why-memory-stocks-have-fallen-for-four-months-while-profits-hit-records/)).

## AI chips fell less, except the network makers

Nvidia ($NVDA) **slid 4.2%** and Broadcom ($AVGO) 5.5%.

The makers of chips that move data inside AI data centers fell harder: Marvell Technology ($MRVL) and Astera Labs ($ALAB) about 10% to 11%, and Credo Technology ($CRDO) 8.7%.

Across the five, the falls lined up with how much investors were paying for expected profits. Astera Labs, at 46 times its expected earnings at Friday's close, fell most; Nvidia, the cheapest of the five, fell least.

## Chipmaking machines and TSMC fell about 6% to 7%

Applied Materials ($AMAT), Teradyne ($TER), ASML ($ASML), Lam Research ($LRCX) and KLA ($KLAC), which sell the machines that make and test chips, **fell 5.7% to 7.5%**.

Taiwan Semiconductor's shares fell nearly 2% in Taipei, where trading ended before New York opened, Reuters reported. Its US-listed shares ($TSM) then slid 6.0%.

## Server and PC makers took Intel's PC warning

Dell ($DELL) **plunged 10.8%** and HP ($HPQ) 9.6%, as Intel's warning hit the companies that build PCs ([our Dell story](/news/2026-10-30/why-dell-technologies-dell-stock-is-sinking-today/)).

Server makers fell less: Hewlett Packard Enterprise ($HPE) 6.4% and Supermicro ($SMCI) 4.3%.

Here, PC exposure mattered more than the run-up: HP, up 43% this year before Friday, fell harder than Hewlett Packard Enterprise, which had more than doubled.

## Analog chips held up best

Texas Instruments ($TXN), Analog Devices ($ADI), NXP Semiconductors ($NXPI), Microchip Technology ($MCHP) and ON Semiconductor ($ON) **fell 2.4% to 4.7%**, and none had doubled this year. Monolithic Power Systems rose 1.1%.

## Nvidia did the most damage to the S&P 500

By our estimate, Nvidia's fall took **0.31 percentage point** off the S&P 500, more than Intel's: Nvidia was worth more than ten times as much at Thursday's close.

Chip and chip-equipment stocks made up 16% of the index's value at Thursday's close but did 58% of its fall, by our estimate.

Tom's Hardware and Benzinga put the market value chip stocks lost on the day at about $1 trillion.

## Our read

**We think Friday's damage followed two lines: exposure to PCs, and how far a stock had run.** Excluding Intel, the stocks in our groups that had more than doubled this year fell 9.1% on average, and the rest 6.3%.

Exposure to PCs and phones mattered even without a run-up: Qualcomm, up just 8% this year before Friday, fell 11.1%.

Micron is the exception: up 227% this year before Friday, it fell only 5.3%.

Neither line is about AI demand: only three of the 27 stocks in our groups fell less than Nvidia, the biggest maker of AI chips, and all three make analog chips.

What would change our mind is a weak AI forecast: AMD's on Tuesday is the first test, and Nvidia's on 17 November the biggest. Arm and Qualcomm on Wednesday will show how far the memory squeeze reaches into phones.
