## What happened
Shares of chip-equipment maker Lam Research ($LRCX) **rose 15.2%** by 10:15 a.m. ET on Monday, with Applied Materials ($AMAT) up 13.8% and chip-testing specialist Teradyne ($TER) up 15.4%. KLA ($KLAC) gained 13.5%, and Dutch lithography maker ASML ($ASML) was up 10.3% at 9:58 a.m. ET. Seeking Alpha and Bloomberg tied the jump to the rally in chip stocks after Nvidia ($NVDA) reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive before the open ([our story](/news/2026-11-23/nvidia-nvda-shares-soar-29-before-the-open-after-it-reinstates-jensen-huang/)).

None of the toolmakers put out news of their own on Monday. They rose with the chipmakers that buy their machines: TSMC ($TSM) was up 15.9% at the same time ([our story](/news/2026-11-23/tsmc-intel-and-marvell-shares-jump-what-happened/)).

## Why it matters
Equipment makers sit one step further down the AI chain than Nvidia's suppliers. Their sales depend on chipmakers' factory budgets for next year, so they rise and fall on whether investors believe AI demand will keep those budgets growing. That is why they fell hardest: Lam, Applied Materials, KLA and Teradyne all closed Friday about **58% below** their 30 June highs, against 34% for TSMC.

Monday's rebound doesn't change a factory plan. The next evidence on spending comes from the chipmakers and memory makers themselves, starting with Micron's report in December ([our analysis](/news/2026-11-21/wall-street-is-betting-the-memory-boom-has-peaked-microns-17-december-report-will-test-it/)).

## What the market is telling us
Lam's shares moved 5% or more on 59 of the last 252 sessions, and its last move at least as big as Friday's 10.0% fall was a 16.4% drop on 5 November. It closed Friday up 7.1% this year, at $183.27, 57.7% below its June closing high of $433.33. Even after Monday's jump, Lam would need to more than double to get back there, so we read the day as a rebound after a steep fall, not a vote on next year's orders.
