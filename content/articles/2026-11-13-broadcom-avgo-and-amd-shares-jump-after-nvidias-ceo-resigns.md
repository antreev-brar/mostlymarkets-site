## What happened
Shares of custom AI-chip designer Broadcom ($AVGO) **jumped 5.5%** and AMD ($AMD), Nvidia's closest rival in AI graphics chips, rose 5.9% on Thursday, after Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive of Nvidia ($NVDA). Traders bought the two on hopes they pick up orders if Nvidia's customers hesitate, mlq.ai and Proactive Investors reported, and Barron's ran the day as "Nvidia's loss is AMD and Broadcom's gain."

Hours before the resignation, William Blair analyst Sebastien Naji upgraded AMD to outperform and cut Nvidia to market perform, CNBC and Benzinga reported. "Nvidia is facing increased scrutiny and a potential leadership vacuum," he wrote, seeing a chance for AMD to win custom programs and sell more of its server processors alongside them. Broadcom closed at $364.36 and AMD at $585.30.

## Why it matters
Big buyers of AI chips tend to keep a second supplier, and both companies already have one foot in: AMD sells its Instinct accelerators, and Broadcom builds custom chips for cloud giants. A leaderless Nvidia gives buyers a reason to shift some orders, and the market priced that in quickly: since last Friday's close AMD is **up 30.3%** and Broadcom 24.1%, while Nvidia is down 11.0%, in our data.

That puts AMD's market value near $960 billion, about a quarter of Nvidia's. Nvidia's report on Tuesday 17 November is the first chance to see whether any orders actually moved.

## What the market is telling us
Broadcom's shares moved 5% or more on 19 of the last 252 sessions. The last move at least this big in the same direction was on 9 November 2026, when the stock rose 8.8%. The stock is up 5.3% this year, and at $364.36 it sits 24.3% below its 52-week closing high of $481.57 from 2 June 2026. AMD is up 173.3% this year and 9.9% below its October high.

Both moves rest on an expectation, not an order, and the stocks were giving some of it back before Friday's open, AMD down 1.9% and Broadcom 2.2% as Nvidia rose 2.8%.
