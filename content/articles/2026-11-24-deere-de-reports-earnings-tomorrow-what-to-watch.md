Deere ($DE) reports results for its fiscal fourth quarter, the three months to early November, before the open on Wednesday, 25 November. The shares **fell 3.0%** on Tuesday to $613.76, leaving them 13.5% below their September high, and the number that will move them is less the quarter than Deere's first forecast for fiscal 2027.

Last quarter, Deere earned **$5.10 a share**, up from $4.75 a year earlier, as revenue rose 5% to $12.61 billion, the company said in its 20 August release. That beat the $4.69 analysts expected, according to Investing.com, and Deere lifted the bottom of its fiscal 2026 net income forecast to a range of $4.75 billion to $5.00 billion. The stock jumped 6.9% that day.

## What analysts expect

The Zacks consensus calls for **$4.07 a share** on revenue of $10.30 billion, against $3.93 a share and $10.58 billion a year ago, on Zacks's equipment-sales basis; Barchart's survey also has $4.07. That would be earnings up about 4% on sales down about 3%.

Deere's own forecast frames it. It earned $3.81 billion in the first nine months, so its full-year range implies fourth-quarter net income of **$0.94 billion to $1.19 billion**.

## What to watch

- **The 2027 outlook.** In August Deere called 2026 the bottom of the cycle for farm machinery, according to Zacks and Transport Topics. A net income forecast for fiscal 2027 is the figure the stock will trade on; previews from Investing.com and TipRanks both frame the report as that test.
- **Large farm machinery.** Deere expects industry sales of big tractors and combines to fall 15% to 20% this fiscal year. Any comment on early orders for the 2027 model year will show whether farmers are ready to spend again.
- **Construction and forestry.** Sales in that segment rose 18% to $3.62 billion last quarter, the release showed, offsetting the farm slump. Whether that holds says how much of the recovery rests on farming alone.

## How the stock has done

Deere is **up 31.8% this year** but down 4.3% over the past month, and it sits 13.5% below its 52-week closing high of $709.48 from 23 September.

That run has made it expensive. At 29.3 times expected earnings, Deere trades well above the 19.9 times median for the industrial companies we cover, and above Caterpillar ($CAT) at 17.9 times, according to our data from its SEC filings. Its trailing multiple of 34.1 is near the top of its range since 2024, against a median of 24.8. A price like that assumes the 2027 recovery shows up in Wednesday's guidance, so a cautious forecast would leave little support under the shares.
