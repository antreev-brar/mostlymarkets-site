Merck ($MRK) said early on Friday that its Phase 3 TroFuse-015 trial of sacituzumab tirumotecan, or sac-TMT, **met its main goal of longer overall survival** in 473 patients with advanced stomach and esophageal cancer who had already had at least two rounds of treatment, against the chemotherapy their doctors chose. The drug, licensed from China's Kelun-Biotech, missed a secondary goal of delaying disease progression, the company said in a release reported by Reuters and Newsquawk.

Survival is the result regulators weigh most, and Merck said it is the first time a drug of this type, an antibody that delivers chemotherapy to cancer cells carrying a protein called TROP2, has shown it in a global Phase 3 trial in these patients. It is the second survival win for sac-TMT this year, after endometrial cancer, where it is under FDA priority review, OncLive and Fierce Pharma reported.

Merck did not give the size of the benefit; the full data go to an upcoming medical meeting. The shares were **up 0.5%** at $133.50 at 7:14 a.m. ET, after falling 10.1% over the previous four sessions in our data.
