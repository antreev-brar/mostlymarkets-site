Toyota ($TM) said on Thursday that its operating profit for July to September rose **34%** from a year earlier to 1.13 trillion yen, its first increase in six quarters and well above the 992 billion yen that the median of nine analysts polled by LSEG had expected, Reuters reported. It raised its full-year operating profit forecast to 3.9 trillion yen from 3.4 trillion yen. Toyota's US-listed shares rose 9.4% to $218.11 on Thursday, their best day since July 2025.

## What stood out

The quarter's profit came from selling more profitable cars, not more cars. Strong demand for hybrids carried the result, Reuters and the Seoul Economic Daily reported, and a weaker yen raised the value of the money Toyota earns abroad when it is counted in yen. A year earlier, the same quarter's operating profit had fallen 27% to 839.6 billion yen under the first full weight of US tariffs, Reuters reported at the time, so the comparison was easy.

Net profit went the other way, falling to 759.4 billion yen, about 19% lower than a year earlier, AP and Nippon.com reported. For a carmaker, operating profit is the cleaner read of how the business is doing, and that is the number the market traded.

## The outlook

The new forecast is what moved the stock. Toyota now expects **3.9 trillion yen** of operating profit for the year to March 2027, half a trillion more than in August, and 3.57 trillion yen of net profit, AP and Reuters reported. It trimmed its sales forecast to 53.5 trillion yen from 54 trillion yen, so the raise rests on margins, not volume.

Toyota also set out a plan to return as much as 14 trillion yen, about $89 billion, to shareholders by March 2031, CNBC and BusinessDay reported. Its shares in Tokyo rose more than 14%, Reuters and Sowetan reported.

## Our take

This was a good quarter: a 14% beat on operating profit and a forecast raise of about 15%, from a company whose earnings had fallen for five straight quarters. Even after Thursday, the US shares trade at **15.9 times** forward earnings against a median of 19.6 times for the consumer stocks we track, and they are 12.2% below their 52-week high. The risk is that much of the lift is the yen, which Toyota does not control; the next test is its full-year results in May, and whether hybrids keep their margins if the yen strengthens.
