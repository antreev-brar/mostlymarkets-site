*Arista is one of the best businesses in AI hardware, and after Friday's fall it trades at about its usual multiple of past earnings: what decides it is whether two customers, which bought 42% of what it sold last year, keep spending.*

Arista Networks ($ANET) **fell 11.2% to $168.30** by 12:15 p.m. ET on Friday, by our quotes, with no news from the company. Alphastreet put the drop down to sector-wide selling of networking and chip stocks, the same sell-off that [hit AI hardware suppliers](/news/2026-11-20/micron-arista-and-celestica-shares-slide-what-happened/) after Anthropic's shares slid on their second day of trading. That leaves the stock 22% below its 9 October closing high of $216.74.

## How it got here

Arista began in 2004, as Arastra, when Andy Bechtolsheim, David Cheriton and Kenneth Duda set out to build faster network switches for big data centers. Jayshree Ullal, a Cisco veteran, became chief executive in October 2008, and the company listed on the New York Stock Exchange in June 2014. Cisco sued it that December; Arista settled in August 2018 by paying **$400 million**, its 10-Q shows, which is why 2018 is the one dip in its operating margin.

The cloud built Arista, and AI rebuilt it. Revenue rose from $2.32 billion in 2020 to $9.01 billion in 2025, by our analysis of its SEC filings, and the third quarter of 2026 brought $3.66 billion, up 58.5%, its fastest growth since its IPO ([our recap](/news/2026-11-03/arista-networks-anet-beats-q3-estimates-as-sales-grow-58-5/)).

Since 5 November the stock has fallen at least 5% in a day three times without a word from the company. **Is the fall telling us something about Arista's business, or only about the market's mood on AI?**

## The business in one line

Cloud giants, AI labs and large companies pay Arista for Ethernet switches and the software that runs them, buying new hardware with each data-center build and paying for support every year after.

## How it makes money

Arista reports one segment, but it splits its sales by type of customer. The split shows how much rides on the biggest buyers.

| Customer group | What they buy | Share of 2025 sales |
|---|---|---|
| Cloud and AI titans (fewer than five buyers) | Switches for giant data centers and AI clusters | 48% |
| Enterprise and financial firms | Data-center, campus and Wi-Fi networks | 32% |
| AI and specialty providers (including Apple and Oracle) | AI clusters and cloud networks | 20% |

The mix comes from Arista's fourth-quarter call in February, as reported by the transcript and Next Platform. Products, mostly switches, were **$3.19 billion** of third-quarter revenue, up 67%, and services and software support made up the rest.

## The numbers that matter

### Growth
Revenue grew **31.2% a year** over the five fiscal years to 2025, against a median of 5.6% for the 31 hardware makers in our data, a top-tenth rank. Earnings per share compounded faster still, at 40.6% a year, because margins widened as it grew. *Verdict: growth few hardware companies have ever sustained.*

### Profitability
Arista kept **43.8%** of its sales as operating profit in the 12 months to September 2026, more than twice the peer median of 18.2% and above Cisco's ($CSCO) 26.3%. That margin is 12.6 points higher than five years ago. *Verdict: it earns software-like margins on hardware.*

### Cash
Free cash flow, the cash left after running the business and investing in it, was **42.2%** of sales over the past 12 months, against a peer median of 13.5%. The past two quarters ran lower, at about 32%, because inventory rose to $3.21 billion at 30 September, its 10-Q shows. *Verdict: excellent, with a stockpile to watch.*

### Returns
Return on equity was **28.9%**, above the 20.0% peer median. That understates the business, because $14.4 billion of the $16.4 billion of equity sits in cash and securities earning interest, not in the business. *Verdict: high returns, diluted by a cash pile.*

### Balance sheet
Arista had **$14.4 billion** of cash and marketable securities and no debt at 30 September, according to its results and call summaries. The share count fell 0.3% over two years: buybacks have roughly matched the new shares it issues. *Verdict: no balance-sheet risk.*

## Why the stock is where it is

From the worried investor's side, four forces explain the 22%:

1. **The AI trade turned.** Arista fell 10.8% on 5 November, when the [Nasdaq sank 5.5%](/news/2026-11-05/stock-market-today-nasdaq-sinks-5-5-into-a-correction-on-midterm-ai-fears/) on fears that the midterms would slow data-center building, 5.2% on 16 November and 11.2% by midday Friday, all without company news, Trefis and Alphastreet reported.
2. **Margins are being squeezed.** Gross margin fell to 64% in the third quarter from 65.2% a year earlier on rising part costs, and Arista guided 2027 to 62% to 64%, according to the call transcript and Investing.com.
3. **Nvidia is taking share.** Nvidia ($NVDA) had **20.4%** of data-center Ethernet switch sales in the second quarter, against Arista's 18.7%, research firm IDC said; Arista had 20.7% in the first quarter, Data Center Knowledge reported.
4. **Two customers carry the company.** Microsoft ($MSFT) bought 26% of Arista's 2025 sales and Meta ($META) 16%, according to its annual filing as reported by The Globe and Mail and BofA via Seeking Alpha, up from a combined 35% in 2024.

## Testing the worry

**The AI sell-off: temporary for the business, so far.** Arista raised its 2026 forecast for the fourth time this year on 3 November, to just over $13.3 billion, or about 48% growth, and gave a first 2027 outlook of 38% to 40% growth, about $18.5 billion, according to 247 Wall St and the call transcript. The company has not changed that outlook since; the falls tracked Nvidia, Anthropic and the Nasdaq.

**Margins: real, but industry-wide and contained.** The squeeze comes from memory and chip costs that hit every hardware maker: Cisco's gross margin fell 2.5 points in its latest quarter ([our recap](/news/2026-11-13/cisco-csco-beats-and-raises-as-ai-networking-sales-jump-43-but-margins-slip/)). Ullal chose the trade-off in May. "We see multiyear demand, and we are going to do everything, including hurt our gross margins, to supply to that demand this year and next year," she said on the first-quarter call, as reported by Stocktwits and XTB. Arista still guides its 2027 adjusted operating margin to about 49%, in line with the 49.1% of the third quarter, so the cost is absorbed lower down.

**Nvidia: lasting, and the worry that survives.** Nvidia sells its Spectrum-X switches alongside its own AI chips, and IDC's figures show it overtook Arista in data-center switching this year. Arista's sales still grew 58.5% in the third quarter because the market is growing faster than its share is shrinking: IDC put second-quarter Ethernet switch sales up 43.4%, to $18.9 billion. The risk is that the gap widens when that growth slows.

**Concentration: lasting, and rising.** Microsoft and Meta went from 35% of sales in 2024 to 42% in 2025. When the two biggest buyers of AI hardware spend, Arista wins; if either pauses, there is no customer of similar size to replace it.

*Verdict: two worries are about the market's mood and costs every rival shares; the two that survive are Nvidia and the customer list.*

## The moat

1. **One software system across every switch.** Arista runs a single operating system, EOS, on its whole range, which lets customers automate huge networks. The evidence is pricing power: a 63.5% gross margin over two years against the 43.2% peer median.
2. **Built into the biggest data centers.** Arista's cloud and AI titans bought 48% of its 2025 sales, and it said on the third-quarter call that its AI networking sales should roughly double to about $7 billion in 2027, according to TradingKey and SimpleTechTrend summaries in [our recap](/news/2026-11-03/arista-networks-anet-beats-q3-estimates-as-sales-grow-58-5/).
3. **Ethernet is winning inside AI clusters.** Ethernet switch sales grew 43.4% in the second quarter by IDC's count, and Ethernet is the only kind of switch Arista has ever built its business on.

The rivals: Nvidia leads data-center Ethernet with a 20.4% share to Arista's 18.7% (IDC, second quarter). Cisco grew revenue 12.4% a year over two years to Arista's 34.1%, by our data. Will Arista still be here, and stronger, in 10 years? Yes, as long as AI clusters stay on Ethernet, but probably as one of two big suppliers rather than the leader.

## The risks we'd watch

**1. A cut from Microsoft or Meta.** Microsoft bought 26% of 2025 sales. If it stayed at that share of the $18.5 billion 2027 outlook and then halved its orders, Arista would lose about $2.4 billion, 13% of sales (18.5 × 26% × 50%), and 2027 growth would fall from about 39% to about 21%. That is a worst case; the offsets are the enterprise and campus business, a third of sales, and the AI and specialty providers, which include Oracle and Apple. Even the worst case leaves Arista growing faster than the 16.6% two-year peer median.

**2. Share loss to Nvidia.** Every point of data-center share Arista gives up widens Nvidia's lead in the market that drives its growth; IDC's figures show two points lost between the first and second quarters.

**3. A parts bill if demand pauses.** Arista had committed to buy **$11.4 billion** of parts at 30 September, up from $9.7 billion in June, Yahoo Finance and GuruFocus reported from the call. That is about a year of sales at the current rate, so a pause in AI orders would leave it holding expensive parts, though its $14.4 billion of cash covers the bill.

*Verdict: the risks are about who buys, not about what Arista makes.*

At 32.7 times expected earnings, Arista costs about twice the peer median, so its earnings would have to roughly double for the multiple to look ordinary. That is less far-fetched than it sounds. If Arista hits its $18.5 billion 2027 outlook and keeps the 40.0% net margin of the past 12 months, it would earn about $7.4 billion, or about $5.78 a share on 1.28 billion shares, which puts Friday's price at about 29 times 2027 earnings.

*Verdict: the price assumes the 2027 outlook is met, not that it is beaten.*

## Our verdict

**Business: Strong. Price: About its usual price.** Arista grows faster than almost any hardware company, keeps more of each sale than Cisco, carries no debt and has raised its forecast four times this year. At 51.0 times past earnings it sits close to its median of 52.0 times since November 2024, so Friday's fall has taken out the premium of October, not created a bargain against its own history. What would change our mind is a cut in spending by Microsoft or Meta, or a lower 2027 outlook; the next test is when the two customers next report and update their spending plans, typically in late January, followed by Arista's own fourth-quarter results early next year.

The business has not broken; what the market is pricing is the risk that two customers decide its next two years.
