Four big reporters got very different receptions on Friday, 13 November. Cisco ($CSCO) **slid 6.2%** and Applied Materials ($AMAT) fell 2.7% even though both beat estimates and raised their forecasts, while Nu Holdings ($NU) soared 11.8% on a record profit and Nebius ($NBIS) plunged another 9.9% after trimming its 2026 outlook.

Our screen took the $10 billion-plus companies we recapped in the past seven days and judged each on our analysis of SEC filings: growth over five and two years, margins, cash flow, returns and valuation against peers and its own history.

```picks
like: CSCO, AMAT, NU
skip: NBIS
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Cisco ($CSCO)
**Revenue growth last quarter: 24%, to $18.5 billion (quarter to October 2026)**. Cisco sells the switches and routers that connect offices, data centers and AI clusters. It beat estimates and raised its fiscal 2027 revenue forecast to $74.8 billion to $75.8 billion, but its adjusted gross margin fell to 65.6% from 68.1% ([our recap](/news/2026-11-13/cisco-csco-beats-and-raises-as-ai-networking-sales-jump-43-but-margins-slip/)).

1. **Growth is speeding up after five slow years.** Revenue grew 4.9% a year over five fiscal years, behind most of the 31 hardware makers in our coverage, but 8.5% a year over the past two, and Cisco now expects $9 billion of AI revenue from cloud giants this fiscal year, more than double last year's.
2. **Even the lower margin is high.** Its gross margin was 64.7% over two years, in the top quarter of hardware makers against a 43.2% median, so the AI mix squeezes a cushion rather than a thin edge.
3. **The profits arrive as cash.** Free cash flow was 21.7% of revenue over two years, against a 13.4% median.

**The price:** 18.6 times forward earnings, about the 18.0 median for hardware makers, and 28.6 times trailing earnings against its own median of 28.0 since 2024. The stock is 26.4% below its June high.

**What could go wrong:** earnings per share grew only 5.9% a year over five years, in the bottom tenth of its group. If the gross margin slips below the 65% to 66% Cisco guided for its February quarter, the AI growth would stop reaching the bottom line.

### 2. Applied Materials ($AMAT)
**Revenue growth last quarter: 53%, to a record $10.41 billion (quarter to October 2026)**. Applied makes the deposition and etch machines chip factories use. Memory makers buying for AI drove the beat, and it guided to $11.1 billion next quarter ([our recap](/news/2026-11-13/applied-materials-amat-tops-q4-estimates-as-memory-chipmakers-spend-more/)).

1. **Each dollar it invests earns more than its peers'.** Return on capital was about 27.4%, in the top quarter of the 25 chipmakers we cover, against a 17.8% median.
2. **It shrinks the share count.** Diluted shares fell 4.0% over two years, against a slight rise for the median chipmaker, and it bought back $2.0 billion of stock last quarter.
3. **The lag is ending.** Revenue grew 7.2% a year over two years, a third of the 22.0% chipmaker median, but sales in the latest quarter were 53% above a year earlier.

**The price:** 20.8 times forward earnings, below the 22.3 median for chipmakers. On trailing earnings it trades at 32.0 times, above its own median of 25.9 since 2024, and the stock sits 48.8% below its June record.

**What could go wrong:** free cash flow was 18.2% of revenue over the past 12 months, below the 22.2% median, and memory makers cut equipment orders fast when chip prices turn.

### 3. Nu Holdings ($NU)
**Net income: a record $1.1 billion, up 31% currency-neutral (quarter to September 2026)**. Nu is the Brazilian digital bank with 143 million customers across Brazil, Mexico and Colombia. It added 3.7 million customers in the quarter and its efficiency ratio, costs as a share of revenue, improved to 19.3% from 20.3% ([our recap](/news/2026-11-13/nu-holdings-nu-q3-profit-hits-a-record-1-1-billion-as-it-adds-3-7-million-customers/)).

1. **No bank we cover has grown faster.** Revenue grew 79.8% a year over the five fiscal years to 2024, the fastest of the 15 banks in our data, against a 4.3% median.
2. **It earns a lot on its capital.** Return on equity was 32% in the quarter, the company said.

**The price:** 13.7 times forward earnings against an 11.0 median for banks. The stock is 29.5% below its January high.

**What could go wrong:** Nu US and Nu Global launched this quarter and will spend before they earn; a rise in the efficiency ratio next quarter would be the first sign that growth is getting expensive.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Nebius ($NBIS)
**Revenue growth last quarter: 571%, to $980.8 million (quarter to September 2026)**. Nebius rents out Nvidia-powered computing for AI. It beat estimates, but said 2026 sales would land at the low end of $3 billion to $3.4 billion as delays at other companies' data centers push revenue into 2027 ([our recap](/news/2026-11-13/nebius-nbis-q3-sales-jump-571-but-it-trims-its-2026-outlook-and-plunges-17/)).

1. **Spending dwarfs sales.** It raised 2026 capital spending to about $27 billion, about nine times this year's expected revenue, and set 2027 at $40 billion to $45 billion.
2. **Profit is years away.** It has no profits in our data and expects to reach pre-tax breakeven by the end of 2027, Reuters reported.
3. **It doesn't control its own schedule.** The delays sit in buildings others are putting up, so the timetable can slip again.

**The price:** no earnings to measure, and 6.9 times book value at the end of 2025. The stock is 59.6% below its June high but still up 38.4% this year.

**What would change our mind:** the delayed Microsoft capacity going live on the new schedule, with more than half due by the end of 2026, and revenue reaching its promised run rate of more than $7 billion by February 2027.
