CrowdStrike ($CRWD) agreed before dawn on Monday to buy rival Zscaler ($ZS) for about **$43.9 billion** in its own shares, three days after Bloomberg reported the talks, and reported a record quarter alongside it. Zscaler rose 13.4% before the open and CrowdStrike fell 9.3%, as of 6:15 a.m. ET in our data: the market likes the price for the seller more than the deal for the buyer.

The rest of the market is bouncing after the S&P 500's worst week since April 2025 left it in a correction. Nvidia ($NVDA) led the rebound after President Donald Trump said he hopes its board rehires Jensen Huang, and Asian regulators moved to steady their own markets. The week's tests are still ahead: HPE reports tonight, Dell on Tuesday and PCE inflation on Wednesday, before markets close on Thursday for Thanksgiving.

Trump wrote on Truth Social that he hopes Nvidia's board **rehires Jensen Huang**, Yahoo Finance and Forbes reported, five days after board chair Harvey Jones said Nvidia would not reinstate him. The board has not changed its position, and Huang still faces a California probe that its attorney general says covers possible criminal violations ([our brief](/news/2026-11-21/californias-nvidia-nvda-probe-covers-possible-criminal-violations-bonta-says/)). A bounce built on a post can fade as fast as it came.

S&P 500 futures **rose about 2.5%** and Nasdaq 100 futures 3.3% early on Monday, Reuters reported, as the WSJ described futures rallying "as dust settles." Regulators in Asia stepped in over the weekend, with Taiwan loosening its margin-call rules so fewer borrowers are forced to sell, Reuters and the WSJ reported.

### CrowdStrike will pay 1.135 of its shares for each Zscaler share

```stat
[{"value": "$43.9bn", "label": "The deal's enterprise value", "note": "All in CrowdStrike stock"},
 {"value": "34%", "label": "Premium to Friday's close", "note": "$260.41 a share at CrowdStrike's 20 November price", "tone": "up"},
 {"value": "$10bn+", "label": "Combined annual recurring revenue", "note": "At the start, CrowdStrike said"}]
```

Each Zscaler share becomes **1.135 CrowdStrike shares**, worth $260.41 at Friday's prices, the companies said, as CNBC and Reuters reported, a price equal to almost a fifth of CrowdStrike's own $235 billion market value in our data. CrowdStrike is buying Zscaler's network security, the zero-trust service that checks every user before letting them reach company systems, to sit next to its own endpoint software. It targets $1 billion of extra yearly revenue within 30 months and $500 million of yearly cost savings within 18 to 24 months ([what CrowdStrike is buying](/news/2026-11-21/zscaler-zs-is-a-takeover-target-what-would-crowdstrike-be-buying/)).

Goldman Sachs's strategists said the correction could **deepen** unless one of three things happens: more confidence in AI earnings, a friendlier Fed, or a bigger washout in investor positioning, Fortune and Seeking Alpha reported. Morgan Stanley's Mike Wilson called the drop a "fat pitch" for buyers and kept his 8,000 target, CNBC and MarketWatch reported. The two agree that AI stocks led the fall; they disagree on whether the selling is nearly done.
