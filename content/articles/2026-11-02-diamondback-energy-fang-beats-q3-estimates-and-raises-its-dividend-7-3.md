Diamondback Energy ($FANG) said after the close on Monday that it earned an adjusted **$5.86 a share** in the third quarter, ahead of the $5.60 consensus of S&P analysts, StockStory reported; other tallies, from Investing.com and MarketBeat, ran from $5.46 to $5.72. Revenue rose 38% from a year earlier to $5.43 billion, against the $4.87 billion consensus, and the Permian Basin oil producer raised its base dividend 7.3%. The shares, which **fell 5.8%** to $199.74 in the regular session as US crude dropped, were up 1.8% after hours at 4:04 p.m. ET.

## What stood out

The quarter turned high oil into cash. Diamondback sold its oil for an average of **$87.30 a barrel**, a third more than a year earlier, and its cash operating costs rose only to $10.91 per barrel of oil equivalent from $10.05, the release showed. Free cash flow of $2.1 billion was about twice its $1.0 billion of capital spending.

It handed out $582 million of that, through the dividend and $251 million of buybacks at an average $185.94 a share, and it used more to cut debt: it redeemed $698 million of 2026 notes at par on 1 October and bought back long-dated bonds below face value. Net debt stood at $12.8 billion at the end of September.

## The outlook

Diamondback raised its 2026 oil production forecast to about 524,000 barrels a day, from 522,000 or more, and its total output forecast to about 1.015 million barrels of oil equivalent a day. Capital spending rises slightly, to $3.95 billion to $4.00 billion from about $3.9 billion. For the fourth quarter it expects oil output of 518,000 to 528,000 barrels a day.

## The one debate

The debate is the oil price, not the company. US crude **fell 7.9%** to $95.87 on Monday in our data on hopes for diplomacy with Iran, OilPrice.com and Finimize reported, and Diamondback's 5.8% drop was its biggest since 16 September. The third quarter's $87.30 realized price is already below where crude trades today, so the fourth quarter starts from a high base unless a peace deal takes the war premium out of oil.

## Our take

A clean beat with higher output, a bigger dividend and lower debt, from a producer whose stock trades at 9.3 times forward earnings against a peer median of 12.2. The shares are up 32.9% this year on the oil spike, so they will follow the Iran talks more than this report; the next marker is Wednesday's government inventory data.
