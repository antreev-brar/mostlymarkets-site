Altria ($MO), the maker of Marlboro in the US, confirmed early on Thursday that it is considering an offer for Britain's Imperial Brands, and that any offer would likely be **entirely in cash**, according to a statement under UK takeover rules reported by Reuters and Dow Jones. Altria said it is also in talks with an unnamed third party about that party buying part of Imperial's business, and that no agreement has been reached and no offer is certain.

Imperial's shares jumped to their highest level since 2017 in London, Investing.com reported, as Bloomberg also said Japan Tobacco was studying a deal. A deal would take Altria back outside the US for the first time since it spun off Philip Morris International ($PM) in 2008, buying cigarette sales abroad to offset shrinking volumes at home. Altria is worth about **$127 billion** in our data, and its shares are up 32.3% this year.

US markets are shut for Thanksgiving, so Altria's own shares first trade on the news in Friday's shortened session. The price is not known yet: under the Takeover Panel's rules Altria has until 5 p.m. London time on 24 December to make a firm offer or walk away, and only the panel can extend that.
