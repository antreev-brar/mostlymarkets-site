Dominion Energy ($D), the Virginia-based utility whose territory is crowded with data centers, reported third-quarter operating earnings of $1.28 a share on Friday, 30 October, up from $1.06 a year earlier and above the $1.20 Zacks consensus cited by Yahoo Finance; Investing.com put the bar at $1.19. Revenue of $5.62 billion beat the roughly $4.8 billion expected. The stock **rose 2.3%** to $66.62 on Friday, then slipped 1.6% to $65.57 after hours, Investing.com reported.

## What stood out

Part of the beat won't repeat. Operating earnings included **10 cents** a share from two one-offs, income tied to renewable natural gas tax credits and better-than-normal weather, Investing.com and the release showed. Without them, EPS of $1.18 would have been about in line with estimates.

The lasting number is data-center demand. Contracted data-center capacity at Dominion Energy Virginia reached 54.9 gigawatts, up 13% from December 2025, the company's slides showed, according to Investing.com and KuCoin. A gigawatt is about the output of a large power plant, so those contracts are a claim on decades of new generation and transmission that Dominion can put in its rate base, the asset value regulators let it earn a return on.

Its offshore wind project off Virginia's coast is 85% complete, with 44 turbines installed, the slides showed. GuruFocus flagged rising operating costs and costs regulators won't let it recover on its offshore wind work as a drag on the outlook.

## The outlook

Dominion now expects 2026 operating earnings in the **top half** of its $3.45 to $3.69 range and reaffirmed its long-term growth, dividend and credit guidance, the release said.

The stock is up 13.7% this year and rose 10.1% in October, by our price files, but sits 7.1% below its 52-week closing high of $71.69 from 16 July. It trades at 17.5 times expected earnings, against a median of 18.6 for the utilities we cover.

## Our take

A beat flattered by weather and tax credits, but the guidance raise and a 13% jump in contracted data-center capacity are the parts that compound. The risk is the cost of building for that demand: net debt was $51.4 billion at the end of March, about 11 years of operating profit, by our count, so next quarter we'd watch offshore wind costs and what regulators let Dominion charge for them.
