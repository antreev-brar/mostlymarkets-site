Thursday was the day the market decided the midterms were a problem for AI. Investors sold the chipmakers, memory makers and equipment suppliers that had led the bull market, on fears that the Democrats who swept Congress and the Texas governor's race will slow the building of data centers. The Nasdaq Composite **sank 5.5%** into a correction, its worst day since April 2025, and the selling rolled on through Tokyo, Seoul and Taipei overnight.

By 6:15 a.m. ET the mood had turned. Micron ($MU) was up 7.9% and Nvidia ($NVDA) 4.2% before the open in our data, and Nasdaq-100 futures were up about 1%, Yahoo Finance and CNBC reported. Whether that bounce holds now depends on one number: the October jobs report at 8:30 a.m. ET, which decides how likely a December rate hike is.

### Chip stocks had their worst day since March 2020

The Philadelphia Semiconductor index fell about 12%, its worst day since March 2020, Benzinga and BigGo Finance reported. Sandisk ($SNDK) **plunged 22.3%**, Micron 17.5% and AMD ($AMD) 17.3%, and Micron's drop was its third-biggest of the year ([our chip story](/news/2026-11-05/sandisk-micron-and-amd-shares-plunge-what-happened/)). The trigger was Texas, where the governor-elect wants to keep new data-center permits paused, and the state holds about 27% of planned US capacity, CNBC reported, citing Wells Fargo.

### Nvidia did the most damage, even falling less

Nvidia fell 9.3% to $181.60, its worst day since 27 January 2025, about half Micron's drop, but its size made it the S&P 500's biggest drag. It now sits **24.1%** below its 6 October closing high. The S&P 500 lost 4.4% in all ([our market wrap](/news/2026-11-05/stock-market-today-nasdaq-sinks-5-5-into-a-correction-on-midterm-ai-fears/)).

Cloudflare ($NET) was **up 17.0%** at $286.00 before the open after third-quarter revenue grew 39% to $782.5 million, its fastest in nearly four years ([our recap](/news/2026-11-05/cloudflare-net-jumps-after-hours-as-q3-revenue-growth-speeds-up-to-39/)). Both had fallen in Thursday's regular session.

### Atlassian beat, and is down 13.5% anyway

Atlassian ($TEAM) beat estimates for revenue and adjusted earnings, but forecast subscription growth slowing to about 18.5% for its fiscal year, SiliconANGLE and Seeking Alpha reported. After a run in which the stock nearly doubled from August to late October, slower growth was enough to send it down. It is a reminder that this sell-off punishes any sign of slowing, in software as well as chips.

AppLovin ($APP) **soared 31.0%** to $356.10 after its revenue rose 55%, its best day since 7 November 2024 ([our recap](/news/2026-11-05/applovin-app-soars-31-as-q3-sales-rise-55-and-its-forecast-tops-estimates/)). It sells ad software, not data-center parts, so the Texas fear does not touch it. We think that split is the clearest read of Thursday: the market sold the buildout's suppliers, not every company with AI in its pitch, and it is still down 47.2% this year.

## What's next

All times ET.

- **8:30 a.m. Friday:** the October jobs report. Economists expect about 65,000 jobs and an unemployment rate of 4.2%, according to FactSet and Dow Jones estimates cited by CNBC and FXStreet. Futures-market odds of a December rate hike fell from about 83% to 66% on Thursday, CNBC reported, citing CME FedWatch; a strong number would push them back up.
- **Before the open Friday:** Humana ($HUM), which reported at about 6 a.m., plus Moderna ($MRNA), Vistra ($VST), PPL ($PPL), Charter ($CHTR) and Franklin Resources ($BEN).
- **Saturday:** Berkshire Hathaway's ($BRK.B) third-quarter results.
- **Next week:** whether a governor, legislature or company names a specific data-center delay, the evidence the sell-off has so far priced in without seeing ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-05/wall-street-is-betting-the-midterms-will-slow-the-ai-buildout-3-numbers-will-decide-it/)).
