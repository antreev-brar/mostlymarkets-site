Apple ($AAPL) reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $114.5 billion on Monday, **up 12% year on year** and ahead of the $113.4 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC. Diluted EPS of $2.10 beat the $1.99 forecast. But the shares **fell 2.8% after hours** to $328.81 by 8 p.m. ET, after Apple guided its holiday-quarter gross margin below estimates because memory chips are getting more expensive.

## What stood out

The iPhone did the work. Its revenue **rose 17% to $57.6 billion**, a September-quarter record, with only about eight days of iPhone 18 Pro sales inside the quarter, CNBC reported. Mac sales rose 24% to $10.8 billion, while iPad fell 10% and wearables, home and accessories fell 8%, the release shows.

Greater China was the soft spot against forecasts. Sales there rose 21% to $17.5 billion, but that was short of the roughly $18.7 billion analysts had pencilled in, Yahoo Finance reported. Services grew 10% to $31.6 billion, an all-time high, and Chief Executive John Ternus, reporting for the first time since he succeeded Tim Cook, called fiscal 2026 "our best fiscal year ever" at $479 billion of revenue.

The cost side is moving too. Research and development spending **rose 32% to $11.7 billion** in the quarter, and inventories ended September at $14.5 billion against $5.7 billion a year earlier, the balance sheet in the release shows.

## The outlook

Apple guided December-quarter revenue up 7% to 10%, above the 6.1% growth LSEG's poll expected, according to Investing.com and CNBC. The problem was the margin: Chief Financial Officer Kevan Parekh guided gross margin to **45% to 46%**, against an average estimate of 46.3% (CNBC) and 47% in Bloomberg's survey, cited by TheStreet. He tied it mainly to higher memory costs that come with the ramp of the new Siri AI.

Apple also said it expects memory prices to keep rising after December, with less help from inventory it bought earlier at lower prices, the Seoul Economic Daily and Investing.com's call transcript reported. Services growth is guided to slow to 8% to 9%.

## Our take

The demand half of the quarter was strong: a 17% iPhone gain before the new Pro models had a full quarter is the best sign yet that the upgrade cycle is real. The margin half is what moved the stock, because the guide sits below September's 47.9% even after the one-off point from tariff refunds, and Apple says memory costs keep climbing past December. At 38.8 times trailing earnings, above its median of 35.5 times since August 2024, as our [breakdown](/news/2026-11-01/apple-aapl-became-wall-streets-ai-haven-is-the-business-worth-the-price/) set out, the price leaves little room for a margin slide; the March-quarter guide at the next report is the test of whether memory costs ease or keep eating the iPhone's gains.
