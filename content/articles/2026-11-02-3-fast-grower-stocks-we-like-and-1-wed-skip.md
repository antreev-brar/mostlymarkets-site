Friday's chip rout hit the fastest growers hardest: Astera Labs fell 10.9% and Credo 8.7% on Friday 30 October, and both are now about half their June highs. This week several of them report, so we went back to the numbers to separate growth that makes money from growth that burns it.

Our screen ranked the companies we cover by revenue growth over their latest 12 months of SEC filings, then judged the leaders on margins, free cash flow and dilution against their industry peers, using our own data from SEC filings. We passed over the memory makers, which we covered in last week's lists.

```picks
like: CRDO, ALAB, NVDA
skip: IONQ
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Credo Technology ($CRDO)
**Revenue growth: 165%, in the 12 months to 1 August 2026.** Credo makes active electrical cables, the copper links that connect AI servers inside a data center, and the chips in them.
1. Sales grew 86.8% a year over five fiscal years, the fastest of the 22 chipmakers we cover with five years of data, against a median of 14.2%.
2. It turns that into cash: its operating margin was 31.7% over the last 12 months, up 76 points in five years, and free cash flow was 27.6% of sales.
3. The worry is new: on 22 October MaxLinear said it would compete for a cable market where it put Credo's share at 93% to 99%, and Credo's stock fell sharply the next day, TIKR and Stocktwits reported. MaxLinear expects its first revenue from the newest cables only in 2027.

**The price:** 20.2 times forward earnings, below the chipmaker median of 24.5.
**What would change our mind:** a drop in gross margin from its two-year average of 66.8%, the first sign that a rival is forcing prices down.

### 2. Astera Labs ($ALAB)
**Revenue growth: 98.5%, in the 12 months to June 2026.** Astera sells connectivity chips that move data between AI processors, memory and networks inside servers.
1. Its gross margin was 75.3% over two years, in the top tenth of our chipmakers and 20 points above their median.
2. Free cash flow was 26.9% of sales over two years, in the top quarter.
3. It guided to third-quarter revenue of $540 million to $560 million in its August results, against $230.6 million a year earlier and $392.4 million in the second quarter, its filings show.

**The price:** 43.7 times forward earnings, well above the peer median, though the stock is 50% below its 30 June high.
**What would change our mind:** dilution. Its share count rose 18.1% in two years; another year like that would eat much of the growth per share.

### 3. Nvidia ($NVDA)
**Revenue growth: 83.4%, in the 12 months to July 2026.** The largest company here, and still growing like a small one.
1. Revenue grew 66.9% a year over five fiscal years, while earnings per share grew 95.2% a year.
2. Its operating margin was 65.2% over the last 12 months, against a peer median of 29.6%, and it has cut its share count by 2.3% in two years.
3. It fell 4.2% in Friday's rout and is 14.5% below its 6 October closing high, by our price data.

**The price:** 25.9 times trailing earnings, the bottom of its range since August 2024 (median 46.0), and 17.1 times forward earnings.
**What would change our mind:** a fall in its free cash flow margin, now 41.9%, below 35%, which would say its customers had stopped paying up.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### IonQ ($IONQ)
**Revenue growth: 371%, in the 12 months to June 2026.** IonQ builds quantum computers and sells access to them.
1. It lost $4.08 of operating profit for every $1 of revenue over the last 12 months: an operating margin of -408%, against a median of 16.9% for the hardware makers we cover.
2. Free cash flow was -196% of sales, about $484 million spent against $246 million of revenue.
3. Its diluted share count rose 73.7% in two years, so each share's slice of the future has shrunk fast.

**The price:** 3.8 times book value with no profits, and the stock is 58% below its 29 May high.
**What could go wrong:** a breakthrough contract that lifts revenue far faster than costs. IonQ reports third-quarter results on Thursday 5 November after the close, the company said.
