On Tuesday, Bank of America's November survey showed fund managers cut their tech holdings by the most since 2006, Bloomberg and CNBC reported. When investors sell growth wholesale, businesses that have compounded for years get sold with ones that only had a good quarter.

We took the large US stocks with an **operating margin above 20%** over the 12 months to their latest quarter and ranked them on revenue growth over the same period, from SEC filings in our fundamentals data, leaving out stocks we listed in the last 14 days. The median of the 16 grew 22.1%. We then checked five-year growth, cash and returns against each company's industry. We passed over Lumentum, which has agreed to an all-stock takeover by Marvell, and Nvidia, which reports tonight ([our preview](/news/2026-11-16/what-to-expect-from-nvidias-nvda-q3-earnings/)).

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Intuitive Surgical ($ISRG)
**Revenue growth: 20.5% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Intuitive makes the da Vinci surgical robot and sells the instruments used in every operation.

1. **Growth that has lasted.** Sales grew **18.2% a year** over five years, against a 10.0% median for the 19 medical device makers we cover, and third-quarter revenue rose 22% to $3.05 billion, its results release showed.
2. **Real cash.** Free cash flow was 27.7% of sales over two years, in the top tenth of its peers, whose median is 16.5%. That held through a $450 million third-quarter charge to settle a hospital antitrust suit, MLex and Cohen Milstein reported.
3. **The scare was about pace, not demand.** The stock fell 14.1% on 17 July after US da Vinci procedure growth slowed to 12%, Medtech Dive and Reuters reported. In the third quarter, systems placed rose 28% to 546.

**The price:** 52.9 times trailing earnings, against its own median of 68.1 since October 2024, though still 37.5 times expected earnings against a 20.1 peer median.

**What could go wrong:** US procedure growth slowed to 10% in the third quarter, and planned price cuts will lower instrument revenue per procedure by 3% to 5%, the release said. A weak 2027 procedure forecast in its next report would make the premium hard to defend.

### 2. Howmet Aerospace ($HWM)
**Revenue growth: 22.0% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Howmet makes turbine blades, castings and fasteners for jet engines and gas turbines.

1. **Profit compounds faster than sales.** Earnings per share grew **44.4% a year** over five years, in the top tenth of the 89 industrials we cover, as its operating margin rose 12.9 points to 28.1%.
2. **The latest quarter beat and raised.** Third-quarter adjusted earnings rose 54% to $1.46 a share against the $1.37 consensus, and Howmet lifted its 2026 revenue forecast to about $10.3 billion and pointed to about $11.5 billion in 2027, its release, Zacks and Finimize reported.
3. **The two scares didn't touch the contracts.** It fell 7.5% on 31 August after SpaceX said it would cast its own turbine blades, TIKR and Crypto Briefing reported, and 10.7% on 8 September after GE Aerospace agreed to buy rival caster CPP for nearly $12 billion, Stockopedia and TIKR reported.

**The price:** 41.7 times trailing earnings, near the bottom of its 39.0 to 71.5 range since late 2024, though 36.0 times expected earnings against a 21.1 peer median.

**What could go wrong:** GE steering new engine programs to its own caster; a 2027 forecast cut below $11.5 billion would be the first sign.

### 3. Alphabet ($GOOGL)
**Revenue growth: 22.2% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Alphabet owns Google, YouTube and Google Cloud.

1. **Faster, not slower.** Third-quarter sales grew **24.7%**, the fastest in our two years of quarterly data, as Cloud revenue more than doubled, the release showed ([our breakdown](/news/2026-11-07/alphabet-googl-had-its-worst-day-since-2004-is-the-business-broken/)).
2. **The widest margin of its peers.** It kept 33.3% of sales as operating profit over 12 months, top of the 11 consumer internet companies we rank, against a 13.1% median, and 9.4 points more than five years ago.
3. **The cash is the catch.** Free cash flow was just 4.2% of sales as quarterly capital spending reached $51.36 billion, and the stock fell 12.6% on 29 October, its worst day since 2004.

**The price:** 21.5 times expected earnings against an 18.7 peer median. Its trailing P/E of 14.8 is flattered by gains on stakes such as Anthropic, which prices its IPO this week ([our story](/news/2026-11-17/anthropic-is-set-to-price-its-ipo-above-its-70-to-78-range/)).

**What could go wrong:** spending that outruns its operating cash flow for another year; single-digit search growth would break the case.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Microchip Technology ($MCHP)
**Revenue growth: 33.0% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Microchip makes microcontrollers and analog chips for cars, factories and appliances.

1. **A rebound, not a compounder.** Sales shrank **2.8% a year** over five years, in the bottom tenth of the 25 chipmakers we cover, whose median grew 14.2%. Its 42.2% September-quarter jump, to $1.622 billion, is a recovery from a slump, its release showed.
2. **Per-share earnings went backwards.** EPS fell 19.5% a year over five years, and return on capital is 7.9%, against a 17.8% median.
3. **The recovery stalled this month.** Its December-quarter forecast of $1.573 billion to $1.638 billion fell short of the roughly $1.62 billion to $1.63 billion expected, and about $53 million of September sales won't recur, TIKR and Weiss Ratings reported. The stock fell 13.9% on 5 November and 10.0% the next day.

**The price:** 15.6 times expected earnings, cheaper than three in four chipmakers, but net debt is 4.3 years of operating profit.

**What would change our mind:** a forecast in its next report showing growth beyond the one-off sales.
