Almost all Federal Reserve officials expect further interest-rate increases to be needed if the economy evolves as they expect, according to the minutes of the 27-28 October meeting released at 2 p.m. ET on Wednesday, CNBC and Reuters reported. A couple of officials said a hike **larger than a quarter point** could be considered at the next meeting if inflation data disappoint.

The Fed held its target range at 3.75% to 4.00% in October in a 10-2 vote, with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack wanting a quarter-point hike ([our Fed story](/news/2026-10-28/fed-held-rates-but-two-officials-wanted-a-hike/)). The minutes add a new worry: many officials saw the AI buildout as "a material source of inflation risk", and some flagged fast-growing, opaque AI-related debt that could amplify losses if returns disappoint, CNBC and Quartz reported.

That backs Governor Christopher Waller, who said on Wednesday that a half-point hike is probably needed ([the debate](/news/2026-11-18/the-debate-over-decembers-hike-waller-says-50-williams-says-no-dramatic-action/)). The Fed decides on 9 December.
