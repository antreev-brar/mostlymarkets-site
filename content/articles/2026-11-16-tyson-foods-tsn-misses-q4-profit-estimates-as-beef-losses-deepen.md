Tyson Foods ($TSN) reported fiscal fourth-quarter sales of $14.59 billion before the open on Monday, up 5.2% year on year thanks to an extra week in the quarter, but its adjusted earnings of **$0.61 a share fell 47%** and missed the $0.65 Zacks consensus. It also guided to a steeper sales drop for fiscal 2027 than Wall Street expected, as a shortage of cattle keeps its beef business losing money. The shares were up 1.1% at $59.44 in pre-market trading as of 9:08 a.m. ET.

## What stood out

The quarter split Tyson in two. Beef sales volumes fell 6% and the segment's reported operating loss widened to **$514 million** from $277 million, according to the release; Reuters tied the squeeze to US cattle herds at a 75-year low, which drove up what processors pay for animals. Chicken, the business that carried Tyson through the beef downturn, saw its adjusted operating margin drop to 8.7% from 12.7%.

Prepared Foods was the bright spot. The unit behind Jimmy Dean and Hillshire Farm lifted adjusted operating income to $321 million, a 12.6% margin, up from 10.9%. Chief Executive Jeff Schomburger credited "the strength of our diversified multiprotein model" in the release, and the company cut total debt by $1.08 billion over the fiscal year.

## The outlook

Tyson expects fiscal 2027 sales to **fall 3.5% to 5.5%**, against analysts' average forecast of a 1% decline, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters. It blamed lower beef volumes from closing and restructuring plants and from tight cattle supplies, with the USDA projecting domestic beef production down about 2%.

The profit picture is less bleak than the sales line. Guidance for adjusted operating income of $1.7 billion to $2.15 billion brackets this year's $1.98 billion: beef's loss should narrow to between $300 million and a $100 million profit, from $654 million, but chicken is guided to $1.3 billion to $1.6 billion, below this year's $1.86 billion. Free cash flow is expected at $0.8 billion to $1.3 billion, down from $1.66 billion, and the board raised the annual dividend by 2% to $2.08 a share.

## Our take

This was a weak quarter and a cautious outlook: Tyson missed on profit, beef keeps losing money, and the chicken profits that cushioned it are guided lower. The shares sat 14.4% below their 52-week closing high on Friday and trade at 17.4 times forward earnings, the median for packaged-food peers, even though Tyson's 2.3% operating margin over the last 12 months ranks near the bottom of the group in our data. The quiet pre-market reaction suggests investors had braced for worse; the test is whether beef losses actually narrow in the first half of fiscal 2027, which its fiscal first-quarter report will show.
