ConocoPhillips ($COP) agreed early on Monday to sell its Norwegian business and its Teesside oil terminal in the UK to Vår Energi for an enterprise value of **$4.425 billion**, the two companies said, as Reuters and Investing.com reported. The deal covers stakes in 13 producing fields and Conoco's operatorship of the Greater Ekofisk area. Bloomberg put the cash price at about $2.5 billion, with Vår Energi also taking on a tax liability of about $1.4 billion.

The price is below the roughly $7 billion that Capital One Securities analysts estimated the assets were worth in October, when Conoco said it was weighing an unsolicited offer, Offshore Engineering and EnergyNow reported. For Conoco, with a market value of about $161 billion in our data, the sale is under 3% of its size, and Vår Energi plans to pay from its existing cash and the cash the business holds at closing. The shares were up 0.4% at $136.72 before the open, as of 5:50 a.m. ET.

The deal needs approval from Norwegian and UK authorities and is expected to close toward the end of the first quarter of 2027.
