## What happened
Shares of chip-equipment maker Lam Research ($LRCX) **fell 9.8%** by 10:15 a.m. ET on Wednesday, Applied Materials ($AMAT) lost 8.4% and chip-testing maker Teradyne ($TER) 8.7%, while Dutch lithography maker ASML ($ASML) fell 6.3% in US trading. KLA ($KLAC) was down 6.9% in our data. All four now trade below where they closed on Friday, 20 November, so Monday's rally on Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia has been fully undone in two and a half sessions.

Two things hit the group at once. The first is the third day of the AI chip sell-off, which CNN and Yahoo Finance tied to doubts about AI spending, the new US tariff proposals and hotter inflation; [Micron and the AI connectivity stocks fell about 10%](/news/2026-11-25/micron-astera-labs-and-credo-shares-slide-what-happened/). The second is specific to the toolmakers: Dutch lawmakers on Tuesday urged their government to resist the MATCH Act, a US bill that would push allies such as the Netherlands and Japan to tighten chip-tool export controls on China, or face US limits on their equipment, Reuters reported in a story carried by Euronext and Yahoo Finance. Invezz and Stocktwits tied ASML's drop to that push-back.

## Why it matters
China is expected to make up about **20% of ASML's sales** this year, Reuters reported, and the bill would hit its immersion DUV machines, the older lithography tools that China still buys. Lam and Applied Materials sell etch and deposition tools to the same Chinese chipmakers, so a tighter regime would cut into the same pool of orders. One member of the Dutch VVD party called the bill disastrous for ASML and the Dutch economy, Reuters reported.

The bill is not law. Dutch Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen was expected to raise it on a visit to China this week, according to Reuters.

## What the market is telling us
Lam's shares have moved 5% or more on 60 of the last 252 sessions, and the last drop this big was on 20 November, when they fell 10.0%. At $196.16 on Tuesday, Lam was up 14.6% this year but 54.7% below its 30 June high; Applied Materials sat 55.2% below its high and ASML 32.8% below. We think the export bill matters more for ASML than for Lam today: it names ASML's tools, while the toolmakers' 10% drops look like the same AI sell-off that hit chipmakers with no China exposure.
