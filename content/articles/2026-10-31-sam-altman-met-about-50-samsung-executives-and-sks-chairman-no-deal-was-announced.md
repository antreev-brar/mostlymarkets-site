OpenAI Chief Executive Sam Altman met **about 50** senior Samsung executives on Saturday at the group's training center in Yongin, south of Seoul, Korea's Yonhap news agency reported. The closed-door session was a Samsung leadership seminar on how AI could reshape decision-making across its electronics, finance, and biotech affiliates. SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won hosted Altman for lunch the same day with SK hynix and SK Telecom executives; what they discussed wasn't disclosed, the Seoul Economic Daily reported.

Both groups signed letters of intent in October 2025 to supply memory chips for Stargate, OpenAI's data-center project. OpenAI's request, about 900,000 memory wafers a month, was **more than twice** global capacity for HBM, the stacked memory in AI chips, SK Group said then.

A letter of intent isn't an order, and the makers now sell future output on long-term contracts. SK hynix said on 28 October that contracts with 14 customers cover more than half its 2027 and 2028 capacity; Samsung said a day later that its 2027 HBM is sold out.

We think that leaves the leverage with the makers, at least for 2027: any new OpenAI volume would come on their terms, so the sign that matters is a contract with volumes and prices, not a meeting.

Seoul's main market, where both makers are listed, opens at 7 p.m. ET on Sunday, its first session since the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index sank 7.2% on Friday ([our story on Seoul's week](/news/2026-10-31/koreas-kospi-jumped-6-6-then-fell-3-5-how-seouls-week-set-up-the-chip-rout/)).
