Kimberly-Clark ($KMB) reported third-quarter net sales of $4.13 billion before the open on Thursday, down 0.4% from a year earlier and in line with the LSEG consensus, and adjusted earnings from continuing operations of **$1.70 a share** against the $1.66 LSEG estimate, CNBC reported. The figures matched the preliminary results it gave on 15 October. Organic sales fell 1.3%, and a social-media scare over its diapers in China took about 2.7 points off; the shares rose 1.5% to $110.46 on a day the S&P 500 fell 4.4%.

## What stood out

**China did the damage.** In International Personal Care, organic sales fell 4.6%, with the China disruption costing about 7.7 points, and operating profit fell by a third, the release said. Kimberly-Clark says the social-media allegations about its Huggies diapers in China were unfounded, but the sales damage lingers, Benzinga reported.

*Verdict: a reputational hit in one market, not a sign the brands are losing shoppers elsewhere.*

**North America held up, but on promotions.** Volume grew 2.0% in North America, and organic sales rose 0.4%, held back by lower prices from heavier promotions and by retailers cutting inventory. Segment operating profit rose 2.7% on productivity savings and lower advertising. EPS growth came mostly below the operating line, from lower interest costs and a lower tax rate.

*Verdict: steady volume, but bought with price.*

## The outlook

Kimberly-Clark reaffirmed its standalone 2026 outlook: organic sales growth about 1 point below its categories' growth of about 1.7%, mid-single-digit growth in adjusted operating profit, and high single-digit growth in adjusted EPS from continuing operations, all at constant currency. That outlook excludes Kenvue, the Tylenol and Listerine maker it bought for $48.7 billion and [closed on Monday](/news/2026-11-02/kimberly-clark-kmb-completes-its-48-7-billion-purchase-of-kenvue/), so the first combined forecast is still to come.

## Our take

We were impressed that volume kept growing in North America and that the quarter landed where the October preview said it would. On the other hand, gross margin fell 1.3 points as prices lagged costs, and adjusted operating profit slipped 0.9%. Overall, a quarter that matters less than the Kenvue integration now starting.

The stock rose as investors moved out of AI and data-centre names into defensive stocks on Thursday, CNBC said, but it gave back 2.1% by midday Friday. By our data, Kimberly-Clark's sales shrank 3.0% a year over five fiscal years, among the weakest of 32 consumer staples companies we track, which is why the company is betting on Kenvue for growth. The next test is fourth-quarter results early next year, with the first guidance for the combined company.
