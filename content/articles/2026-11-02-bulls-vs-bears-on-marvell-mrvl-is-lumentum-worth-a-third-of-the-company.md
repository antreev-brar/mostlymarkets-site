On Monday morning, Marvell Technology ($MRVL) signed its all-stock deal for Lumentum ($LITE), the laser maker whose parts move data through AI data centers on light instead of copper. Lumentum holders will get 4.5 Marvell shares each and own about **33% of the combined company**, the companies said. The question the market is arguing about is simple: is the laser business worth a third of Marvell?

Investors have answered so far with their feet. Marvell has fallen 26.4% from its 28 October close, the day Bloomberg first reported the talks, to $185.22 as of 10:15 a.m. ET on Monday. Lumentum, the target, fell 10.4% on the day and traded 9.7% below the value of the shares it is due to receive ([our story on the moves](/news/2026-11-02/lumentum-lite-and-marvell-mrvl-shares-slide-on-the-day-their-deal-is-signed/)).

## The bull case

### Matt Murphy, chief executive, Marvell

**The shift from copper to light in AI data centers is a "once in a generation" opportunity, and Marvell needs the lasers to own it.** Murphy, who will run the combined company, made the case in Monday's announcement, as reported by Stocktwits and Benzinga. The companies say they see more than $1 billion a year of cost savings and expect the deal to add to Marvell's adjusted earnings per share in the first year after it closes, according to the release, citybiz and The Next Web.

### Tore Svanberg, analyst, Stifel

**The deal makes strategic sense, even if it costs earnings at first.** Svanberg kept his Buy rating and $370 price target as the talks surfaced, Investing.com and Finanztrends reported. He pointed to drivers including selling whole systems rather than chips and securing Marvell's supply of lasers, Seeking Alpha reported, while expecting the deal to dilute earnings per share and gross margins in the near term, Taiwan's TechNews reported.

### Piper Sandler

**Marvell's AI business is still worth far more than today's price.** Piper Sandler reiterated its Overweight rating and $400 price target as the stock fell on the talks, Investing.com and Finanztrends reported, while noting that Marvell would need a large package of new stock or debt to pay for a company of Lumentum's size.

## The bear case

### Raymond James

**The deal could cut Marvell's earnings rather than add to them.** Raymond James calculated that, depending on its size and structure, a Lumentum deal would be roughly neutral to Marvell's earnings per share in calendar 2028, or dilutive by more than 10%, as reported by Benzinga and Germany's Trading-Treff. It kept its price target unchanged.

### Bernstein

**Paying for a company this big brings financing and integration risks.** Bernstein's analysts told Reuters that a deal for a company then valued at about $83 billion would need sizable extra financing and could bring integration problems, as Finimize and other outlets reported. Marvell had about $3.9 billion of cash at the end of its last quarter, one reason the deal is paid in stock.

### The dot-com comparison

**Megadeals for optical companies have marked tops before.** MarketWatch ran a column on Monday saying the deal was giving flashbacks to the dot-com bubble, and a Yahoo Finance segment noted that Lumentum was spun out in 2015 from JDS Uniphase, one of the best-known optical failures of that era. Neither named a fund manager making the call; we include it because it is the worry behind the 26% fall.

## Where they agree

Nobody quoted here disputes the logic. Marvell already buys lasers from Lumentum, Bloomberg reported, and both sides accept that owning them lets Marvell sell more of the optical link in a data center, bringing it closer to rivals Broadcom ($AVGO) and Coherent ($COHR) in AI networking. The argument is about price, and about how many new Marvell shares that logic is worth.

## The number that decides it

The test is earnings per share. Marvell says the deal adds to adjusted EPS in year one; Raymond James's math says it could take more than 10% away. The merger filing, with each side's projections, will be the first chance to check, and the first quarter after closing, in 2028, settles it.

## Our read

The strategy is sound and the price is full. Marvell traded at 32.8 times expected earnings and Lumentum at 34.6 times as of Monday morning, according to our data, so Marvell is swapping its stock for growth at about its own multiple plus a premium. That is fair for a business whose sales more than doubled in the quarter to June, but it leaves little room for the cost savings to slip. Because the ratio is fixed, the cost to Marvell holders stays at a third of their company whatever the stock does; the offer has shrunk from $1,132 a Lumentum share to $833 at 10:15 a.m. only because Marvell's own shares fell. **Too early to tell**, and the earnings projections in the merger filing are the number we'll watch.
