Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust said on Monday that it will buy a **75% stake** in two joint ventures with Amazon ($AMZN) that own 13 newly built data center buildings, a portfolio valued at about $5 billion. Amazon Web Services will lease back all of the space on a triple-net basis, meaning it pays the running costs, for an initial 12 years that can be extended to 32, the trust said. It is the trust's first investment since it listed in May, and the deal is expected to close in the fourth quarter.

The deal lets Amazon recover cash it has already spent on buildings while keeping the capacity it needs for AI. That matters because Amazon's free cash flow was **negative $46.9 billion** over the 12 months to September as its capital spending outran operating cash flow ([our Amazon breakdown](/news/2026-11-15/amazon-amzn-spends-all-its-cash-on-ai-is-the-business-still-strong/)). The trade-off is rent: the trust put the initial yield on the deal at 7.0% a year, a cost that now sits on AWS's books for at least 12 years.

Amazon affiliates keep the other quarter of the ventures, and the trust is managed by Blackstone ($BX). Amazon shares were down 1.3% at $263.26 in pre-market trading as of 9:14 a.m. ET, in line with a broad slide in AI stocks ahead of Nvidia's report on Tuesday.
