Microchip Technology ($MCHP) reported fiscal second-quarter sales of $1.62 billion on Thursday evening, **up 42.2%** from a year earlier and above the $1.61 billion analysts expected, Reuters and StockStory reported. Adjusted earnings of $0.97 a share beat the $0.93 consensus. But its forecast for the December quarter came in below estimates on sales, gross margin and earnings, and the stock was down 10.2% at $58.23 as of 10:15 a.m. ET on Friday.

## What stood out

The recovery from the 2024-25 chip inventory glut is now in full swing. Sales grew across its markets, "driven by industrial and data center applications," Chief Executive Steve Sanghi said in the release, and orders kept running ahead of shipments, with a book-to-bill ratio "well above one." Its data-center design wins for PCIe Gen6 switches and retimers, chips that move data between processors in AI servers, doubled to 24 from 12 at the end of June. It also closed two small deals in the quarter, Hailo, which makes AI processors for devices, and Vorago Technologies, which makes radiation-hardened chips for space, and kept its quarterly dividend at 45.5 cents.

## The outlook

Microchip guided December-quarter sales **down 1%** from September at the midpoint. Sanghi argued that is 5 to 7 points better than the usual seasonal drop of 3% to 5% once $53 million of one-off September revenue is stripped out. The weaker number is gross margin: the adjusted range of 64% to 65% is below September's 66.5%, which management tied on the call to lower factory utilization and supply limits on its higher-margin products, according to a transcript summary from TradingKey. Lead times are lengthening and some shipments are slipping into the March quarter, the release and call said.

## Our take

The quarter was strong and the guidance, read Sanghi's way, is better than seasonal; the problem is margin, and a stock that fell 13.9% with the rest of the chip sector on Thursday had little patience for that. Over five fiscal years Microchip's sales have shrunk 2.8% a year against 14.2% growth for the median chipmaker in our data, so this upturn is a recovery, not yet a new growth story. At 14.9 times forward earnings against a peer median of 21.0, the price already assumes less. The margin line in its March-quarter guidance, due with its next report, is the number to watch.
