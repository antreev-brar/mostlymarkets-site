LyondellBasell ($LYB), one of the world's biggest makers of plastics and chemicals, reported third-quarter sales of $8.50 billion on Friday, 30 October, up 10% from a year earlier but short of the roughly $8.87 billion analysts expected, according to Investing.com and GuruFocus. Earnings excluding one-time items were $2.54 a share, 3 cents below the $2.57 Investing.com cited. The stock still **jumped 5.0%** to $68.64 on Friday, a day the S&P 500 fell 1.8%.

## What stood out

Cash, not profit, moved the stock. Cash rose by about **$600 million** in the quarter even after LyondellBasell repaid €500 million of debt, the release showed, and Investing.com's account of the call led with profit and cash flow topping expectations. Chief Executive Peter Vanacker called it "another quarter of strong earnings and robust cash conversion" in the release.

That matters because LyondellBasell carries a lot of debt for a cyclical company: net debt was $9.1 billion at the end of September, about 3.3 years of operating profit, by our calculation from its filings. It has been cutting fixed costs under a cash improvement plan, which the release said made further progress.

The quarter's charges came from closing capacity. Identified items cut 22 cents a share from GAAP earnings, mainly costs to shut a propylene oxide joint venture in Europe, the release said.

## The outlook

LyondellBasell's chemicals sell at prices that swing with the economy and with oil, which sets the cost of its feedstocks. Over five years its sales grew 1.7% a year, against a 6.4% median for the 28 materials companies we cover, and its gross margin of 12.1% over the last two years is the lowest in the group, by our calculation, so cash discipline is what investors are paying for.

The stock is up 58.5% this year and rose 19.4% in October, by our price files, but it sits 16.7% below its 52-week closing high of $82.38 from 30 March.

## Our take

A small miss on profit and a bigger one on sales, rescued by cash: the debt repayment and the rising cash pile ease worries about a balance sheet carrying $9.1 billion of net debt. At 9.4 times expected earnings, against a materials median of 16.1, the price still assumes a cyclical business, so next quarter we'd watch whether the cash keeps coming as sales slip from the June peak.
