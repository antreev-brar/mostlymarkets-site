## How it got here

Johnson & Johnson ($JNJ) spent most of its history as a conglomerate of baby shampoo, bandages, medical devices and medicines. In 2023 it separated Kenvue, its consumer-health arm, and became two businesses: prescription drugs, which it calls Innovative Medicine, and surgical and heart devices, which it calls MedTech.

The test since then has been Stelara, its best-selling immune drug, which lost US exclusivity to cheaper copies. In the third quarter, falling Stelara sales cut about **6.9 points** off the drug unit's growth, the company said on its 13 October call. Yet that unit still grew 7.7%, because newer drugs grew faster, and J&J raised its 2026 forecast to sales of $101.0 billion to $101.4 billion.

Investors rewarded the drug story along with the rest of big pharma: J&J, Merck, Eli Lilly and AbbVie are each up more than 30% this year in our price data. But the stock fell **5.7%** on 13 October, its worst day since April 2025, when MedTech missed estimates. **Is J&J now a growth company that has earned a growth multiple, or a steady one priced as if nothing can go wrong?**

## The business in one line

Insurers, governments and hospitals pay J&J for cancer, immune and brain drugs taken for months or years, and hospitals buy its heart pumps, surgical tools and joint implants for every procedure.

## How it makes money

| Segment | What it sells | Share of Q3 sales | Growth, Q3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Innovative Medicine | Cancer (Darzalex, Carvykti), immunology (Tremfya, Stelara), neuroscience (Spravato, Caplyta) | 66% ($16.8 billion) | +7.7% |
| MedTech | Heart devices (Shockwave, Abiomed, electrophysiology), orthopaedics, surgery, vision | 34% ($8.6 billion) | +2.5% (operational) |

Darzalex, a multiple-myeloma drug, is the biggest product, with sales of about $4.24 billion in the quarter, up 15% to 16%, BioSpace and Zacks reported. Tremfya, the immune drug replacing Stelara, grew **68%** to $2.4 billion, according to the release coverage in Pharmaceutical Technology and Zacks.

Revenue rose 5.9% from a year earlier in the third quarter, to **$25.4 billion**, its highest quarter in our data. *Verdict: the engine is the drug unit, and it is running faster than the patent loss is braking it; MedTech is the weak half.*

## The numbers that matter

### Growth
Our five-year figure, **2.7% a year**, is in the bottom quarter of 18 drugmakers, but it is distorted: the 2020 base still includes Kenvue. On the restated basis, revenue grew about 4.6% a year from fiscal 2021 to fiscal 2025, and 6.4% a year over the last two years, above the peer median of 4.6%.

### Profitability
Gross margin averaged **67.8%** over two years, in the bottom quarter of the group, against a median of 72.6%. That is the device half at work: implants and surgical tools cost more to make than pills, so MedTech pulls the average down.

### Cash
Free cash flow was **$20.6 billion** in the 12 months to September, 20.7% of sales, just below the peer median of 21.9%. It has barely grown: $20.2 billion in fiscal 2020, $19.7 billion in fiscal 2025, SEC data show, though the earlier years included consumer products.

### Returns and balance sheet
Return on equity was **19.7%**, above the group median of 10.8%. Net debt was $27.7 billion at the end of September, about 1.3 years of free cash flow, which leaves room for deals such as the $2.2 billion upfront it agreed to pay for eye-drug developer Ollin on 16 November, Reuters reported.

### Earnings
Reported earnings swing with legal charges: third-quarter GAAP EPS was **$0.09**, after a $5.5 billion talc charge, while adjusted EPS was $2.59, above the $2.54 LSEG consensus cited by Reuters, but below $2.80 a year earlier, Quartr's summary shows. J&J guides 2026 adjusted EPS to $11.05 to $11.15.

*Verdict: steady growth, middling cash conversion, and a balance sheet strong enough to keep buying pipeline.*

## Why the stock is where it is

From the worried investor's angle, four things stand between J&J and its premium.

1. **The price itself.** The stock rose 8.3% between 31 July and 4 November while revenue barely changed, so the multiple did the work, Trefis's breakdown shows. The forward P/E is now **24.5x**, in the bottom quarter for cheapness among its peers.
2. **MedTech is slowing.** MedTech grew 2.5% in the third quarter, short of the $8.71 billion LSEG consensus, MassDevice and Zacks reported, and the shares fell 5.7% that day.
3. **Stelara is still shrinking.** Its loss of exclusivity cut about 6.9 points off drug-unit growth in the quarter, the call summaries from Longbridge and Benzinga's transcript show.
4. **Talc is not settled.** J&J booked a $5.5 billion charge for a proposed resolution of the ovarian talc cases, its 10-Q shows; the deal needs lead plaintiff firms representing at least 95% of remaining claims to take part, its 27 July release said. On 16 November trial lawyer Mark Lanier joined an investor lawsuit over its talc disclosures, Reuters reported.

## Testing the worry

**Stelara: temporary, and nearly done.** By our arithmetic, adding back the 6.9 points the patent loss took off leaves the drug unit growing about 14.6% underneath. Tremfya alone added roughly $1 billion of quarterly sales from a year earlier, by the 68% growth reported, and oncology grew about 16% to 17%, Zacks and the call summary show. Each quarter the shrinking base matters less.

**MedTech: partly temporary, and contained.** Surgery fell 1.0%, which management put down to China's bulk-buying tenders and disruption in the Middle East, and Abiomed's heart pumps fell 1.5% after a UK study questioned their use, Zacks and the call transcript show. Shockwave grew 17.2%, so cardiology is not broken. Orthopaedics, the slowest part, is to be separated by mid-2027, Chief Financial Officer Joe Wolk told CNBC, as MassDevice and Reuters reported.

**Talc: contained in the accounts, open in court.** The charge is already taken, so a deal that passes costs earnings nothing more; a deal that fails sends the cases back to trial, where the amount is unknowable.

**The price: this worry survives.** The first three have answers in the numbers. The fourth has none: a 24.5x multiple asks for growth that analysts and management have not yet put in a 2027 forecast, which J&J has promised at an investor event on 8 December, MDDI and MassDevice reported.

## The moat

- **A cancer franchise growing faster than the company.** Darzalex at about $4.24 billion a quarter and still growing 15% or more, plus Carvykti up 35.5% to $710 million, Zacks reported, give J&J a myeloma business rivals have to displace patient by patient.
- **A replacement already on sale.** Tremfya met its goals in a Phase 4 psoriatic arthritis trial on 22 November, Yahoo Finance reported, and the oral Icotyde eased arthritis in a late-stage trial in November, as [we reported](/news/2026-11-07/johnson-johnson-jnj-says-its-psoriasis-pill-icotyde-also-eased-arthritis-in-a-late-stage-trial/).
- **Cash to buy the next one.** $20.6 billion of yearly free cash flow funds deals without strain, such as the Ollin purchase, which Reuters reported comes with a late-stage eye drug.

Against its rivals: Merck ($MRK) grew revenue 3.3% a year over two years and trades at 16.6x forward earnings; Eli Lilly ($LLY) grew 45.5% a year at 29.6x; AbbVie ($ABBV) trades at 18.8x and Pfizer ($PFE) at 10.0x, our data show. J&J is priced closer to Lilly than to Merck, while growing closer to Merck. Will it still be here, and stronger, in 10 years? Almost certainly here; stronger depends on whether its pipeline keeps outrunning patent expiries.

## The risks we'd watch

1. **The multiple coming back to its peers.** This is the biggest risk, and simple to size: if J&J traded at the peer median of 16.6x with the same expected earnings, the stock would be **32% lower** (1 − 16.6 ÷ 24.5). That is a worst case: J&J's two-year growth beats the median by almost 2 points, and its EPS grew 14.9% a year over five years, above the 13.4% median, both reasons for some premium.
2. **A second MedTech miss.** MedTech is 34% of sales; each point of its growth is worth about $350 million a year on roughly $34.6 billion of annual sales, our arithmetic shows. Small for the group, but it was enough to knock 5.7% off the stock in October.
3. **The talc vote.** If fewer than 95% of claimants sign up, the $5.5 billion estimate stops being a ceiling.

Put in proportion: the business risks are modest, but the valuation magnifies any of them.

## What the price assumes

| Price to free cash flow | 32.1x | n/a | n/a |

At 24.5 times expected earnings, J&J earns about 4.1% on its share price, below the 4.81% the 10-year Treasury paid on Wednesday morning, and it yields 1.95% in dividends. On its own adjusted guidance midpoint of $11.10, the stock trades at about 24.8 times 2026 earnings. The market is effectively assuming that earnings grow fast enough, for long enough, that J&J deserves to be priced like a growth drugmaker rather than a steady one.

## Our verdict

**Business: Solid. Price: Above its usual price.** J&J has done the hard part: it lost its biggest drug's exclusivity and still grew drug sales 7.7%, with Darzalex, Tremfya and Carvykti doing the lifting and $20.6 billion of free cash flow to buy what comes next. But MedTech grew just 2.5%, free cash flow is no higher than in 2020, and the talc deal is still a proposal. At 24.5 times expected earnings, roughly half as much again as its peers, the stock prices in the good outcome on all three. What would change our mind is a 2027 forecast at the 8 December investor event showing adjusted earnings growth near the drug unit's underlying pace. The tension is plain: investors are buying J&J as a safe haven, and paying a price that is anything but safe.
