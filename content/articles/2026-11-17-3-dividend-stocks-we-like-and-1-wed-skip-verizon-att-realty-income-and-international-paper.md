On Tuesday, St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said interest rates may need to rise by a full point by spring ([our story](/news/2026-11-17/feds-musalem-says-rates-may-need-to-rise-a-full-point-by-spring/)), and the 10-year Treasury yield closed at **5.10%**. That is the bar for any stock bought for its dividend: a 5% yield beats a Treasury only if the company can keep paying it, and the test is whether free cash flow, the cash left after capital spending, covers the payout.

Our screen ranked the companies in our coverage by dividend yield, then checked each payout against free cash flow over the latest 12 months or the year to date, using SEC filings in our fundamentals data, the companies' third-quarter releases, and the dividend implied by each stock's current yield and market value.

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Verizon ($VZ)
**Dividend yield: 6.16%, as of 17 November 2026.** Verizon sells mobile and home internet service in the US.

1. The cash covers the dividend twice: free cash flow was **$18.0 billion** in the first nine months of 2026, up 14.3% from a year earlier, against $8.8 billion of dividends paid, its third-quarter release said.
2. The outlook keeps rising: on 26 October it raised its full-year free cash flow forecast to growth of about 12%, from 9% to 10%, the third straight quarterly raise, the release and Investing.com reported.
3. Its operating margin of 20.9% in the 12 months to September 2026 beats the 18.3% median of the 25 communication-services companies we compare it with.

**The price:** 9.0 times expected earnings against a peer median of 15.7 times; its trailing P/E of 11.7 is a little above its two-year median of 10.7 after a 12.8% rise this year.

**What would change our mind:** growth. Revenue has grown 1.5% a year over five years, in the bottom quarter of its peers, and net debt is $162 billion, 5.6 years of operating profit, so a year of falling free cash flow would leave little room for both the dividend and the debt.

### 2. AT&T ($T)
**Dividend yield: 4.98%, as of 17 November 2026.** AT&T sells mobile service and fiber home internet in the US.

1. The dividend takes less than half of its cash: free cash flow was **$17.8 billion** in the 12 months to September 2026, and at its current yield the dividend costs about $7.5 billion a year, so cash covers it 2.4 times.
2. The rest goes to buybacks: diluted shares fell 5.0% in two years, in the top half of its peers, and the company plans about $10 billion of repurchases in 2026, its release said.
3. The plan runs years out: AT&T reaffirmed on 21 October free cash flow of $18 billion or more in 2026 and $19 billion or more in 2027, and reported its best service-revenue growth in more than four years, its release and the call transcript show.

**The price:** 9.2 times expected earnings against a peer median of 15.7 times, and **8.4 times free cash flow**. The stock is down 10.3% this year and 23.4% below its 52-week high.

**What would change our mind:** the debt. Net debt of $149 billion is 6.3 years of operating profit, and revenue shrank 2.6% a year over five years; a miss on the $19 billion 2027 cash target would be the warning.

### 3. Realty Income ($O)
**Dividend yield: 5.6%, as of 17 November 2026.** Realty Income owns freestanding stores and other properties leased long-term to tenants, and pays its dividend monthly.

1. The payout has a cushion: its monthly dividends took **72.6%** of adjusted funds from operations (AFFO, the cash measure REITs pay dividends from) in the third quarter, its release said.
2. It raised its 2026 AFFO forecast to $4.45 to $4.46 a share in early November, with occupancy guidance above 98.5%, the release and the call transcript show.
3. Revenue has grown 28.4% a year over five years, in the top tenth of the 30 real-estate companies we compare it with, though partly through acquisitions paid for in shares.

**The price:** about 13.0 times this year's forecast AFFO at $58.04, and its trailing P/E of 40.0 is below its two-year median of 52.7.

**What would change our mind:** the share count. Diluted shares rose 8.5% in two years, in the bottom quarter of its peers, and AFFO per share rose only 3.7% in the third quarter; at 5.10% Treasury yields, more stock sold to fund deals would leave less growth for each share.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### International Paper ($IP)
**Dividend yield: 5.75%, as of 17 November 2026.** International Paper makes corrugated boxes and packaging in North America and, since buying DS Smith, in Europe.

1. The dividend is not paid from cash: free cash flow was **$75 million** in the 12 months to September 2026, against a dividend that costs about $980 million a year at its current yield. It paid $735 million of dividends in nine months, its release said.
2. The quarter went backward: free cash flow was negative $267 million, it cut its full-year free cash flow forecast to $100 million to $200 million, and it took a $1.38 billion goodwill charge on its European packaging unit, the release and Investing.com reported.
3. Shareholders were diluted to buy DS Smith: diluted shares rose 49.9% in two years, in the bottom tenth of the 28 materials companies we compare it with, while return on equity was negative 26.3%.

**The price:** 20.0 times expected earnings against a peer median of 15.9 times, despite a 34.6% fall from its 52-week high.

**What would change our mind:** a fourth quarter in which inventories unwind as management promised and free cash flow covers the quarterly dividend, which its results in late January will show.
