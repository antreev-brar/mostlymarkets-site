Arthur J. Gallagher ($AJG) reported third-quarter adjusted earnings of $3.00 a share, up 30% from $2.31 a year earlier and in line with consensus estimates of $2.99 to $3.03, according to MarketBeat and TradingKey. Revenue of about $3.93 billion, up 18% year on year in its brokerage and risk management businesses, fell short of estimates of roughly $3.98 billion to $4.03 billion, according to MarketBeat and Chartmill. The results came out after Thursday's close, and the stock was up 3.1% at $276.38 as of 9:31 a.m. ET on Friday.

That put the shares above their 52-week closing high of $271.99 from 24 August. Gallagher had also risen 3.0% on Thursday, to $268.20, before the report.

## What stood out

Organic growth, the measure insurance brokers are judged on, held up. Gallagher, which arranges insurance for businesses and earns commissions and fees on it, grew revenue 5% in brokerage after stripping out acquisitions and currency, and 10% in Gallagher Bassett, its claims-handling arm, the release showed. Insurance Business magazine noted that 5% stood out as softer insurance prices slowed growth at rival brokers such as Brown & Brown.

Profit grew much faster than revenue. Adjusted EBITDAC, Gallagher's measure of operating profit before interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization, and changes in earnout payables, rose 25%, the 26th straight quarter of double-digit growth, according to the release. The brokerage margin on that measure widened two points to 35.3%.

Part of the growth came from buying. AssuredPartners, a US broker Gallagher acquired, contributed $285 million of adjusted EBITDAC in the quarter, and management said on the call that AssuredPartners' organic growth is running about 1 to 1.5 points below Gallagher's own, which will weigh on total organic growth in 2027.

## The outlook

Management still expects full-year organic growth of about 6%, according to the call coverage. It also said it has about $12 billion of capacity for acquisitions, GuruFocus reported.

## Our take

This was a quarter of steady organic growth and wider margins, with a small revenue miss the market looked past. The risk is the insurance cycle: as premiums soften, brokers' commissions grow more slowly. Next quarter we'd watch whether organic growth holds near 5% and how AssuredPartners is folded in.
