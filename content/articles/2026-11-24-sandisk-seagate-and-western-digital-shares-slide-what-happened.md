## What happened
Shares of flash-memory maker Sandisk ($SNDK) **sank 10.5%** to $78.49 by 10:15 a.m. ET on Tuesday, and hard-drive makers Seagate ($STX) and Western Digital ($WDC) fell 7.0% and 5.8%, as the storage stocks gave back part of Monday's rally on Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia's chief executive. Micron ($MU), the memory-chip maker, lost 8.8%.

StreetInsider, Investing.com and Benzinga put the drop down to profit-taking after Monday's surge and renewed scrutiny of Nvidia ($NVDA), which was itself down 5.9% at 10:15 a.m. ET. For Sandisk, Investing.com and StreetInsider also pointed to a Form 144 filed on Tuesday, a notice that an insider may sell restricted shares; the filing does not mean a sale has happened.

## Why it matters
Sandisk has now given back all of Monday's **10.7%** gain and sits about 1% below Friday's close. Seagate and Western Digital, which both rose 13.8% on Monday, are still roughly 6% and 7% above Friday. These stocks trade on one question, whether AI data centers keep buying storage at today's prices, and the Nvidia crisis has made them swing with Nvidia's news rather than their own.

That is the debate in [our narrative on the memory boom](/news/2026-11-21/wall-street-is-betting-the-memory-boom-has-peaked-microns-17-december-report-will-test-it/): the market is pricing a peak while the makers say 2027 supply is sold out. Micron's results on 17 December are the next hard data.

## What the market is telling us
Sandisk's shares moved 5% or more on 117 of the last 252 sessions, Seagate's on 74 and Western Digital's on 81, so days like this are routine for the group. Seagate closed Monday up 88.9% this year and Western Digital 72.6%, and $1,000 put into Seagate five years ago would be worth $5,120. We read Tuesday as the storage trade moving with Nvidia's mood, not news about demand, and Micron's 17 December report is the first number that could break that link.
