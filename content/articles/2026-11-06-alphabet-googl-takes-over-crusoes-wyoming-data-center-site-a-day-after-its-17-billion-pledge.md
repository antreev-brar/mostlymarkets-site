On Friday, Crusoe said Google had bought its southeast Wyoming data center site, the land where Alphabet ($GOOGL) announced a **$17 billion** data center in Laramie County a day earlier. Terms weren't disclosed, according to Crusoe's statement and reports from Unite.ai and Securities.io. Bloomberg reported that Crusoe, a private AI-cloud builder, was pushed aside after failing to sign customers for the campus, with Google raising concerns about costs and timing.

The deal is a small, telling data point for [the debate over how many planned data centers will stall](/news/2026-11-06/bulls-vs-bears-on-the-midterm-ai-sell-off-how-many-data-centers-will-stall/). A third-party developer without signed tenants lost the project, and a giant with its own balance sheet took it over. Google says it will pay for all of the site's power and grid upgrades. Its electricity comes partly from a **702-megawatt** fuel-cell plant that American Electric Power ($AEP) plans to build for about $4 billion under a 20-year contract, using cells from Bloom Energy ($BE), according to AEP's October announcement as reported by DatacenterDynamics and Baxtel.

Crusoe says it still has more than 6 gigawatts under contract. Next comes construction: AEP has said the fuel-cell plant should be fully running by the end of 2028.
