Colgate-Palmolive ($CL) reported third-quarter net sales of $5.37 billion on Friday, 30 October, up 4.6% year on year and above the $5.34 billion analysts expected, according to Investing.com. Its adjusted earnings, which Colgate calls Base Business EPS, rose 7% to 97 cents a share, beating the 93 cents expected. The stock **rose 2.3%** to close at $95.29 on Friday, as the rest of the market fell.

## What stood out

Growth came from outside the US. Organic sales, which strip out currency moves, acquisitions and exits, rose 6% in Latin America and 4.6% in Asia Pacific, but fell 1.6% in North America, the release showed. Latin America's net sales rose 12.5% once currencies were counted, Alphastreet reported.

Profit grew faster than sales: Base Business EPS **rose 7%** on 4.6% sales growth. On a GAAP basis, which includes one-time charges, EPS rose 3% to 94 cents, GuruFocus and the release showed, which is why some trackers scored the quarter as a match rather than a beat.

Over the long run Colgate is a slow grower with unusually high profitability. Its gross margin over the last two years was 60.4%, against a median of 39.3% for the 35 consumer staples companies we cover, and its sales grew 4.4% a year over five years, about the group's pace, by our calculation from its filings. It turns that into cash: free cash flow was 17.8% of sales over the last 12 months, against a peer median of 10.3%, and its diluted share count fell 2.8% over two years as it bought back stock.

## The outlook

Colgate narrowed its 2026 forecast to net sales growth of 4% to 5%, from 2% to 6%, including a low-single-digit lift from currencies, and now expects organic growth broadly in line with its nine-month pace, the release said. That raises the bottom of the range by two points.

The stock is up 20.6% this year and sits 3.9% below its 52-week closing high of $99.14 from 27 February. It rose 11.7% in October, by our price files, while the Nasdaq fell.

## Our take

A clean quarter: sales and adjusted profit both beat, and the forecast now has a higher floor. The weak spot is North America, where organic sales shrank. At 24.4 times expected earnings against a median of 16.6 for consumer staples, the stock already prices in steady growth, so next quarter we'd watch whether the US turns positive.
