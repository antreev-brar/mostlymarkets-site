## What happened
Shares of Chinese e-commerce and cloud group Alibaba ($BABA) **jumped 4.5%** to $90.15 by 10:16 a.m. ET on Friday, after China's cabinet promised to prop up its sinking stock market. A State Council meeting chaired by Premier Li Qiang called for a bigger role for the "national team" of state-backed investors, The Standard and Yahoo Finance reported.

The meeting also urged listed companies to pay more dividends and buy back more shares, and told Central Huijin, the state fund that buys stocks in a crisis, to act as a source of liquidity during turmoil, according to The Standard. Mainland markets had fallen to 16-month lows before the pledge, having lost about a fifth of their value since June. CITIC Securities said the meeting should steady confidence.

## Why it matters
The pledge helps the mood around Chinese stocks, but it doesn't change Alibaba's own numbers, as TipRanks noted. Those have been the problem: in its [September-quarter results](/news/2026-11-19/alibaba-baba-beats-revenue-estimates-as-ai-cloud-sales-jump-53-but-profit-misses/) cloud sales grew 53%, but adjusted profit missed as AI spending deepened its cash outflow.

Washington is the other weight. On 23 November the Commerce Department [put Alibaba's Singapore unit on its Entity List](/news/2026-11-23/us-blacklists-alibabas-baba-singapore-unit-and-megaspeed-over-chip-diversion-risk/) over the risk of chip diversion, a move Alibaba called erroneous. State buying can lift the index; it can't loosen US limits on the chips Alibaba's cloud needs.

## What the market is telling us
Alibaba's shares moved 5% or more on 14 of the last 252 sessions, and the last rise at least this big was on 23 November, when the stock rose 6.1%. At Wednesday's close of $86.26 it was down 41.1% this year and 51.3% below its January high, and $1,000 invested five years ago would be worth $632. We'd treat Friday as a policy bounce: the test of whether it holds is whether state funds actually buy, which the next weeks' market data will show.
