Zscaler ($ZS) reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of **$949.3 million** on Monday, up 20% from a year earlier and above its own forecast of $935 million to $939 million, its release showed. Adjusted earnings of $1.23 a share beat the $1.16 analysts expected, according to LSEG IBES data cited by Reuters, the same figure Investing.com reported. The numbers landed in the same release that announced CrowdStrike ($CRWD) would buy the company, so the stock trades on the deal: it closed up 17.7% on Monday and was up 2.4% at $234.63 as of 12:15 p.m. ET Tuesday.

## What stood out

The number Zscaler investors watch is annual recurring revenue, the yearly value of its subscriptions, and its growth slowed to **21%** from 25% in the fourth quarter, the release showed, as GuruFocus and Panabee reported. Revenue growth cooled the same way, to 20.5% from 24.9% a quarter earlier in our filings data. Part of that is arithmetic: a year ago the quarter included $98 million of net new ARR bought with Red Canary, and stripping that out, net new ARR grew 22%, the company said. *Verdict: a clean beat on the old guidance, but the business is decelerating into the sale.*

Cash was the strong spot. Free cash flow of $416.5 million edged past $413.3 million a year earlier, and operating cash flow was a record $541 million. Under standard accounting (GAAP) the loss widened, because of stock pay and acquisition costs that the adjusted figures leave out.

## The outlook

There isn't one. Zscaler **withdrew** its fiscal 2027 forecast of $3.908 billion to $3.938 billion in revenue and cancelled its call, citing the deal, the release said. That forecast, reaffirmed at its 6 October investor day, implied growth of 16.6% to 17.5% for the year, slower again than this quarter.

What matters for holders now is the exchange ratio: each Zscaler share becomes 1.135 CrowdStrike shares. At CrowdStrike's $213.34 as of 12:15 p.m. ET, that is worth about $242, so Zscaler trades **3.1% below** the offer, down from 6.4% before Monday's open ([the deal](/news/2026-11-23/crowdstrike-crwd-agrees-to-buy-zscaler-for-43-9-billion-in-stock/)). Founder Jay Chaudhry and his affiliates, with about 34% of the shares, have agreed to vote for it, the merger filing shows, and the companies expect it to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028.

## Our take

The quarter beat on every line the company had promised, which helps CrowdStrike's case that it isn't buying a broken business ([CrowdStrike's own quarter](/news/2026-11-24/crowdstrike-crwd-beats-q3-estimates-with-record-new-business-but-zscaler-sinks-the-stock/)). The slowdown to 20% is the weak spot, and analysts moved first: Bernstein cut the stock to Market Perform and its target to $229 from $298, Investing.com and TradingView reported. From here Zscaler's price tracks CrowdStrike's, so the next test is CrowdStrike's net new ARR in early March, plus the regulatory review ([what CrowdStrike is buying](/news/2026-11-21/zscaler-zs-is-a-takeover-target-what-would-crowdstrike-be-buying/)).
