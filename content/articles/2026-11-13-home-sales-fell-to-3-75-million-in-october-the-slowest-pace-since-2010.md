Sales of previously owned US homes **fell 5.5%** in October to a seasonally adjusted annual pace of 3.75 million, the lowest since August 2010, the National Association of Realtors said on Thursday, 12 November. Sales were down 8.8% from a year earlier, the report showed, as HousingWire and HNGN also reported.

The cause is borrowing costs. Lawrence Yun, the Realtors' chief economist, said buying is hard with mortgage rates at their highest since 2000, apart from one month in 2023, Yahoo News and the Seoul Economic Daily reported. Prices have not given way: the median sale price rose 0.4% from a year earlier to **$416,700**, while unsold homes rose to 5.1 months of supply. Fewer sales hit first the brokers and mortgage lenders who are paid per deal; homebuilders slipped too, as D.R. Horton ($DHI) fell 2.1% and Lennar ($LEN) 3.2% on Thursday, in our data.

The rate pressure is building, not easing: on Friday wholesale inflation showed an annual rate of 6.5% ([our story](/news/2026-11-13/wholesale-prices-rose-less-than-expected-in-october-but-the-annual-rate-hit-6-5/)). The next test for housing is October's new-home sales and housing starts.
