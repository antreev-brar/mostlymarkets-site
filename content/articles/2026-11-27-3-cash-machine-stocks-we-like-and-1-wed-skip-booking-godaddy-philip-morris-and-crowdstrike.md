Free cash flow, the money left after running a business and paying for its equipment, is the profit that pays for buybacks and dividends. On Friday, after the Black Friday rally, we screened the companies we cover for the highest free cash flow margin, cash as a share of sales, over the latest 12 months in their SEC filings, and asked whether the cash comes from the business itself.

Three of the strongest pass that test and trade below the multiples of their peers or their own history. One, CrowdStrike, has a cash margin that flatters the business behind it.

```picks
like: BKNG, GDDY, PM
skip: CRWD
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Booking Holdings ($BKNG)
**Free cash flow margin: 32.8% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Booking runs Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda and OpenTable.

1. **Cash at the top of its group.** The margin ranks in the top tenth of the 66 consumer companies we cover, against a 9.2% median, and 32.4% over two years. Its operating margin of 32.8% is more than twice the group's 13.8%.
2. **The quarter was better than its headline.** Third-quarter room nights rose 7% to 345 million, but reported revenue fell 1% because Booking cut it by $900 million over a Federal Trade Commission case involving Priceline, the 10-Q showed and Reuters reported. The FTC may seek more.
3. **Buybacks do the compounding.** Diluted shares fell 11.1% over two years, in the top quarter of the group.

**The price:** 12.8 times expected earnings against a 20.0 times group median, and 16.2 times trailing earnings against its own median of 31.2 times since late 2024, with the shares 31.9% below their 52-week high after a 25-for-1 split in April.

**What could go wrong:** Booking guided to fourth-quarter room-night growth of only 1% to 3%, citing softer international travel and disruption in the Middle East, the prepared remarks and Finviz showed. If that slowdown runs into 2027, the low multiple is deserved.

### 2. GoDaddy ($GDDY)
**Free cash flow margin: 33.7% in the 12 months to September 2026.** GoDaddy sells web domains and hosting to small businesses.

1. **Twice its peers' cash.** The margin is in the top quarter of the internet-infrastructure companies we cover, against a 16.6% median, and up from 32.7% over two years.
2. **It is getting more profitable.** Its operating margin rose 14.6 points over five years to 25.7%, against a 3.6-point gain for the median peer.
3. **The cash goes back to owners.** Diluted shares fell 12.8% over two years, in the top tenth of its group. Third-quarter free cash flow rose 9% to $480.8 million on revenue up 6% to $1.342 billion, above the company's own guidance, StockStory and Webhosting.Today reported.

**The price:** 12.1 times expected earnings against a 17.0 times peer median, and just 7.2 times free cash flow, with the shares 21.8% below their 52-week high.

**What could go wrong:** growth is single-digit and carries net debt of 2.0 years of operating profit, so if its 6% pace slows further, the cheap multiple becomes the new normal.

### 3. Philip Morris International ($PM)
**Free cash flow margin: 31.5% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Philip Morris sells Marlboro cigarettes outside the US, IQOS heated tobacco and ZYN nicotine pouches.

1. **Pricing power few staples have.** Its two-year gross margin of 67.3% is in the top tenth of the 35 consumer-staples companies we cover, against a 38.7% median, and the cash margin rose from 28.5% over two years.
2. **It is growing faster than its peers.** Revenue rose 7.7% a year over two years, against 3.1% for the median staple. Third-quarter revenue rose 5.5% to $11.4 billion, or 7.1% organically, the company's release and Grafa reported.
3. **The weak spot is already in the price.** ZYN shipment growth slowed to 4.2% against a promotion a year earlier, and the shares fell after the 21 October report, Sherwood News and NACS reported. They are 12.4% below their high.

**The price:** 21.6 times expected earnings, above the 17.8 times staples median, but 27.4 times trailing earnings is right at its own median of 28.0 times since late 2024.

**What could go wrong:** net debt of $40.9 billion is 2.5 years of operating profit, and the share count hasn't fallen, so a second soft quarter for ZYN would leave little else to carry earnings growth.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### CrowdStrike ($CRWD)
**Free cash flow margin: 31.3% in the 12 months to October 2026.** CrowdStrike sells cybersecurity software that protects companies' computers and cloud systems.

1. **The cash isn't profit.** Its operating margin under standard accounting was -1.2% over the same 12 months, in the bottom quarter of the 32 software companies we cover. The gap is mostly pay in stock ([our breakdown](/news/2026-11-23/crowdstrike-crwd-bets-its-pricey-stock-on-zscaler-is-the-business-worth-it/)).
2. **Owners pay for it in dilution.** Diluted shares rose 7.1% over two years, against a 0.3% software median, and the $43.9 billion all-stock deal for Zscaler ($ZS) adds 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for every Zscaler share ([the deal](/news/2026-11-23/crowdstrike-crwd-agrees-to-buy-zscaler-for-43-9-billion-in-stock/)).
3. **The business itself is strong.** Third-quarter revenue rose 28% to $1.58 billion, with record net new recurring revenue, the release and Benzinga showed ([our recap](/news/2026-11-24/crowdstrike-crwd-beats-q3-estimates-with-record-new-business-but-zscaler-sinks-the-stock/)). This is about the price, not the company.

**The price:** 141 times expected earnings against a 23.8 times software median, and 124.6 times free cash flow, after the shares rose about 67% over the past year while Booking and GoDaddy fell.

**What would change our mind:** a positive operating margin under standard accounting, or a share count that stops rising once the Zscaler shares are issued.
