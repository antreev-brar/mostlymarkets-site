Five companies worth about $168 billion together report on **Tuesday, 24 November**: Dollar Tree ($DLTR) and Best Buy ($BBY) before the open, Autodesk ($ADSK), Workday ($WDAY) and HP ($HPQ) after the close. They cover three of the market's open questions at once: whether AI is eating into business software, how memory costs are squeezing PC makers, and how shoppers are spending going into the holidays.

## Autodesk ($ADSK), after the close

Analysts expect adjusted earnings of **$3.07 a share** on revenue of $2.13 billion, according to MarketBeat and Daily Political, inside Autodesk's guidance of $3.04 to $3.09 and $2.125 billion to $2.140 billion. Last quarter it beat on revenue, up 16% to $2.05 billion, and on earnings, but the stock fell 3.7% the next day because its new forecast for earnings came in below estimates: the MaintainX maintenance-software deal adds sales but loses money, SiliconANGLE and Briefing.com reported. Zacks expects its sales reorganization to have weighed on new subscriptions again. We named it a high-margin stock [we like](/news/2026-11-15/2-high-margin-stocks-we-like-and-1-wed-skip-adobe-autodesk-and-arm/) on 15 November.

## Workday ($WDAY), after the close

Analysts expect adjusted earnings of **$2.74 a share** on revenue of $2.69 billion, MarketBeat and Barchart reported, in line with Workday's forecast of 11% growth and subscription revenue of $2.515 billion, up 12%. Last quarter subscription revenue rose 13.9% and adjusted earnings of $2.75 beat the Zacks consensus of $2.62; the stock rose 5.8% the next day. The number to watch is the subscription backlog: BigGo reported that Workday's outlook for total subscription backlog, about $27.4 billion, fell short of the $28.6 billion analysts wanted.

## Dollar Tree ($DLTR), before the open

Zacks puts consensus revenue at **$5.05 billion**, and analysts expect $0.89 to $0.91 a share, down from $1.21 a year ago, according to Zacks and MarketBeat. The drop is planned: Dollar Tree guided to $0.80 to $0.95, including a hit of about 50 cents from reinvesting tariff refunds, its release said. Last quarter sales rose 7% to $4.89 billion with comparable-store sales up 3.7%, but the stock fell 3.9% that day on the weak third-quarter forecast. It was [one of three momentum stocks we like](/news/2026-11-22/3-momentum-stocks-we-like-and-1-wed-skip-veeva-iqvia-dollar-tree-and-moderna/) on Sunday.

## HP ($HPQ), after the close

Analysts expect adjusted earnings of **$0.74 a share**, down 20.4% from a year earlier, on revenue of $15.3 billion, up 4.5%, Seeking Alpha reported; HP guided to $0.69 to $0.79. Last quarter revenue rose 12.5% to $15.7 billion and earnings of $0.83 cleared its own forecast, helped by 11 cents of tariff refunds, but HP warned that rising input costs would pressure margins in its PC business this quarter, and the stock fell 2.9% the next day. Memory prices are the number that matters: our [HPE recap](/news/2026-11-23/hewlett-packard-enterprise-hpe-beats-q4-estimates-as-server-sales-nearly-double/) showed the same pressure on server margins on Monday.

## Best Buy ($BBY), before the open

Analysts expect **$1.49 a share** on revenue of $9.90 billion, against $1.40 and $9.67 billion a year ago, Alphastreet reported; MarketBeat has $1.48. Best Buy guided to comparable sales growth of 1% to 3%. Last quarter comparable sales rose 4.1% and it raised its full-year outlook, but the shares fell 4.4% that day as investors focused on costs and margins, Finviz reported. Options imply a 9.7% move either way, TipRanks said. We put it on a list of [momentum stocks we'd skip](/news/2026-11-14/3-momentum-stocks-wed-skip-and-1-we-like-charles-river-best-buy-snowflake-factset/) on 14 November.

## The one with the most riding on it

Workday. Its shares have **fallen 16.2%** in the past month and are down 18.0% this year, as investors priced in the risk that AI agents shrink the number of seats companies pay for ([our narrative](/news/2026-11-18/wall-street-is-betting-ai-will-empty-softwares-seats-3-numbers-will-decide-it/)). At 14.6 times expected earnings it costs well below the 24.0 times median for the software companies we cover, so the price already assumes slowing growth. A backlog that grows faster than its 12% subscription pace would challenge that; a weaker backlog outlook would confirm it, and Autodesk's report the same evening will show whether the pressure is spreading across design software too.
