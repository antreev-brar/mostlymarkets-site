The S&P 500 fell 3.7% on Friday into a correction, its seventh straight loss, as [we reported](/news/2026-11-20/stock-market-today-the-s-p-500-enters-a-correction-as-nvidia-sinks-14/). In a sell-off, companies with fast-rising sales look like shelter, but a sales line can grow fast for reasons a shareholder shouldn't pay for: an acquisition, thin-margin volume, or spending that never turns into profit.

We screened the stocks in our coverage on revenue growth over the 12 months to their latest quarter (September or October 2026 for most), using SEC filings, then left out anything we've listed in the past 14 days. We then judged each finalist on whether the growth is organic, profitable and paid for in cash, against its industry peers, five-year record first.

```picks
skip: WAT, CVNA, RBRK
like: LLY
```

## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. Waters ($WAT)
**Revenue growth: 80.7% in the 12 months to October 2026.** Waters makes the liquid-chromatography and mass-spectrometry instruments that drug and food labs use to test what's in a sample.

1. **The growth was bought.** Of third-quarter revenue of $1.77 billion, about $896 million came from the businesses Waters acquired from Becton Dickinson ($BDX), the company said in its results release on 3 November, and the acquired diagnostics unit was flat. Over five fiscal years, sales grew 6.0% a year, below the 8.2% median for the health care stocks we cover.
2. **Shareholders paid with dilution.** Diluted shares rose **66.4%** over two years, the worst record among 60 health care peers, while the median company shrank its count by 3%.
3. **The deal hasn't paid yet.** Operating margin over the last 12 months was 4.7%, against a 13.4% peer median, and free cash flow was 5.2% of sales. Net debt of $4.5 billion equals 17 years of current operating profit.

**The price:** 28.4 times forward earnings, against 25.6 for Thermo Fisher ($TMO) and 26.0 for Agilent ($A), whose operating margins are about four to five times its own.

**What would change our mind:** management has guided to mid-single-digit-plus organic growth in 2027; operating margin back above 15% on the combined business would show the merger is working.

### 2. Carvana ($CVNA)
**Revenue growth: 50.3% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Carvana sells used cars online and delivers them to the buyer's door.

1. **The growth is real and long-running.** Sales grew 29.5% a year over five fiscal years, and the company sold a record 217,385 cars in the third quarter, its release showed. That isn't the problem.
2. **Each car earns little.** Gross margin over two years was **20.0%**, against a 39.9% median for the consumer stocks we cover, and free cash flow was 3.6% of sales. Third-quarter earnings of 39 cents a share missed the 47-cent Zacks consensus as fuel and interest costs rose, Zacks and the company reported.
3. **Rates hit it twice.** Carvana's buyers borrow to pay, and the company sells the loans on, so higher benchmark rates squeeze both demand and its finance income. Management put the hit to third-quarter unit economics at about $200 a car.

**The price:** 31.7 times forward earnings against a 19.5 peer median, and 63 times free cash flow, even after a 33.3% drop from its January closing high.

**What would change our mind:** two quarters of free cash flow margin above 6%, which would put it near the middle of retail.

### 3. Rubrik ($RBRK)
**Revenue growth: 42.4% in the 12 months to July 2026.** Rubrik sells software that backs up company data and recovers it after a cyberattack.

1. **Growth hasn't produced profit.** Operating margin over the last 12 months was **-18.3%**, in the bottom quarter of the 32 software companies we cover, whose median is 9.7%.
2. **Shareholders fund part of it.** Diluted shares rose 14.6% over two years, against 0.3% for the median peer.
3. **The cash is good, not great.** Free cash flow was 19.7% of sales over 12 months, below the 28.8% software median and the 27.9% that CrowdStrike ($CRWD) earns.

**The price:** 214 times forward earnings and 73 times free cash flow. The stock rose 8.5% on Thursday morning with the cybersecurity group after Palo Alto Networks ($PANW) raised its forecast, then gave it back to close Friday down 6.8%.

**What would change our mind:** a positive operating margin. Its fiscal third quarter ended 31 October, and it reported the same quarter last year on 4 December.

## 1 stock we like

### Eli Lilly ($LLY)
**Revenue growth: 45.5% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Lilly sells Mounjaro and Zepbound, the tirzepatide drugs for diabetes and obesity, alongside cancer, immunology and neuroscience medicines.

1. **The fastest compounder in pharma.** Sales grew **21.6% a year** over five fiscal years, first of the 18 drugmakers we cover, whose median is 8.1%. Third-quarter revenue rose 39% to $24.4 billion, and on 29 October Lilly raised its 2026 revenue forecast to $91 billion to $92 billion from $85 billion to $87 billion, its release and Zacks reported.
2. **It keeps most of each dollar.** Gross margin over two years was 83.4%, in the top quarter of its peers, and earnings per share grew 27.6% a year over five years against a 13.4% median.
3. **It returns more on capital than anyone in the group.** Return on equity was 68.5%, against a 10.8% drugmaker median, while its diluted share count fell 1.3% over two years.

**The price:** 43.7 times trailing earnings, below its own two-year median of 50.2, but 33.3 times forward earnings is twice the drug-industry median of 16.7.

**What could go wrong:** about $900 million of third-quarter revenue came from one-time items, including $410 million of Jardiance milestones that won't recur, the company said, and obesity-drug prices are falling. If price cuts outrun volume growth, the premium shrinks. The 2027 forecast in early February is the test.
