The story investors are telling about gadgets is that AI has started an upgrade cycle: people are replacing PCs, phones and TVs with models built to run AI features, and the retailers and device makers that sell them are growing again. Best Buy ($BBY) gave it its best evidence yet before the open on Tuesday, reporting comparable sales growth of **6.1%**, its highest in five years, and Reuters wrote that the retailer is betting on holiday demand for AI-enabled phones, TVs and smart glasses.

The trouble is in the units. Global PC shipments fell 20.1% in the third quarter and smartphone shipments about 22%, according to IDC data reported by several outlets, because memory chips, bought up by AI data centers, became so expensive that device prices rose sharply. Dell Technologies ($DELL) and HP ($HPQ) report after the close on Tuesday, and their PC numbers are the next test of which story is true.

## The story in one sentence

> Best Buy credited "disciplined execution, a compelling product cycle and continued market share gains" for its quarter, in its results release on 24 November.

The chain runs like this: if AI features make old devices feel out of date, people replace them sooner; that lifts revenue at Best Buy, Apple ($AAPL), Dell and HP; and because those devices need more memory, it also lifts demand for chips from Micron ($MU). The weak link is the price. Memory makers are selling first to AI data centers, so device makers pay more for every gigabyte, and the question is whether shoppers keep paying it.

## The evidence for it

### 1. Best Buy's sales beat by the widest margin in years

Comparable sales **rose 6.1%** against the 2.3% StreetAccount consensus cited by CNBC, and online sales in the US grew 10.8%, the release showed. Computing, mobile phones, AI glasses and trading cards led, and the company raised its full-year comparable-sales forecast to 3.4% to 4.0% from 1.9% to 3.0%. ### 2. Apple's iPhone had a record September quarter

Apple's iPhone revenue **rose 17% to $57.6 billion** in the three months to September, a record for that quarter, MacRumors and Silicon Republic reported. Total revenue grew 12% to $114.5 billion. That is the strongest single piece of evidence that people are buying new phones at higher prices.

### 3. The PC makers grew revenue too

HP's personal-systems revenue grew 18% to $11.8 billion in its quarter to July, the company said, and Dell's PC business grew 20% to $15.0 billion in its quarter to July, according to CNBC and Dell's release. HP's finance chief, Karen Parkhill, said in August that it had raised prices to offset higher component costs, Quartz reported.

## The case against it

The sharpest doubter is Bank of America's Christopher Nardone, who kept an Underperform rating on Best Buy last week and argued that memory costs will force promotions, hurt margins and curb demand next year, Investing.com and MarketScreener reported. The trackers who count boxes agree on the mechanism.

1. **Units are falling fast.** IDC counted 62.7 million PCs shipped in the third quarter, down 20.1%; Gartner had a 22.4% drop and Omdia 21.2%. Smartphones had their weakest third quarter in 14 years by Counterpoint's count, down 20%, as Telecoms.com and FoneArena reported. A real upgrade cycle adds units; this one subtracts them. This is the strongest counter-point.
2. **The price rise is a cost, not pricing power.** TrendForce forecast on 30 September that contract prices for standard DRAM would rise another 10% to 15% in the fourth quarter and NAND storage 15% to 20%, driven by cloud and AI server buying while consumer demand is weak. Gartner analyst Namrata Banerjee said higher memory and storage costs are "now fully reflected in PC pricing," GIGAZINE and TechEdgeAI reported.
3. **Margins are already paying for it.** Apple guided its December-quarter gross margin to 45% to 46%, down from 47.9%, "primarily the impact of higher memory costs," Chief Financial Officer Kevan Parekh said, as Quartz and 24/7 Wall St. reported. HP's PC units fell 16% in its last quarter even as revenue rose 18%, and its segment margin slipped to 4.6% from 5.2%, Quartz reported.

## What would change minds

- **HP reports after the close on Tuesday, 24 November.** Analysts expect adjusted EPS of $0.74, within its $0.69 to $0.79 guide, according to Seeking Alpha and Zacks via Yahoo Finance; HP had called this quarter the low point for PC margins. A fiscal 2027 forecast that assumes units keep falling would be the first crack in the price-led story.
- **Dell's PC numbers tonight,** next to its AI servers. Revenue growth with shrinking units would confirm the pattern; unit growth would be the first real sign of an upgrade.
- **Micron reports on 17 December.** If memory prices are still rising, device makers' costs go up again in the new year; we set out that debate in [our memory narrative](/news/2026-11-21/wall-street-is-betting-the-memory-boom-has-peaked-microns-17-december-report-will-test-it/).

## Our read

**Partly supported.** The demand for new devices is real in dollars: Best Buy's 6.1% and Apple's 17% are not small numbers. But a fifth fewer PCs and phones shipped in the third quarter, so most of the gain is higher prices set by memory costs, and that kind of growth reverses when chip prices stop rising or squeezes margins while they keep going. The number we'll watch is HP's and Dell's PC unit trend tonight; until units turn up, this is a memory-price story wearing an upgrade-cycle label.
