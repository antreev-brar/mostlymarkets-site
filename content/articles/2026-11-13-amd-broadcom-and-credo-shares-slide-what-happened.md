## What happened
Shares of AMD ($AMD), Nvidia's closest rival in AI chips, **sank 9.6%** by 10:15 a.m. ET on Friday, with custom-chip maker Broadcom ($AVGO) down 6.0% and data-cable chipmaker Credo ($CRDO) down 8.8%. Traders were unwinding Thursday's bet that Jensen Huang's resignation at Nvidia ($NVDA) would push its customers toward rivals, Invezz and Investing.com reported, and Yahoo Finance said the rest of the chip sector fell with them.

The reversal started before the bell, when Nvidia rose and AMD and Broadcom slipped about 2%, Invezz reported. Investing.com put it down to profit-taking after AMD's run. Once trading opened, Nvidia turned lower too, and the selling spread across AI hardware.

## Why it matters
Thursday's rally priced in orders that haven't moved yet: AMD gained 30% and Broadcom 24% in the week to Thursday's close, as we [reported](/news/2026-11-13/broadcom-avgo-and-amd-shares-jump-after-nvidias-ceo-resigns/). At 10:15 a.m., AMD at $529.10 was below Wednesday's close, so Thursday's whole gain was gone.

The pressure isn't only about Nvidia. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to about **5.15%** on Friday after the producer price report, CNBC reported, and Yahoo Finance said bets on more Fed rate hikes were weighing on riskier assets. Higher yields hit the most richly valued stocks hardest. Dell ($DELL) fell 9.1%, Coherent ($COHR) 9.5% and Marvell ($MRVL) 7.9% without news of their own.

## What the market is telling us
AMD's shares moved 5% or more on 59 of the last 252 sessions, and Friday's drop is its biggest since 5 November, when it fell 17.3%. The stock is up 173.3% this year and closed Thursday 9.9% below its October high. Broadcom, Credo and Coherent are also on course for their worst day since that 5 November rout. Nvidia's results on Tuesday 17 November are the first test of whether any orders shifted; until then, these stocks are trading on guesses, and the guesses are swinging 10% a day.
