Alphabet ($GOOGL) had its worst day since its 2004 listing on Thursday, after a search-revenue miss and free cash flow of **negative $9.07 billion**. Its spending on property and equipment more than doubled from a year earlier to $51.36 billion in the September quarter. Meta ($META) and Amazon ($AMZN) burned cash last quarter too.

We compared seven tech giants: Microsoft ($MSFT), Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, and Oracle ($ORCL), which are building the data centers; Nvidia ($NVDA), which sells them chips; and Apple ($AAPL), which mostly isn't building. We set revenue growth and free cash flow (cash from operations minus capital spending) over the latest four quarters in SEC filings against the forward P/E, Friday's close over the next 12 months of expected earnings, from CNBC data.

```picks
like: NVDA, MSFT, GOOGL
skip: AAPL
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Nvidia ($NVDA)

**Revenue growth, 12 months to July 2026: 83.4%**. Nvidia designs the chips most AI models are trained and run on.

1. That's the fastest of the seven, and its operating margin of 65.2% is the widest.
2. Microsoft said on Wednesday that customer demand "continues to exceed available supply," and in September Goldman Sachs forecast the same five builders would spend about $1.2 trillion on AI infrastructure in 2027, up from about $800 billion this year.
3. Free cash flow was $127.01 billion over the same 12 months, second only to Apple's, because Nvidia owns no chip factories.

**The price:** 16.9 times forward earnings, less than half Apple's multiple. Earnings per share more than doubled in the past year while the stock went nowhere, so at 25.9 times the past year's earnings, its multiple is lower than at the end of any of its last five fiscal years, by our calculation.

**What could go wrong:** its customers' cash. The low forward multiple rests on analysts expecting earnings per share more than 50% above the past year's; if Alphabet, Meta, and Amazon, all burning cash, trim their 2027 budgets, that forecast falls with them.

### 2. Microsoft ($MSFT)

**Free cash flow, 12 months to September 2026: $59.96 billion**. Microsoft sells Windows, Office, and Azure, its cloud-computing service.

1. It's the only one of the five builders with positive free cash flow in every quarter this year: last quarter it spent 68 cents of each dollar of operating cash flow on property and equipment, the other four more than a dollar each, by our calculation. Chief Financial Officer Amy Hood expects free cash flow to stay positive through June 2027.
2. Azure revenue grew 48% from a year earlier in the September quarter, faster than Microsoft had forecast even with outside data-center capacity arriving late, and Hood expects 49% to 50% growth at constant currency this quarter.
3. Its commercial backlog, signed contracts not yet booked as revenue, rose 73% from a year earlier to $677 billion, nearly twice its revenue over the past 12 months.

**The price:** 26.5 times forward earnings, second only to Apple among the seven, which we think is fair for the widest operating margin after Nvidia's.

**What could go wrong:** more delays. Hood said some capacity expected this quarter has slipped into the first half of 2027; if it slips again, supply, not demand, will cap Azure's growth.

### 3. Alphabet ($GOOGL)

**Forward P/E at Friday's close: 21.2**. Alphabet owns Google search, YouTube, and Google Cloud.

1. At 30.5% below its May high at Friday's close, the price assumes AI chatbots are eating into search.
2. Search is still growing. Its revenue rose 12% from a year earlier in the September quarter, and operating profit at Google Services, which includes search and YouTube, rose 17%.
3. The spending is paying off in Google Cloud: its revenue more than doubled from a year earlier to $30.71 billion, and its operating profit tripled to $11.12 billion.

**The price:** about a fifth below Microsoft's forward multiple and more than 40% below Apple's, for faster revenue growth than either.

**What could go wrong:** the cash. Alphabet plans to raise capital spending significantly in 2027 and is already borrowing: it issued $29.0 billion of debt in the September quarter and spent nothing on buybacks, against $11.5 billion a year earlier, the release showed. If search slows too, the discount is deserved.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Apple ($AAPL)

**Forward P/E at Friday's close: 37.4**. Apple sells the iPhone, Mac, and iPad, plus App Store and iCloud services.

1. That's the highest of the seven, for the slowest revenue growth: 14.2% over the 12 months to June. We think investors are paying for distance from the AI spending race: Apple's $10 billion of capital spending over those 12 months is about a fifth of Alphabet's in the September quarter alone.
2. Growth is slowing: Apple forecast 9% to 11% revenue growth for the September quarter, down from 16% in the June quarter.
3. Memory, the shortage Intel blamed on Thursday, keeps getting pricier. Tim Cook, then chief executive, warned in July that Apple's memory bill would rise further, and market researcher TrendForce expects contract prices for conventional DRAM to rise another 10% to 15% this quarter.

**The price:** 39.5 times the past year's earnings, higher than at the end of any of its last four fiscal years, by our calculation. The forward multiple is barely lower because analysts expect earnings per share only about 6% above the past year's: a premium price for single-digit growth.

**What would change our mind:** a holiday-quarter forecast with faster growth and a gross margin that holds up against memory costs, when Apple reports at 4:30 p.m. ET on Monday ([our preview](/news/2026-10-29/what-to-expect-from-apples-aapl-q4-earnings/)).
