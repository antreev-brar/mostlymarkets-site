## What happened

Shares of data-analytics software maker Palantir Technologies ($PLTR) **fell 7.6%** to $164.84 before the open on Tuesday, as of 6:15 a.m. ET, after its third-quarter report showed new business growing far more slowly than sales. The total value of contracts Palantir signed in the quarter rose 6% from a year earlier to $2.9 billion, Fortune and FourWeekMBA reported, while revenue grew 96%.

The slowdown sits in US commercial, Palantir's fastest-growing business. Contract value signed with US companies came to $1.46 billion, up 11% from a year earlier but down from a record $2.13 billion in the second quarter, FourWeekMBA reported, and Dolphin Research noted that total contract value fell from the quarter before. The stock had risen 1.8% after hours on Monday, when the results came out, before turning lower overnight.

## Why it matters

Bookings are next year's revenue. Palantir raised its 2026 sales forecast to about $8.53 billion and guided to fourth-quarter revenue of $2.65 billion, well above estimates ([our recap](/news/2026-11-02/palantir-pltr-beats-q3-estimates-as-us-commercial-sales-overtake-government/)), but a stock valued on years of fast growth needs the pipeline to keep pace. US commercial remaining deal value, the contracts signed but not yet billed, still grew 79% to $6.52 billion, StreetInsider reported, so one soft quarter of signings is not yet a trend; big deals land unevenly.

## What the market is telling us

Palantir's shares moved 5% or more on 38 of the last 252 sessions, and the last drop this big came on 30 October, when it fell 6.3% in the chip rout. Before this morning the stock was up 0.3% this year and 14.7% below its 52-week closing high of $209.05 from 9 October; $1,000 invested five years ago would be worth $6,880. A close at this morning's price would be the lowest since 6 August. We read the drop as the market pricing the next few quarters' bookings, not this quarter's sales: fourth-quarter contract value, in its next report, is the number that settles it.
