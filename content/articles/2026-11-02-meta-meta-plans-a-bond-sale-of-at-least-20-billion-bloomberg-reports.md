Meta ($META) is weighing a bond sale of **at least $20 billion**, split between US dollars and euros, Bloomberg News reported on Monday, as relayed by Reuters and Newsquawk. The size could change with demand, and the company has not filed terms with the SEC. It would be Meta's first bond sale in euros, Quartz and The Next Web reported, citing the same Bloomberg story.

The timing is what matters. On Friday, S&P Global turned its outlook on Meta's debt negative because capital spending now outruns the cash the business brings in, as [our breakdown](/news/2026-11-01/meta-meta-now-spends-more-than-it-makes-in-cash-is-the-business-still-strong/) of the company laid out. Meta last borrowed in April, raising about $25 billion, according to Bloomberg reports carried by Business Recorder and The Star.

It is the third big tech borrower in one day: Oracle ($ORCL) filed to raise [$20 billion in stock and bonds](/news/2026-11-02/oracle-orcl-shares-slide-after-it-files-to-raise-20-billion-in-stock-and-bonds/), and Alphabet ($GOOGL) [returned to the euro bond market](/news/2026-11-02/alphabet-googl-returns-to-the-euro-bond-market-days-after-its-record-drop/). Meta's shares were down 0.6% at $611.25 as of 2:15 p.m. ET.

The next thing to learn is the final size and the interest rate Meta pays over Treasuries, which will show what lenders now charge for funding the AI build-out.
