Target ($TGT) reports its fiscal third quarter, the three months to 1 November, before the open on Wednesday, 18 November, Earnings Whispers and Barchart reported. Chief Executive Michael Fiddelke took over from Brian Cornell on 1 February. The stock **rose 63.2%** this year to Tuesday's close in our data, part of the bet that the shopper can carry this market ([our story](/news/2026-11-17/wall-street-is-hiding-in-the-shopper-this-weeks-retailers-will-test-it/)), and slipped 1.0% after hours to $157.88 as of 6:26 p.m. ET.

Last quarter, comparable sales, those at stores and websites open a year or more, **rose 3.8%**, with customer traffic up 3.6%, the 19 August release showed. Adjusted EPS was $4.11, but $1.65 of that came from refunds of tariffs paid earlier; without them it was $2.46, ahead of the $2.30 Zacks consensus, Zacks and RTTNews reported. The shares fell before the open, RTTNews reported, then closed up 4.3% and reached their closing high of $169.89 on 24 August.

## What analysts expect

The Zacks consensus is **$2.04 a share** on an adjusted basis, up about 15% from $1.78 a year earlier, on revenue of $26.41 billion, Zacks reported. Earnings Whispers puts it at $2.03 on $26.38 billion, and Nasdaq's data shows $2.05. Revenue was $25.27 billion a year earlier in Target's filings, so analysts expect growth of about 4.5%.

The debate this report settles: **can Target grow profit without the refunds?** In August it raised its full-year EPS forecast to $9.90 to $10.90 from $7.50 to $8.50, a range that includes the $1.65; without it, the midpoint rose $0.75, RTTNews and Zacks reported. Earnings Whispers' whisper number of $2.17 sits about 7% above consensus, so an in-line quarter may not be enough.

## What to watch

- **Comparable sales.** A figure near August's 3.8% backs the raised full-year sales forecast of about 5%; a fall back toward zero would say the turnaround was a summer bounce.
- **Traffic.** More visits drove last quarter's growth. Home Depot ($HD) on Tuesday grew on bigger tickets with fewer visits, and its stock fell 2.5% ([our recap](/news/2026-11-17/home-depot-hd-tops-q3-estimates-and-names-cfo-richard-mcphail-ceo/)).
- **The full-year range.** Any cut to the underlying forecast, with Walmart ($WMT) reporting on Thursday ([our breakdown](/news/2026-11-14/walmart-wmt-reports-thursday-at-46-times-earnings-is-the-business-worth-it/)), would test a stock 6.1% from its high.

## How the stock has done

Target closed at $159.53 on Tuesday, down 0.5% on the day and up 4.1% over the past month. It trades at 16.6 times trailing earnings, above its two-year median of 14.0 times in our data, and at 17.1 times forward earnings, near the 17.7 times median of its retail peers. $1,000 invested five years ago would be worth $629 today.
