General Dynamics ($GD) reported third-quarter revenue of $14.57 billion on Friday morning, up 12.9% year on year and well above the roughly $13.7 billion consensus cited by GuruFocus. Diluted earnings of **$4.49 a share** beat the $4.14 average estimate in Nasdaq's earnings calendar, and the company raised its full-year profit forecast for the third time this year. The shares rose as much as 2.3% in the morning, Yahoo Finance reported, and closed up 0.5% at $372.59 on a day the Nasdaq fell 2.9%.

## What stood out

**The order book nearly doubled in a year.** New orders came to almost **$99 billion** in the quarter, 6.8 times revenue, the release showed, mostly Navy submarine awards. Defense backlog jumped 73% in three months to $195 billion, so the shipyards have years of work booked before they win another contract.

*Verdict: demand is not the question; how fast the yards can build is.*

**Gulfstream kept pace.** The business-jet unit delivered 40 aircraft and booked nearly $5 billion of orders, and aerospace revenue rose 9.3% to $3.54 billion with $513 million of operating earnings, the release showed. That is a margin of about 14.5%, against about 10.5% for the company as a whole.

**Margins are the weak spot.** General Dynamics kept its full-year operating margin forecast at about 10.5%, according to the call coverage. By our data, its 10.4% margin over the past 12 months ranks in the bottom third of our industrials group, where the median is 17.5%.

## The outlook

The company raised its 2026 EPS forecast to **$17.20 to $17.30**, from $16.80 to $16.90, and lifted its revenue outlook to $56.5 billion to $56.8 billion. The raise implies fourth-quarter EPS of roughly $4.37 to $4.47, after $12.83 in the first nine months.

It was Chief Executive Phebe Novakovic's last earnings call in the role. President Danny Deep becomes CEO on 1 January 2027 and Novakovic, CEO since 2013, becomes executive chairman, the company announced on 7 October.

## Our take

We were impressed by the size of the beat: revenue came in about 6% above the consensus, well above the 2% to 4% typical of a beat, and the orders lock in growth for years. On the other hand, the stock barely held its gains, sits 5.9% below its August high, and had already jumped 4.7% on Thursday, so much of the good news was in the price. Overall, a strong quarter for a business whose problem is capacity, not customers.

Revenue has grown 10.8% a year over the past two years, in the top quarter of our industrials group, but free cash flow margins of 9.8% trail the group's 12.9% median. At 21.3 times forward earnings, the price assumes that backlog turns into higher margins as well as higher sales. The next test is the 2027 forecast in January, Deep's first as CEO.
