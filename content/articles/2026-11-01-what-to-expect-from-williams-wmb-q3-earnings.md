Williams ($WMB), which runs the Transco gas pipeline from Texas to New York, reports third-quarter results after the market closes on Monday, 2 November, with a call at 9:30 a.m. ET on Tuesday, the company said on 13 October. It is the first quarter to include **Momentum Midstream**, the Haynesville gas-gathering business Williams bought for up to $5.5 billion and closed on 3 September.

Last quarter, adjusted EBITDA rose 6% to $1.92 billion, and Williams raised its 2026 forecast to $8.3 billion to $8.5 billion, from $8.05 billion to $8.35 billion, including the deal. It also lifted its long-term target to growth of more than 11% a year through 2030. Adjusted EPS of 50 cents, up from 46, matched some forecasts and missed Zacks's 52 cents. The stock **rose 1.5%** the next day, 4 August.

## What analysts expect

- **Adjusted EPS:** 55 cents, up 12% from 49 cents a year ago, according to Zacks; TipRanks and MarketBeat show about 54 cents.
- **Revenue:** estimates differ by about $200 million between compilers, from $2.99 billion (MarketBeat) to about $3.19 billion (Zacks).

Williams missed Zacks's EPS consensus in three of the last four quarters, Zacks said.

## What to watch

- **Power for data centers.** In July Williams sold 49% of five on-site power projects for data centers to a group led by Blackstone for $5.34 billion, Yahoo Finance and Hart Energy reported. The first phase of its Socrates project in Ohio is running; new customers or projects would show this side of the business growing beyond pipes.
- **Debt.** The Momentum deal was paid with about $3.5 billion of cash and debt plus about $2 billion of Williams shares. Jefferies cut its price target to $83 from $85 on 14 October, TD Cowen to $85 from $87 on 16 October, both keeping buy ratings. The stock **fell 4.9%** on 15 October, its biggest drop of the year by our count.
- **The peer bar.** Kinder Morgan ($KMI) reported adjusted EPS up 21% to 35 cents in October, and ONEOK ($OKE) raised its 2026 EBITDA forecast for the third time this year. Gas pipes are having a good season; a Williams raise would be in step, not a surprise.

## How the stock has done

Williams closed Friday at $74.35, **up 23.7%** this year and 7.4% over the past month, but 6.4% below its 52-week closing high of $79.40 from 19 May. It hasn't moved 5% in a session in the past year, and $1,000 invested five years ago is worth $2,647 today.
