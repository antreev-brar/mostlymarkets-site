Live Nation Entertainment ($LYV) reported third-quarter revenue of $10.1 billion after the close on Thursday, up 19% from a year earlier and above the $9.59 billion analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters. Earnings were $1.69 a diluted share on a GAAP basis, against $0.73 a year ago and the $1.50 consensus listed by Nasdaq and MarketBeat. The shares slipped 2.8% after hours, opened Friday down 5.6%, and as of 2:15 p.m. ET were **down 2.6% at $176.49**.

## What stood out

**International crowds did the heavy lifting.** Concerts revenue rose 20% to $8.7 billion, and fans outside North America **rose 29%**, accounting for 60% of the growth, the release said. Through October the company had sold about 170 million tickets for 2026 shows, already more than in all of 2025. "In a world of endless screens and AI-generated everything, the one thing that can't be replicated is being there," Chief Executive Michael Rapino said in the release.

*Verdict: demand is broad, across regions and venue sizes, with no sign of a stretched consumer.*

**Concerts still keep little of each ticket.** Concerts AOI, operating profit before depreciation, deal costs and stock pay, rose 17% to $603 million, but that is only **6.9% of revenue**, down from 7.1% a year earlier, by our sums from the release. Most of the profit comes from the smaller businesses: Ticketmaster earned $316 million on $870 million of revenue and sponsorship $350 million on $506 million.

*Verdict: growth is coming from the low-margin side, so profit grows slower than sales.*

**The one debate: can Ticketmaster grow while it is in court?** In April a federal jury found that Live Nation and Ticketmaster illegally monopolized primary ticketing, NBC News and law firm Paul Weiss reported; remedies, which the states want to include selling Ticketmaster, go to a separate hearing. Live Nation has set aside $450 million this year for that case and other investigations. This quarter, resale ticket value was flat, which the company tied to its own curbs on scalpers, yet ticketing AOI still **rose 10%**.

## The outlook

Live Nation gave no numbers for revenue or profit. It now expects Ticketmaster AOI to grow at a high single-digit rate this year, up from mid single digits in its second-quarter release, and still expects double-digit AOI growth in Concerts and sponsorship. Concerts AOI is up only 4% after nine months, so that target needs a stronger fourth quarter; Chief Financial Officer Joe Berchtold repeated it when an analyst pressed on the math, according to call coverage by GuruFocus and The Motley Fool. Ticket sales for 2027 primary shows are up by double digits on the same point last year.

## Our take

We were impressed by the size of the beat and the raised Ticketmaster outlook. On the other hand, the Concerts margin slipped and its full-year target rests on the fourth quarter. Overall, a strong quarter that doesn't change the long-run picture: revenue has grown 9.4% a year over two years by our analysis of SEC filings, below the 11.6% median of the 25 communication services stocks we track, and free cash flow was 3.2% of revenue in the past 12 months.

Coverage of Friday's drop gave no single cause; Investing.com pointed to the stock's run, **up 27.2% this year** to Thursday's close, 3.8% below its August high. The next tests are the court's remedies ruling and fourth-quarter results early next year, when Concerts has to show its double-digit growth.
