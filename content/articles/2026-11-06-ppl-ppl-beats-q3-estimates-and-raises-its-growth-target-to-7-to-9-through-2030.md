PPL ($PPL) reported third-quarter revenue of **$2.50 billion** before the open on Friday, up 11.5% from a year earlier and above the $2.44 billion that analysts surveyed by Zacks expected, according to the AP. Earnings from ongoing operations, PPL's adjusted measure, were 54 cents a share against a 53-cent Zacks consensus, and the Pennsylvania and Kentucky utility raised its long-term earnings growth target to 7% to 9% a year. The shares **edged up 0.3% to $36.12** as of 2:15 p.m. ET, while Southern ($SO) and Duke Energy ($DUK) fell about 1%.

## What stood out

Rate increases did most of the work. Kentucky added 4 cents a share of ongoing earnings on retail rates that took effect on 1 January, and Pennsylvania added 4 cents from new distribution rates on 1 July and more transmission revenue, the release said. Operating income **rose 17% to $665 million**. Interest expense rose 11%, to $234 million, as borrowing grew to pay for the building.
*Verdict: a clean quarter, driven by rate rises regulators had already approved.*

The demand behind the plan is still growing. PPL Electric's data-centre pipeline grew for the 11th straight quarter, and in Kentucky the load under signed reimbursement agreements reached **2.3 gigawatts**, 1 GW more than three months earlier. Invitium Energy, PPL's generation venture with Blackstone Infrastructure, agreed nonbinding terms to supply about 2 GW to a QTS data-centre project in Salem Township, Pennsylvania, for an initial 20 years.

"We're beginning to see those opportunities translate into tangible commercial progress," Chief Executive Vincent Sorgi said in the release.

The one debate is who pays for the growth. PPL now plans about **$3.0 billion of new equity**, on top of more than $1.5 billion already sold forward in 2025 and 2026, and expects dividend growth at the low end of its 4% to 6% target. Diluted shares were already 1.5% higher than a year ago, which is why ongoing profit grew 16% but ongoing EPS only 13%.
*Verdict: faster growth, partly paid for by shareholders through new stock and slower dividend rises.*

## The outlook

The new 2027 range has a midpoint of $2.10, 8.2% above this year's $1.94 midpoint. PPL said $3.5 billion to $4.5 billion of further Kentucky generation spending, not yet in the plan, could lift growth to 9% or more through 2030. Neither that nor any Invitium earnings is counted in the new targets. We found no published consensus for 2027 on the same basis, so we can't say whether the range beat it.

## Our take

We were impressed that PPL raised its growth target while keeping 2026 on track and putting a named customer behind Invitium. On the other hand, the QTS terms are not binding, and the equity bill means each extra dollar of spending earns shareholders a little less. At 17.2 times expected earnings, against a median of 19.0 times for the 33 utilities we track, the price gives little credit for the top half of the new range that management is aiming for.

The stock is up 2.8% this year and sits 9.6% below its April high. We read a small gain on a down day for utilities as belief in the plan, with the binding contract still to come.
