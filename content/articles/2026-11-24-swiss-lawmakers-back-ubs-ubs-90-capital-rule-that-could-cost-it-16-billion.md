On Tuesday, the economic affairs committee of Switzerland's lower house backed a rule that would make UBS ($UBS) back its foreign subsidiaries with common equity, a bank's top-quality capital, covering **90% of their value**, Reuters and Seeking Alpha reported. The upper house had already voted 29 to 16 for the same 90% level, swissinfo reported.

The rule is Switzerland's answer to the 2023 collapse of Credit Suisse, which UBS absorbed, and the cost is large: by UBS's own estimate it would need about **$16 billion** more of that capital, GuruFocus and Crypto Briefing reported, capital it could not hand back to shareholders. UBS says the rule would hurt its ability to compete, and Reuters reported the debate has revived talk that the bank could move abroad.

UBS's US-listed shares were little changed before the open, up 0.2% at $44.96 as of 9:05 a.m. ET, and sit 19.4% below their August high. The full lower house still has to vote; any change sends the bill back to the upper house, and swissinfo reported a final decision is more likely in 2027. UBS plans to set out its capital and buyback priorities in February.
