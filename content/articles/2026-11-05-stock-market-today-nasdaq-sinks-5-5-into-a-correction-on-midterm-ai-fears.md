The AI trade broke on Thursday. Two days after Democrats swept Congress and key governor races, investors sold the chipmakers, memory makers and power suppliers that had led the market on fears that tougher rules will slow the building of AI data centers, Reuters and CNBC reported. The Nasdaq Composite **sank 5.5%**, the S&P 500 fell 4.4% and the Dow lost 1,833 points, or 3.4%.

It was the Nasdaq's biggest one-day drop since the tariff sell-off of April 2025, and it left the index in a correction, more than 10% below its record close of 6 October, Reuters and CNBC reported. Nvidia ($NVDA) fell 9.3% to $181.60, its worst day since 27 January 2025 in our data, and it now sits 24.1% below its October high.

## Why the election hit chips two days late

On Wednesday the market had shrugged off the result. On Thursday it focused on what new officials can do to data centers: in Texas, Democrat Gina Hinojosa, who campaigned for a moratorium on new ones, won the governor's race, and Wells Fargo estimates the state holds about 27% of planned US data-center capacity, CNBC and The Daily Reporter reported. Qualcomm's ($QCOM) weak profit forecast on Wednesday evening added a second weight, CNBC reported, and the stock fell 9.5%.

The stocks priced for the longest AI boom fell hardest. Sandisk ($SNDK) plunged 22.3%, Micron ($MU) 17.5% and AMD ($AMD) 17.3%; our [chip story](/news/2026-11-05/sandisk-micron-and-amd-shares-plunge-what-happened/) has the detail. Fuel-cell maker [Bloom Energy fell 16.4%](/news/2026-11-05/why-bloom-energy-be-stock-is-sinking-today/) even on a 900-megawatt deal. The VIX, Wall Street's fear gauge, jumped 69.7% to 28.18, and Reuters and CNBC reported heavy selling by retail traders.

## Musk's companies kept falling

Tesla ($TSLA) **plunged 10.8%** to $343.69, after a smaller drop on Wednesday, and SpaceX fell about 9%, Bloomberg and Yahoo Finance reported. Investors fear a Democratic Congress will look harder at Elon Musk's federal contracts after he spent heavily backing Republicans, Quartz and Yahoo Finance reported. Tesla is now down 23.6% this year.

## The few that rose

AppLovin ($APP), the mobile-ad software company, **soared 31.0%** after reporting on Wednesday evening that third-quarter revenue rose 55% from a year earlier to $2.18 billion and net income rose 72%, according to its results release. It was one of the few big gains in a market where buyers went to bonds: the 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.84%.

## What's next

After the bell, Cloudflare ($NET) was up 14.9% in after-hours trade at 5:15 p.m. ET on its results, in our data, a first test of whether good numbers can still lift AI-linked stocks. The October jobs report comes out at 8:30 a.m. ET on Friday.
