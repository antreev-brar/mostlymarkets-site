While chip stocks bounced on Monday, a different group stayed near its lows: profitable companies that have lost half their value or more for reasons that have nothing to do with AI. On Monday Piper Sandler became the third broker in a week to downgrade Insulet, the latest sign that some of these falls are still being argued over.

We screened our coverage for profitable companies furthest below their 52-week highs, then judged each on growth, margins, cash and valuation against its peers from SEC filings (`analyse.py`, data to September 2026), long run first. Being cheap was not enough: we asked whether the reason for the fall is temporary or lasting.

```picks
skip: PODD, ROL, CHTR
like: BSX
```

## 3 stocks we'd skip

### 1. Insulet ($PODD)
**60.9% below its 52-week high.** Insulet makes the Omnipod, a tubeless insulin pump worn as a patch.
1. Its growth record is the best in its group: revenue rose **24.5% a year** over five fiscal years, against 10.0% for the median medical device maker. That record is now under attack.
2. Rivals' patch pumps arrive next year. Baird cut the stock to Neutral on 20 November and projects Insulet could lose about 20 points of patch-pump market share next year, Investing.com reported; Piper Sandler followed on Monday, citing clinicians who expect Omnipod's share of patients to fall, Investing.com and TipRanks reported.
3. It turns too little of its profit into cash: free cash flow was 8.2% of revenue in the 12 months to September 2026, the lowest of the 18 device makers we track.

**The price:** 35.7 times free cash flow and 17.8 times expected earnings, exactly the peer median, so the price doesn't yet allow for a share loss.
**What would change our mind:** new-patient starts holding up in the first half of 2027, after rival pumps launch.

### 2. Rollins ($ROL)
**42.8% below its 52-week high.** Rollins owns Orkin and other pest-control brands.
1. A high-return business: return on capital of about **29%**, against 13.4% for the median industrial, and a free cash flow margin of 16.8% over the last 12 months.
2. Growth is slowing where it matters. In July it cut its 2026 organic growth forecast to about 6% from roughly 7% to 8%, blaming fewer residential leads, its quarterly filing and Investing.com showed. Revenue rose 6.2% in the September quarter, below the 10.3% median for industrials.
3. Wells Fargo rates it Underweight, citing softer residential demand and rivals marketing more aggressively, CNBC and Investing.com reported.

**The price:** 31.4 times expected earnings, against 19.8 times for the median industrial. That is still a growth multiple for a company whose growth is slowing.
**What would change our mind:** residential leads growing again, or a price nearer the industrial median.

### 3. Charter Communications ($CHTR)
**50.5% below its 52-week high.** Charter sells Spectrum cable internet, TV and mobile service.
1. It is barely growing: revenue rose 2.6% a year over five fiscal years, against 13.6% for the median communications company.
2. It is losing its core customers faster. Counting Cox, which it bought in August, Charter lost **233,000** internet customers in the third quarter, against 171,000 a year earlier, Deadline and TelecomLead reported. It answered with a plan to cut $2 billion of yearly costs, Quartz reported.
3. Debt leaves no room for error: net debt of $108 billion is about 18 years of operating profit, by its September balance sheet.

**The price:** Charter looks very cheap on earnings, but the multiple is low because the debt, not the shareholders, has first claim on the cash.
**What would change our mind:** a quarter in which internet customer losses shrink at both Charter and Cox.

## 1 stock we like

### Boston Scientific ($BSX)
**58.6% below its 52-week high.** Boston Scientific makes heart and blood-vessel devices, including the Watchman stroke-prevention implant.
1. Strong over the long run: revenue grew **15.2% a year** over five fiscal years, against 10.0% for its peers, and its operating margin rose 18.8 points over that time, the biggest gain in the group.
2. The fall has two parts, and one is temporary. A cyberattack halted shipments for eight business days and cost about $150 million of third-quarter sales, StocksToTrade and Yahoo Finance reported; that is why revenue rose only 1.4% in the quarter. The lasting part is slower Watchman demand, which the company blamed in July on new clinical evidence that changed doctors' referrals, Yahoo Finance and Trefis reported.
3. Its fourth-quarter forecast of $0.77 to $0.79 of adjusted earnings a share was below the $0.83 analysts expected, Investing.com and StockStory reported, so the bar for 2027 is already lower.

**The price:** 13.7 times expected earnings, against 17.8 times for the median device maker and a median trailing P/E of 54 times over the last two years. The market is pricing Boston Scientific as a slow grower.
**What could go wrong:** Watchman keeps slowing into 2027, and the debt it is raising to buy Penumbra, $3.0 billion and 4.25 billion euros of new notes priced last week, Reuters and Seeking Alpha reported, makes a miss costlier.
