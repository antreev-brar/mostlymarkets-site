The administration is preparing new tariffs on 16 trading partners after a probe into excess factory capacity and could announce them as soon as this week, Reuters reported, and the Wall Street Journal said duties on China and dozens of other countries would rise. The list includes China, the European Union, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Mexico, Vietnam and India, the Korea Times and Sourcing Journal reported. No rates had been published by 6 a.m. ET.

Nasdaq 100 futures **fell 2.2%**, Reuters reported, and the selling hit the companies whose supply chains run through Taiwan and Korea hardest: AMD ($AMD) was down 6.4% and Nvidia ($NVDA) 4.6% before the open, in our data. The duties would partly replace the global tariffs the Supreme Court struck down on 20 February, and partners that already struck deals, such as Japan and South Korea, are expected to keep their agreed rates, Reuters' sources said. That makes the rates on China and Taiwan the numbers that matter for chip costs.

The rates, and any product exemptions, are the next thing to watch. October PCE inflation, due at 8:30 a.m. ET today, is the other test for a market already pricing a December rate hike ([today's Daily](/daily/2026-11-25/)).
