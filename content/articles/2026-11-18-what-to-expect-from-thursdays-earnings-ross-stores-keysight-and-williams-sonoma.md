Three companies worth a combined $152 billion report on Thursday, 19 November: Williams-Sonoma ($WSM) before the open, and Ross Stores ($ROST) and Keysight Technologies ($KEYS) after the close. The two retailers both beat last quarter partly on one-off tariff refunds, so the question for them is what profit looks like without the help; for Keysight, it is whether its orders boom survived a month in which its stock fell 14%.

## Ross Stores ($ROST), after the close

The Zacks consensus is **$1.82** of earnings per share, up 15.2% from $1.58 a year earlier and near the top of Ross's own guide of $1.75 to $1.83, on revenue of about $6.2 billion, MarketBeat and Futu show. Last quarter Ross earned $2.66 a share, about 60 cents of it from tariff refunds, and comparable-store sales **rose 10%**, its release said; the shares rose 4.4% the next day in our price data. It guided third-quarter comparable sales to 6% to 7%, so that range, and the fourth-quarter guide of 4% to 5%, is what moves the stock. Ross closed at $227.64 on Wednesday, up 3.3% on the day and 26.4% this year.

## Keysight Technologies ($KEYS), after the close

Analysts tracked by MarketBeat expect adjusted earnings of **$3.37 a share** on revenue of $1.95 billion, inside the company's guide of $3.34 to $3.40 and $1.93 billion to $1.95 billion, against $1.91 a share a year ago. Last quarter its orders rose 56% to $2.09 billion and it raised its full-year sales outlook, its release and Benzinga show, yet the shares closed 6.3% lower the next day in our data. Orders are the number: Keysight sells the test equipment chip and network makers use to design new products, so orders say what those customers plan to spend. The stock is 26.2% below its 6 October high.

## Williams-Sonoma ($WSM), before the open

The consensus is **$2.18** of adjusted earnings per share on revenue of $2.00 billion, against $1.96 on $1.88 billion a year earlier, MarketBeat and Benzinga report; Zacks has $2.17. Last quarter comparable brand revenue rose 6.2% and adjusted EPS was $2.10, but excluding tariff refunds the gross margin fell 1.6 points, its release shows; the shares rose 1.1% that day. The gross margin is the number to watch, and the full-year comparable-revenue guide of 4% to 6.5%. The stock closed at $260.09, up 4.0% on Wednesday and 45.6% this year, 3.7% below its 4 November high.

## The one with the most riding on it

Keysight. At 21.6 times forward earnings it is still above the 17.6 times median of its peers in our data, and the stock has already lost a quarter of its value since October. Orders above last quarter's $2.09 billion would say its chip and network customers are still buying; a drop would confirm what the stock has been pricing. Williams-Sonoma carries the higher bar on the price, at 26.6 times trailing earnings against its own two-year median of 21.7, but its business is less tied to one boom.
