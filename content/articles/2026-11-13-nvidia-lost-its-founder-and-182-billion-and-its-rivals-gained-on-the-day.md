On Thursday morning Nvidia ($NVDA) said Jensen Huang, who co-founded the company in 1993 and ran it ever since, had resigned as chief executive and director with immediate effect, and that Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress would serve as interim CEO while the board searches inside and outside the company, according to its SEC filing. The resignation followed a week of reports about Huang's affairs, one of them with a Megaspeed executive US officials reportedly suspect of spying for China, CNBC and Al Jazeera reported. The stock fell as much as 14.4% at the open, then recovered to **close down 4.3%** at $166.19, a loss of about $182 billion in market value.

The money did not leave AI chips. It moved to Nvidia's rivals: AMD ($AMD) rose 5.9% and Broadcom ($AVGO) 5.5%, while the S&P 500 slipped only 0.35%. Before the bell on Friday that trade was partly unwinding, with Nvidia up 2.8% at 6:15 a.m. ET and AMD and Broadcom each down about 2%, ahead of October's producer prices at 8:30 a.m.

Nvidia opened at $151.01 and touched $148.80, **down 14.4%**, before buyers came in; it traded 748 million shares, 4.5 times its 20-day average, in our data. The $166.19 close is the stock's lowest since 30 March and sits 30.5% below its 6 October record. Nvidia said its operations, customer commitments and product roadmap would continue without interruption, and the board named Harvey Jones its first-ever chair.

Since last Friday's close AMD has **risen 30.3%** and Broadcom 24.1%, while Nvidia has fallen 11.0%, in our data. Traders are betting the two win AI-chip orders if Nvidia's customers hesitate, mlq.ai and Proactive Investors reported. AMD is now worth about $960 billion, a quarter of Nvidia's roughly $4.0 trillion.

### Nebius plunged 17% on a quarter that beat estimates

Nebius ($NBIS), which rents out Nvidia-powered computing, **fell 16.8%** even though third-quarter revenue rose 571% to $980.8 million, above the roughly $907.5 million analysts expected, TheStreet and Benzinga reported. It said 2026 revenue would come in at the low end of its $3 billion to $3.4 billion range, because delays at third-party data centers push some sales into 2027. CoreWeave ($CRWV) fell 6.9%, giving back much of Wednesday's rally on its own results.
