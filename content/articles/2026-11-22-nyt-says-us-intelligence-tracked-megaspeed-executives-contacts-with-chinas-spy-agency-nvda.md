The New York Times reported on Sunday that US intelligence tracked contacts between Alice Huang, an executive at Singapore-based Nvidia ($NVDA) customer Megaspeed, and an officer of China's Ministry of State Security from spring 2025 until this fall, citing US officials, according to Reuters and Gizmodo. The period covers her reported relationship with Jensen Huang, who resigned as Nvidia's chief executive on 12 November.

The report adds to a story that began with a US memo the Financial Times described on 7 November ([our story](/news/2026-11-07/ft-says-a-us-memo-ties-nvidia-nvda-ceo-to-a-suspected-chinese-spy-days-before-a-house-briefing/)). It does not settle whether she was a spy: US officials have cast doubt on that, Reuters said, and Beijing has called the claims a smear. Megaspeed has been under US investigation since 2024 over concerns it smuggled Nvidia chips to China. A spokesperson for Jensen Huang declined to comment, and Reuters could not reach Alice Huang.

The stock **fell 14.0%** on Friday, its biggest drop since 27 January 2025 in our data. The next test is Jensen Huang's closed-door interviews with three House committees on 1 and 2 December.
