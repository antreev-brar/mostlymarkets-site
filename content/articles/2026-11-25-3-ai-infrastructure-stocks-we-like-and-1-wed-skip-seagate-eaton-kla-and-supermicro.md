The AI hardware trade had another rough session on Wednesday: chip-equipment makers fell on a [US bill to curb tool sales to China](/news/2026-11-25/lam-research-applied-materials-and-asml-shares-slide-what-happened/), and memory stocks slid for a second day. Our screen asks which of the AI infrastructure stocks in the rout are businesses worth owning, and which only look cheap. Seagate sits **56.8% below its June high**, KLA 55.5% and Eaton 30.9%, though each turns AI demand into cash.

We screened chip, networking, power and data-center companies on revenue growth in their latest 12 months against a year earlier, from SEC filings, leaving out stocks we listed in the past 14 days. Then we judged each on the metrics that decide quality in its industry, against the peers `analyse.py` ranks it with.

```picks
like: STX, ETN, KLAC
skip: SMCI
```

## 3 stocks we like

### 1. Seagate Technology ($STX)
**Revenue growth: 44.4% in the 12 months to October 2026.** Seagate makes the hard drives that store most of the data AI models train on.

1. It's among the most profitable hardware makers we cover: operating margin was **39.6%** in the past 12 months, in the top tenth of 31 peers, and it rose 19.6 points over five years.
2. The profits are cash: free cash flow was 30.1% of revenue in the past 12 months, above its 23.0% two-year figure, so it's improving. In its fiscal first quarter, data-center revenue rose 63% and it guided to $4.8 billion of revenue for its fiscal second quarter, give or take $100 million, according to its release.
3. Per-share earnings compounded 21.0% a year over five years against a 14.0% peer median, even though revenue grew only 2.7% a year over that span: this is a cyclical business at a good point in the cycle.

**The price:** 10.0 times expected earnings, against a 16.4 times median for its peers.

**What would change our mind:** a quarter of falling data-center exabyte shipments, the first sign that cloud buyers have stocked up.

### 2. Eaton ($ETN)
**Revenue growth: 20.1% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Eaton sells the switchgear, transformers and power gear that data centers run on.

1. Growth is speeding up: revenue rose 14.0% a year over two years, against a 6.5% median for the 89 industrial stocks we cover, and 28% in the September quarter, a record [$8.95 billion](/news/2026-11-03/eaton-etn-beats-q3-estimates-and-raises-its-2026-forecast-again/).
2. Orders are running ahead of sales: its electrical backlog was **up 46%** from a year earlier, Eaton said in its results release.
3. Per-share earnings grew 24.5% a year over five years, in the top quarter of its peers, more than twice the 11.3% median.

**The price:** 20.0 times expected earnings, in line with the 20.1 times peer median, and its trailing multiple of 31.8 is below its own two-year median of 36.4.

**What would change our mind:** net debt of $19.6 billion is the weak spot; a slowdown in orders while that debt stays high would be the time to reassess.

### 3. KLA ($KLAC)
**Revenue growth: 15.8% in the 12 months to September 2026.** KLA makes the inspection and measurement tools that chip factories use to catch defects.

1. It turns a lot of revenue into cash: free cash flow was **29.3% of revenue** over two years, in the top quarter of the 25 chipmakers we cover.
2. Returns are exceptional: return on equity was 80.6%, in the top tenth, helped by buybacks that cut its share count 2.8% in two years.
3. Growth is about to pick up: it guided to revenue of $4.7 billion, give or take $200 million, for the December quarter, after $4.14 billion in September, according to its release.

**The price:** 21.5 times expected earnings, above the 18.1 times median for chipmakers, a premium its cash flow earns. Its trailing multiple of 33.6 is below its two-year median of 36.7. The share price reflects a 10-for-1 split in June.

**What would change our mind:** the China bill becoming law with tight limits, which would hit every tool maker's order books.

## 1 stock we'd skip

### Supermicro ($SMCI)
**Revenue growth: 156.5% in the 12 months to September 2026.** Supermicro assembles AI servers and full racks, mostly around Nvidia's chips.

1. The growth is real: September-quarter sales nearly quadrupled to $19.9 billion and it [raised its fiscal 2027 forecast](/news/2026-11-04/supermicro-smci-shares-jump-as-sales-hit-19-9-billion-and-it-raises-its-forecast/), with a backlog of more than $60 billion.
2. It still doesn't turn into cash: free cash flow was **negative $6.5 billion** in the past 12 months, minus 12% of revenue, in the bottom quarter of its peers, because building racks ties up money in inventory.
3. Its margins are thin and owners get diluted: gross margin was 11.2% over two years against a 43.2% median, and its share count rose 17.2%. On top of that, it fell 11.3% on 13 November on a [report of a Justice Department smuggling inquiry](/news/2026-11-13/supermicro-smci-shares-plunge-on-a-report-of-a-doj-smuggling-inquiry/).

**The price:** 3.7 times expected earnings, the cheapest tenth of its group: the market doubts the profits will become cash.

**What could go wrong (for our skip):** two quarters of positive operating cash flow would show the cash problem was timing.
