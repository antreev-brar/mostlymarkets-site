Sempra ($SRE) reported third-quarter adjusted earnings of **$1.40 a share** before the open on Thursday, up from $1.11 a year earlier and above the $1.20 Zacks consensus cited by AP, as its Texas utility earned more. Revenue of $3.19 billion missed the $3.35 billion Zacks estimate. Sempra said it now expects 2026 adjusted EPS at the high end of its $4.80 to $5.30 range and a roughly 20% increase in the capital plan of Oncor, its Texas power-line utility, yet the shares **fell 3.7% to $79.83** as the S&P 500 fell 4.4%.

## What stood out

**Texas is now the growth engine.** Earnings from Sempra Texas, mostly its stake in Oncor, rose to **$460 million** from $306 million, as Oncor recovers spending on its grid through rates. Oncor set a record peak demand of 33.3 gigawatts, the release said. Reuters headlined the quarter as a beat driven by Texas investments.

*Verdict: the part of Sempra investors want more of is delivering.*

**The spending plan is getting bigger, sooner.** Sempra said it expects Oncor's base capital plan, $47.5 billion over five years as of the summer, to rise by about 20%, with the full plan due in February, Seeking Alpha reported. For a regulated utility, more approved spending means a bigger rate base, the asset value regulators let it earn a return on.

## The outlook

Sempra expects 2026 adjusted EPS at the **high end of its $4.80 to $5.30** range and reaffirmed long-term growth of 7% to 9% a year. It expects to close the sale of a stake in its infrastructure arm in November, which it estimates will bring a gain of about $2.0 billion to $2.4 billion.

## Our take

We were impressed by the Texas numbers and the earlier-than-expected signal on Oncor's spending. On the other hand, revenue missed, and a bigger capital plan has to be funded, which for a utility with $35.6 billion of net debt by our data means more borrowing or more shares. Overall, a strong quarter that the market did not reward on a day it sold almost everything; we found no outlet that tied Sempra's fall to anything in the report.

Sempra is down 9.6% this year and 20% below its April high, and it trades at 15.2 times forward earnings against a utility median of 19.0 by our data, a discount that matches its revenue record: sales grew 3.8% a year over five years, the slowest of 31 utilities we track. The next test is the full Oncor plan in February.
