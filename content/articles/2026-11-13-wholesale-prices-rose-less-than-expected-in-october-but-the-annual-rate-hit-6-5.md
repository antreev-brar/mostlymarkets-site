The prices businesses charge each other **rose 0.3%** in October, the Labor Department said at 8:30 a.m. ET on Friday, a little below the 0.4% economists polled by Dow Jones expected, CNBC and the Washington Times reported. But the annual rate climbed to **6.5%**, the highest since November 2022, StockTitan and TokenPost reported, so the report eased the monthly worry without changing the trend.

## What the numbers said

Core producer prices, which leave out volatile food and energy, rose 0.3% on the month, in line with forecasts, and held at **5.0%** over the year, the same as in September, according to the Labor Department figures reported by Newsquawk and the Rio Times. The monthly pace slowed sharply from September, when headline producer prices jumped more than 1%.

Goods drove the month: goods prices rose 0.5% while services rose 0.2%, the Labor Department said. Producer prices matter to stock investors for two reasons: they feed into the consumer prices the Fed targets a month or two later, and they show whether companies can pass their own rising costs on.

## What it means for the Fed

The report landed three days after October's consumer price index, which rose 0.4% on the month with core prices up 0.3%, as Wolf Street and Inflation Guy reported. Taken together, the two reports keep a hike at the Fed's 8-9 December meeting as the base case. After the release, CME FedWatch data cited in Friday's reports put the odds of a quarter-point hike on 9 December at about 87% to 88%, with the rest on a half-point move.

October's jobs report, with 165,000 new payrolls against 65,000 forecast, put a December hike on the table, as we [reported on 6 November](/news/2026-11-06/employers-added-165-000-jobs-in-october-keeping-a-december-hike-in-play/), and nothing since has taken it off.

## How markets took it

Dow futures rose about 200 points after the release, CNBC reported, as Nvidia steadied after its chief executive's resignation and the monthly number came in a little soft. The 10-year Treasury yield stood at **5.14%** at 9 a.m. ET, up 0.04 percentage points on the day, in our data, and bitcoin was down 2.7% at about $69,490.

## What's next

The Fed decides on Wednesday 9 December. Before then, the open question is whether the cooler monthly pace in October holds: a second month at 0.3% or less would ease the case for a half-point move, while another jump like September's would revive it.
