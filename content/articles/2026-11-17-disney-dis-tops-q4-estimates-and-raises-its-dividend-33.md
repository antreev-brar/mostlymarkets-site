Walt Disney ($DIS) reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of **$25.9 billion** before the open on Tuesday, 17 November, up 16% year on year and above the $25.26 billion analysts expected, according to the Zacks consensus reported by Zacks and Breaking The News. Adjusted EPS of **$1.77** beat the $1.67 Zacks consensus and rose from $1.11 a year earlier. The stock was little changed in pre-market trading, at $118.50 as of 7:13 a.m. ET, down 0.2%, after trading 1.3% higher shortly before 6:45 a.m.

## What stood out

The headline growth is flattered by the calendar. This quarter ran 14 weeks against 13 a year earlier, and Disney estimates the extra week added about **$1.6 billion** of revenue and $0.17 of adjusted EPS, all of it landing in this quarter. Take that week out and revenue grew roughly 8%, close to the pace of the rest of the year.

The parks carried the quarter. Experiences revenue passed $10 billion for the first time, at $10.1 billion, and its operating income rose 20% to $2.25 billion, with the extra week supplying about half of that growth, the release showed. Global guests rose 4% excluding the extra week, led by Walt Disney World and Disneyland Paris, while the company said attendance at its Hong Kong and Shanghai parks "worsened during the quarter" and that it expects the pressure to continue in fiscal 2027.

Streaming more than doubled its profit, to $824 million, though about $160 million of that came from the extra week. Disney also said this is the last time it will break out streaming operating income, a disclosure investors have used to track the shift away from cable, Variety and TheWrap reported.

## The outlook

Disney kept its call for double-digit adjusted EPS growth in fiscal 2027, excluding the extra week, and guided total segment operating income up about **8%**, with most of the growth in the first half. It expects first-quarter segment operating income of about $5.4 billion. Cash content spending will rise about 9% to $26 billion, and it targets $9 billion of buybacks and about $11 billion of free cash flow.

The cost program put a number on last month's job-cut reports ([our story](/news/2026-11-03/disney-dis-plans-thousands-of-job-cuts-with-a-20-savings-target/)): about $3 billion of annual run-rate savings by fiscal 2029, which the company said will be used "primarily to fund future growth", with only a portion reaching profit.

## Our take

The beat is real but mostly calendar: on a 13-week basis revenue grew about 8%, and the 8% profit guide for 2027 is no acceleration. The good news is cash, with a 33% dividend increase and $9 billion of buybacks on top. The stock came into the report 0.8% below its 52-week closing high, so the bar was the 2027 guide, and the muted pre-market reaction says it cleared it without surprising anyone ([our preview](/news/2026-11-16/what-to-expect-from-disneys-dis-q4-earnings/)). Next quarter, watch whether the Asian parks drag spreads, and how investors track streaming without the profit line.
