Diamondback Energy ($FANG) agreed on Monday to buy subsidiaries of private shale driller Double Eagle for **$2.15 billion in cash** and about 15.56 million of its shares, a deal Reuters valued at $5.03 billion. The assets cover about 176,000 net acres in the Midland Basin of West Texas, with estimated 2027 oil output of about 35,000 barrels a day, the company said in its announcement, as Bloomberg also reported.

The price is about a tenth of Diamondback's own market value of $51.6 billion at Friday's close, in our data. Diamondback will fund the cash with cash on hand, its credit facility and a term loan, its announcement said, so it adds debt just as oil trades near $88 a barrel, down from a one-year high of $119. It is the company's second purchase from Double Eagle, after a deal that closed on 1 April 2025, its filings show. The shares were down 1.5% before the open as of 7:00 a.m. ET.

The deal is expected to close on 21 December. The Financial Times first reported the talks on Sunday.
