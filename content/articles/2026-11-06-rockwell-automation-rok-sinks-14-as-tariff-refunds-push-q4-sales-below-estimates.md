Rockwell Automation ($ROK) reported fiscal fourth-quarter sales of $2.33 billion before the open on Thursday, up 1% from a year earlier and short of the $2.39 billion Zacks consensus, after it set aside $85 million to refund customers for tariff surcharges. Adjusted earnings of $3.88 a share beat the $3.72 Zacks consensus. The shares **plunged 14.2% to $381.05**, Rockwell's biggest one-day move in a year and its worst day since 31 January 2024, on a day the S&P 500 fell 4.4%.

## What stood out

**The miss was a refund, not lost orders.** Rockwell received $103 million in refunds of tariffs it had paid under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), and promised to hand back the price increases it had charged customers to cover them, booking an **$85 million** reduction in sales, the release said. TIKR and Chartmill both tied the sales miss to that accrual. Without it, organic growth would have been about 7%.

*Verdict: an accounting hit that leaves underlying demand intact, which makes the size of the drop the real story.*

**Software & Control did the heavy lifting.** The segment that sells controllers and automation software grew sales **15%** and widened its margin to 34.0% from 31.2%. "We continue to gain share, strengthen our leadership in core markets, and expand our position in high-growth areas including semiconductor and data centers," Chief Executive Blake Moret said in the release. Lifecycle Services, its project business, shrank 9% after a divestiture.

## The outlook

For fiscal 2027, Rockwell guided to adjusted EPS of **$14.50 to $15.50**, up from $13.42 in fiscal 2026, and organic sales growth of 6% to 9%. The release notes that the growth range includes about 1 percentage point from the refund, because it lowers this year's base, so underlying growth is closer to 5% to 8%. Published estimates for the year ranged from about $14.88 to $15.03, which puts the midpoint about in line; Chartmill called the low end "modestly conservative".

## Our take

We were impressed by the margins: enterprise operating margin rose to 24.5% from 21.3%, and the software business keeps growing in the mid-teens. On the other hand, the reported sales line missed, and a guide that is merely in line was not enough for a stock that traded at 26 times forward earnings, against 21 for the median industrial by our data. Overall, a solid year judged on a noisy quarter.

The stock was already 10% below its June high before Thursday and now sits **23% below it**, and over five years its EPS has fallen 2.6% a year by our data, so it needed a clean beat to hold its premium. The next test is fiscal first-quarter results early in 2027, when the refund is out of the numbers and organic growth has to show the 6% to 9% pace.
