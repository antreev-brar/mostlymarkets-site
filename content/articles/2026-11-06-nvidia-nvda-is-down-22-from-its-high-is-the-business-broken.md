## How it got here

Nvidia began in 1993 making graphics chips for video games, and spent the next two decades building the software that let those chips run other kinds of maths. That bet turned it into the default supplier of chips to train and run AI models: revenue rose from $27.0 billion in the fiscal year to January 2023 to $215.9 billion in the year to January 2026, its filings show.

The latest turn came on 26 August. Nvidia reported second-quarter revenue of $96.2 billion, up 106%, and Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress said revenue should grow about 70% in fiscal 2028, the year to January 2028, against the 44% analysts had expected, CNBC and Fortune reported. The stock jumped 8.7% the next day and peaked at $239.24 on 6 October.

Since then it has fallen 22.0%, including 9.3% on Thursday 5 November, when investors bet the newly elected Democrats will slow data-center building ([our story](/news/2026-11-05/sandisk-micron-and-amd-shares-plunge-what-happened/)). **Is the business weakening, or only the story around it?**

## The business in one line

The world's biggest cloud and AI companies pay Nvidia for complete AI computers, chips, networking and software, and they come back every quarter for the next generation.

## The numbers that matter

### Growth

Revenue grew **66.9% a year** over five fiscal years, in the top tenth of the 25 chipmakers in our coverage, whose median is 14.2%. It is still speeding up in dollars: the latest quarter added $14.6 billion on the one before.

### Profitability

Its operating margin was **65.2%** in the 12 months to July 2026, against a chip median of 29.6%, and has widened 33.2 points in five years. The gross margin was 75.0% last quarter, its release said, and it guided to about 74.0% for this one.

### Cash

Here is the soft spot. Free cash flow was **$21.4 billion** in the quarter to July, down from $48.6 billion the quarter before, even as revenue rose 18%, our data from its filings shows. Operating cash fell to $24.1 billion from $50.3 billion as money owed by customers piled up; its top five direct customers accounted for 70% of receivables in July, up from 56% in January, its 10-Q shows, as Webull and Hudson Labs summarized.

### Returns

Return on equity was 84.2%, first among chipmakers, whose median is 23.1%. Nvidia earns more on each dollar it keeps in the business than almost any large company in our data.

### Balance sheet

Net debt was $10.9 billion in July, about a tenth of a year's operating profit. The bigger number sits off the balance sheet: supply commitments more than doubled to **$279 billion** from $119 billion in a quarter, mostly for memory, CNBC and Promptea reported.

## Why the stock is where it is

1. **The midterms put the 2027 build-out in doubt.** On 3 November Democrats won Congress and the Texas governorship, which went to a candidate who backs a pause on new data centers,, and Nvidia fell 9.3% on 5 November ([our debate story](/news/2026-11-06/bulls-vs-bears-on-the-midterm-ai-sell-off-how-many-data-centers-will-stall/)).
2. **China and policy keep getting worse.** A New York Times report on 29 October about an executive at a Singapore cloud customer, and a rule that cloud renters sell at least 51% of their services to non-Chinese firms, reported on 3 November, kept Washington's scrutiny on Nvidia ([our story](/news/2026-11-03/nvidia-nvda-tells-cloud-clients-to-sell-at-least-51-of-services-outside-china/)).
3. **Cash is lagging sales.** The fall in free cash flow and the rise in what customers owe feed the argument that Nvidia is helping to finance its own demand, a charge Fortune said Huang rejected on the August call.
4. **Older chips are aging fast.** On Friday Bloomberg reported that Microsoft is winding down a deal to rent AI capacity from Oracle that ran on older Nvidia chips no longer suited to its needs, which TradingKey also reported.

## Testing the worry

**The midterms: temporary, and so far contained.** Bernstein estimated on 6 November that new state rules cut credible US data-center capacity by only 2% to 4%, while UBS put 30% of 2028 additions at risk of delay, Benzinga and Finimize reported. Nvidia guided for supply-limited growth: Kress said customer forecasts pointed to growth doubling, CNBC reported, so a delay in some sites would more likely shift orders than cancel them.

**China: contained.** Nvidia's $108.0 billion forecast for the quarter to October already assumes no data-center compute sales in China, its release said. The policy risk is to clouds outside China, which its 10-Q flags.

**Cash: the worry that survives.** One quarter of slow payment is not a pattern, but it comes alongside $279 billion of supply commitments and three direct customers that made up 44% of first-half revenue, the 10-Q shows. If customers' own cash tightens, Nvidia's sales can keep rising while its cash does not.

**Aging chips: real, but slow.** Microsoft's move shows that each new generation shortens the life of the last, which helps Nvidia sell the next one but makes buyers more careful about what they pay.

## The moat

1. **Scale.** Nvidia guided to $108.0 billion of revenue for one quarter; AMD ($AMD), its nearest chip rival, guided to about $17 billion for its whole company in the December quarter ([our AMD recap](/news/2026-11-03/amd-amd-beats-q3-estimates-and-guides-q4-sales-to-17-billion/)).
2. **Pricing power.** A 73.0% gross margin over two years, against a chip median of 55.6%, says customers pay up rather than switch.
3. **Locked-up supply.** The $279 billion of commitments, mostly memory, secures parts every rival also needs.

The rivals: AMD earns a 17.7% operating margin over 12 months; Broadcom ($AVGO), which designs custom chips for clouds, earns 48.0%; and Alphabet ($GOOGL) has started selling its own TPU chips through partners, CNBC and The Next Web reported in October.

## The risks we'd watch

1. **Customer concentration.** One direct customer was 16% of revenue last quarter, the 10-Q shows. If it cut its orders by half, sales would fall about 8%.
2. **Supply commitments.** The $279 billion equals about 11 months of current sales. If fiscal 2028 growth came in at the 44% analysts expected before August rather than 70%, Nvidia would carry more memory than it needs.
3. **Policy.** Its 10-Q says a bill to bar cloud services to companies with a Chinese parent could have a material impact, and this week's election raised the odds of tougher rules at home.

At 15.4 times forward earnings, the market expects earnings per share of about $12.10 over the next year, roughly 53% above the $7.91 of the last 12 months, by our arithmetic. That is close to what the guidance implies; what investors won't pay for is growth after 2027. Analysts expect about $109 billion to $111 billion of revenue for the quarter to October, MarketBeat and Chartmill show.

## Our verdict

**Business: Strong. Price: Below its usual price.** Nvidia's growth, margins and returns lead every chipmaker we track, and nothing in the last month changed demand on the evidence available. The stock trades at 23.6 times trailing earnings, against a median of 45.9 since August 2024, because investors fear 2027 orders and doubt the cash. What would change our mind is a second quarter of free cash flow falling while sales rise, or a cut to the 70% fiscal 2028 forecast. Nvidia reports on Tuesday 17 November after the close.

The tension: the best business in chips is priced as if its best year is the last one.
