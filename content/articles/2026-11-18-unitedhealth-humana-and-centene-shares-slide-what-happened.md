## What happened
Shares of the biggest US health insurers fell for a second day on Wednesday. As of 12:15 p.m. ET, UnitedHealth ($UNH) was **down 8.4%** at $356.94, Humana ($HUM) 8.6% at $405.35 and Centene ($CNC) 6.0%, with Elevance Health ($ELV) down 6.1%, CVS Health ($CVS) 3.3% and Cigna ($CI) 2.6%.

The cause is a proposed rule from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), which it calls CRUSH, for Comprehensive Regulations to Uncover Suspicious Healthcare. Healthcare stocks dragged the Dow lower on Wednesday after the proposal, Yahoo Finance's live coverage reported, and the Motley Fool's midday report also tied the insurers' fall to it. Tuesday's drop, which our [morning story](/news/2026-11-17/why-unitedhealth-unh-stock-is-down-today/) could not pin on any news, has since been tied to the same rule by GuruFocus and Barchart.

## Why it matters
The rule's sharpest edge is in risk-adjustment audits, known as RADV, which check whether Medicare Advantage plans were paid for diagnoses their records support. CMS would extrapolate an error rate found in a sample of records across a whole contract, going back to **payment year 2018**, and without the adjustment insurers had argued for, Becker's Payer Issues and GuruFocus reported. CMS puts the recovery at about $9 billion a year on average; across Medicare, Medicaid and the ACA marketplaces, the government expects nearly $90 billion of savings over a decade, Becker's Hospital Review reported.

Mizuho analyst Ann Hynes named Humana and UnitedHealth as the most exposed, given how much of their business is Medicare Advantage, with CVS, Elevance, Centene and Molina behind them, The Fly and GuruFocus reported. The rule also reaches Medicaid and ACA marketplace enrollment, which is why Centene, which mainly sells those plans, fell with the Medicare-heavy insurers. UnitedHealth has not said what the rule could cost it, and the proposal is open for comment until 19 January 2027, so the final version could change.

## What the market is telling us
UnitedHealth's shares moved 5% or more on 5 of the last 252 sessions, and if Wednesday's fall holds it will be the stock's biggest one-day drop since 27 January, when a near-flat Medicare rate plan cut it 19.6%. The stock was up 18.1% this year at Tuesday's close of $389.68, 10.7% below its 52-week closing high of $436.35 from 21 July. Humana had closed at a 52-week high on Monday and was still up 73.1% this year at Tuesday's close. We think the selling prices a back-bill that no company has sized yet: the first insurer to put a number on its 2018-to-2024 exposure, or a sign of a court challenge, will tell us whether two days and $55 a share of UnitedHealth is too much or too little.
