The Dutch government will fight a proposed US law that would force it to tighten exports of ASML's ($ASML) chipmaking machines to China, Trade Minister Sjoerd Sjoerdsma told parliament on **Thursday**. The government opposes the reach of the MATCH Act beyond US borders, and "that is where the Netherlands draws the line," he said, according to Bloomberg and NL Times.

The bill would make allies such as the Netherlands and Japan match US curbs on China, or face US limits on their own equipment, including ASML's immersion DUV lithography tools. China makes up about **20% of ASML's sales**, Benzinga and NL Times reported, so a fifth of the company's revenue is at stake. The push-back escalates Tuesday's call by Dutch lawmakers, which [helped knock ASML, Lam Research and Applied Materials lower](/news/2026-11-25/lam-research-applied-materials-and-asml-shares-slide-what-happened/) on Wednesday. Sjoerdsma urged the European Union to look quickly at ways to shield itself if lobbying in Washington fails.

What isn't known yet is whether the bill passes Congress. ASML's US-listed shares were up 2.6% at $1,337.47 premarket as of 6:29 a.m. ET, with the wider chip rebound.
