California Attorney General Rob Bonta served Nvidia ($NVDA) with an investigative subpoena on Thursday, looking into whether its advanced chips or confidential information reached China and whether the company misled investors, CNBC and CBS News reported. A subpoena demands documents; it is not a finding of wrongdoing.

It adds a state inquiry to the Securities and Exchange Commission's probe of Nvidia's disclosures, [reported on Tuesday](/news/2026-11-17/sec-probes-nvidia-nvda-disclosures-and-muddy-waters-says-it-is-short/), and to shareholder lawsuits filed since Jensen Huang [resigned as chief executive](/news/2026-11-13/broadcom-avgo-and-amd-shares-jump-after-nvidias-ceo-resigns/) last week. Bonta had said in a Bloomberg Television interview that "red flags have been raised" over possible chip diversion to China.

The stock **closed down 3.3% at $145.98** on Thursday, its lowest close in a year, before the subpoena was reported, and slipped 0.9% after hours, by our price data. Nvidia has not responded publicly. What isn't known yet is what the documents show, or whether the board's new [export-control committee](/news/2026-11-17/nvidia-nvda-board-sets-up-export-control-committee-hours-before-its-q3-report/) finds anything first.
